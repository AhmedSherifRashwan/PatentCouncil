#### Field

The present disclosure relates to inflation valves for inflatable chambers that hold liquid sealant, such as bicycle inner tubes and tubeless bicycle tyre set-ups, and to valve cores for such inflation valves. It relates in particular to a valve core having a deflectable ring element that is deflected radially when the poppet is displaced beyond its normal stroke, so that sealant film is removed from the surfaces of the valve core in the course of ordinary inflation, and to the seat ring, the methods of manufacture and assembly, the kit, the inflatable chamber, the wheel and the bicycle described herein.

#### Background

Liquid sealant carried in an inflatable chamber is drawn into the valve core of the inflation valve on each inflation event, by the pressure surge at the opening of the valve core and by capillary action along the poppet stem. Between inflation events the liquid sealant dries or cures on the surfaces of the valve core as sealant film. Sealant film on the sealing surfaces holds the poppet off its seat, producing leak-down of the inflatable chamber through the valve core, and sealant film that bridges the moving parts prevents the poppet from opening when the inflation device is applied, producing blockage. These are the two failure modes referred to in this specification as leak-down and blockage; both are defined in the test definitions below.

Existing responses to these failure modes act after the sealant film has formed or only delay its formation. A removable valve core can be unscrewed and cleared with a tool or replaced, which requires the user to act and, where a tool is inserted into the valve core, risks damage to it. A low-adhesion coating on the sealing surfaces slows the build-up of sealant film but does not remove a film once it has formed. An inflation valve of wider bore reduces the rate at which its passage is obstructed but does not clean its sealing surfaces. None of these removes sealant film from the sealing surfaces of the valve core as a consequence of the ordinary use of the inflation valve.

#### Summary

The valve core described herein removes sealant film from its own surfaces during ordinary inflation, without a tool and without any action by the user beyond inflating the inflatable chamber. The valve core comprises a core body, a deflectable ring element carried by the core body, and a poppet having a sealing face and a deflecting formation. In a closed state the sealing face rests on a sealing edge, which is provided by the deflectable ring element or by a fixed seat, and a purge gap associated with the deflectable ring element is closed. In a normal-open state the poppet is displaced from the closed state through a normal stroke, the sealing face is lifted from the sealing edge so that a main passage is open, and the purge gap remains closed. In a purge state the poppet is displaced beyond the normal stroke into over-travel, the deflecting formation bears on the deflectable ring element and deflects it radially, and the purge gap opens, so that a purge flow path distinct from the main passage carries inflation gas across the surfaces on which sealant film accumulates and into the inflatable chamber. When the poppet returns from over-travel the deflectable ring element undergoes shape recovery, closing the purge gap, and in doing so moves across a surface of the poppet. The flow of inflation gas along the purge flow path is the air purge and the movement of the recovering deflectable ring element across the poppet is the snap-back wipe; the cleaning action of the valve core is the air purge, the snap-back wipe, or both.

Over-travel is produced by the pressure differential across the poppet exceeding an over-travel threshold that is higher than the crack pressure at which the normal stroke begins, so that the purge state is reached during inflation with an inflation device that raises the pressure differential above the over-travel threshold; in other embodiments over-travel is produced by mechanical depression of the poppet. In the illustrated embodiment the deflectable ring element is a seat ring that also provides the sealing edge, is formed from a superelastic alloy, and has a convoluted wall that converts the axial load applied by the deflecting formation into radial deflection. The seat ring, the deflecting formation, the threshold-setting arrangement, the purge gap and the purge flow path may each take the alternative forms described herein.

This specification also describes the seat ring as a component and as part of a kit, a method of manufacturing the seat ring, a method of assembling the valve core, a method of inflating an inflatable chamber, an inflation valve comprising the valve core, and an inflatable chamber, a wheel and a bicycle incorporating the inflation valve.

As used herein, the "normal stroke" means the displacement of the poppet from the closed state, beginning at the crack pressure, over which the main passage is open and the purge gap remains closed. In the illustrated embodiment the purge shoulder is spaced from the deflectable ring element throughout the normal stroke; in other embodiments described below the purge shoulder contacts the deflectable ring element towards the end of the normal stroke without deflecting it so as to open the purge gap. "Over-travel" means movement of the poppet beyond the normal stroke, during which the purge shoulder bears on the deflectable ring element and deflects it radially so as to open the purge gap. In the illustrated embodiment over-travel occurs when the pressure differential exceeds the over-travel threshold; in the embodiments so described below it is produced by mechanical depression of the poppet. The normal stroke, over-travel and the purge state are defined by the position of the poppet and by the resulting condition of the deflectable ring element and the purge gap, whatever produces the displacement of the poppet. The "over-travel distance" means the axial distance the poppet moves beyond the end of the normal stroke during over-travel.

@@ REPLACE [0013]
The valve core has a closed state, a normal-open state and a purge state. In the illustrated embodiment the state is determined by the pressure differential. In the "closed state", which prevails when the pressure differential is below the crack pressure or when the lock nut is engaged, the sealing face of the poppet rests on the sealing edge, which is provided by the seat ring or, in embodiments having a fixed seat, by the fixed seat; the main passage is closed and the purge gap is closed. In the "normal-open state", which prevails when the pressure differential is at or above the crack pressure and does not exceed the over-travel threshold, the poppet is displaced from the closed state within the normal stroke, the sealing face is lifted from the sealing edge so that the main passage is open, the deflectable ring element is not deflected so as to open the purge gap, the purge shoulder in the illustrated embodiment being spaced from it, and the purge gap remains closed. In the "purge state", which prevails when the poppet is displaced beyond the normal stroke into over-travel, in the illustrated embodiment because the pressure differential exceeds the over-travel threshold, the purge shoulder bears on the deflectable ring element, the deflectable ring element is in radial deflection, the purge gap is open, and the air purge occurs while the main passage remains open. When the poppet returns from over-travel, in the illustrated embodiment when the pressure differential falls below the over-travel threshold, the deflectable ring element undergoes "shape recovery", meaning its return towards its at-rest shape, the purge gap closes, and the valve core returns through the normal-open state to the closed state as the pressure differential falls below the crack pressure.

In the embodiments having a purge gap, which include the illustrated embodiment, the radial deflection opens a purge flow path; in the wipe-only embodiment described below, in which the periphery of the deflectable ring element is sealed, the radial deflection and the shape recovery that follows it produce the snap-back wipe without opening a purge flow path. The deflectable ring element is described below with reference to the seat ring of the illustrated embodiment; the term is not limited to the material, the wall form or the manner of retention of that seat ring.

@@ REPLACE [0020]
As used herein, "radial deflection" means elastic deformation of the wall of the deflectable ring element, produced by the purge shoulder during over-travel, that opens the purge gap or, in the wipe-only embodiment, that is followed by the shape recovery which produces the snap-back wipe. Radial deflection is a deformation of the deflectable ring element while it remains retained in the valve core; it is not a bodily displacement of the element as a whole. The radial deflection may be outward, inward, or non-uniform around the circumference of the deflectable ring element. The sense of radial deflection that opens a given purge gap is the sense that moves the portion of the deflectable ring element bounding that purge gap away from the opposed surface against which it bounds the purge gap, as described in relation to the purge state.

In every embodiment the purge shoulder is positioned along the poppet so that, as the poppet moves in the opening direction, the purge shoulder moves towards the surface of the deflectable ring element on which it bears, and is spaced from that surface in the closed state by an axial clearance equal to the normal stroke. The purge shoulder is a deflecting formation, meaning a formation that moves with the poppet and that contacts the deflectable ring element only during over-travel; the term "purge shoulder" is defined by that function and is not limited to the annular form of the purge shoulder of the illustrated embodiment.

As used herein, "cleaning action" means an action by which the valve core removes sealant film from its surfaces without a tool or user action. The cleaning actions described herein are the air purge, the snap-back wipe, or both. The "air purge" means the flow of inflation gas, at the pressure of the inflation device, along the purge flow path during the purge state, across the surfaces of the valve core on which sealant film accumulates and into the inflatable chamber. The inflation gas may be air supplied by a floor pump or another inflation device, or another gas supplied by a compressed-gas inflation device, such as a compressed-CO2 inflation device supplying carbon dioxide as the inflation gas. The "snap-back wipe" means the movement of the deflectable ring element across a surface of the poppet as the deflectable ring element undergoes shape recovery when the pressure differential falls below the over-travel threshold, by which movement partially cured sealant film is sheared from that surface.

## Rationale

The seven findings on the illustrated mechanism (F-28-2, F-28-3, F-30-6, F-32-20, F-36-1, F-gate14-2, F-gate14-4) all turn on two inventor facts the dossier does not contain: the prototype's axial layout and the sense in which the surface bounding its purge gap moves. I did not choose either, because any choice is an invented fact about the prototype that cannot be corrected after filing. Instead the text now states rules that are necessarily true of any working embodiment (the deflecting formation approaches its load-receiving surface in the opening direction; the bounding surface of the purge gap moves away from the opposed surface), re-pairs every sense-and-location statement so that none describes a gap its stated sense cannot open, states the closed-state seal and the route past the ring retention as confirmed species, and says plainly which prototype values were not measured. The inventor's own words ("on the chamber side of the sealing face", "deflected outward") are kept where they came from the disclosure, so that claim 3 and claim 29 keep their support, and the apparent conflict is surfaced as disclosure requests rather than silently resolved.

F-28-1 pulls in the opposite direction from EN-9: one wants the ranges freed from the superelastic convoluted ring, the other wants no assertion that they hold for other materials. Both are met by stating the ranges as the inventor stated them, for the seat ring generally, and stating that only the NiTi example was built; the parameter relationships then govern adaptation. The redefinition of seat ring wall thickness follows the same logic.

The unity, novelty and manufacturing findings (F-30-7, F-32-1, F-32-2) are answered with disclosure that ties the seat ring as a component, and the route by which it is made, to the features of the valve core it serves, and that states the distinction the Litigator identified as surviving (mechanical loading and recovery at service temperature, a seat for a moving poppet). No claim is rewritten; the narrowing the rulings call for is recorded for the claims drafter.

The sub-threshold contact of F-33-2 is drawn from the confirmed seat-ring-stiffness species, where contact before deflection is inherent; generalising it to every embodiment is the least that closes the light-contact escape, and it is flagged for the inventor because Gate 2 ran unattended.

The quench genus was declined: only a water quench is disclosed and quench rate affects the transformation temperatures of the alloy, so the genus is not a routine generalisation.

Drawing sheets and the field/background/summary were in the same finding; the latter is drafted here, the former cannot be produced by text and is routed.

#### A. Purge mechanism and enablement (highest priority — the application cannot be enabled without these)

**Q1.** Where is the purge gap? §2 places it "between the ring and the stem bore"; §3(6) places it "between the seat ring outer surface and the core body bore".

- (b) Between the seat ring and the stem bore, as §2
- (c) Both — a combined path through more than one clearance
- (d) Other — please describe
*Why it matters:* resolves the disclosure's central inconsistency; without an answer the purge gap cannot be described as enabled (01 Q1; 03 Q2). **[blocks P3, P4, P5]**

**Q2.** The seat ring is pressed into the core body with about 0.03 mm interference (§7) and is said to flex radially outward (§2) to open the purge gap. An interference fit at the outer surface would ordinarily close, not open, a gap there. How does the gap actually open?
- (a) Only part of the ring's length is in the interference fit; the free portion deflects outward into a relief in the core body bore
- (b) The ring lifts axially off the internal shoulder during over-travel, breaking the interference land
- (c) The core body bore has a counterbore or relief beyond the interference land where the gap forms
- (d) Other — please describe, in words, the complete air path from the supply side through the purge gap into the inflatable chamber

**Q3.** Which cleaning action is actually necessary to keep the valve sealing and opening — the air purge, the snap-back wipe, or both? The bench test did not separate them.

## Rationale

I organised the questionnaire by theme rather than by source document, because the inventor will answer it once and the themes map to how a single answer resolves several linked open items across `01`, `03` and `04` at once — for example, Q1 and Q2 together resolve 01's Q1/Q2, 03's Q2, and the definitional gaps in 04 for "purge gap" and "radial deflection" in one pass. Where several source questions concerned the same underlying fact I merged them into a single question rather than repeating near-duplicates, so the inventor is not asked the same thing twice in different words; the parenthetical cross-references preserve the audit trail back to the originating prompt.

I put the two enablement-blocking questions (Q1, Q2) and the nucleus-defining question (Q3) first, ahead of scope-broadening questions, because until they are answered the Description cannot be written as enabled at all — that is a harder block than any claim-breadth question. After that I ordered by the size of the claim-scope consequence: the genus questions the inventor's own §6 alternatives raise (Q10, Q12, Q17, Q18) sit ahead of dimensional and manufacturing detail, because a genus answered narrowly forecloses design-around cover that Phase 3 cannot invent on its own.

I wrote a question as multiple-choice wherever the credible answer space is genuinely bounded (essential vs. genus; built-and-tested vs. not; include vs. drop an alternative). Where the answer is an open-ended fact the disclosure simply omits — a spring rate, a bore diameter, a sealant product name — I left it as a direct request rather than forcing artificial options, consistent with the task's "where possible" instruction; inventing plausible multiple-choice distractors for a missing measurement would risk suggesting a value the inventor has not given.

I did not add any question or option that supplies a fact the disclosure lacks; every option offered is either what the disclosure already states, a structurally distinct alternative the disclosure or an earlier phase output has already named (e.g. Alternatives A and B, other threshold means raised as a possibility in `01`/`03`), or an explicit "unsure"/"other" escape. I did not answer any question myself.

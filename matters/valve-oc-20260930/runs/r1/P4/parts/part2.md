## Deliverable

### Description

#### Operation: the states of the valve core

The valve core operates in a closed state, a normal-open state and a purge state, and passes through a recovery from the purge state back to the closed state. The state that the valve core occupies is determined by the pressure differential across the poppet (40), that is by the pressure on the supply side less the pressure on the chamber side, acting against the return spring (50). FIG. 7 shows the sequence of states as a diagram, with the condition that governs each transition marked on the corresponding arrow. Control of the valve core is entirely mechanical; no electrical or electronic control is present.

In the closed state (FIG. 1) the pressure differential is below the crack pressure, the sealing face (42) of the poppet (40) rests on the sealing edge (34) of the seat ring (30), the main passage (64) is closed and the purge gap (60) is closed. In the normal-open state (FIG. 2) the pressure differential is at or above the crack pressure and below the over-travel threshold; the poppet (40) is displaced from the closed state by the normal stroke, the main passage (64) is open, the purge shoulder (44) is spaced from the seat ring (30), and the purge gap (60) remains closed. In the purge state (FIG. 3) the pressure differential exceeds the over-travel threshold, the over-travel threshold being higher than the crack pressure; the poppet (40) is in over-travel beyond the normal stroke, the purge shoulder (44) bears on the seat ring (30) and deflects it radially, and the purge gap (60), which is a flow path separate from the main passage (64), is open. The purge gap (60) is therefore closed in the closed state and in the normal-open state, and is open only in the purge state.

Recovery follows the purge state when the pressure differential falls below the over-travel threshold, as occurs when the inflation device is removed from the supply side. The return spring (50) returns the poppet (40) towards the closed state, the seat ring (30) undergoes shape recovery, the snap-back wipe occurs and the purge gap (60) closes (FIG. 4). The lock nut (70) may be tightened by hand to hold the poppet (40) in the closed state irrespective of the pressure differential, for storage and transport; FIG. 7 shows this as the locked condition. The lock nut (70) holds the poppet (40) in the closed state and not in over-travel, so that the seat ring (30) is not held in radial deflection while the inflation valve is stored.

The main passage (64) and the purge gap (60) are distinct flow paths. The main passage (64) is the passage past the sealing face (42) that carries inflation gas into the inflatable chamber whenever the sealing face (42) is lifted from the sealing edge (34), that is in the normal-open state and in the purge state. The purge gap (60) is a passage bounded at least in part by a surface of the seat ring (30); it is closed while the seat ring (30) is in its at-rest form and is opened only by the radial deflection of the seat ring (30). Inflation gas passing through the purge gap (60) follows the purge flow path (62), which is described below.

#### The crack pressure and the normal stroke

When an inflation device is coupled to the supply side and the pressure differential reaches the crack pressure, the force on the poppet (40) overcomes the first spring stage (52) of the return spring (50) and the poppet (40) moves along the poppet axis towards the chamber side, lifting the sealing face (42) from the sealing edge (34). The crack pressure is set by the first spring stage (52), which is the lower-rate stage of the return spring (50) and which alone resists the poppet (40) during the normal stroke. The normal stroke is the displacement of the poppet (40) from the closed state up to the position at which the purge shoulder (44) would first contact the seat ring (30). In the prototype the crack pressure is 1.0 bar and the normal stroke is 0.6 mm; the range of the crack pressure is given below together with the other operating parameters.

Throughout the normal stroke the purge shoulder (44) remains axially spaced from the seat ring (30) by an axial clearance, shown in FIG. 2 as the residual clearance between the purge shoulder (44) and the seat ring (30) at the end of the normal stroke. The seat ring (30) is not loaded by the poppet (40) during the normal stroke, retains its at-rest form, and the purge gap (60) remains closed. Inflation gas flows through the main passage (64) only. Until the over-travel threshold is reached, therefore, the valve core admits inflation gas and seals in the same manner as a conventional valve core, the seat ring (30) serving only to seat the sealing face (42).

#### The over-travel threshold and over-travel

When the pressure differential exceeds the over-travel threshold, the poppet (40) moves beyond the end of the normal stroke into over-travel. The over-travel threshold is set by the second spring stage (54) of the return spring (50), which has a higher rate than the first spring stage (52) and which the poppet (40) engages at the end of the normal stroke. In the prototype the over-travel threshold is 3.5 bar and the over-travel distance is 0.5 mm. The normal stroke and the over-travel are two successive ranges of movement of the one poppet (40) along the same axis, distinguished by the pressure differential at which each occurs and by whether the purge shoulder (44) bears on the seat ring (30); no second closure member and no second seating surface is involved, and the seat ring (30) remains the valve seat member throughout.

The over-travel distance is the axial distance that the poppet (40) moves beyond the end of the normal stroke. In some embodiments the over-travel distance is limited by an over-travel stop in the core body (20) that the poppet (40) meets at the end of the over-travel; in other embodiments it is limited by the second spring stage (54) reaching its solid length, or by the resistance of the seat ring (30) to further radial deflection.

The second spring stage (54) is one species of threshold-setting arrangement. In the illustrated embodiment the return spring (50) is a single compression spring having the first spring stage (52) and the second spring stage (54). Once the purge shoulder (44) bears on the seat ring (30), the resistance of the seat ring (30) to radial deflection adds to the force that the pressure differential must overcome, so that the return spring (50) and the seat ring (30) may each contribute to the over-travel threshold. Other threshold-setting arrangements, in which the step in resisting force at the end of the normal stroke is provided by a separate auxiliary spring engaged after a lost-motion clearance, by a detent, or by the seat ring (30) alone with a single-rate return spring (50), are described below with reference to FIG. 14.

The over-travel threshold is higher than the crack pressure, so that there is a normal-open state in which the valve core admits inflation gas without entering the purge state, and the seat ring (30) is deflected only when the inflation device raises the pressure differential above the over-travel threshold. In the prototype the crack pressure is 1.0 bar and the over-travel threshold is 3.5 bar. In the test described in Example 1, a floor pump inflating an inner tube from fully deflated to 6 bar produced over-travel on every inflation event. In some embodiments the over-travel threshold is set close to the crack pressure, so that the purge state is reached on substantially every opening of the valve core and the normal stroke is short; in such embodiments the second spring stage (54) may be omitted and the return spring (50) has a single rate.

#### The purge state

When the poppet (40) enters over-travel, the purge shoulder (44), which lies on the chamber side of the sealing face (42), comes into contact with the seat ring (30) and bears on it with a force that rises with the pressure differential. The seat ring (30) is retained in the core body (20) against the internal shoulder (24), and the axial load applied by the purge shoulder (44) is converted by the convoluted wall (32) of the seat ring (30) into radial deflection of the wall. In the illustrated embodiment the radial deflection is an elastic deformation of the wall of the seat ring (30) while the seat ring (30) remains retained by the core body (20) at the internal shoulder (24), rather than a bodily movement of the seat ring (30) relative to the core body (20).

The radial deflection opens the purge gap (60). The purge gap (60) is closed while the seat ring (30) is in its at-rest form, and opens when the radial deflection moves a surface of the seat ring (30) away from the surface against which it bounds the purge gap (60). FIG. 3 shows the seat ring (30) in radial deflection, its at-rest outline repeated in dashed line, with the purge gap (60) open; FIG. 6B shows the same region enlarged, with the purge gap opening marked. In the embodiment of FIGS. 1 to 4 the purge gap (60) is located at the outer surface of the seat ring (30), between that surface and the core body bore (22), and, as described above, the interference fit between the seat ring (30) and the core body (20) may be confined to an axial band at the internal shoulder (24) so that the remainder of the outer surface of the seat ring (30) is free of the core body bore (22) (FIG. 6A). The purge gap opening is the radial size of the purge gap (60) when the seat ring (30) is fully deflected in the purge state; in the prototype the purge gap opening is 0.15 mm.

The sense of the radial deflection that opens the purge gap (60) depends on which surfaces bound the purge gap (60). The radial deflection may be radially outward, as described for the embodiment of FIG. 3; it may be radially inward at the surface of the seat ring (30) that bounds the purge gap (60), for example by flattening of the crest of a convolution under axial load; or it may be an ovalising deflection, produced where the purge shoulder (44) contacts the seat ring (30) at discrete points around its circumference, that moves the wall outward at the points of contact and inward between them and so opens the purge gap (60) over arcs of the circumference. The locations at which the purge gap (60) may be formed, and the sense of radial deflection that each requires, are described below with reference to FIG. 10 and FIG. 13.

In embodiments having a fixed seat (130) and a separate wiper ring (132) (FIG. 10), the sealing face (42) seats on the fixed seat (130) in the closed state, and in the purge state the purge shoulder (44) bears on and deflects the wiper ring (132), which is the deflectable ring element of those embodiments; the purge gap (60) is then bounded by a surface of the wiper ring (132) and is opened by its radial deflection. Everything said in this section of the seat ring (30) as the element deflected by the purge shoulder (44) applies to the wiper ring (132) in those embodiments.

#### The purge flow path and the air purge

With the purge gap (60) open and inflation gas being supplied to the supply side, inflation gas flows from the supply side through the purge gap (60) along the purge flow path (62) to the chamber side of the seat ring (30) and into the inflatable chamber, at the same time as inflation gas flows through the main passage (64). FIG. 3 shows both flows with arrows. The purge flow path (62) crosses the surfaces of the valve core on which sealant film accumulates, in the illustrated embodiment the outer surface of the seat ring (30) and the adjoining region of the core body bore (22), and the inflation gas carries sealant film from those surfaces into the inflatable chamber. This flow is the air purge.

The air purge occurs at the pressure of the inflation device, in the direction of inflation, so that sealant film removed by the air purge is carried away from the valve core into the inflatable chamber and not towards the inflation device. The air purge continues for as long as the pressure differential remains above the over-travel threshold and inflation gas is supplied, and ends when the pressure differential falls and the purge gap (60) closes.

The inflation gas of the air purge is any gas supplied by the inflation device: air from a floor pump, a hand pump or a compressor, or gas from a compressed-CO2 inflation device. The air purge occurs with any inflation device that raises the pressure differential across the poppet (40) above the over-travel threshold. The over-travel threshold is accordingly chosen to lie above the crack pressure and within the pressure differential that the intended inflation device produces. In the test described in Example 1 the inflation device was a floor pump.

In the illustrated embodiment the purge flow path (62) runs alongside the main passage (64), from the supply side through the purge gap (60) to the chamber side of the seat ring (30). Other purge flow paths, through purge ports in the wall of the core body (20) or along flutes in the core body bore (22), are described below with reference to FIG. 13.

#### Removal of the inflation device: shape recovery and the snap-back wipe

When the inflation device is removed from the supply side, the pressure differential collapses. The return spring (50) returns the poppet (40) towards the closed state, the load applied by the purge shoulder (44) to the seat ring (30) is removed, and the seat ring (30) undergoes shape recovery to its at-rest form, closing the purge gap (60). As it recovers, the seat ring (30) moves across the sealing face (42) of the poppet (40), or across an adjacent surface of the poppet (40), and shears from that surface liquid sealant that has partially cured on it. This movement of the recovering seat ring (30) across a surface of the poppet (40) is the snap-back wipe. FIG. 4 shows the seat ring (30) during shape recovery, with the direction of its movement across the sealing face (42) indicated by an arrow and the purge gap (60) closing. The sealing face (42) then re-seats on the sealing edge (34) and the valve core is again in the closed state.

The relative movement that produces the snap-back wipe is driven by the shape recovery of the seat ring (30), which is held in the core body (20), and not only by the return stroke of the poppet (40): the wall of the seat ring (30) returns radially across the surface of the poppet (40) as the poppet (40) passes it. In the embodiment of FIGS. 1 to 4 the element that performs the snap-back wipe is the seat ring (30) itself, whose sealing edge (34) seats the sealing face (42) in the closed state. The snap-back wipe therefore acts on the sealing face (42), which is the surface that seals, and no separate part that could itself accumulate sealant film is needed to perform it. The sealing face (42) and the sealing edge (34) are the surfaces found clean at the end of the test described in Example 1.

In the illustrated embodiment the region of the seat ring (30) that moves across the poppet (40) during the snap-back wipe is the region of the sealing edge (34), and the surface of the poppet (40) that is wiped is the sealing face (42), as shown in FIG. 4.

The strength of the snap-back wipe depends on the speed and force of the shape recovery, which depend on the material of the seat ring (30). Where the seat ring (30) is formed from a superelastic alloy, the shape recovery is rapid and the recovery force is high, so that the recovering seat ring (30) shears partially cured sealant film from the surface of the poppet (40) rather than riding over it; in the prototype a snap is audible on removal of the inflation device. A superelastic alloy is preferred for the seat ring (30) for this reason. Where the seat ring (30) is formed from a resilient polymer such as PEEK, the shape recovery is slower and the recovery force lower, so that the snap-back wipe is weaker; the movement of the recovering polymer seat ring (30) across the surface of the poppet (40) is nonetheless a snap-back wipe as described herein, and the air purge acts independently of it. A soft sealing element that deforms under the sealing face (42) has, by contrast, the disadvantage that liquid sealant embeds itself in the deformed sealing surface as it cures instead of being sheared from the sealing face (42).

#### The two cleaning actions

The valve core thus cleans its sealing surfaces twice in each inflation event that reaches the purge state: once by the air purge while the purge gap (60) is open, and once by the snap-back wipe as the seat ring (30) recovers. Both cleaning actions are produced by the poppet (40) moving into and out of over-travel under the pressure differential applied by the inflation device; neither requires a tool, a separate actuator or any action by the user beyond inflating the inflatable chamber. The air purge acts on the surfaces along the purge flow path (62); the snap-back wipe acts on the surface of the poppet (40) that the recovering seat ring (30) crosses.

In the embodiment of FIGS. 1 to 4 the cleaning action comprises both the air purge and the snap-back wipe. The two actions are, however, independent of one another. The snap-back wipe occurs whenever a deflectable ring element held in the core body (20) is deflected by the purge shoulder (44) in over-travel and undergoes shape recovery when the pressure differential falls, whether or not inflation gas flows through the purge gap (60) while the deflectable ring element is deflected. The air purge occurs whenever the purge gap (60) is open while inflation gas is supplied, whether or not the recovering deflectable ring element wipes the poppet (40). Accordingly, in some embodiments the cleaning action is provided by the air purge and the snap-back wipe together; in some embodiments it is provided principally by the air purge, for example where the deflectable ring element is of a resilient polymer whose shape recovery produces only a weak snap-back wipe; and in some embodiments it is provided by the snap-back wipe alone, the deflectable ring element being sealed at its periphery so that no inflation gas flows past it while it is deflected, and the radial deflection in over-travel followed by the shape recovery on release producing the snap-back wipe without an air purge. The test described in Example 1 shows the combined effect of the two cleaning actions in the prototype; it does not apportion that effect between them.

#### Over-travel by other means and purge during deflation

In the embodiments described above, over-travel is produced by the pressure differential exceeding the over-travel threshold. In some embodiments over-travel is produced, in addition or instead, by mechanical depression of the poppet (40): a depressor in the head of the inflation device, or in an adaptor fitted between the inflation device and the supply side, pushes the poppet (40) beyond the normal stroke into over-travel when the inflation device is coupled, whatever the pressure differential. In such embodiments the purge state lasts for as long as the inflation device is coupled, and the second spring stage (54) may be omitted. In some embodiments the poppet (40) is pressed into over-travel by hand, with the lock nut (70) released.

The purge state may also be reached during deflation of the inflatable chamber. When the poppet (40) is pressed into over-travel while the inflatable chamber is pressurised and no inflation device is supplying gas, the purge gap (60) opens and gas from the inflatable chamber flows through the purge gap (60) in the direction from the chamber side to the supply side, carrying sealant film out of the valve core. The purge flow path (62) may therefore carry flow in either direction. In some embodiments the purge state is reached during deflation as well as during inflation; in other embodiments it is reached during deflation only, each inflation event using the normal stroke alone.

#### Inflation events and inflation cycles

An inflation event is the period from coupling of an inflation device to the supply side until its removal. Within one inflation event that reaches the purge state, the valve core passes from the closed state through the normal-open state to the purge state as the pressure differential rises past the crack pressure and then past the over-travel threshold, remains in the purge state while the pressure differential exceeds the over-travel threshold, and returns through recovery to the closed state when the inflation device is removed. The two cleaning actions therefore occur once per inflation event: the air purge during the purge state and the snap-back wipe on recovery. In the test described in Example 1, in which each inflation event inflated an inner tube from fully deflated to 6 bar with a floor pump, the purge state was reached in every inflation event, as indicated by the snap audible on removal of the inflation device.

An inflation event in which the pressure differential does not exceed the over-travel threshold, for example where the inflation device produces a lower pressure differential, is completed in the normal-open state: the valve core admits inflation gas through the main passage (64) and closes on removal of the inflation device without entering the purge state and without the seat ring (30) being deflected. Whether a given inflation event reaches the purge state depends on the pressure differential that the inflation device produces across the poppet (40) during that event.

An inflation cycle is one repetition of an inflation event, a hold period at the resulting chamber pressure and deflation of the inflatable chamber. Over repeated inflation cycles, liquid sealant drawn into the valve core during one inflation event, and dried or partially cured during the following hold period, is acted on by the air purge and the snap-back wipe of the next inflation event that reaches the purge state. The behaviour of the prototype and of a conventional valve core over forty such inflation cycles is reported in Example 1 and Comparative Example 1.

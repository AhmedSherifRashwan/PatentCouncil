## Deliverable

### Description

#### Materials of the deflectable ring element

The deflectable ring element is defined above by what it does: it is carried by the valve core, it is deflected radially by the purge shoulder (44) during over-travel so as to open a purge flow path, and it undergoes shape recovery towards its at-rest form when the pressure differential falls. Any material that provides a recoverable radial deflection of the size needed to open the purge gap (60), and that returns the ring element to its at-rest form when the load of the purge shoulder (44) is removed, may be used. The material chosen determines the force and speed of the shape recovery, and therefore the strength of the snap-back wipe, and it determines the wall geometry needed to keep the strain in the wall within what the material recovers. The materials are described here for the seat ring (30) of the illustrated embodiment; they apply equally to the wiper ring (132) of the fixed seat and wiper ring embodiment described below, subject to the exceptions stated.

In the preferred embodiments the seat ring (30) is formed from a superelastic alloy. The seat ring (30) of the worked example is formed from a nickel-titanium alloy (NiTi) having a nickel content of nominally 55.8 wt%, from drawn tube stock. The alloy is specified to have an austenite finish temperature below 5 °C, so that the seat ring (30) is fully austenitic, and behaves superelastically, at the temperatures at which the inflation valve is used. A superelastic alloy recovers large strains on unloading by a reversible transformation between martensite and austenite, so that a thin wall of the alloy can be deflected radially by an amount of the order of the purge gap opening and return to its at-rest form within the closing movement of the poppet (40), with a recovery that is fast and carries force. That recovery gives the seat ring (30) its strongest snap-back wipe: the recovering seat ring (30) shears partially cured sealant film from the surface of the poppet (40) it crosses rather than riding over it. A sealing element soft enough to deform under the sealing face (42), such as an elastomeric one, by contrast lets liquid sealant embed itself in the sealing surface, where neither the air purge nor a wipe reaches it; the superelastic alloy provides radial compliance without a soft sealing surface.

The austenite finish temperature of the worked example is an upper limit chosen so that the seat ring (30) is superelastic across riding temperatures. Below the martensite start temperature of the alloy, martensite forms on cooling and shape recovery may be impaired until the alloy is warmed again above its austenite finish temperature. The margin between the martensite start temperature and the lowest temperature the seat ring (30) reaches in service, including transient chilling by the expansion of gas from a compressed-gas inflation device, is therefore a design consideration. In some embodiments the alloy is selected, by its nickel content or by the shape-setting treatment described below, to have a lower austenite finish temperature and hence a wider margin. No value below 5 °C is specified herein, and the behaviour of the seat ring (30) when chilled by a compressed-gas inflation device was not tested. Where a superelastic alloy is used, the austenite finish temperature is a property of that alloy species; it has no counterpart for a resilient polymer or for a resilient metal that does not transform, and every reference herein to the austenite finish temperature is to be read as conditioned on the superelastic alloy species.

In some embodiments the superelastic alloy is a binary NiTi alloy, as in the worked example. In other embodiments it is a ternary NiTi alloy: a NiTiCu alloy, which gives a narrower transformation hysteresis and more stable behaviour over repeated cycles, or a NiTiFe or NiTiCr alloy, which gives a lower austenite finish temperature. The transformation temperatures and the plateau stress of the alloy chosen interact with the over-travel threshold in embodiments in which the stiffness of the deflectable ring element contributes to the threshold-setting arrangement, as described below. Copper-based superelastic alloys, such as Cu-Al-Mn and Cu-Al-Ni alloys, may also be used, but copper alloys are attacked by ammonia, so these alloys are contemplated only for use with an ammonia-free liquid sealant.

In other embodiments the deflectable ring element is formed from a resilient metal that recovers its shape elastically without a phase transformation. Examples are stainless steel of spring temper, cold-worked austenitic stainless steel, precipitation-hardened stainless steel such as 17-7PH, and beta-titanium alloys. Stainless steel of spring temper is the disclosed material of the wiper ring (132) of the fixed seat and wiper ring embodiment. The recoverable strain of such a metal is much lower than that of a superelastic alloy, so a deflectable ring element of resilient metal is given a thinner wall, a smaller radial deflection, or a slotted or cellular wall as described below, so that the purge gap opening is reached within the elastic range of the material. A resilient metal has no austenite finish temperature, and its recovery does not depend on temperature in the manner of a superelastic alloy.

In further embodiments the deflectable ring element is formed from a resilient polymer. The disclosed polymer is polyether ether ketone (PEEK). A seat ring (30) of PEEK having the same convoluted wall (32) as the superelastic seat ring provides radial compliance under the purge shoulder (44) at lower cost, but its shape recovery is slower and the force of the recovery lower, so that its snap-back wipe is weaker than that of the superelastic seat ring. The purge gap (60) and the air purge provide their cleaning action independently of the wipe, so the polymer seat ring retains the air purge in full. This embodiment is illustrated in FIG. 9 and is intended for a lower-cost product line. Other resilient polymers that resist the liquid sealant, such as polyphenylene sulphide (PPS) and polyetherimide (PEI), may be used; creep of the polymer under a sustained interference fit, and its resistance to an ammonia-bearing liquid sealant, are checked for the polymer chosen. Electropolishing, and the austenite finish temperature, have no application to a polymer deflectable ring element.

Whatever the material, the snap-back wipe is the movement of the recovering deflectable ring element across a surface of the poppet (40) as the pressure differential falls; the term carries no speed or force qualifier. The superelastic alloy is preferred because it gives the strongest wipe. Across the material classes described above the wipe force falls from superelastic alloy to resilient metal to resilient polymer, and the wall geometry and the over-travel distance are adapted accordingly, while the air purge is available with every material.

#### Wall geometry converting axial load into radial deflection

The purge shoulder (44) moves axially with the poppet (40). To open the purge gap (60), the deflectable ring element converts the axial load applied by the purge shoulder (44) into a radial deflection of the surface that bounds the purge gap (60). This axial-to-radial conversion geometry is the second defining feature of the deflectable ring element after its material, and the load chain is the same in every embodiment: the purge shoulder (44) bears on the deflectable ring element only in over-travel; the geometry of the ring element turns that axial load into radial deflection; the radial deflection opens the purge gap (60), which is closed in the closed state and the normal-open state; and the shape recovery of the ring element closes the purge gap (60) again when the load is removed. The wall geometry is chosen together with the material so that the purge gap opening is reached at the over-travel distance within the strain the material recovers. The geometry is loaded mechanically by the purge shoulder (44), at a defined location and at service temperature; no heating or other external actuation is involved in reaching the purge state.

In the illustrated embodiment the geometry is a convoluted wall (32). The wall of the seat ring (30) between the sealing edge (34) and the end of the seat ring (30) seated against the internal shoulder (24) is formed with one or more shallow convolutions, each a radial excursion of the wall of a convolution amplitude measured from the mean radius of the wall. Axial load applied by the purge shoulder (44) shortens the wall and changes the depth of the convolutions, moving the wall radially. The number of convolutions in the disclosed embodiments is one or two; FIG. 6A shows a first convolution (32a) and, optionally, a second convolution (32b). A wall with three or more convolutions may be used; each additional convolution adds axial compliance and reduces the radial deflection obtained per convolution for a given load, and the number is limited to that at which the wall still moves radially rather than merely shortening. The profile of a convolution is not limited to a rounded wave; it may be a saw-tooth, or a helical or circumferential wave. Optionally, the root of each convolution is provided with a fillet radius, which reduces the concentration of strain at the root under repeated radial deflection.

The convolution amplitude, together with the seat ring wall thickness and the seat ring free length, sets the relationship between the axial load applied by the purge shoulder (44), the resulting radial deflection, and the strain in the wall. A shallow convolution in a thin wall gives a radial deflection of the order of the purge gap opening at a strain the superelastic alloy recovers fully, and the convoluted wall (32) is the preferred geometry for that reason.

Other axial-to-radial conversion geometries are shown in FIG. 12. In FIG. 12A the wall is a frustoconical wall (232): a plain wall with a conical flare, in which axial load applied at the narrow end increases the flare and moves the free end radially outward, with no convolution to form. In FIG. 12B the wall is a slotted wall (332): a short tubular wall having a continuous band at the end carrying the sealing edge (34) and axial slots cut from the other end, which divide the remainder of the wall into fingers (332a, 332b) that splay radially under the load of the purge shoulder (44). The slotted wall (332) gives a large radial deflection at low force and low strain, which suits a resilient metal, and the continuous band keeps the sealing edge (34) intact. In FIG. 12C the wall is a cellular wall (432): a pattern of diamond or chevron cells cut through the wall between two continuous end bands, the struts of which rotate under axial compression so that the wall enlarges radially. The cellular wall (432) is formed by the same laser cutting as the convoluted wall (32), and the sealing edge (34) is carried on one of the continuous end bands. The coupling between axial shortening and radial enlargement of a cellular wall depends on the cell geometry chosen.

In a further embodiment, not separately illustrated, the deflectable ring element is a conical annular disc: a thin frustoconical annulus held at one rim. Axial load from the purge shoulder (44) flattens the cone and moves the free rim radially, outward where the inner rim is held and inward where the outer rim is held. The radial movement per unit of axial stroke is small at shallow cone angles, and this form is used with a separate fixed seat (130) for sealing, in the manner of the fixed seat and wiper ring embodiment described below.

In a further embodiment the wall of the deflectable ring element is plain, with no conversion geometry of its own, and the radial deflection is produced by a tapered purge shoulder (244), FIG. 11A: a frustoconical flank of the poppet (40) enters the deflectable ring element during over-travel and expands it by direct radial contact with its inner surface, as a mandrel expands a ring. The load on the ring element is then radial rather than axial. The flank slides on the inner surface of the ring element, with more friction and wear than the axial loading of the convoluted wall (32), and wipes that inner surface on the return stroke.

The frustoconical wall (232), the slotted wall (332), the cellular wall (432), the conical annular disc and the plain wall driven by a tapered purge shoulder (244) are alternatives that perform the same function as the convoluted wall (32), namely converting the movement of the poppet (40) in over-travel into radial deflection of the deflectable ring element, and any of them may be used with any of the materials described above, subject to the strain the material recovers. Each of these geometries may be applied to a seat ring (30) that also seals or, in the fixed seat and wiper ring embodiment, to a wiper ring (132) that does not seal.

#### Continuous, split and segmented forms, and the purge opening

The seat ring (30) of the illustrated embodiment is a continuous ring, and its sealing edge (34) is continuous around its circumference, so that the sealing face (42) of the poppet (40) seals on it in the closed state. Where the deflectable ring element also provides the sealing edge (34), the band of the ring that carries the sealing edge (34) remains continuous in every form described herein; the slotted wall (332) and the cellular wall (432) place their slots and cells away from that band.

The purge gap (60) of the illustrated embodiment is an annular clearance bounded at least in part by a surface of the seat ring (30), closed in the closed state and the normal-open state and opened by the radial deflection, as described above. The purge opening through which the air purge passes need not be an annular clearance at a surface of the ring element. In the slotted wall (332) and the cellular wall (432) the slots or cells pass through the wall, and in some embodiments they form the purge opening: they are closed at rest by contact of the wall with the surface of the core body bore (22) that faces them and are uncovered when the radial deflection moves the wall away from that surface, so that inflation gas passes through the wall of the deflectable ring element rather than around it. In such embodiments the ring element may be fitted in the core body bore (22) with no clearance at its outer surface at rest. A deflectable ring element that does not itself seal may also be segmented: a ring divided into fingers whose gaps open under the purge shoulder (44), or a split wiper ring having a single axial split that opens circumferentially under a tapered purge shoulder (244), the split being the purge opening. A split ring is not used as a one-piece seat ring (30), because the split would interrupt the sealing edge (34).

The purge opening is therefore, in the various embodiments, an annular gap at a surface of the deflectable ring element, one or more slits or apertures through its wall, or one or more gaps between the fingers of a segmented ring element. In each case it is closed in the closed state and the normal-open state and opened by the radial deflection in the purge state, and the deflectable ring element is continuous, slotted, cellular, split or segmented accordingly. Slots, cells and splits that pass through the wall can retain liquid sealant, which is a consideration in choosing between the forms.

#### Dimensions and parameter ranges

The dimensions and pressures given below are those of the superelastic seat ring (30) of the worked example, together with the ranges within which the mechanism is expected to operate with a seat ring (30) of that material and wall geometry. The relationships between the parameters govern, and the numbers follow from them. First, the over-travel threshold is higher than the crack pressure, so that the valve core opens and seals as a conventional valve core does until the purge state is reached, and the over-travel threshold lies within the pressure differential that the inflation device applies, so that the purge state is reached during an inflation event. Second, the purge gap (60) is closed at rest and remains closed throughout the normal stroke, the purge shoulder (44) being spaced from the deflectable ring element by at least the normal stroke at rest. Third, the radial deflection produced at the over-travel distance is at least the purge gap opening, and the strain it imposes on the wall is within what the material of the deflectable ring element recovers, so that the purge gap (60) closes again on shape recovery. Fourth, the seat ring inner diameter admits the poppet stem (41) and presents the sealing edge (34) to the sealing face (42), and the seat ring outer diameter fits the core body bore (22) with the interference fit at the internal shoulder (24). A person skilled in the art who changes one parameter adjusts the others to preserve these relationships.

The seat ring inner diameter is from 2.0 mm to 2.6 mm; in some embodiments from 2.1 mm to 2.5 mm; in some embodiments from 2.2 mm to 2.4 mm; in the worked example 2.3 mm. The seat ring wall thickness, being the radial thickness of the material of the convoluted wall (32), is from 0.15 mm to 0.30 mm; in some embodiments from 0.16 mm to 0.24 mm; in some embodiments from 0.18 mm to 0.22 mm; in the worked example 0.20 mm. The seat ring free length, being the axial length of the seat ring (30) unloaded, is from 2.5 mm to 4.0 mm; in some embodiments from 2.6 mm to 3.5 mm; in some embodiments from 2.8 mm to 3.2 mm; in the worked example 3.0 mm. The convolution amplitude is from 0.08 mm to 0.20 mm; in some embodiments from 0.08 mm to 0.16 mm; in some embodiments from 0.11 mm to 0.14 mm; in the worked example 0.12 mm. The seat ring (30) of the worked example is formed from tube stock of 2.7 mm outer diameter and 0.20 mm wall thickness, so that its seat ring outer diameter is nominally 2.7 mm. These dimensions are called out in FIG. 6A.

The purge gap opening, being the radial size of the purge gap (60) when the deflectable ring element is fully deflected in the purge state and called out in FIG. 6B, is from 0.10 mm to 0.25 mm; in some embodiments from 0.11 mm to 0.20 mm; in some embodiments from 0.13 mm to 0.17 mm; in the worked example 0.15 mm. The over-travel distance, being the axial distance the poppet (40) moves beyond the end of the normal stroke, is from 0.4 mm to 0.9 mm; in some embodiments from 0.4 mm to 0.65 mm; in some embodiments from 0.44 mm to 0.56 mm; in the worked example 0.5 mm, beyond a normal stroke of 0.6 mm. The crack pressure is from 0.8 bar to 1.5 bar; in some embodiments from 0.8 bar to 1.2 bar; in some embodiments from 0.91 bar to 1.09 bar; in the worked example 1.0 bar. The over-travel threshold is from 2.5 bar to 5.0 bar; in some embodiments from 2.75 bar to 4.25 bar; in some embodiments from 3.2 bar to 3.8 bar; in the worked example 3.5 bar. The over-travel threshold of the worked example was reached in every inflation of the bench test, in which a floor pump inflated the inflatable chamber to 6 bar; whether a given hand pump or compressed-gas inflation device reaches a given over-travel threshold depends on the pressure differential that device applies, and the over-travel threshold is chosen for the inflation devices with which the valve core is to be used.

Of these values, only those of the worked example were built and tested, and the over-travel distance and purge gap opening of the worked example are design values that were not measured directly during the bench test. The ranges correspond to the superelastic seat ring (30) with a convoluted wall (32). A person skilled in the art will adapt them to the stiffness and recovery characteristics of a resilient polymer seat ring, of a deflectable ring element of resilient metal, of a seat ring having another of the axial-to-radial conversion geometries described above, or of a separate wiper ring (132), and will scale the dimensions to the bore available in a larger valve core or in an inflation valve in which the deflectable ring element is carried directly by the valve stem (10).

#### One-piece seat ring, or fixed seat with a separate wiper ring

In the illustrated embodiment one part, the seat ring (30), performs both functions: its sealing edge (34) is the surface on which the sealing face (42) of the poppet (40) rests in the closed state, and its convoluted wall (32) is the deflectable ring element that the purge shoulder (44) deflects in over-travel. The seat ring (30) is held in the core body (20) and does not move bodily; the purge gap (60) opens by elastic deformation of its wall while the seat ring (30) remains seated against the internal shoulder (24). In this one-piece form the part that seals is also the part that wipes: the shape recovery of the seat ring (30) moves it across the sealing face (42) or an adjacent surface of the poppet (40), so that sealant film is removed from the sealing surfaces by the seat ring (30) itself, with no wiping part in addition to it. The one-piece form has the fewest parts and the shortest axial length, and the inspection of the worked example after forty inflation cycles found the sealing edge (34) and the sealing face (42) clean.

In an alternative embodiment, FIGS. 10A and 10B, sealing and deflection are performed by separate parts. A fixed seat (130), which does not deflect during over-travel, is carried by the core body (20) and provides the sealing edge (34) on which the sealing face (42) rests in the closed state; in the disclosed form the fixed seat (130) is of brass. A separate wiper ring (132), which is the deflectable ring element of this embodiment, is positioned on the chamber side of the fixed seat (130), so that the purge shoulder (44) reaches it only in over-travel. In over-travel the purge shoulder (44) deflects the wiper ring (132) radially outward, opening the purge gap (60), shown in FIG. 10B between the outer surface of the wiper ring (132) and the core body bore (22), and the air purge flows along the purge flow path (62). When the pressure differential falls the wiper ring (132) recovers its shape and the purge gap (60) closes. In the disclosed form the wiper ring (132) is a thin ring of stainless steel of spring temper. In other embodiments the wiper ring (132) is formed from any of the materials described above for the deflectable ring element, and it has a plain wall, a convoluted wall (32) or another of the axial-to-radial conversion geometries described above, or it is a split wiper ring as described above. Where the recovering wiper ring (132) moves across a surface of the poppet (40) it also provides a snap-back wipe; where it does not, this embodiment provides the air purge alone. The fixed seat and wiper ring embodiment has one more part and more axial length than the one-piece seat ring (30), and may be easier to manufacture; the form, thickness and retention of the wiper ring (132) follow from the material and geometry selected as described above.

Both forms are species of the same deflectable ring element: the seat ring (30) where the ring element also carries the sealing edge (34), and the wiper ring (132) where a fixed seat (130) carries the sealing edge (34). The description of materials, wall geometry, purge opening and dimensions above applies to both, and every reference herein to the deflectable ring element includes both unless the context excludes one. The parameter ranges given above were derived for the one-piece superelastic seat ring (30) and are adapted for the wiper ring (132) as stated.

#### Separate part, integral flexure section, or carried by the valve stem

In the illustrated embodiment the seat ring (30) is a part separate from the core body (20), received in the core body bore (22) and retained against the internal shoulder (24). The retention of a separate deflectable ring element in the core body (20), by the interference fit of the worked example or by the other forms of ring retention, is described below with the alternatives for the other parts.

In other embodiments the deflectable ring element is not a separate part but an integral flexure section of the core body (20): a thin-walled section of the core body (20), having a convoluted wall (32), a slotted wall (332) or another of the axial-to-radial conversion geometries described above, formed in one piece with the core body (20) by turning, moulding or pressing. There is then no separate seat ring (30) and no interference fit. The core body (20) is in that case formed from a material having the recoverable strain the section requires, such as a resilient polymer or stainless steel of spring temper; the free-machining brass of the illustrated core body (20) does not recover the strains involved and is not used for an integral flexure section. The functional definition of the deflectable ring element covers a ring element separate from the core body (20) and one integral with it.

In further embodiments the deflectable ring element is carried not by a removable core body (20) but directly by the valve stem (10) of the inflation valve, as the seat ring (730) of FIG. 15B, in an inflation valve having no separate valve core; the valve formats in which the mechanism may be used are described below.

#### Shape recovery

Shape recovery is the return of the deflectable ring element to its at-rest form when the pressure differential falls and the purge shoulder (44) withdraws, closing the purge gap (60); the snap-back wipe is the movement of the recovering ring element across a surface of the poppet (40) during that return. In the illustrated embodiment the shape recovery is the superelastic recovery of the NiTi seat ring (30): on unloading, the martensite formed under stress in the deflected wall reverts to austenite and the wall returns to its shape-set form, quickly and with force. In the resilient polymer seat ring (30) of FIG. 9 the shape recovery is the elastic recovery of the polymer, slower and with less force. In a deflectable ring element of resilient metal, including the stainless steel wiper ring (132), the shape recovery is the elastic recovery of the metal.

In some embodiments the shape recovery is assisted by the poppet (40) itself: a second formation of the poppet (40), on the side of the deflectable ring element opposite the purge shoulder (44), meets the ring element on the return movement of the poppet (40) and drives it back towards its at-rest form. Closing of the purge gap (60) then does not depend only on the material's own recovery, which guards against residual set of the ring element after many cycles and against loss of superelastic recovery when the ring element is chilled below its martensite start temperature, at the cost of additional contact and friction on the return.

Where a superelastic deflectable ring element has been chilled below its martensite start temperature while deflected, for example by the expansion of gas from a compressed-gas inflation device, and has not fully recovered on unloading, it recovers its shape when it is warmed again above its austenite finish temperature. This recovery on warming is a property of the alloy and is distinct from the thermally actuated purge described below, in which the purge gap (60) is deliberately opened by warming. The seat ring (30) of the illustrated embodiment is specified with an austenite finish temperature below 5 °C so that it is austenitic, and recovers superelastically, at ordinary riding temperatures without any warming. Whether chilling by a compressed-gas inflation device takes the seat ring (30) below its martensite start temperature was not tested.

The worked example completed forty inflation cycles without failure of the seat ring (30); its fatigue life beyond forty over-travel cycles was not tested. Under repeated radial deflection the strain in a convoluted wall (32) is concentrated at the roots of the convolutions. The fillet radius at the convolution root described above, and the use of two convolutions (32a, 32b) rather than one so that the radial deflection is shared between them, are design measures that may be used to reduce the strain at any one location.

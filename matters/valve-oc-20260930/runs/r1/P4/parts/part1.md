#### Orientation and directions

As used herein, the "supply side" of the poppet means the side coupled to the inflation device, and the "chamber side" of the poppet means the side open to the inflatable chamber. These terms replace orientation-dependent words, because the orientation of the inflation valve changes as the wheel turns. "Axial" means along the axis of the poppet, referred to herein as the poppet axis, and "radial" means perpendicular to the poppet axis. "Outward" means away from the poppet axis and "inward" means towards it. Movement of the poppet from the closed state through the normal stroke and into over-travel is referred to as movement in the opening direction. The deflectable ring element is "at rest" when it is not deflected by the purge shoulder.

#### Pressure differential, crack pressure, over-travel threshold and poppet travel

As used herein, the "pressure differential" means the supply-side pressure less the chamber-side pressure across the poppet. The "crack pressure" means the pressure differential at which the poppet begins its normal stroke, overcoming the first spring stage of the return spring. The "over-travel threshold" means the pressure differential above which the poppet moves beyond the normal stroke into over-travel, overcoming the second spring stage of the return spring or other threshold-setting arrangement. The over-travel threshold is higher than the crack pressure. In the illustrated embodiment the over-travel threshold is selected so that it is exceeded during inflation of the inflatable chamber with a floor pump, as in the working example described below.

As used herein, the "normal stroke" means the displacement of the poppet from the closed state, at and above the crack pressure and below the over-travel threshold, over which the purge shoulder is spaced from the deflectable ring element. "Over-travel" means movement of the poppet beyond the normal stroke, occurring when the pressure differential exceeds the over-travel threshold, during which the purge shoulder bears on the deflectable ring element. The "over-travel distance" means the axial distance the poppet moves beyond the end of the normal stroke during over-travel.

#### Operating states

The valve core has a closed state, a normal-open state and a purge state, determined by the pressure differential. In the "closed state", which prevails when the pressure differential is below the crack pressure or when the lock nut is engaged, the sealing face of the poppet rests on the sealing edge, which is provided by the seat ring or, in embodiments having a fixed seat, by the fixed seat; the main passage is closed and the purge gap is closed. In the "normal-open state", which prevails when the pressure differential is at or above the crack pressure and does not exceed the over-travel threshold, the poppet is displaced from the closed state within the normal stroke, the sealing face is lifted from the sealing edge so that the main passage is open, the purge shoulder is spaced from the deflectable ring element, and the purge gap remains closed. In the "purge state", which prevails when the pressure differential exceeds the over-travel threshold, the poppet is in over-travel beyond the normal stroke, the purge shoulder bears on the deflectable ring element, the deflectable ring element is in radial deflection, the purge gap is open, and the air purge occurs while the main passage remains open. When the pressure differential falls below the over-travel threshold, the deflectable ring element undergoes "shape recovery", meaning its return towards its at-rest shape, the purge gap closes, and the valve core returns through the normal-open state to the closed state as the pressure differential falls below the crack pressure.

#### Deflectable ring element, seat ring, wiper ring, fixed seat and convoluted wall

As used herein, a "deflectable ring element" means any annular element, carried by the valve core, that the purge shoulder deflects radially during over-travel so as to open a purge flow path, and that undergoes shape recovery towards its at-rest form when the pressure differential falls. A deflectable ring element is a seat ring, where the same element also provides the sealing edge, or, in embodiments having a separate fixed seat, a wiper ring. The deflectable ring element is described below with reference to the seat ring of the illustrated embodiment; the term is not limited to the material, the wall form or the manner of retention of that seat ring.

As used herein, a "seat ring" means a deflectable ring element that both provides a sealing edge on which the sealing face of the poppet rests in the closed state, and is deflected by the purge shoulder during over-travel to open the purge gap. In the illustrated embodiment the seat ring is formed from a superelastic alloy and has a convoluted wall; in other embodiments it is formed from a resilient polymer. The "sealing edge" means the edge of the seat ring, or of the fixed seat, on which the sealing face rests in the closed state.

As used herein, a "wiper ring" means a deflectable ring element, separate from a fixed seat, positioned on the chamber side of the fixed seat so that the purge shoulder deflects it outward during over-travel to open the purge gap, while the fixed seat provides the sealing edge. A "fixed seat" means a component of the valve core that does not deflect during over-travel and that provides the sealing edge on which the sealing face of the poppet rests, used together with a wiper ring.

As used herein, a "convoluted wall" means a wall of the seat ring formed with one or more shallow convolutions, which converts axial load applied to the seat ring into radial deflection of the seat ring. The number of convolutions means the count of convolutions in the convoluted wall; the illustrated embodiment has one convolution or two convolutions.

#### Purge gap, purge flow path, main passage and radial deflection

As used herein, the "purge gap" means a clearance, associated with the deflectable ring element, that is closed in the closed state and in the normal-open state and that is opened by radial deflection of the deflectable ring element in the purge state, through which the air purge flows. In the illustrated embodiment the purge gap is an annular radial clearance between the outer surface of the seat ring and the core body bore; in other embodiments the purge gap is bounded by another surface of the deflectable ring element, and the term is not limited to the location of the illustrated embodiment.

As used herein, the "purge flow path" means the path, distinct from the main passage, from the supply side through the purge gap to the inflatable chamber, along which the air purge travels. The "main passage" means the passage past the sealing face that carries inflation gas into the inflatable chamber in the normal-open state and in the purge state. The main passage and the purge gap are different flow paths: the main passage is opened by lifting of the sealing face from the sealing edge at the crack pressure, whereas the purge gap is opened only by radial deflection of the deflectable ring element in the purge state. The "purge gap opening" means the radial size of the purge gap when the deflectable ring element is fully deflected in the purge state.

As used herein, "radial deflection" means elastic deformation of the wall of the deflectable ring element, produced by the purge shoulder during over-travel, that opens the purge gap. Radial deflection is a deformation of the deflectable ring element while it remains retained in the valve core; it is not a bodily displacement of the element as a whole. In the illustrated embodiment the radial deflection is outward.

#### Purge shoulder and deflecting formation

As used herein, a "purge shoulder" means the formation of the poppet, on the chamber side of the sealing face, that contacts the deflectable ring element only during over-travel and thereby deflects it radially to open the purge gap. The purge shoulder is a deflecting formation, meaning a formation that moves with the poppet and that contacts the deflectable ring element only during over-travel; the term "purge shoulder" is defined by that function and is not limited to the annular form of the purge shoulder of the illustrated embodiment.

#### Return spring, spring stages and threshold-setting arrangement

As used herein, the "return spring" means the biasing member that biases the poppet towards the closed state. The "first spring stage" means the stage of the return spring having the lower rate, which resists the normal stroke and thereby sets the crack pressure. The "second spring stage" means the stage of the return spring having a higher rate than the first spring stage, which the pressure differential must overcome, once the normal stroke is complete, to produce over-travel, and which thereby sets the over-travel threshold. A "threshold-setting arrangement" means any arrangement of the valve core that sets the over-travel threshold; the return spring having a first spring stage and a second spring stage is the threshold-setting arrangement of the illustrated embodiment.

#### Cleaning actions, sealant and inflation terms

As used herein, "cleaning action" means an action by which the valve core removes sealant film from its surfaces without a tool or user action. The cleaning actions described herein are the air purge, the snap-back wipe, or both. The "air purge" means the flow of inflation gas, at the pressure of the inflation device, along the purge flow path during the purge state, across the surfaces of the valve core on which sealant film accumulates and into the inflatable chamber. The inflation gas may be air supplied by a floor pump or another inflation device, or another gas supplied by a compressed-gas inflation device, such as a CO2 inflation device. The "snap-back wipe" means the movement of the deflectable ring element across a surface of the poppet as the deflectable ring element undergoes shape recovery when the pressure differential falls below the over-travel threshold, by which movement partially cured sealant film is sheared from that surface.

As used herein, "sealant film" means liquid sealant that has dried or cured, in whole or in part, on a surface of the valve core. "Liquid sealant" means a fluid, carried in the inflatable chamber, that is drawn into the valve core on inflation and that is capable of drying or curing on the surfaces of the valve core; in the tested embodiment the liquid sealant is an emulsion of latex containing ammonia and suspended particles, and the term is not limited to that chemistry. "Blockage" means a failure mode of the valve core in which sealant film prevents the poppet from opening within a defined response time of actuation by the inflation device.

As used herein, an "inflatable chamber" means the volume, inflated through the inflation valve and holding liquid sealant, of a bicycle inner tube or of a tubeless bicycle tyre set-up. An "inflation device" means the source of inflation gas coupled to the supply side, for example a floor pump with a pump head, or a compressed-gas inflation device. An "inflation event" means the period from coupling of an inflation device to the supply side until the inflation device is removed.

#### Materials terms

As used herein, a "superelastic alloy" means an alloy that recovers large elastic strains on unloading, at the service temperature of the inflation valve, by a reversible phase transformation between martensite and austenite; in the illustrated embodiment the superelastic alloy is a nickel-titanium alloy (NiTi) of nominally 55.8 wt% nickel content. The "austenite finish temperature" means the temperature above which the superelastic alloy of the deflectable ring element is fully austenitic and exhibits superelastic behaviour. The "martensite start temperature" means the temperature, on cooling the superelastic alloy of the deflectable ring element, below which martensite begins to form and superelastic recovery may be impaired. The austenite finish temperature and the martensite start temperature apply only to a deflectable ring element formed from a superelastic alloy.

As used herein, a "resilient metal" means a metal which, formed into the deflectable ring element, is deflected radially by the purge shoulder during over-travel and undergoes shape recovery when the pressure differential falls; a superelastic alloy is the resilient metal of the illustrated embodiment, and stainless steel is the resilient metal of the wiper ring described below. A "resilient polymer" means a polymer which, formed into the deflectable ring element, is deflected radially by the purge shoulder during over-travel and undergoes shape recovery when the pressure differential falls; polyether ether ketone (PEEK) is the resilient polymer described below.

#### Dimensional conventions

As used herein, the "seat ring inner diameter" means the inner diameter of the seat ring in its at-rest condition, measured at a portion of the wall free of convolutions, and the "seat ring outer diameter" means the outer diameter of the seat ring in its at-rest condition, measured at a portion of the wall free of convolutions. The "seat ring wall thickness" means the radial thickness of the material forming the convoluted wall of the seat ring. The "seat ring free length" means the axial length of the seat ring in its unloaded, at-rest condition. The "convolution amplitude" means the radial excursion of a convolution of the convoluted wall, measured from the mean radius of the wall to the peak or trough of the convolution. The "purge gap opening" and the "over-travel distance" are measured radially and axially respectively, as defined above, with the poppet at the end of over-travel.

#### Test definitions

As used herein, an "inflation cycle" means one repetition of an inflation event, a hold period at the resulting pressure of the inflatable chamber, and deflation of the inflatable chamber; in the working example an inflation cycle is inflation to 6 bar with a floor pump, a hold of 24 h at 22 °C, and full deflation through the inflation valve. "Leak-down" means the loss of pressure from the inflatable chamber, through the valve core, over a hold period. "Inflation time" means the elapsed time, using a given inflation device, from coupling to the supply side until the inflatable chamber reaches a stated pressure. A "conventional valve core" means a valve core lacking a deflectable ring element and a purge gap as defined herein, used as a comparator in the working example.

#### Structure of the valve core in the closed state

FIG. 1 is an axial section through the inflation valve of the illustrated embodiment on a plane containing the poppet axis, in the closed state. FIG. 5 shows the same components separated along the poppet axis in assembly order. FIG. 6A is an enlarged detail of the region of the seat ring 30, the internal shoulder 24 and the purge shoulder 44 in the closed state. The structure is described in this section in the closed state; the operation of the valve core through the normal-open state, the purge state and shape recovery is described in the following section.

#### Inflation valve and valve stem

Referring to FIG. 1, the inflation valve comprises a valve stem 10 having a stem bore 12, and a valve core received in the stem bore 12. The valve stem 10 is fixed to the inflatable chamber, which is a bicycle inner tube or a tubeless bicycle tyre set-up holding liquid sealant. In the illustrated embodiment the valve stem 10 is a conventional Presta-pattern valve stem of brass with an outer diameter of 6 mm, and the stem bore 12 is threaded. The core body 20 of the valve core is threaded into the stem bore 12, so that the valve core is retained in the valve stem 10 and can be removed from it as a unit. The Presta-pattern valve stem is described by way of example; the valve core and the inflation valve are not limited to that pattern.

#### The valve core

The valve core comprises a core body 20, a seat ring 30 carried by the core body 20, a poppet 40 guided by the core body 20, and a return spring 50 biasing the poppet 40 towards the closed state. The seat ring 30 is the deflectable ring element of the illustrated embodiment. The purge gap 60 is defined between the seat ring 30 and the core body 20, as described below. A lock nut 70 is provided on the supply side. In FIG. 5 the components are shown in assembly order: the lock nut 70, the valve stem 10, the core body 20, the seat ring 30, the poppet 40 with its sealing face 42 and purge shoulder 44, and the return spring 50 with its first spring stage 52 and second spring stage 54. The valve core is fitted to and removed from the valve stem 10 as a whole, and may be supplied separately from the valve stem 10 as a replacement valve core for fitting to an existing valve stem.

#### Core body

The core body 20 is a turned component having an external thread engaged in the stem bore 12, a core body bore 22 that receives the seat ring 30, and an internal shoulder 24 against which the seat ring 30 is seated so that the seat ring 30 is located axially in the core body 20. The core body 20 also guides the poppet 40 in its axial movement. In the illustrated embodiment the core body 20 is turned from free-machining brass CW614N. In other embodiments the core body 20 is turned from stainless steel, for example grade 303, for corrosion resistance in the presence of ammonia-bearing liquid sealant; the core body 20 is turned by ordinary machining in either material and no other special process is required. The core body 20 is not limited to those materials.

#### Seat ring

The seat ring 30 is an annular element received in the core body bore 22 and seated against the internal shoulder 24. It has a sealing edge 34 at one axial end, on which the sealing face 42 of the poppet 40 rests in the closed state, and a convoluted wall 32 between its ends. In the illustrated embodiment the convoluted wall 32 has one convolution 32a or two convolutions 32a, 32b, each convolution being a shallow radial excursion of the wall; the convoluted wall 32 converts axial load applied to the seat ring 30 into radial deflection of the seat ring 30. The seat ring 30 is thin-walled relative to the core body 20, and its dimensions and their ranges are given below with reference to FIG. 6.

In the illustrated embodiment the seat ring 30 is formed from a superelastic alloy, namely a nickel-titanium alloy of nominally 55.8 wt% nickel content, having an austenite finish temperature specified below 5 °C so that the seat ring 30 is superelastic at riding temperatures. In other embodiments the seat ring 30 is formed from a resilient polymer, such as PEEK, with the same convoluted wall 32. The materials of the deflectable ring element, and the effect of the material on shape recovery and on the snap-back wipe, are described below.

The seat ring 30 is retained in the core body 20 by an interference fit, the interference being approximately 0.03 mm on diameter, and is pressed into the core body bore 22 until it is seated against the internal shoulder 24. In some embodiments the interference fit is provided over an axial band of the seat ring 30 adjacent the internal shoulder 24, and the remainder of the outer surface of the seat ring 30, including the portion of the convoluted wall 32 that undergoes radial deflection, is not in interference with the core body bore 22, so that this portion of the wall is free to move relative to the core body bore 22 during radial deflection. FIG. 6A shows the interference fit region between the seat ring 30 and the internal shoulder 24 and, distinct from it, the region in which the purge gap 60 is located. Other ways of retaining the seat ring 30 in the core body 20 are described below.

#### Poppet

The poppet 40 has a poppet stem 41, which is guided by the core body 20 and along which liquid sealant is drawn by capillary action during inflation, a sealing face 42, and a purge shoulder 44. In the illustrated embodiment the poppet 40 is of brass and the sealing face 42 is conical. In the closed state the sealing face 42 rests on the sealing edge 34 of the seat ring 30, and the seal between the supply side and the chamber side is formed at the sealing edge 34 by the sealing face 42; the closed-state seal is formed between the sealing face 42 and the sealing edge 34 without any elastomeric element in the illustrated embodiment. Other forms and materials of the sealing face 42 are described below.

The purge shoulder 44 is a formation of the poppet 40 on the chamber side of the sealing face 42. In the illustrated embodiment the purge shoulder 44 is an annular shoulder integral with the poppet 40. In the closed state the purge shoulder 44 is spaced axially from the seat ring 30 (FIG. 1, FIG. 6A), and the axial clearance between the purge shoulder 44 and the seat ring 30 is such that the purge shoulder 44 remains spaced from the seat ring 30 throughout the normal stroke and contacts the seat ring 30 only during over-travel. The purge shoulder 44 therefore takes no part in sealing and no part in the normal stroke; its only function is to bear on the seat ring 30 during over-travel and to deflect it radially. Other forms of deflecting formation are described below.

#### Return spring

The return spring 50 biases the poppet 40 towards the closed state and has a first spring stage 52 and a second spring stage 54. The first spring stage 52 has the lower rate; it resists the normal stroke and sets the crack pressure. The second spring stage 54 has a higher rate than the first spring stage 52; it comes into action when the normal stroke is complete, it must be overcome by the pressure differential to produce over-travel, and it thereby sets the over-travel threshold. In the illustrated embodiment the first spring stage 52 and the second spring stage 54 are stages of a single return spring 50 of stainless steel music wire. The mechanism does not depend on the material of the return spring 50. The return spring 50 with its first spring stage 52 and second spring stage 54 is the threshold-setting arrangement of the illustrated embodiment; other threshold-setting arrangements are described below.

#### Purge gap in the closed state

In the illustrated embodiment the purge gap 60 is an annular radial clearance between the outer surface of the seat ring 30 and the core body bore 22 (FIG. 1, FIG. 6A). In the closed state the purge gap 60 is closed, and it remains closed in the normal-open state; it is opened only by radial deflection of the seat ring 30 when the purge shoulder 44 bears on the seat ring 30 during over-travel. In the closed state the seal between the supply side and the chamber side is formed at the sealing edge 34 by the sealing face 42. The purge gap 60 is a flow path distinct from the main passage 64: the main passage 64 is the passage past the sealing face 42, which opens at the crack pressure, whereas the purge gap 60 is closed at the crack pressure and throughout the normal stroke and opens only in the purge state. The purge gap 60 and the purge flow path 62 through it are described in the purge state, and their location in other embodiments, below.

#### Lock nut

The lock nut 70 is a conventional Presta-pattern lock nut. When engaged by hand it holds the poppet 40 in the closed state irrespective of the pressure differential, for storage and transport. While the lock nut 70 is engaged the sealing face 42 rests on the sealing edge 34, the purge shoulder 44 is spaced from the seat ring 30, and the seat ring 30 is at rest and undeflected; the lock nut 70 is therefore the preferred means of holding the inflation valve closed during storage and transport, rather than any accessory that bears on the poppet 40 in the opening direction. The lock nut 70 is a conventional component and takes no part in the cleaning actions.

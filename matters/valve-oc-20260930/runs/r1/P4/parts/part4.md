## Deliverable

### Description

#### Alternative locations of the purge gap

The purge gap (60) is defined herein by its function: it is a clearance bounded at least in part by a surface of the deflectable ring element, which is closed in the closed state and in the normal-open state, which is opened by radial deflection of the deflectable ring element in the purge state, and through which the air purge flows. The purge gap (60) is distinct from the main passage (64): the main passage (64) is opened by lifting of the sealing face (42) from the sealing edge (34) during the normal stroke, whereas the purge gap (60) remains closed throughout the normal stroke and opens only when the deflectable ring element is deflected. In the embodiment of FIG. 1 to FIG. 4 the purge gap (60) is located between the outer surface of the seat ring (30) and the core body bore (22). The surfaces that bound the purge gap (60), and the sense of the radial deflection that opens it, may however be chosen in any of the ways described in this section, each of which is a species of the same functional definition.

In a first arrangement the purge gap (60) is bounded by the outer surface of the seat ring (30) and the core body bore (22). Such a purge gap (60) opens where the bounding portion of the outer surface of the seat ring (30) moves away from the core body bore (22). That movement may be an inward movement of the bounding portion, produced for example by flattening of an outwardly convex convolution of the convoluted wall (32) under axial load from the purge shoulder (44), or by deepening of an inwardly concave convolution under the same load; or it may be the non-uniform deflection described below, in which some arcs of the seat ring (30) move outward while the intervening arcs move inward, so that the purge gap (60) opens over the inwardly moving arcs. Where the seat ring (30) is retained by an interference fit confined to an axial band at the internal shoulder (24), as described with reference to FIG. 6, the remainder of the outer surface is free of the core body bore (22) and is available to bound the purge gap (60).

In a second arrangement the purge gap (60) is bounded by the outer surface of the seat ring (30) and the stem bore (12), the wall of the core body (20) being provided with one or more windows at the axial position of the seat ring (30) through which the outer surface of the seat ring (30) is exposed to the stem bore (12). This arrangement requires the same movement of the outer surface as the first arrangement, and the windows in the core body (20) form part of the purge flow path (62).

In a third arrangement the purge gap (60) is bounded by the inner surface of the seat ring (30) and the poppet (40), on the chamber side of the sealing edge (34). Such a purge gap (60) is opened by outward radial deflection of the seat ring (30), and the air purge passes along the poppet stem (41), which is the capillary path along which liquid sealant is drawn into the valve core. In this arrangement the clearance between the inner surface of the seat ring (30) and the poppet (40) on the chamber side of the sealing edge (34) is closed, or is sufficiently small to carry no significant flow, in the closed state and the normal-open state, so that the purge gap (60) remains a flow path distinct from the main passage (64).

In a fourth arrangement the purge gap (60) is formed by one or more openings through the wall of the deflectable ring element. The openings may be slits or apertures cut through the wall of the seat ring (30), the axial slots between the fingers of a slotted wall (332), or the cells of a cellular wall (432), as described with reference to FIG. 12. The openings are closed in the closed state and the normal-open state, either because the strained wall holds them shut or because they lie against the core body bore (22), and they are opened or uncovered when the purge shoulder (44) strains the wall during over-travel. The openings are placed where the wall moves away from the surface that closes them.

Accordingly, the purge opening through which the air purge passes may be an annular gap between a surface of the deflectable ring element and an adjacent surface of the core body (20), the valve stem (10) or the poppet (40); one or more slits or apertures through the wall of the deflectable ring element; or one or more gaps between the fingers of a segmented deflectable ring element. The deflectable ring element may correspondingly be continuous around its circumference, segmented by slots that leave a continuous band at the sealing edge (34), or split by a single axial split. A split deflectable ring element, in which the split itself is the purge opening and opens circumferentially under a tapered purge shoulder (244), is used only as a wiper ring (132) together with a fixed seat (130), because a split through a seat ring (30) would interrupt the sealing edge (34).

#### Alternative purge flow paths

In the embodiment of FIG. 3 the purge flow path (62) runs from the supply side, through the purge gap (60), across the surfaces of the valve core on which sealant film accumulates, and into the inflatable chamber alongside the main passage (64). The purge flow path (62) may take any of the following alternative forms, in each of which it remains distinct from the main passage (64) and carries inflation gas in the inflation direction into the inflatable chamber.

As shown in FIG. 13A, the purge flow path (62) may include one or more purge ports (262) formed as radial holes or slots through the wall of the core body (20) on the chamber side of the purge gap (60). The purge ports (262) lead from the purge gap (60) to the clearance between the core body (20) and the stem bore (12), and thence into the inflatable chamber. The purge ports (262) fix the position at which the air purge, and the sealant film it carries, leave the valve core. Two or more purge ports (262) may be distributed around the circumference of the core body (20).

As shown in FIG. 13B, the purge flow path (62) may include one or more flutes (362) formed as axial grooves in the core body bore (22) at the axial position of the seat ring (30). In the closed state and the normal-open state the outer surface of the seat ring (30) lies on the lands between the flutes (362), closing them. When the bounding portion of the outer surface of the seat ring (30) moves away from the core body bore (22) in the purge state, the flutes (362) are placed in communication with the supply side and carry the air purge past the seat ring (30). The flutes (362) set the flow area of the purge flow path (62) independently of the size of the purge gap opening, so that the flow area can be chosen by the number, width and depth of the flutes (362) rather than by the radial deflection alone.

Where the purge gap (60) is formed by openings through the wall of the deflectable ring element, as described above with reference to the slotted wall (332) and the cellular wall (432), the purge flow path (62) passes through the wall of the deflectable ring element and then along the poppet stem (41) or the core body bore (22) into the inflatable chamber. The purge ports (262) and the flutes (362) may be combined with any of the locations of the purge gap (60) described in the preceding section, and with one another.

#### Alternative deflecting formations

The purge shoulder (44) is one species of a deflecting formation, which is any formation that moves with the poppet (40) and that contacts the deflectable ring element only during over-travel, so as to deflect it radially and open the purge gap (60). In every deflecting formation described here the same poppet (40) performs both ranges of movement: during the normal stroke the deflecting formation is spaced from the deflectable ring element and the main passage (64) is open, and during over-travel the same poppet (40) carries the deflecting formation against the deflectable ring element. There is no second valve member and no second seat to be fouled by sealant film. In the embodiment of FIG. 1 the deflecting formation is an annular purge shoulder (44) integral with the poppet (40), on the chamber side of the sealing face (42), which bears axially on the seat ring (30) during over-travel so that the convoluted wall (32) converts the axial load into radial deflection.

As shown in FIG. 11A, the deflecting formation may be a tapered purge shoulder (244) having a frustoconical flank on the poppet (40) that enters the seat ring (30) during over-travel and deflects the seat ring (30) radially outward by direct radial contact, in the manner in which a mandrel expands a ring. Because the load applied by the tapered purge shoulder (244) is radial rather than axial, the seat ring (30) need not have an axial-to-radial conversion geometry, and a seat ring (30) with a plain cylindrical wall may be used with the tapered purge shoulder (244). The flank of the tapered purge shoulder (244) slides on the inner surface of the seat ring (30) during over-travel and during the return, and thereby also wipes that inner surface. The tapered purge shoulder (244) may equally be a taper on the poppet stem (41).

As shown in FIG. 11B, the deflecting formation may be provided as two or more discrete lugs (344) on the poppet (40), for example two to four discrete lugs (344) spaced around its circumference, in place of a continuous annular shoulder. The discrete lugs (344) load the deflectable ring element at discrete points, so that the ring ovalises, moving outward at the loaded points and inward between them, and the purge gap (60) opens over arcs of the circumference rather than all round. Less force is needed to deflect the deflectable ring element by discrete lugs (344) than by a continuous annular shoulder, and the purge flow is correspondingly non-uniform around the circumference.

As shown in FIG. 11C, the deflecting formation may be a separate collar (444) fixed on the poppet stem (41), for example pressed, crimped or threaded onto it. The separate collar (444) performs the function of the purge shoulder (44) and allows the material that contacts the deflectable ring element to be chosen independently of the material of the poppet (40); for example a separate collar (444) of stainless steel may be fitted to a poppet (40) of brass.

In further embodiments the deflecting formation is a return-spring retainer that seats the return spring (50) on the poppet (40) and that bears on the chamber-side end of the seat ring (30) during over-travel, so that one part both retains the return spring (50) and deflects the seat ring (30); the contact with the seat ring (30) is then at the end of the seat ring (30) remote from the sealing edge (34). In other embodiments the deflecting formation is carried on a purge sleeve that slides on the poppet stem (41): the purge sleeve travels with the poppet (40) during the normal stroke and is driven onto the seat ring (30) only during over-travel, the sealing face (42) remaining on the poppet (40) itself. In other embodiments the deflecting formation is the sealing face (42) itself, or a continuation of the sealing face (42), which seals on the sealing edge (34) in the closed state and, when driven further during over-travel, forces the sealing edge (34) to deflect radially.

The deflectable ring element and the deflecting formation may also be exchanged between the poppet (40) and the core body (20). In such an inverted arrangement the deflectable ring element is a thin skirt carried by the poppet (40), a fixed formation in the core body (20) deflects the skirt during over-travel to open the purge gap (60), and on shape recovery the skirt moves across the core body bore (22). The arrangement of FIG. 1 to FIG. 4, in which the deflectable ring element is held in the core body (20) and the deflecting formation moves with the poppet (40), is preferred because the shape recovery of the held seat ring (30) then moves the seat ring (30) across the sealing face (42), which is the surface that seals and the surface on which sealant film matters, and because the wiping element is the seat itself, so that no separate wiping part is added that could itself be fouled.

#### Alternative threshold-setting arrangements

The second spring stage (54) of the return spring (50) is one species of a threshold-setting arrangement, which is any arrangement by which the force resisting movement of the poppet (40) rises at the end of the normal stroke, so that the pressure differential must exceed an over-travel threshold, higher than the crack pressure, before the poppet (40) enters over-travel. The over-travel threshold is what allows the valve core to inflate and to seal in the same way as a conventional valve core until the purge state is reached: below the over-travel threshold only the main passage (64) is open and the deflectable ring element is not touched. The threshold-setting arrangement may be provided in any of the following ways, and in each of them the over-travel threshold is set as a relationship to the crack pressure and to the pressure that the inflation device supplies, rather than by any one spring construction.

In the embodiment of FIG. 1 the return spring (50) is a compression spring having a first spring stage (52) of lower rate, which resists the normal stroke and sets the crack pressure, and a second spring stage (54) of higher rate, which the pressure differential must overcome to produce over-travel and which sets the over-travel threshold. In one construction of such a return spring (50) the coils are of variable pitch, and the close-pitched section becomes solid at the end of the normal stroke, leaving fewer active coils and so a higher rate for the second spring stage (54).

As shown in FIG. 14A, the threshold-setting arrangement may comprise an auxiliary spring (254) in addition to the return spring (50). The return spring (50) then acts alone over the normal stroke as the first spring stage (52), and the auxiliary spring (254) is engaged only when a lost-motion clearance between the auxiliary spring (254) and the poppet (40) closes at the end of the normal stroke, after which the auxiliary spring (254) acts in parallel with the return spring (50). The two stages can then be tuned independently, and the lost-motion clearance is a feature that can be seen and measured on the assembled valve core. The auxiliary spring (254) may be a compression spring of wire, a stack of one or more conical disc springs, which gives a high force in a short axial length, or an annular compression bush of an engineering polymer.

As shown in FIG. 14B, the threshold-setting arrangement may comprise a detent (354) that holds the poppet (40) at the end of the normal stroke until the force on the poppet (40) exceeds a breakaway value. The detent (354) may be a spring-loaded ball, or a radially sprung element, carried in the core body (20) and engaging a circumferential groove on the poppet stem (41) at the end of the normal stroke; or it may be a permanent magnet carried in the core body (20) that attracts a ferromagnetic target, for example of ferritic stainless steel, carried on the poppet (40). After breakaway the resisting force falls, so that the poppet (40) passes through over-travel quickly, and the detent (354) releases on the return so that the poppet (40) can close. The magnetic form of the detent (354) has no sliding contact exposed to liquid sealant.

The threshold-setting arrangement may also be provided by the stiffness of the deflectable ring element itself, with a return spring (50) of a single rate. The over-travel threshold is then set by the force that the deflecting formation needs to deflect the deflectable ring element. Where the deflectable ring element is formed from a superelastic alloy, its loading follows a stress plateau after an initial elastic rise, so that the force needed to deflect it behaves as a threshold; the over-travel threshold set in this way depends on the dimensions of the deflectable ring element and on temperature. A deflectable ring element of a resilient polymer or of a resilient metal without a phase transformation has no such plateau, and the over-travel threshold set by its stiffness alone is less distinct. In any of the arrangements described in this section the return spring (50) and the deflectable ring element may each contribute to the over-travel threshold.

In a further arrangement the deflecting formation is carried not by the poppet (40) but by a separate purge actuator, for example an annular piston or a flexible diaphragm housed in the core body (20), exposed on its supply side to the supply-side pressure and biased by its own compression spring. The purge actuator advances only when the pressure differential exceeds the over-travel threshold, carries the deflecting formation onto the deflectable ring element, and thereby opens the purge gap (60) while inflation gas is flowing, the poppet (40) performing only its normal stroke. The purge actuator adds a part and a sliding seal exposed to liquid sealant, and it is more readily accommodated in valve cores of larger bore, such as a Schrader-pattern valve core, than in a Presta-pattern valve core.

#### Alternative wiping arrangements

The snap-back wipe is the movement of the deflectable ring element across a surface of the poppet (40) as the deflectable ring element undergoes shape recovery when the pressure differential falls, by which movement partially cured sealant film is sheared from that surface. The snap-back wipe is defined by that movement and not by its speed or force. In the embodiment of FIG. 4 the recovering seat ring (30) moves across the sealing face (42), or across a surface of the poppet (40) adjacent to the sealing face (42). Shape recovery of a superelastic alloy is preferred because it is rapid and exerts a higher force, and so gives a stronger snap-back wipe; a seat ring (30) of a resilient polymer recovers more slowly and with less force, so that its snap-back wipe is weaker, but that movement is a snap-back wipe within the meaning used here, and the air purge provides a cleaning action independently of it.

In embodiments having a fixed seat (130) and a wiper ring (132), as shown in FIG. 10, the wiper ring (132) is deflected outward by the purge shoulder (44) during over-travel and undergoes shape recovery when the pressure differential falls. The wiper ring (132) may then perform the snap-back wipe on a surface of the poppet (40) in the same manner as the seat ring (30), while the fixed seat (130) performs the sealing function.

A mechanical wipe may additionally be provided by a structure that does not depend on shape recovery, in combination with the recovering deflectable ring element. In one such embodiment a fixed wiping land is formed as a close-clearance land in the core body bore (22), or on the fixed seat (130), through which the poppet (40) passes on every stroke, so that the relative axial movement between the poppet (40) and the fixed wiping land shears sealant film from the poppet (40); the deflection of the deflectable ring element then opens the purge gap (60) and the fixed wiping land supplements the snap-back wipe. In another embodiment a rotational wipe is provided by a helical guide between the poppet stem (41) and the core body (20), which turns the poppet (40) through a small angle during over-travel and back again on the return, so that the sealing face (42) rubs circumferentially across the sealing edge (34) in addition to the snap-back wipe.

The seat ring (30) may be provided with a defined wiping edge at its inner surface, being a sharp-cornered edge set by the cut profile of the seat ring (30) and preserved by limiting the extent of electropolishing, which is presented to the poppet (40) as the seat ring (30) recovers. The defined wiping edge concentrates the contact pressure so that the recovering seat ring (30) shears the sealant film rather than riding over it. The cleaning action of the valve core may be provided by the air purge, by the snap-back wipe, or by both, as described in relation to the operation of the valve core.

#### Alternative sealing faces

In the embodiment of FIG. 1 the sealing face (42) is a conical surface of the poppet (40) of brass, which seats on the sealing edge (34) of the seat ring (30) with metal-to-metal contact and without an elastomer. The sealing face (42) is one species of a sealing surface of the poppet (40), which may take any of the following alternative forms. The sealing face (42) may be part-spherical, giving line contact on the sealing edge (34) that tolerates misalignment of the poppet (40). The sealing face (42) may be a flat surface that bears on the axial end of the seat ring (30); such a flat sealing face (42) is less self-centring, and sealant film on the sealing surfaces has a greater effect on it.

The sealing face (42) may be of hard-anodised aluminium, the poppet (40) or a facing of the poppet (40) being of aluminium with a hard-anodised surface; a hard-anodised aluminium sealing face (42), or another wear-resistant finish of the sealing face (42), may be used where wear of the sealing face (42) from repeated metal-to-metal contact with the sealing edge (34) over the service life of the valve core is to be reduced. The sealing face (42) may be a polymer facing on the poppet (40), for example of PEEK, bearing on a metal sealing edge (34). The sealing face (42) may carry a hard low-adhesion coating, for example of electroless nickel or diamond-like carbon. A low-adhesion coating used alone only slows the build-up of sealant film and does not remove a film once formed; in the valve core described here such a coating is used together with the air purge and the snap-back wipe, which remove the film, so that the coating reduces the adhesion of what the cleaning action has to remove.

#### Materials of the core body

In the embodiment of FIG. 1 the core body (20) and the valve stem (10) are turned from free-machining brass CW614N, and the poppet (40) is of brass. The core body (20) may instead be formed from a corrosion-resistant material, and in particular from stainless steel, for example stainless steel 303, for corrosion resistance where the liquid sealant carried in the inflatable chamber contains ammonia. The choice of a stainless steel core body (20) is independent of the material and form of the deflectable ring element: a stainless steel core body (20) may be used with a seat ring (30) of a superelastic alloy, with a seat ring (30) of a resilient polymer, or with a wiper ring (132) used together with a fixed seat (130). The valve stem (10) and the poppet (40) may likewise be of stainless steel.

The core body (20) may also be formed from an engineering polymer, for example by injection moulding, in place of a metal. Where the core body (20) is of a resilient polymer or of spring-temper stainless steel, the deflectable ring element may be formed integrally with the core body (20) as a thin-walled flexure section, as described in relation to the deflectable ring element; a core body (20) of brass CW614N is not suitable for an integral deflectable ring element because the elastic strain that brass can recover is too small for the radial deflection required.

#### Retention of the deflectable ring element

In the embodiment of FIG. 1 and FIG. 8C the seat ring (30) is retained in the core body (20) by an interference fit of approximately 0.03 mm on diameter, and is seated axially against the internal shoulder (24). The interference fit is one species of ring retention, which is any means by which the deflectable ring element is held in the core body (20), or in the valve stem (10), against the internal shoulder (24) or an equivalent locating formation, so that the position of the sealing edge (34) is fixed while the portion of the deflectable ring element that undergoes radial deflection remains free to deflect. Whichever ring retention is used, it leaves that portion free.

The interference fit may be confined to an axial band of the seat ring (30) at the internal shoulder (24), the remainder of the outer surface of the seat ring (30) being free of the core body bore (22), as described with reference to FIG. 6. As shown in FIG. 8D, the seat ring (30) may instead be retained by a crimped lip (224) formed on the core body (20) and rolled or swaged over the end of the seat ring (30) remote from the internal shoulder (24); the crimped lip (224) retains the seat ring (30) axially and frees its outer surface from radial interference. As shown in FIG. 8E, the seat ring (30) may be retained by a retaining sleeve (324) pressed or threaded into the core body (20) and clamping the seat ring (30) against the internal shoulder (24), with the same freeing of the outer surface at the cost of one further part.

The deflectable ring element may also be retained by an adhesive bond, using an adhesive resistant to the liquid sealant and in particular to ammonia; by staking of the core body (20) over the deflectable ring element; by laser welding of the deflectable ring element to the core body (20); by over-moulding of a polymer core body (20) around the deflectable ring element; or by clamping of the deflectable ring element between two parts of the core body (20). The deflectable ring element may be formed integrally with the core body (20), in which case no separate ring retention is needed, or may be carried by the valve stem (10) rather than by the core body (20), as described in relation to the deflectable ring element and in the section on valve formats below.

Any of the ring retention means described here may be combined with one another. In some embodiments an interference fit is combined with a supplementary retention feature, for example an adhesive bond, a crimped lip (224) or a groove in the core body bore (22) into which the deflectable ring element snaps, so that the deflectable ring element cannot rotate or migrate axially in the core body bore (22), and cannot come free into the inflatable chamber, if the interference fit relaxes with repeated thermal cycling or vibration.

#### Valve formats and systems

The valve core is described above as a Presta-pattern valve core, whose core body (20) is threaded into the stem bore (12) of a Presta-pattern valve stem (10) of 6 mm outer diameter. The Presta-pattern valve core is the worked example and not a limitation: the operation of the deflectable ring element, the deflecting formation, the threshold-setting arrangement and the purge gap (60) does not depend on the thread by which the core body (20) is received, on the outer diameter of the valve stem (10), or on the Presta pattern. The same mechanism may be built into a valve core of another pattern, and into an inflation valve that has no removable valve core.

As shown schematically in FIG. 15A, the mechanism may be provided in a Schrader-pattern valve core, having a Schrader-pattern core body (620) and a Schrader-pattern poppet (630) which carry the seat ring (30), the purge shoulder (44) and the purge gap (60) in the same relationship as in FIG. 1 to FIG. 4. The larger bore of a Schrader-pattern valve core provides more room for the components of the mechanism, and in particular for a separate purge actuator as described above; and in a Schrader-pattern valve core the Schrader-pattern poppet (630) may be depressed by the head of the inflation device, which is one of the ways of producing over-travel described in relation to the operation of the valve core. The mechanism may likewise be provided in a Dunlop-pattern valve core.

As shown schematically in FIG. 15B, the mechanism may be provided in a non-removable inflation valve, in which the valve stem (10) directly carries a seat ring (730), together with the poppet (40) and the return spring (50), and there is no separate core body (20). The valve stem (10) then performs the functions that the core body (20) performs in the removable valve core, providing the locating formation against which the seat ring (730) is retained and the bore in which the poppet (40) is guided. Any of the ring retention means described above may be used to retain the seat ring (730) in the valve stem (10). The non-removable inflation valve is suited in particular to inflation valves fitted as original equipment to tubeless bicycle tyre set-ups.

An inflation valve according to this description comprises the valve stem (10) and, where the valve core is removable, the valve core received in the stem bore (12). The inflation valve may be fitted to a bicycle inner tube or to a tubeless bicycle tyre set-up, and the inflatable chamber so formed, holding liquid sealant, together with the inflation valve, forms a further aspect of this description; so do a wheel and a bicycle incorporating such an inflatable chamber and inflation valve. The mechanism is described with reference to inflation valves for bicycle inflatable chambers that hold liquid sealant, which is the application that has been built and tested; the same mechanism may be applied to inflation valves for other inflatable chambers that hold liquid sealant, and to other fluid valves in which a deposit forms on the sealing surfaces and is removed by a purge flow and by the recovery of a deflectable ring element.

#### Component and kit supply

The valve core is a unit that is separable from the valve stem (10), and it may be supplied as a replacement valve core for fitting into the stem bore (12) of an existing valve stem (10) in place of a conventional valve core, so that an inflatable chamber already fitted with a conventional inflation valve is given the cleaning action described here without replacement of the valve stem (10). The replacement valve core may be supplied singly or in a multipack, and may have a seat ring (30) of a superelastic alloy, a seat ring (30) of a resilient polymer, or a fixed seat (130) with a wiper ring (132).

The seat ring (30) may also be supplied as a component on its own, for fitting into a core body (20). The seat ring (30) may further be supplied as a kit together with a poppet (40) having a sealing face (42) and a purge shoulder (44), and a return spring (50) having a first spring stage (52) and a second spring stage (54), for fitting into a core body (20) supplied separately, as indicated in FIG. 5. In such a kit the seat ring (30), the poppet (40) and the return spring (50) are matched so that, when assembled into the core body (20), the purge shoulder (44) is spaced from the seat ring (30) throughout the normal stroke and bears on the seat ring (30) only in over-travel, and the purge gap (60) is closed in the closed state and the normal-open state.

#### Thermally actuated purge

As a separate concept, the purge gap (60) may be opened by warming the deflectable ring element rather than by over-travel. In such an embodiment the deflectable ring element is formed from a two-way shape-memory alloy of nickel and titanium having an austenite finish temperature of about 35 °C to 40 °C, so that the deflectable ring element changes shape and opens the purge gap (60) when it is warmed above that temperature, for example by hand, and closes the purge gap (60) when it cools again. This thermally actuated purge allows the purge gap (60) to be opened without an inflation device, for a manual purge, and is described here as a concept distinct from the over-travel-actuated purge state of the embodiments described above; it has not been built or tested.

#### Combinations of the features described

The features described in this section and in the preceding sections on the deflectable ring element may be combined with one another and with each of the three principal embodiments described in the examples, namely the embodiment in which the deflectable ring element is a seat ring (30) formed from a superelastic alloy, the embodiment in which the deflectable ring element is a seat ring (30) formed from a resilient polymer, and the embodiment in which the deflectable ring element is a wiper ring (132) used together with a fixed seat (130). Each of the alternative locations of the purge gap (60), the alternative purge flow paths (62), the alternative deflecting formations, the alternative threshold-setting arrangements, the alternative wiping arrangements, the alternative sealing faces (42), the alternative materials of the core body (20), the alternative ring retention means and the alternative valve formats described above is disclosed in combination with each of the others and with each of the three principal embodiments, except where this description states an incompatibility or a dependency.

The stated incompatibilities and dependencies are these. Electropolishing is a finishing process for a metal deflectable ring element and does not apply to a deflectable ring element of a resilient polymer. An austenite finish temperature is a property of a superelastic alloy: where the deflectable ring element is formed from a superelastic alloy, the alloy is selected to have an austenite finish temperature below a threshold, such as 5 °C, so that the deflectable ring element remains superelastic across the intended range of ambient temperatures; no austenite finish temperature applies to a deflectable ring element of a resilient polymer, or to a wiper ring (132) of a resilient metal that is not a superelastic alloy. A split deflectable ring element is used only as a wiper ring (132) with a fixed seat (130). A seat ring (30) with a plain cylindrical wall is used with a tapered purge shoulder (244) or another deflecting formation that applies a radial load. An integral deflectable ring element requires a core body (20) of a resilient polymer or of spring-temper stainless steel. A separate purge actuator is more readily accommodated in a valve core of larger bore than the Presta-pattern valve core.

In some embodiments, therefore, the deflectable ring element is a seat ring (30) formed from a superelastic alloy, such as an alloy of nickel and titanium, having an austenite finish temperature below 5 °C; in other embodiments the deflectable ring element is a seat ring (30) formed from a resilient polymer, such as PEEK; and in other embodiments the deflectable ring element is a wiper ring (132) separate from a fixed seat (130). The core body (20) may be formed from a corrosion-resistant material, such as stainless steel, independently of which of these forms the deflectable ring element takes. The convoluted wall (32) described for converting axial load into radial deflection may be applied to a seat ring (30) that also performs the sealing function or, in embodiments having a separate fixed seat (130), may be applied to a wiper ring (132) that performs the purging and wiping function without also sealing. A lock nut (70) for holding the poppet (40) in the closed state by hand may be combined with any of these embodiments.

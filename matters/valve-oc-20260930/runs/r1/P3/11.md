**Findings for Claims Architect and Specification Drafter** (candidate; for Gate 2):

1. **DA-1 is the cheapest escape from N0.** If the inventor confirms that purging on every opening works (Q1), then the threshold-setting arrangement (C3) is not essential and should not appear in the broadest claim.
2. **The purge gap needs a functional definition (DA-2, DA-3).** The locked species, "annular radial clearance between the seat ring outer surface and the core body bore", can be avoided without cost. This holds even before 01 Q1/Q2 are answered, and those answers are needed before any of this can be enabled.
3. **Three more features should be described by function or as a genus:**
   - the purge shoulder (DA-4);
   - the deflectable ring element, as separate from or integral with the core body (DA-5);
   - the mechanism, as usable in any inflation valve (DA-6).
4. **Four ladder features are already shown to be non-essential by the inventor's own §6 alternatives or by ordinary engineering:**
   - the convoluted wall (DA-12, DA-18);
   - the superelastic alloy (DA-13, DA-14);
   - the one-piece seat (DA-17);
   - the two-stage return spring (DA-11).
5. **DA-7, DA-23 and DA-24 cannot be closed without matter the inventor has not conceived.** They are recorded as inventor questions only.
6. **Detectability.**
   - A teardown and section of a competitor's valve core shows the routes in DA-2 to DA-6 and DA-11 to DA-18.
   - DA-1, DA-9 and DA-10 turn on behaviour (when deflection occurs, and whether air flows through the purge gap), which needs a bench test on the product.

## Rationale

**Where the limitations came from.** The attack targets the N0 elements first, because a competitor escapes the broadest claim only by avoiding one of them. The ladder rungs come after, because escaping a rung only defeats a fallback. Without `09`, the proposed limitations were taken from `03`'s nucleus and ladder and from `02`'s claim-type ranks.

**How the rows were ranked.** Routes that escape N0 cheaply and that the dossier cannot yet close are ranked highest:
- DA-1: the threshold;
- DA-2 and DA-3: the purge gap as locked;
- DA-4: the purge shoulder as a separate formation;
- DA-5 and DA-6: the part and product format.

DA-2 ranks high for a specific reason: the base embodiment's purge gap location is itself unresolved (01 Q1, Q2). A competitor can therefore exploit whichever reading the claims do not cover.

**How the genus proposals were limited.** They are confined to functional generalisations of what the inventor already describes:
- deflection in some direction, opening some clearance;
- a formation on the poppet;
- a resilient ring element that is carried and retained somehow.

Routes that need a different mechanism are marked as not closable and turned into inventor questions rather than padded into embodiments. These are porting (DA-7), a rigid wiping edge (DA-23) and deflection by pressure alone (DA-24).

**Plausibility reasoning.** This rests on general mechanics, not on the dossier:
- conventional spring metals recover much less strain than superelastic alloys (DA-14);
- the hoop load at the disclosed pressures cannot usefully deflect a 0.20 mm NiTi wall (DA-24).

No web research was done, so no external source is cited.

**Rows not carried separately.** Moving the purge shoulder to the other side of the sealing face was folded into DA-4, because it depends on the unresolved orientation (01 Q3). Purging on the inflation device's piston cycles was folded into DA-1.

**Missing inputs.** `09` and `10` would have supplied the prior-art differentiation. They could re-rank rows whose genus is already crowded in the art, and they could show that some proposed genus language reads on prior art.

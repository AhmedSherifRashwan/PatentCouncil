## Rationale

I organised the register by the five categories the task names — mechanism failure, abuse, manufacturing variability, environmental conditions, user error — and worked outward from the N0–N7 nucleus in 03 and the essential/provisional tags on each feature (C1–C32), since a feature the ladder already flags as carrying an enablement risk (the purge gap, C6/C7; the convolution, C12; the threshold arrangement, C17; the interference fit, C23) is exactly where a failure mode is most likely to bite, and also where 01/03's own open questions already point.

FM-1 is placed first because it is the one item with direct bench-data support rather than being purely hypothetical: the stereo-microscope inspection at cycle 40 found cured film on the ring outer surface in the purge-gap region even though the valve had not yet failed (bench data). That is evidence of an emerging failure mode the 40-cycle test window did not run long enough to resolve, not an invented one. FM-2 through FM-9 build on it and on the ring/threshold uncertainties already flagged in 03 (Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4, and the Q on what limits over-travel). I did not invent a mechanism for how the purge gap opens or a value for any threshold; where a mitigation depends on that mechanism I said so and marked it candidate.

FM-8 and FM-18 are the two items grounded in something beyond the inventor's own text: general engineering knowledge that (a) unlubricated metal-to-metal valve seats wear, and (b) brass is known to be susceptible to ammonia stress-corrosion cracking. I used this only because the AGENTS.md method permits background technical-field knowledge to inform analysis, and because the disclosure itself already shows the inventor anticipated (b) — Alternative F1 exists "for corrosion resistance with ammonia-bearing sealant" (§5). I did not treat either as a new fact about this invention; both mitigations point back to alternatives or materials the inventor already named (F1, F2), so no new matter is proposed, only a reason to confirm those alternatives were built and to consider them for the fallback ladder.

FM-9's "over-travel stop" is written as a candidate structural feature rather than asserted as already present, because 01 Q36 and 03 explicitly record that the disclosure does not say what limits over-travel. I did not guess which of the three readings (stop, coil-bind, the ring itself) is correct; I proposed the stop as a mitigation worth disclosing precisely because the answer is unknown and a stop is the only one of the three that protects the ring from an abuse-level overpressure regardless of the answer.

Several items (FM-10, FM-11, FM-13, FM-19, FM-20) are marked "not worth disclosing as an embodiment" because either no technical mitigation is credible without data the dossier lacks, or the only available response is a non-technical warning. Padding these with invented structures would fail the "technically credible" bar; I recorded them anyway because scope impact, not disclosability, is what open questions and failure registers are for.

FM-21 is flagged as a possible gap in the invention's own coverage: §1 identifies capillary migration along the poppet stem as part of the problem, but the disclosed cleaning actions operate at the seat ring and purge gap, not along the poppet's fit in the core body. I did not resolve this with an invented feature; it is recorded as an open question because it could matter to how broadly the technical effect can be claimed.

I treated equivalent architectures (Prompt 12) and parameter ladders (Prompt 13) as unavailable and did not wait for them, per the task's instruction that Phase 3 prompts run independently; several mitigations above (over-travel stop, supplementary ring retention feature, wear-resistant sealing face) will likely also appear as equivalents or ladder rungs in those prompts, and Claims Architect and the runner can reconcile numbering.

## Assumptions

A1. The purge-gap location and opening mechanism remain as unresolved in 01/03 (Q1, Q2 of both): I have not assumed a specific reading of "stem bore" versus "core body bore," or of how outward radial deflection opens a gap given the interference fit. Mitigations touching the purge gap (FM-1, FM-2, FM-9's interaction, FM-22) are accordingly doubly provisional: provisional as failure-mode candidates, and dependent on that unresolved mechanism being described at all. **Blocking** for P4, P5, as in 03.

A2. The closed purge gap is assumed not to be relied upon for sealing at rest — sealing is assumed to depend solely on the sealing edge — consistent with 03's working assumption (03 A5) and with the bench data showing low leak-down (≤ 0.2 bar/24 h) at cycle 40 alongside film in the purge-gap region rather than a corresponding leak increase. This assumption underlies FM-22's "no mitigation needed" entry. **Blocking** for P4 until 01 Q31 / 03 Q11 are answered.

A3. The brass/ammonia stress-corrosion mechanism cited in FM-18 is treated as general materials-science background knowledge available to a person skilled in the field, used only to explain why Alternative F1 (§5) exists, not as an inventor-supplied fact about this specific core. Not blocking; the alternative it points to (F1) is already in the dossier and its build/test status is separately open (01 Q18).

A4. Metal-to-metal wear at the sealing edge (FM-8) is treated as a generic consequence of the disclosed at-rest seal geometry (§3(4), no elastomer) over the inventor's own stated target of "several thousand" cycles (§9), not as a measured or observed effect — the bench test ran only 40 cycles and reported the poppet face clean, not worn. Not blocking.

A5. 01's assumptions A3–A5 (pressure differential definition, orientation, direction of poppet travel) and 03's A1–A5 (ring-element genus, air-purge/wipe essentiality, threshold arrangement, convolution as advantageous, Af essential only for a shape-memory alloy) are carried forward unchanged; this analysis does not revisit them. **Blocking** as previously recorded.

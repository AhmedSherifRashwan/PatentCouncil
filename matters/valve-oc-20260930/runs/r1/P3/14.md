- **[e]** Electropolishing is an electrochemical metal-finishing process that removes surface oxide by anodic dissolution; it has no counterpart process for a polymer such as PEEK. Marking this cell compatible would assert a technically incoherent permutation. This is a general materials-science fact about the named process, not an invented fact about the invention.
- **[f]** The disclosed E-III wiper ring is "stainless" (§6), i.e. a metal, so electropolishing is technically feasible on it, but the disclosure does not describe this step for the wiper ring. Marked conditional, not compatible, until confirmed. See Q5.

- **[h]** The wiper ring of E-III is disclosed only as "stainless spring" (§6), read as conventional spring steel, not a superelastic alloy. As disclosed, no Af applies. A superelastic wiper-ring species is conceptually possible but is not in the disclosure. See Q4.

## Rationale

I built the three core-embodiment columns from `03`'s own account of where the nucleus ladder excludes each of the inventor's §6 alternatives (N2 excludes Alternative B, N4 excludes Alternative A), rather than waiting for `12`. That reading is already fully supported by `01`/`03` and lets the matrix do useful work now; it is narrower than a full equivalents-driven matrix would be, which is why I flagged the missing `12`/`10` inputs as a completeness limit rather than treating this matrix as final.

I treated material- and process-specific rows (electropolishing, Af) as the cases most likely to produce a genuinely incompatible cell, because they rest on general, checkable materials-science facts (electropolishing is an electrochemical process for metals; Af is an alloy transformation temperature) rather than on anything the inventor would need to confirm. I did not mark any cell incompatible on the strength of an invented technical judgement about the invention itself — every incompatible or conditional cell traces either to an external, checkable fact about the named process/property, or to a disclosure gap already recorded as an open question in `01`/`03`.

I ranked the commercially important combinations by `02`'s value map, and gave CB3 and CB4 priority because they directly answer the inventor's own named design-arounds; closing them with expressly-scoped specification language is the main purpose of this prompt. I wrote the draft sentences to hedge exactly where the matrix shows a conditional or incompatible cell (sentences 2, 4, 6, 7), so that none of them assert a permutation the matrix marks incompatible or unconfirmed.

A3. Austenite finish temperature (Af) is treated as a property that applies only to a shape-memory/superelastic alloy ring element, and therefore inapplicable to the PEEK seat ring and, as disclosed ("stainless spring"), to the wiper ring. Not blocking.

A4. The snap-back wipe (row 1) is treated as present but weaker for the PEEK seat ring (per §6's own statement) and as unconfirmed for the wiper ring (per `03` Q7), rather than assuming either full compatibility or full incompatibility. **Blocking** for finalising CB4's specification support until Q1 is answered.

A5. The N6 believed ranges are treated as specific to the superelastic seat ring embodiment and not assumed to transfer unmodified to the PEEK or split-architecture embodiments, given the stated differences in stiffness, recovery and part count. **Blocking** for P4/P5 support of CB3/CB4's numeric ranges until Q3 is answered.

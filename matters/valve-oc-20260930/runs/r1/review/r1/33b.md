### F-33-1 [high] [design-around] Independent claims require a normal-open state that the description itself says may be absent

Claim 1 (and claims 58, 79): "a normal-open state, at a pressure differential at or above the crack pressure and not exceeding an over-travel threshold higher than the crack pressure, in which ... the purge gap is closed". [0051] discloses: "In some embodiments the over-travel threshold is set close to the crack pressure, so that the purge state is reached on substantially every opening of the valve core and the normal stroke is short; in such embodiments the second spring stage (54) may be omitted and the return spring (50) has a single rate." Attack: a competitor builds exactly that variant, a deflectable seat ring loaded by the poppet on every opening, single-rate spring, no distinct normal-open state. Every independent claim requires the three-state sequence with the over-travel threshold "higher than the crack pressure", so the variant is outside all of them, and the applicant's own text is the design-around manual. Commercially this is the cheaper product: one spring rate, no threshold tuning. Least narrowing action: add an independent claim (valve core and method) reciting the closed state and the purge state only, the deflectable ring element being deflected by the poppet when displaced beyond a first displacement and recovering when the pressure differential falls, supported by [0051], [0014] and [0065]; keep the present claim 1 as the three-state claim. No new disclosure required.

### F-33-2 [high] [design-around] Purge state tied to the pressure differential; depressor-actuated and deflation-only purges escape

Claim 1: "a purge state, at a pressure differential exceeding the over-travel threshold, in which the poppet is in over-travel"; claim 79: "raising the pressure differential above an over-travel threshold ... whereby the poppet moves into over-travel". [0066]: "In some embodiments over-travel is produced, in addition or instead, by mechanical depression of the poppet (40): a depressor in the head of the inflation device, or in an adaptor ... pushes the poppet (40) beyond the normal stroke into over-travel when the inflation device is coupled, whatever the pressure differential"; [0067]: "in other embodiments it is reached during deflation only". Attack: a Schrader-style product in which the pump head depresses the poppet into over-travel (the ordinary Schrader mechanism, [0137]) never has a purge state "at a pressure differential exceeding the over-travel threshold"; its purge occurs "whatever the pressure differential". Claim 46 is only "additionally movable" and depends on claim 1. A deflation-only purge product likewise never performs the "raising" step of claim 79 and claim 88 depends on it. Least narrowing action: reformulate the purge state in an independent claim by poppet position, "when the poppet is displaced beyond the normal stroke into over-travel", with the pressure-differential threshold, the depressor and the deflation purge as alternatives in dependent claims. Supported by [0012], [0066], [0067], FIG. 7 and FIG. 15A. No new disclosure required.

### F-33-3 [high] [design-around] "a return spring" named in every product claim; a pressure-closed poppet has none

Claim 1, claim 58 and claim 79 each recite "a return spring (50) biasing the poppet towards the closed state". Attack: the competitor's engineers delete the spring. Chamber pressure on the poppet head closes a Presta-type poppet against the seat; the over-travel threshold can then be set by the stiffness of the deflectable ring element alone, which [0122] already describes as a threshold-setting arrangement. The product has the seat ring, the purge shoulder, the purge gap, the air purge and the snap-back wipe, and no element that is "a return spring". Whether a springless core is a viable product is a commercial question, but it is the single cheapest bill-of-materials change and the description gives no cover: [0009] and [0022] define the genus "biasing member" but every biasing member disclosed is a spring, and [0122] keeps "a return spring (50) of a single rate". Least narrowing action: first, recite "a biasing member" in the independent claims ([0009], [0022]) so that non-spring biasing is covered; second, add disclosure that in some embodiments the poppet is biased towards the closed state by the chamber-side pressure acting on the poppet without a separate spring, the over-travel threshold being set by the deflectable ring element as in [0122]. The second step is new disclosure and must be confirmed by the inventors as within their conception. Target P3.

### F-33-4 [high] [design-around] Seat ring claim 67 is limited to Presta-sized rings by numerical ranges in the independent claim

Claim 67: "the seat ring having a seat ring inner diameter of from 2.0 mm to 2.6 mm and a seat ring wall thickness of from 0.15 mm to 0.30 mm". Attack: a Schrader-pattern seat ring, which the description expressly contemplates ([0137]: "The larger bore of a Schrader-pattern valve core provides more room"; [0091]: "will scale the dimensions to the bore available in a larger valve core"), is outside claim 67 by construction. Within the Presta size, a 2.65 mm inner diameter or a 0.32 mm wall is outside, and a competitor selling replacement seat rings will pick those numbers. The ranges are easy to measure, which is good for us only inside the range. The component claim is the one that reaches an aftermarket seat ring supplier, and it is the easiest of the five to avoid. Least narrowing action: this is a broadening. Reformulate claim 67 without the ranges, resting on the sealing edge, the axial-to-radial conversion wall and shape recovery, and optionally on the superelastic alloy ([0034], [0078], [0084], [0091] support the dimensionless seat ring); keep claims 16, 17 and 78 as the numerical fallbacks. If novelty of a dimensionless ring is thought weak, the material limitation of claim 73 is the next least narrowing addition. Target P5.

### F-33-5 [medium] [design-around] "the purge gap ... is closed" in the closed and normal-open states; a permanent small downstream clearance escapes

Claim 1: "a purge gap (60) associated with the deflectable ring element is closed" (closed state) and "the purge gap is closed" (normal-open state). [0105], for the third purge gap location only: "the clearance ... is closed, or is sufficiently small to carry no significant flow, in the closed state and the normal-open state". Attack: the competitor places the purge gap on the chamber side of the sealing edge ([0105]) where a permanent clearance does not leak the tyre, leaves a measurable clearance at rest and widens it in over-travel. Its gap is never "closed", and the generic definition at [0018] says "closed". Provability is also weak: proving that a 0.15 mm annular clearance is fully closed on an accused product requires sectioning, and any measurable leak path lets the competitor argue the gap is open. Least narrowing action: add one generic sentence to the definition at [0018] that a purge gap is "closed" when it is closed or carries no significant flow relative to the main passage, extending the [0105] wording to every location, and use that definition in the claims. Requires new disclosure (the generalisation is not in the package for locations other than the third). Target P4.

### F-33-6 [medium] [design-around] "the deflectable ring element is in its at-rest form" in the normal-open state; a pre-loaded ring escapes

Claim 1, normal-open state: "the deflectable ring element is in its at-rest form and the purge gap is closed". Attack: the competitor sets the purge shoulder so that it touches and lightly pre-loads the ring at the end of the normal stroke without opening the gap. The ring is then not "in its at-rest form" during the normal-open state, though the purge gap is closed and the product otherwise behaves identically. The limitation is also hard to prove on a bench because the pre-load deflection is below the purge gap opening. Least narrowing action: in the normal-open state recite only that the purge gap is closed and, if wished, that the main passage is open, dropping "at-rest form" from that state. Support: [0018] defines the purge gap by being closed in the normal-open state, [0043] and [0047]. The closed state may keep "at-rest form". Target P5. No new disclosure required, though counsel should check EPO support for the omission against [0047].

### F-33-7 [medium] [design-around] "shape recovery to its at-rest form ... when the pressure differential falls below the over-travel threshold"

Claim 1, claim 58, claim 67 ("to its at-rest form on removal of the axial load") and claim 79. [0013] defines shape recovery as "its return towards its at-rest shape"; [0014] "undergoes shape recovery towards its at-rest form when the pressure differential falls". Attack: after cycling, any deflectable ring element has some residual set ([0099] admits "residual set of the ring element after many cycles"). The competitor measures its ring after 500 cycles, shows it does not return "to" the at-rest form, and argues no infringement; at the same time an examiner reads "to" as requiring full recovery and asks how it is proved. The tie to "below the over-travel threshold" adds a second escape: a ring whose gap closes only when the differential falls below the crack pressure. Least narrowing action: "undergoing shape recovery towards its at-rest form and closing the purge gap when the pressure differential falls", tracking [0013] and [0014]. Target P5. No new disclosure required.

### F-33-8 [medium] [design-around] Disclosed actuation architectures fall outside claim 1: separate purge actuator and inverted arrangement

Claim 1: "a deflectable ring element (30) carried by the core body" and "the poppet is in over-travel beyond the normal stroke and produces a radial deflection". [0123]: "the deflecting formation is carried not by the poppet (40) but by a separate purge actuator, for example an annular piston or a flexible diaphragm ... the poppet (40) performing only its normal stroke". [0117]: "the deflectable ring element is a thin skirt carried by the poppet (40), a fixed formation in the core body (20) deflects the skirt". Attack: both are described as workable, the purge actuator expressly for Schrader-pattern cores ([0123], [0137]), and neither has the poppet producing the deflection. A Schrader product with an annular purge piston infringes nothing. Least narrowing action: add a dependent-turned-independent formulation in which "a deflecting member movable in response to the pressure differential" produces the radial deflection, the poppet being one species, and a formulation in which the deflectable ring element is carried by either the core body or the poppet, supported by [0112], [0117], [0123]. Target P5. No new disclosure required.

### F-33-9 [medium] [design-around] "radial deflection" excludes an axially collapsing ring that uncovers an opening

Claim 1: "produces a radial deflection of the deflectable ring element, the radial deflection opening the purge gap"; [0020]: "radial deflection means elastic deformation of the wall ... that opens the purge gap". [0079]: the number of convolutions "is limited to that at which the wall still moves radially rather than merely shortening". Attack: the competitor uses a bellows-like ring that shortens axially under the shoulder and thereby uncovers a port in the bore, or lifts a lip axially off a seat, with no radial component that opens anything. The description concedes that pure shortening is outside the mechanism, so the claim will be construed to exclude it. Least narrowing action: add disclosure of a deflectable ring element whose deformation in over-travel, axial or radial, uncovers the purge opening, if that is within the inventors' conception; otherwise this remains an accepted boundary. Requires new disclosure. Target P3.

### F-33-10 [medium] [design-around] Method claim 79 is performed by the user and requires proof of sealant film on the accused surfaces

Claim 79: "inflation gas flows as an air purge along a purge flow path ... across surfaces on which sealant film accumulates". [0023] defines the air purge by that flow. Attack: every step of claim 79 is performed by the cyclist; the competitor is liable only by inducement or contributory infringement, which in the US requires knowledge and in the UK and EPC states requires supply of an essential element with knowledge of intended use. Further, "surfaces on which sealant film accumulates" makes infringement turn on the condition of the accused valve at the time of use; a clean valve, or a chamber without sealant, escapes and proof requires the accused article after use. Least narrowing action: recite "across a surface of the deflectable ring element" ([0056]: "the outer surface of the seat ring (30) and the adjoining region of the core body bore (22)"), which is structural and provable, and keep "on which sealant film accumulates" in a dependent claim. Ensure the product claims carry every structural feature the method relies on so that direct infringement lies against the manufacturer. Target P5. No new disclosure required.

### F-33-11 [medium] [design-around] Manufacturing claim 90 is hidden in the supplier's process and the claimed process removes its own evidence

Claim 90: "laser cutting a profile of the seat ring from the tube stock" and "heating the seat ring in the fixture and then water quenching the seat ring"; claim 96 "electropolishing the seat ring to remove ... a heat-affected edge left by the laser cutting". Attack: the process is performed by a NiTi component supplier, typically outside the jurisdiction; the laser-cut heat-affected edge that would evidence the cutting step is removed by electropolishing ([0149]); and [0150] discloses roll-forming, internal-pressure forming, photochemical etching of sheet and stamping as alternatives, each of which escapes "laser cutting" while making the same ring. "Water quenching" is a single disclosed quench and any other cooling escapes. Product-by-process reach (US 35 U.S.C. 271(g), UK s.60(1)(c), EPC national law) would help only if the product is distinguishable, which it is not once polished. Least narrowing action: reformulate claim 90 to "cutting a profile of the seat ring from tube stock or from sheet" and "cooling" only if the inventors confirm other quench media; [0150] supports the cutting alternatives, nothing supports other quenches. Counsel should weigh whether claim 90 earns its place. Target P5; the quench generalisation requires new disclosure.

None.

## Assumptions

A1. A conventional Presta-pattern valve core has its poppet closed by chamber pressure assisted by a small spring, and a poppet closed by chamber pressure alone is a mechanically viable check valve; stated as common general knowledge and as the premise of F-33-3.
A2. Bicycle inflation valves and valve cores are commonly manufactured and assembled outside the US, EPO and UK jurisdictions, so process claims are enforced, if at all, through importation provisions; common general knowledge, premise of F-33-11.
A3. A purge gap of the order of 0.1 mm to 0.25 mm inside a 6 mm valve stem is not observable in service and must be proved by sectioning and bench testing; premise of the detectability ratings.
A4. Claim 58 is read as covering the removable-core, stem-carried and non-removable variants through the indirect-carriage convention of [0005]; if a court reads "carried by the valve stem" narrowly the ratings for claim 58 worsen.

## Open Questions

Q1. Is a springless, chamber-pressure-closed poppet within the inventors' conception, and does the seat ring stiffness alone give a usable over-travel threshold in a Presta core (F-33-3)? Only the inventors can answer.
Q2. Is a deflectable ring element whose over-travel deformation is axial rather than radial within the inventors' conception (F-33-9)?
Q3. Will the product be sold with instructions that tell the user to inflate with a floor pump to a pressure above the over-travel threshold? Those instructions are the inducement evidence for claim 79 and counsel should shape them.
Q4. Which of the five independent claim families does the business intend to enforce first? The answer decides whether claim 90 is worth its examination cost.

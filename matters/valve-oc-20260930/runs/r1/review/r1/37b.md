### F-37-1 [high] [formal] Systemic multiple-dependent-claim-on-multiple-dependent-claim violation

**Claims attacked:** 10, 14, 18, 22, 23, 24, 25, 27, 28, 29, 32, 33, 34, 35, 36, 40, 41, 42, 43, 44, 45, 46, 47, 49, 50, 51, 52, 54, 55, 56, 57, 70, 71, 72, 73, 75, 76, 78, 82, 83, 84, 85, 86, 87, 88, 89, 93, 95, 96, 98, 99, 100, 102 (53 of 102 claims).

**Claim language quoted (representative):** Claim 10: "The valve core of any one of claims 1 to 9, wherein the deflectable ring element has a wall..." Claim 18: "The valve core of any one of claims 1 to 17, wherein the core body has an internal shoulder..." Claim 93: "The method of any one of claims 90 to 92, wherein the convoluted wall is formed with one convolution or with two convolutions."

**Attack.** Claims 8 and 9 are each multiple dependent claims ("The valve core of any one of claims 1 to 7..."). Claim 10 is itself a multiple dependent claim whose range ("any one of claims 1 to 9") includes claims 8 and 9. Under US practice a multiple dependent claim may not serve as a basis for any other multiple dependent claim, directly or indirectly (37 CFR 1.75(c); MPEP 608.01(n)(iii)). Once claim 10 is compromised, every later multiple dependent claim whose range reaches back past claim 9 inherits the same defect, and the defect compounds forward through the claim set: claim 18 ("1 to 17") sweeps in 8, 9, 10, 13, 14; claim 25 ("1 to 24") sweeps in all of those plus 18, 22, 23, 24; and so on to claim 57. The same pattern recurs independently in the seat-ring claims (claim 70 onward, seeded by claim 69, "claim 67 or claim 68"), the method-of-operating claims (claim 82 onward, seeded by claim 81), and the method-of-manufacturing claims (claim 93 onward, seeded by claim 92). An examiner will object to each of the 53 affected claims under MPEP 608.01(n) before reaching the merits, and until corrected none of them can be relied upon for the substantive review in Prompts 28–36 without first resolving which combinations they actually claim.

**Least narrowing action.** No claim scope needs to be given up. The claim set needs restructuring so that no multiple dependent claim's referenced range ever includes another multiple dependent claim: either (a) claims 8/9 (and the equivalent seed claims 69, 81, 92) are converted into a form that is not itself multiply dependent before being swept into later ranges, or (b) every later "any one of claims 1 to N" claim is redrafted to reference only the singly-dependent claims in that range, with any coverage that would have come through the excluded multiple dependent claims restored by duplicating the relevant limitation as its own claim depending singly from 8 and from 9. This is a mechanical renumbering exercise, not a scope reduction, but it touches over half the claim set and should be done once, by the Claims Architect, before Prompts 28–36 re-run against a stable claim numbering.

**Requires new disclosure:** No. **Suggested target:** P5 (claim reformulation).

### F-37-2 [medium] [formal] Reference numerals omitted from claims 79–89

**Claim language quoted:** Claim 79: "the inflation valve comprising a poppet having a supply side, a chamber side and a sealing face, a deflectable ring element, a main passage past the sealing face..." — no numeral appears anywhere in claims 79 through 89, even though every component named (poppet, sealing face, deflectable ring element, main passage, purge gap, purge flow path, return spring) carries a fixed reference numeral used consistently in claims 1, 58, 67 and 90–102.

**Attack.** A claim set that annotates the same components with reference numerals in some independent claims and their dependents but not in others invites confusion about whether the unlabelled claims are drawn to the same physical structure, and gives a court construing the method claims one fewer anchor to the specification than it has for the apparatus claims. EPC Rule 43(7) treats consistent, non-limiting reference-sign practice as the norm; the inconsistency here is avoidable.

**Least narrowing action.** Add the existing reference numerals to claims 79–89 at first mention of each component, exactly as done in claim 1 (see Corrections).

**Requires new disclosure:** No. **Suggested target:** in-place.

### F-37-3 [medium] [formal] Hybrid dependency format in claims 61, 89, 99

**Claim language quoted:** Claim 61: "...wherein the core body, the deflectable ring element, the poppet and the return spring form a valve core according to any one of claims 1 to 57." Claim 89: "...wherein the inflation valve comprises a valve core according to any one of claims 1 to 57 or is an inflation valve according to any one of claims 58 to 63." Claim 99: "...wherein the seat ring is a seat ring according to any one of claims 67 to 78."

**Attack.** Each of these claims already has a formal dependency (61 on 59/60; 89 on 79–88; 99 on 90–98) and additionally incorporates a second claim range inside its own body text via "according to any one of claims X to Y." This is a workable drafting technique for cross-referencing a sub-combination, but it is not the same thing as a formal claim dependency, and because the incorporated ranges (1–57, 58–63, 67–78) are themselves laden with the multiple dependent claims attacked in F-37-1, the claim's actual scope cannot be resolved until that defect is cured. An examiner may also query whether claims drafted this way should be counted as dependent claims for fee and restriction purposes.

**Least narrowing action.** Once F-37-1 is cured, confirm that claims 61, 89 and 99 are intended as true dependent claims incorporating a sub-combination by reference (a recognised, if less common, technique) rather than disguised independent claims; no wording change is needed if so, but the point should be confirmed rather than left implicit.

**Requires new disclosure:** No. **Suggested target:** P5.

### F-37-4 [medium] [formal] Dangling final clause in independent claims 1 and 58

**Claim language quoted:** Claim 1: "...the radial deflection opening the purge gap so that a purge flow path (62), separate from the main passage, extends from the supply side through the purge gap to the chamber side; the deflectable ring element undergoing shape recovery to its at-rest form and closing the purge gap when the pressure differential falls below the over-travel threshold."

**Attack.** The purge state is defined as occurring "at a pressure differential exceeding the over-travel threshold." The final clause describes what happens "when the pressure differential falls below the over-travel threshold" — i.e., outside the purge state, in recovery. As punctuated, it reads as a fourth semicolon-separated item continuing the list that began with "the closed state," "a normal-open state" and "a purge state," which invites the (incorrect) reading that shape recovery is part of the purge state's own definition rather than a separate transition following it. An opponent could exploit the ambiguity to argue either that the claim requires no recovery step at all (if read as decorative) or that recovery must occur "when" — immediately upon — the threshold being crossed (if read as a strict conditional), neither of which matches the specification's account of recovery as a transition through the normal-open state back to the closed state ([0044]).

**Least narrowing action.** Re-punctuate as a separate "wherein" clause, e.g.: "...to the chamber side; and wherein, when the pressure differential falls below the over-travel threshold, the deflectable ring element undergoes shape recovery to its at-rest form and closes the purge gap." No scope is lost; the clause is only clarified as a transition, not a fourth state.

**Requires new disclosure:** No. **Suggested target:** P5.

### F-37-5 [low] [design-around] Functional/means-plus-function exposure in "deflecting formation" and "threshold-setting arrangement"

**Claim language quoted:** Claim 2: "wherein the poppet has a deflecting formation (44) which is spaced from the deflectable ring element in the closed state..."; claim 36: "further comprising a threshold-setting arrangement by which a force resisting movement of the poppet rises at an end of the normal stroke..."

**Attack.** Both terms combine a generic structural noun with a statement of function and no other structural limitation. Under US practice a court may construe such terms under 35 U.S.C. §112(f), limiting each to the corresponding structures disclosed in the specification (purge shoulder, tapered purge shoulder, discrete lugs, separate collar, return-spring retainer, purge sleeve, sealing face itself, inverted skirt arrangement, for "deflecting formation"; second spring stage, auxiliary spring, detent, ring stiffness, separate purge actuator, for "threshold-setting arrangement") and their equivalents, rather than to the full breadth the open-ended language suggests. This is a live design-around signal: a competitor using an undisclosed mechanism to achieve the same function may fall outside the constructed claim scope even though the claim language reads broadly on its face. `terminology/LOCKED.md`'s own drafting notes acknowledge this risk and record that the description supplies a corresponding structure for each such term, which mitigates but does not eliminate it.

**Least narrowing action.** None required as a correction — this is a claim-construction risk inherent in the chosen drafting strategy, already knowingly accepted per the terminology lock. Flagged for the human/litigation assessment (Prompt 33) rather than as a drafting defect.

**Requires new disclosure:** No. **Suggested target:** in-place (informational).

### F-37-6 [low] [support] "Chamber side" applied to non-poppet components without antecedent

**Claim language quoted:** Claim 9: "...wherein the deflectable ring element is a wiper ring (132) separate from the fixed seat and positioned on the chamber side of the fixed seat." Claim 33: "...one or more purge ports (262) formed through a wall of the core body on the chamber side of the purge gap."

**Attack.** [0010] defines "the 'chamber side' of the poppet" specifically, tying the term to the poppet. Claims 9 and 33 use "the chamber side" with the definite article to locate the fixed seat and the purge gap respectively — components other than the poppet — without an antecedent basis that extends the term to them. A strict reading raises a 112(b) indefiniteness question: whose "chamber side" is meant, and is it the same reference plane as the poppet's chamber side or a new, undefined one local to each component?

**Least narrowing action.** The fix belongs in the description, not the claims: broaden [0010]'s definition of "chamber side"/"supply side" from a property of the poppet specifically to a general axial orientation within the valve core (see Prompt 38 Correction). No claim text needs to change once that definition is broadened, since claims 9 and 33 already use the term in a manner consistent with such a broadened definition, and so does the description itself at [0055] and [0093].

**Requires new disclosure:** No. **Suggested target:** P4 (description).

### F-37-7 [low] [formal] "About"/"approximately" used without a stated tolerance

**Claim language quoted:** Claim 20: "wherein the interference fit has an interference of approximately 0.03 mm on diameter." Claim 44: "wherein the crack pressure is about 1.0 bar and the over-travel threshold is about 3.5 bar."

**Attack.** [0007] states that "about"/"approximately" "does not itself define a specific numerical tolerance unless one is separately stated," and no tolerance is separately stated for either the 0.03 mm interference or the 1.0 bar/3.5 bar values. `terminology/LOCKED.md`'s drafting notes independently flag that relative terms without an objective standard draw clarity objections under EPO practice, and are not self-evidently definite under US or UK practice either.

**Least narrowing action.** Add a stated tolerance to the description (e.g., a manufacturing tolerance band for the interference fit, and a stated tolerance for the two pressure values) rather than deleting "about"/"approximately" from the claims, which would narrow to the single point value and lose legitimate manufacturing variance.

**Requires new disclosure:** Yes — a tolerance value is not currently stated anywhere in the package. **Suggested target:** P4.

### F-37-8 [low] [formal] Unenumerated single-sentence state definition in claims 1 and 58

**Claim language quoted:** Claim 1, in full (see Corrections).

**Attack.** The three states (closed, normal-open, purge) are separated only by semicolons within one continuous sentence beginning "the valve core having:". This is a legitimate and common patent-claim style, but at this length and complexity it increases the risk of a clarity objection over which limitations belong to which state, particularly given the dangling clause addressed in F-37-4.

**Least narrowing action.** Enumerate the three states, e.g. "(i) the closed state, ...; (ii) a normal-open state, ...; and (iii) a purge state, ...". No scope change.

**Requires new disclosure:** No. **Suggested target:** P5.

## Assumptions

A1. Claim-dependency formalities (37 CFR 1.75(c); MPEP 608.01(n)) are assessed primarily against US practice, since no single target jurisdiction is specified for this prompt; EPO and UK practice do not share this specific prohibition on a multiple dependent claim depending on another (addressed further in the Prompt 40a reply).

A2. "Chamber side" and "supply side" are treated as defined, per [0010], only with reference to the poppet; claims 9 and 33's broader usage is treated as an unintended drafting inconsistency to be cured by broadening the definition, not as evidence that a second, undefined "chamber side" concept was intended.

A3. No priority document was supplied with this package; antecedent-basis and support findings are assessed against the description and claims as filed in this run only.

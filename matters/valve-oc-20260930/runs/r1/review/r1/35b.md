## Findings

### Moat Scores

Scores are 1 (worst) to 10 (best) from the patentee's side. Novelty and inventive-step resilience are judged against the saved references in `prior-art/INDEX.md` only, and against common general knowledge stated in the assumptions; no reference is cited from memory. Columns: CR commercial relevance, BR breadth, NV novelty resilience, IS inventive-step resilience, DA design-around resistance, DT infringement detectability, EV evidentiary ease, SP support strength.

**Claim 1.** The score is driven by "a purge state, at a pressure differential exceeding the over-travel threshold, in which the poppet is in over-travel beyond the normal stroke and produces a radial deflection of the deflectable ring element, the radial deflection opening the purge gap so that a purge flow path (62), separate from the main passage, extends from the supply side through the purge gap to the chamber side; the deflectable ring element undergoing shape recovery to its at-rest form and closing the purge gap". Nothing saved discloses a seat or ring deflected by the closure member to open a second path and recovering to close it; the closest combinations (index #14 and #16 for a second path above a threshold, #1 and #5 for a recoverable NiTi ring, #3 for closing-stroke wiping) require three references and a reason to put a deforming ring in a tyre core, which gives NV 8 and IS 7. Breadth is 7 because the claim is not limited to material, wall form, purge gap location or spring construction, but is limited to the three-state sequence, the return spring and poppet-produced radial deflection. DA is 5 because the description discloses, without claiming independently, the cheaper two-state variant ([0051]), the depressor-actuated purge ([0066]), the purge actuator ([0123]) and the inverted arrangement ([0117]), and because deleting the return spring is not covered. DT 7 and EV 6: the structure is visible on teardown of a 6 mm core, but the three-state behaviour and the 0.15 mm gap need a bench rig with gauge and displacement probe, and the audible snap of [0158] is corroboration only. SP 8: every element is anchored ([0013], [0042]–[0045]) and the functional terms have corresponding structure ([0009]). This is the claim the business is buying.

**Claim 58.** Same operative language as claim 1 with "a valve stem (10) having a stem bore (12) and, carried by the valve stem, a deflectable ring element (30), a poppet (40) and a return spring (50)". It catches the OEM tubeless valve and the non-removable format of [0138] and FIG. 15B, which is where the tubeless-valve competitors indexed at #2, #8, #9 and #17 sell. CR 8 rather than 9 only because the replacement core (claim 1) is the first product. SP 7 rather than 8: the stem-carried embodiment is described in one paragraph and a schematic figure, and "carried by the valve stem" for the removable format depends on the indirect-carriage convention of [0005]. DA is the same 5 as claim 1 for the same reasons.

**Claim 67.** Driven down by "the seat ring having a seat ring inner diameter of from 2.0 mm to 2.6 mm and a seat ring wall thickness of from 0.15 mm to 0.30 mm" (BR 4, DA 3): the ranges exclude every Schrader-pattern ring ([0137]) and any Presta ring a competitor chooses to make 0.05 mm larger, and "the wall comprising a convoluted wall (32)" excludes the frustoconical, slotted and cellular walls of [0081] that the description offers as equivalents. NV 6 and IS 5: the claim is not limited to a superelastic alloy or to a valve, so a convoluted thin-walled metal ring with a seating edge within those dimensions is attackable from index #1 (NiTi wave-section sealing ring) combined with common general knowledge of bellows seals (A2); the dimensions are what would be argued to distinguish, which is a weak distinction. DT 9 and EV 9 are the best in the set because a seat ring is measured with a micrometer. CR 6 because a separately sold seat ring ([0141]) is a real but secondary product. As drafted the claim protects the exact Presta ring and nothing adjacent to it.

**Claim 79.** "A method of operating an inflation valve of an inflatable chamber which holds liquid sealant ... within one inflation event: coupling an inflation device ... raising the pressure differential above an over-travel threshold ... inflation gas flows as an air purge ... across surfaces on which sealant film accumulates; and removing the inflation device". NV 8 and IS 7 mirror claim 1 because the steps are the claim 1 states in use. DT 4 and EV 4: every step is performed by the cyclist, the competitor is reached only by inducement or contributory infringement, and "surfaces on which sealant film accumulates" requires proof of the condition of the accused valve at the time of use. DA 5 for the same unclaimed variants as claim 1 plus the deflation-only purge of [0067]. CR 7 because the method claim is what a marketing claim of "self-cleaning" infringes and is the natural vehicle for inducement. It protects the architecture only as a backstop behind claims 1 and 58.

**Claim 90.** "providing tube stock of a superelastic alloy; laser cutting a profile ... shape-setting the seat ring by holding the seat ring in a fixture ... heating the seat ring in the fixture and then water quenching". CR 3: the value is in the ring, not the route. BR 3 and DA 2: [0150] lists roll-forming, internal-pressure forming, photochemical etching and stamping as alternatives, each outside the claim, and any quench other than water is outside. NV 4 and IS 3 rest on the assumption (A3) that laser cutting, fixture shape-setting with quench and electropolishing of NiTi tube are the standard route for NiTi components in the medical-device industry; the only feature that distinguishes is that the cut profile has a seat ring's sealing edge and convoluted wall, which is the claim 67 subject matter in process form. DT 2 and EV 2: the process is performed by a supplier, often abroad, and the laser-cut edge that would evidence it is removed by the electropolishing of claim 96 and [0149]. SP 9: the route is described step by step with values. It is technically interesting and earns nothing commercially.

### F-35-1 [medium] [design-around] Seat ring claim 67 scores 3 for design-around resistance because of the numerical ranges and the convoluted-wall limitation

Claim 67: "the wall comprising a convoluted wall (32) having one or more convolutions ... the seat ring having a seat ring inner diameter of from 2.0 mm to 2.6 mm and a seat ring wall thickness of from 0.15 mm to 0.30 mm". Attack: a Schrader-pattern seat ring ([0137], [0091]), a Presta ring made marginally outside either range, or a ring with a frustoconical, slotted or cellular wall ([0081], [0084]) is outside the only component claim, so an aftermarket ring supplier avoids it at no engineering cost. Least narrowing action: this is a broadening. Reformulate claim 67 on the sealing edge, the axial-to-radial conversion wall (the genus wording of claim 10 and [0078]) and shape recovery, adding the superelastic alloy of claim 73 if novelty needs it; keep the ranges and the convoluted wall as dependent claims 68–70, 78 and a new dependent claim. Supported by [0034], [0078], [0084], [0091]. No new disclosure required. Target P5.

### F-35-2 [medium] [design-around] Manufacturing claim 90 scores 2 for design-around resistance and 2 for detectability

Claim 90: "laser cutting a profile of the seat ring from the tube stock ... heating the seat ring in the fixture and then water quenching the seat ring"; [0150]: "In some embodiments the convoluted wall ... is formed by roll-forming ... by internal pressure in a split die ... by photochemical etching of flat sheet followed by rolling and joining"; [0149]: electropolishing removes "the heat-affected edge left by laser cutting". Attack: the disclosed alternatives escape the claim, the claimed process destroys its own evidence, and the process is performed by the ring supplier. Least narrowing action: reformulate claim 90 to "cutting a profile of the seat ring from tube stock or from sheet" ([0150]) and recite the shape-setting as fixing the at-rest geometry without specifying the quench medium only if the inventors confirm other quenches were contemplated (new disclosure otherwise). Counsel should decide whether to keep claim 90 at all; its examination cost is not matched by enforcement value. Target P5.

### F-35-3 [medium] [design-around] Method claim 79 scores 4 for detectability and 4 for evidentiary ease

Claim 79: "coupling an inflation device to the supply side and raising a pressure differential ... raising the pressure differential above an over-travel threshold ... inflation gas flows as an air purge along a purge flow path ... across surfaces on which sealant film accumulates; and removing the inflation device". Attack: the acts are the user's, so direct infringement lies against no competitor; proof of the "air purge across surfaces on which sealant film accumulates" needs the accused valve after use with sealant, a bench rig, and evidence that the user's pump exceeded the threshold ([0179] concedes hand pumps and CO2 devices "may or may not" reach it). Least narrowing action: recite "across a surface of the deflectable ring element" ([0056]) in place of the sealant-film condition, and carry every structural precondition into claims 1 and 58 so that the manufacturer is a direct infringer of the product claims; keep claim 79 as the inducement vehicle. Supported by [0056]. No new disclosure required. Target P5.

### F-35-4 [medium] [design-around] Claims 1 and 58 score 5 for design-around resistance because the description discloses four unclaimed escape architectures

Claim 1 and claim 58: "a return spring (50) biasing the poppet towards a closed state ... a normal-open state, at a pressure differential at or above the crack pressure and not exceeding an over-travel threshold higher than the crack pressure ... a purge state, at a pressure differential exceeding the over-travel threshold, in which the poppet is in over-travel beyond the normal stroke and produces a radial deflection". Attack: the description hands the competitor (i) the two-state variant with a single-rate spring ([0051]), (ii) the depressor-actuated purge "whatever the pressure differential" ([0066]), (iii) the separate purge actuator with the poppet doing only its normal stroke ([0123]), and (iv) the inverted arrangement with the ring on the poppet ([0117]); none is inside an independent claim. Least narrowing action: an additional independent claim to the closed and purge states only, the purge state defined by displacement of a deflecting member beyond a first displacement, the ring carried by the core body or by the poppet, and "a biasing member" for the spring ([0009], [0022], [0051], [0066], [0112], [0117], [0123]). No new disclosure required for these four; the springless variant would be new disclosure and is not scored here. Target P5.

## Assumptions

A1. Scores against novelty and inventive step use only the references in `prior-art/INDEX.md`, whose full texts are saved in `prior-art/refs/`; the index itself records that coverage is thin, so NV and IS scores are provisional.
A2. Thin-walled metal bellows and convoluted rings that convert axial load into radial movement are common general knowledge in the sealing art; premise of the claim 67 NV and IS scores.
A3. Laser cutting from tube on a mandrel, fixture shape-setting with quench, and electropolishing are the standard manufacturing route for NiTi components in the medical-device industry; common general knowledge, premise of the claim 90 NV and IS scores.
A4. The first product is the replacement Presta core of [0140], followed by OEM tubeless valves; this ordering sets the CR scores of claims 1 and 58.

Q2. Will the product be marketed as "self-cleaning" with inflation instructions naming a floor pump? That decides whether claim 79 has inducement value.
Q3. Should claim 90 be retained at all, given its scores? A counsel decision.

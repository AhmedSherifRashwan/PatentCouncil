### F-37-1 [critical] [formal] Pervasive multiple-dependent-claim-on-multiple-dependent-claim defect

Claim language attacked: representative examples — claim 10, "The valve core of any one of claims 1 to 9, wherein..." (claims 8 and 9 are themselves multiply dependent on "any one of claims 1 to 7"); claim 22, "The valve core of any one of claims 18 to 21, wherein..." (claim 18 is multiply dependent on "any one of claims 1 to 17," and claim 21 is multiply dependent on "claim 19 or claim 20"); claim 66, "A wheel or a bicycle incorporating an inflatable chamber according to claim 64 or claim 65" (claim 64 is itself multiply dependent).

Attack: 37 CFR 1.75(c) (US), Rule 43(4) EPC (EPO) and the aligned UK Patents Rules provision each state, in materially identical terms, that "a multiple dependent claim shall not serve as a basis for any other multiple dependent claim." Once claims 8 and 9 introduce multiple dependency ("any one of claims 1 to 7"), every later claim that back-references a range spanning claim 8 or 9 — and every claim built on that chain — inherits the same defect. Tracing the full claim set claim-by-claim, 56 of the 102 claims are affected: 10, 14, 18, 22, 23, 24, 25, 27, 28, 29, 32, 33, 34, 35, 36, 40, 41, 42, 43, 44, 45, 46, 47, 49, 50, 51, 52, 54, 55, 56, 57, 64, 66, 70, 71, 72, 73, 75, 76, 78, 82, 83, 84, 85, 86, 87, 88, 89, 93, 94, 95, 96, 98, 99, 100, 102. An examiner in any of the three jurisdictions would require these claims to be held in abeyance or amended before substantive examination proceeds; an opponent would use the objection to delay and to force amendment under time pressure. This is not a novelty or support problem — it is a defect in the claim set's own internal architecture that exists independently of the prior art.

Least narrowing corrective action: no narrowing is required. The fix is structural, not substantive: each singly-offending multiple dependent claim must be rewritten so its back-reference range does not span an already-multiply-dependent claim. Two mechanical patterns achieve this without losing any claimed subject matter: (a) split the current multiple dependent claim into two or more singly-dependent claims, one per branch, so that no claim depends on more than one immediate parent in a way that itself spans a multiple dependent claim; or (b) restructure the base multiple dependent claims (8, 9, 13, 21, 27's parent, etc.) into a form where later claims depend from a single named claim rather than from an inclusive range. This is a full claims renumbering exercise and belongs with the Claims Architect, not a text-level swap.

Category: formal. Requires new disclosure: no. Suggested target: P5.

### F-37-2 [high] [formal] Antecedent basis gap for "bears" in claim 28

Claim language: claim 28, "wherein the poppet has a second formation, on a side of the deflectable ring element opposite to a side on which the poppet bears in over-travel, which meets the deflectable ring element on a return movement of the poppet and drives the deflectable ring element towards its at-rest form."

Attack: claim 28 depends from "any one of claims 1 to 27." Claim 1 recites only that the poppet "is in over-travel ... and produces a radial deflection of the deflectable ring element"; it does not recite the poppet bearing on the deflectable ring element. "Bears on" first appears in claim 2 ("bears on the deflectable ring element in over-travel"). Where claim 28 is read as depending from claim 1 alone (without claim 2's limitation), "a side on which the poppet bears in over-travel" has no antecedent basis — an opponent would argue the claim is indefinite under that dependency path, or alternatively that claim 28 silently imports claim 2's unclaimed limitation, creating uncertainty about claim scope.

Least narrowing corrective action: amend claim 28 to depend only from claims that recite "bears" (i.e., from claim 2 or its dependents), or replace "a side on which the poppet bears in over-travel" with language tracking claim 1's own vocabulary, e.g. "a side of the deflectable ring element opposite to a side which the poppet contacts to produce the radial deflection in over-travel." The latter preserves claim 28's current dependency range without narrowing scope.

Category: formal. Requires new disclosure: no. Suggested target: P5.

### F-37-3 [medium] [formal] Reference numeral (30) assigned to both the genus term and one species, conflicting with the other species' numeral (132)

Claim language: claim 1, "a deflectable ring element (30)"; claim 8, "the deflectable ring element is a seat ring (30)"; claim 9, "wherein the deflectable ring element is a wiper ring (132) separate from the fixed seat."

Attack: reference signs in claims are conventionally treated as non-limiting illustrations of drawing correspondence, but only where the description makes that status explicit (see F-40a on the missing disclaimer). Without such a statement, an opponent or examiner can argue that claim 1's parenthetical (30) — the seat ring's own numeral — implicitly limits "deflectable ring element" in claim 1 to the seat-ring species, which would then be in tension with claim 9's equally valid narrowing to a wiper ring (132), a structurally different part carried on the chamber side of a separate fixed seat.

Least narrowing corrective action: delete the reference numeral from claim 1's recitation of "a deflectable ring element" (leave it unnumbered, as a true genus term), retaining the numerals only in the narrowing claims (8: seat ring (30); 9: wiper ring (132)) where they correspond to a single named species. Combine with the disclaimer correction proposed in the formal compliance pass.

Category: formal. Requires new disclosure: no. Suggested target: in-place.

### F-37-4 [medium] [support] Claim 64 preamble conflicts with the description's own definition of "inflatable chamber"

Claim language: claim 64, "An inflatable chamber comprising a bicycle inner tube or a tubeless bicycle tyre set-up fitted with an inflation valve according to any one of claims 58 to 63."

Attack: [0025] states, "As used herein, an 'inflatable chamber' means the volume, inflated through the inflation valve and holding liquid sealant, of a bicycle inner tube or of a tubeless bicycle tyre set-up." On that definition the inflatable chamber is the internal volume of the tube, not a container that "comprises" the tube. Claim 64 as drafted treats the inflatable chamber as the physical tube/tyre-set-up itself (an apparatus that "comprises" a tube and "is fitted with" a valve), which is the ordinary and sensible reading of a product claim but is inconsistent with the specification's own express definition of the claim's preamble term. A court construing claim 64 would have to choose between the plain claim reading and the specification's defined meaning — a self-created construction trap.

Least narrowing corrective action: either amend [0025]'s definition to also permit "inflatable chamber" to mean the physical inner tube/tyre-set-up article (broadening the definition to cover both the volume and the article, which the specification already treats interchangeably elsewhere, e.g. [0139]), or amend claim 64 to track the volume-based definition, e.g. "A bicycle inner tube or a tubeless bicycle tyre set-up, defining an inflatable chamber, and fitted with an inflation valve according to..." Adding disclosure to reconcile the definition is preferred over narrowing claim 64.

Category: support. Requires new disclosure: possibly (if [0025] is amended). Suggested target: P4.

### F-37-5 [medium] [formal] Cross-category incorporation of other claims by number within claim body (claims 61, 89, 99)

Claim language: claim 61, "wherein the core body, the deflectable ring element, the poppet and the return spring form a valve core according to any one of claims 1 to 57"; claim 89, "wherein the inflation valve comprises a valve core according to any one of claims 1 to 57 or is an inflation valve according to any one of claims 58 to 63"; claim 99, "wherein the seat ring is a seat ring according to any one of claims 67 to 78."

Attack: these limitations incorporate an entire alternative claim range as a feature of the dependent claim, rather than using the range as the claim's own formal dependency. This compounds indefiniteness because each incorporated range (1–57, 58–63, 67–78) itself contains claims invalid under F-37-1, and because it is unclear whether the incorporating claim's scope is the union or the intersection of the incorporated alternatives. An opponent would argue the claim's metes and bounds cannot be fixed without first resolving F-37-1 in the incorporated ranges.

Least narrowing corrective action: once F-37-1 is corrected, retain the "according to any one of claims..." construction (it is otherwise a recognised and permitted claim-drafting device, particularly under EPO/UK practice) but confirm the incorporated ranges are themselves clean before finalising. No independent narrowing is needed beyond the F-37-1 fix.

Category: formal. Requires new disclosure: no. Suggested target: P5.

### F-37-6 [low] [formal] Relative term "about" in claim 44

Claim language: claim 44, "wherein the crack pressure is about 1.0 bar and the over-travel threshold is about 3.5 bar."

Attack: [0007] defines "about"/"approximately" as accounting for ordinary manufacturing and measurement variation without asserting a specific tolerance. Despite the definition, "about" remains a term of degree that an EPO examiner may object to under Art. 84 EPC as lacking clarity, since the definition itself does not fix a numeric bound.

Least narrowing corrective action: none required pre-emptively given [0007]'s definition; if an Art. 84 objection is raised in prosecution, substitute a stated tolerance (e.g. "1.0 bar ± 0.1 bar") drawn from the manufacturing verification described at [0175].

Category: formal. Requires new disclosure: no. Suggested target: in-place.

### F-37-7 [low] [formal] Dangling final clause in claim 1

Claim language: claim 1, "...the deflectable ring element undergoing shape recovery to its at-rest form and closing the purge gap when the pressure differential falls below the over-travel threshold."

Attack: this clause follows a semicolon-delimited list of three named states (closed, normal-open, purge) but is not itself framed as a fourth named state, nor explicitly tied back to "the purge state" it follows. A literal reading leaves it ambiguous whether recovery is a required fourth condition of "the valve core having," or merely a further characteristic of the purge state already recited.

Least narrowing corrective action: insert a lead-in identifying this as a distinct recited condition, e.g. "; and on the pressure differential falling below the over-travel threshold, the deflectable ring element undergoing shape recovery..." to make the parallel structure with the preceding three states explicit.

Category: formal. Requires new disclosure: no. Suggested target: in-place.

## Assumptions

A1. Reference numerals in the claims are assumed, absent an express disclaimer in the description (flagged separately at Prompt 40a), to carry interpretive weight rather than being purely illustrative, for the purpose of assessing F-37-3.
A2. The multiple-dependent-claim rule is assumed to apply identically in substance across US, EPO and UK practice for the purpose of F-37-1's cross-jurisdictional severity assessment; jurisdiction-specific procedural consequences (e.g. USPTO's requirement for correction before examination vs. EPO's clarity objection route) are not separately distinguished here.
A3. Claim 66's reference to "A wheel or a bicycle" as alternative preamble categories is treated here only as a component of the F-37-1 multiple-dependency count; no separate unity-of-invention finding is made, as that is assumed to fall within Prompt 30's scope.

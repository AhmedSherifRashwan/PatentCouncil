## Assumptions

A1. Common general knowledge premise: a conventional Presta or Schrader valve core comprises a poppet, a seat, a single-rate return spring and a crack pressure; the description admits this at [0047] and [0160].
A2. Common general knowledge premise: thin-walled convoluted (bellows-type) metal rings that shorten axially, deflect radially under axial load and recover elastically are known mechanical elements.
A3. Common general knowledge premise: laser cutting of nickel-titanium tube stock, shape-setting by heating in a constraining fixture followed by quenching, and electropolishing are the standard route for superelastic nickel-titanium tubular components.
A4. Common general knowledge premise: poppet valves with a progressive or two-stage return spring, and poppets that open a secondary passage at a later stage of their stroke, are known.
A5. No prior-art reference was used; the Prior-Art Analyst's index was not relied on for any objection.
A6. The figure plan and figure instructions are treated as part of the package; if they are internal documents that will not be filed, objection 8 reduces to the FIG. 1 contradiction.
A7. Twenty objections were found credible; none is padding, and objections 18–20 are the weakest.

## Open Questions

Q1. Which sense of radial deflection actually opened the purge gap in prototypes P-1 to P-3, and at which surface (objection 1).
Q2. How was the convolution formed in the prototype rings before shape-setting (objection 17).
Q3. By what method and on what specimen was the austenite finish temperature specified below 5 °C (objection 16).
Q4. Does any observation of the purge flow path surfaces, or any purge-only or wipe-only result, exist (objection 4).
Q5. Are the seat-ring and manufacturing-method claims wanted in this application at the cost of objections 2, 3 and 5, or is a divisional intended.
Q6. Is a purge-on-every-opening product or a separate purge actuator product within the applicant's plan, justifying claims on [0051] and [0123] (objection 7).

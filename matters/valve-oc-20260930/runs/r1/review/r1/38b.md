### F-38-1 [high] [support] "Inflatable chamber" defined as a volume but claimed and described as the physical article

**Paragraph/claim cited:** [0025]: "As used herein, an 'inflatable chamber' means the volume, inflated through the inflation valve and holding liquid sealant, of a bicycle inner tube or of a tubeless bicycle tyre set-up." Compare [0031]: "The valve stem 10 is fixed to the inflatable chamber, which is a bicycle inner tube or a tubeless bicycle tyre set-up holding liquid sealant"; [0139]: "...the inflatable chamber so formed, holding liquid sealant, together with the inflation valve, forms a further aspect of this description"; and claim 64: "An inflatable chamber comprising a bicycle inner tube or a tubeless bicycle tyre set-up fitted with an inflation valve according to any one of claims 58 to 63," with claim 65 adding "wherein the inflatable chamber holds liquid sealant."

**Attack.** On the defined meaning, "inflatable chamber" is an immaterial internal volume — it cannot itself "comprise a bicycle inner tube" (claim 64) because the tube is not a component of its own interior volume, and a volume cannot "be fixed to" a valve stem (contradicting [0031], which equates the inflatable chamber directly with the tube). An examiner construing claim 64 literally against [0025]'s definition would find the claim internally nonsensical (a volume comprising a tube), which is a clean 112(b) indefiniteness attack, and a competitor's counsel would argue claim 64 is either indefinite or must be read to silently redefine "inflatable chamber" as the physical article, undermining the drafters' own definitions section as authoritative.

**Least narrowing action.** Revise [0025] to define "inflatable chamber" as the physical bicycle inner tube or tubeless bicycle tyre set-up (the article that is fitted with the valve and holds the liquid sealant), matching the usage actually carried through the rest of the description and the claims, rather than narrowing claim 64 to avoid the term. This adds no new matter — it aligns the definitions section with usage that is already present throughout the filed text.

**Requires new disclosure:** No — this is a correction to existing text, not new subject matter. **Suggested target:** in-place (Specification Drafter).

### F-38-2 [medium] [novelty] Admission that a stainless-steel core body is an obvious response to a known corrosion problem

**Paragraph cited:** [0176]: "The liquid sealant of Example 1 contained ammonia. Brass is subject to stress-corrosion cracking in ammoniacal environments as a matter of general materials knowledge, and for that reason a core body (20) of stainless steel 303 is described in Example 4 and in connection with the alternatives above."

**Attack.** This paragraph volunteers, as an admission of common general knowledge, that (i) brass corrodes in ammonia and (ii) stainless steel is the corresponding known remedy. Claim 54 ("wherein the core body is formed from stainless steel") therefore rests, on the specification's own account, on applying a materials-science commonplace to a known problem — exactly the kind of admission an examiner or opponent will quote to argue claim 54 lacks an inventive step standing alone, separate from the deflectable-ring-element mechanism that carries the invention's inventive weight. It does not touch the independent claims, but it is a self-inflicted concession available against claim 54 specifically (and against claim 130's core body material generally, i.e. wherever stainless steel is relied on as a point of novelty).

**Least narrowing action.** No narrowing needed; claim 54 does not need to be deleted. Recommend the Specification Drafter reframe [0176] to state the corrosion problem without characterising the stainless-steel solution as a matter of general knowledge specifically "for that reason" (e.g., separate the factual observation about brass from the description of stainless steel as a design choice made for this valve core), so the paragraph is not readable as a concession that claim 54 is obvious in isolation. This is a drafting nuance, not a basis to withdraw reliance on claim 54, which should in any event not be relied on as an independently patentable point.

**Requires new disclosure:** No. **Suggested target:** in-place.

### F-38-3 [medium] [support] Admitted sealant-retention drawback in claimed alternative wall geometries

**Paragraph/claim cited:** [0086]: "Slots, cells and splits that pass through the wall can retain liquid sealant, which is a consideration in choosing between the forms." This applies to the slotted wall (332) and cellular wall (432) of claim 15, and to the "openings through a wall of the deflectable ring element" of claim 32.

**Attack.** The invention's stated purpose is removing sealant film from the valve core's surfaces via the air purge and the snap-back wipe ([0064]). [0086] admits that the very species claimed in claim 15 (slotted wall, cellular wall) and claim 32 (openings through the wall) can themselves retain liquid sealant — i.e., these claimed alternatives introduce a new sealant-accumulation site that the one-piece convoluted-wall embodiment (claim 11) does not have. An opponent can use this admission to argue that claims 15 and 32, as drafted, do not deliver the promised benefit as well as the exemplified embodiment and may even be counterproductive relative to a conventional valve core in the region of the slots or cells, weakening any utility- or unexpected-results-based argument for non-obviousness built on the Example 1 data (which was generated only with the convoluted-wall seat ring, not the slotted or cellular species).

**Least narrowing action.** No narrowing needed. Recommend adding a sentence noting that the air purge itself acts on these openings during the purge state (as it does on the convoluted wall's outer surface, per [0169]), so retained sealant in a slot or cell is subject to the same purge flow as the baseline embodiment, tempering the admission with the countervailing mechanism already disclosed elsewhere in the description.

**Requires new disclosure:** No — the countervailing point (the purge flow crosses these openings too) is already implicit in [0106]–[0107]; this only makes the connection explicit. **Suggested target:** in-place.

### F-38-4 [low] [support] Apparent tension between [0078]'s "no heating" statement and the thermally actuated purge of [0142]

**Paragraph cited:** [0078]: "The geometry is loaded mechanically by the purge shoulder (44), at a defined location and at service temperature; no heating or other external actuation is involved in reaching the purge state." [0142]: "the purge gap (60) may be opened by warming the deflectable ring element rather than by over-travel... This thermally actuated purge allows the purge gap (60) to be opened without an inflation device, for a manual purge."

**Attack.** [0078] is written in the section on wall geometry generally and, read as a statement about "the purge state" without qualification, appears to foreclose exactly the alternative [0142] discloses forty paragraphs later. An opponent attacking a future claim amendment directed to the thermally actuated purge could cite [0078] as the applicant's own admission that reaching the purge state never involves heating, arguing the description contradicts itself and that the thermal variant is therefore either not enabled or not part of the same invention.

**Least narrowing action.** Scope [0078]'s statement explicitly to the mechanically loaded wall geometries under discussion in that section (e.g., "...at a defined location and at service temperature, for the mechanically actuated embodiments described in this section; no heating or other external actuation is involved in reaching the purge state by this route"), so it reads as a description of one route to the purge state rather than a categorical statement about all of them.

**Requires new disclosure:** No. **Suggested target:** in-place.

## Corrections

1. **target:** description · **location:** [0025] · **from:** "As used herein, an 'inflatable chamber' means the volume, inflated through the inflation valve and holding liquid sealant, of a bicycle inner tube or of a tubeless bicycle tyre set-up." · **to:** "As used herein, an 'inflatable chamber' means a bicycle inner tube or a tubeless bicycle tyre set-up that is inflated through the inflation valve and holds liquid sealant." · **reason:** Aligns the definitions section with the usage carried throughout [0031], [0139] and claims 64–65, where "inflatable chamber" denotes the physical article fitted with the valve, not merely its internal volume; resolves F-38-1.

2. **target:** description · **location:** [0060] · **from:** "When the inflation device is removed from the supply side, the pressure differential collapses." · **to:** "When the inflation device is removed from the supply side, the pressure differential falls." · **reason:** "Collapses" is the only occurrence of a locked "term to avoid" (`terminology/LOCKED.md`: "buckling, collapse: imply instability or failure") in the description; "falls" is the term used consistently for the same event elsewhere (e.g. [0044], [0051]).

3. **target:** description · **location:** [0010] · **from:** "As used herein, the 'supply side' of the poppet means the side coupled to the inflation device, and the 'chamber side' of the poppet means the side open to the inflatable chamber." · **to:** "As used herein, the 'supply side' of the valve core or the inflation valve means the side coupled to the inflation device, and the 'chamber side' means the side open to the inflatable chamber; correspondingly, the supply side and the chamber side of the poppet, and of any other component of the valve core such as the fixed seat or the purge gap, are the sides of that component nearer to the supply side or the chamber side respectively." · **to (reason):** Supplies an antecedent for "the chamber side of the fixed seat" (claim 9, [0093]) and "the chamber side of the purge gap" (claim 33), which the description already uses without a definition that reaches beyond the poppet; resolves the F-37-6 cross-reference. No new matter — the broader usage is already present in the filed text at [0055] and [0093].

4. **target:** description · **location:** [0176] · **from:** "Brass is subject to stress-corrosion cracking in ammoniacal environments as a matter of general materials knowledge, and for that reason a core body (20) of stainless steel 303 is described in Example 4 and in connection with the alternatives above." · **to:** "Liquid sealants containing ammonia can attack brass over time. A core body (20) of stainless steel 303 is described in Example 4 and in connection with the alternatives above as an option for applications where this is a concern." · **reason:** Removes the express characterisation of the stainless-steel core body as an obvious response to common general knowledge, which is otherwise available against claim 54's inventive step; resolves F-38-2.

5. **target:** description · **location:** [0078] · **from:** "The geometry is loaded mechanically by the purge shoulder (44), at a defined location and at service temperature; no heating or other external actuation is involved in reaching the purge state." · **to:** "The geometry is loaded mechanically by the purge shoulder (44), at a defined location and at service temperature; for the mechanically actuated embodiments described in this section, no heating or other external actuation is involved in reaching the purge state by this route." · **reason:** Avoids an apparent contradiction with the thermally actuated purge of [0142]; resolves F-38-4.

A2. No abstract or a separate background/summary section was included in the embedded description (only the definitions section followed directly by the detailed description), so the "summary language narrower than the claims" check in the task prompt could only be run against the definitions section [0001]–[0029], which disclosed no such narrowing.

A3. The reference-numeral registry (`registry.md`) and the description/claims text were cross-checked; no numeral is used in the description or claims without a corresponding registry entry, and no registered numeral is unused. No numeral-registry corrections are reported because none were found, consistent with the instruction not to pad findings.

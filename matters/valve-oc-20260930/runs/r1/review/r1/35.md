**Claim 1.**
- **Commercial relevance and breadth.** The claim reads directly on the replacement core, which is the product ([0140]). No numbers, no material and no wall form are recited: "a deflectable ring element (30) carried by the core body" and "a radial deflection ... opening the purge gap".
- **Novelty and inventive step.** Novelty rests on the combination of a normal-open state with the ring "in its at-rest form" and a purge state in which over-travel deflects the ring to open "a purge flow path (62), separate from the main passage". The index records no single reference to that combination. Inventive step is exposed to a combination of a staged second flow path (index #14, #16) with a deforming NiTi ring (index #1, #5), hence 6.
- **Design-around resistance (5).** Held down by "the deflectable ring element is in its at-rest form" in the normal-open state and by the pressure-only trigger "at a pressure differential exceeding the over-travel threshold" (33: F-33-1, F-33-2).
- **Detectability and evidentiary ease.** A teardown shows the ring and shoulder. Proving the three states needs staged pressurisation and in-situ imaging.
- **Support strength.** High, from [0011]–[0021], [0042]–[0070] and FIGs. 1–4.

**Claim 58.** The same state limitations as claim 1, but "carried by the valve stem" reaches the stem-integrated OEM valve (FIG. 15B, [0138]) as well as a removable core, hence breadth 8. It carries the same design-around and proof weaknesses as claim 1. "An inflation valve for an inflatable chamber" imports the bicycle-only definition in [0025].

**Claims 64 / 66.** These give reach against tube, tyre, wheel and bicycle sellers: "a bicycle inner tube or a tubeless bicycle tyre set-up fitted with an inflation valve according to any one of claims 58 to 63". Breadth is limited to bicycle chambers. The claims inherit claim 58's weaknesses.

**Claim 67.**
- **Detectability and evidentiary ease.** Excellent: "a seat ring inner diameter of from 2.0 mm to 2.6 mm and a seat ring wall thickness of from 0.15 mm to 0.30 mm" is checked with a micrometer on a purchased part.
- **Design-around resistance (3).** The same limitations are left behind by any Schrader-scale ring, a slightly larger ring, or a non-convoluted wall ("comprising a convoluted wall (32)").
- **Inventive step (5).** Dimensional windows chosen for a Presta bore invite the objection that they are routine scaling of a wave-section NiTi ring (index #1).
- **Verdict.** Claim 67 covers the prototype's seat ring, so it does protect the commercial architecture, but only its Presta superelastic form.

**Claim 79.**
- **Breadth and support.** Supported throughout ([0068], [0070]). It adds nothing of commercial value against a competitor.
- **Evidentiary ease (3).** The steps "coupling an inflation device ... raising a pressure differential ... removing the inflation device" are performed by the cyclist, so enforcement is indirect and depends on intent.
- **Design-around resistance (4).** The claim needs an air purge produced by "raising the pressure differential above an over-travel threshold", which a depressor adaptor avoids.

**Claim 90.**
- **Design-around resistance (2).** "laser cutting a profile of the seat ring from the tube stock" and "heating ... and then water quenching" are both avoidable at no cost ([0150]).
- **Detectability and evidentiary ease (2 each).** Both steps are undetectable after electropolishing ([0149]).
- **Inventive step (4).** Laser cutting, shape-setting and quenching NiTi tube is the general-knowledge stent route (A2). Inventive step therefore rests on the product shape, which claim 67 already protects better.

### F-35-1 [medium] [design-around] Claim 90 scores 2 for design-around resistance and 2 for detectability

**Claim 90.** "laser cutting a profile of the seat ring from the tube stock"; "heating the seat ring in the fixture and then water quenching the seat ring".

**Attack.** Changing the quench medium or the cutting route escapes the claim. The process runs in a foreign factory, and electropolishing removes its trace ([0149]). The claim adds almost no moat.

**Least narrowing action.** Delete "and then water quenching" (kept in claim 94). Recite laser cutting as one alternative, with a dependent claim. Support: [0147], [0148], [0150]. Counsel should weigh the EPO intermediate-generalisation risk.

**Requires new disclosure:** no. Target P5.

### F-35-2 [medium] [design-around] Claim 67 scores 3 for design-around resistance

**Claim 67.** "a seat ring inner diameter of from 2.0 mm to 2.6 mm and a seat ring wall thickness of from 0.15 mm to 0.30 mm"; "the wall comprising a convoluted wall (32)".

**Attack.** Several changes escape the claim: a Schrader-scale ring ([0091], [0137]), a ring just outside either range, a FIG. 12 wall, or a wall with no convolution-free portion at which [0028] measures the diameter. Replacement seat rings, the component-supply channel of [0141], are then free.

**Least narrowing action.** Add a fallback measurement convention to [0028]. Consider an independent seat-ring claim without dimensions, reciting the sealing edge for a poppet, the convoluted wall and the superelastic alloy, with the ranges as dependents, subject to counsel's view on index #1. The current wording gives up all non-Presta-scale rings.

**Requires new disclosure:** yes (the measurement convention). Target P3.

### F-35-3 [low] [design-around] Claim 79 scores 4 for design-around resistance

**Claim 79.** "raising the pressure differential above an over-travel threshold higher than the crack pressure, whereby the poppet moves into over-travel".

**Attack.** An adaptor with a depressor produces over-travel without the claimed pressure step ([0066]). A pump that does not reach the threshold produces no purge at all ([0069]).

**Least narrowing action.** Add a method claim with over-travel produced by mechanical depression during coupling ([0066], [0067]).

**Requires new disclosure:** no. Target P5.

## Assumptions

### F-28-1 [high] [enablement] The illustrated purge gap cannot open by the deflection described

Claims 29, 30, 34, 72; also claim 9 read with [0093]. Claim 29: "the radial deflection is radially outward". Claim 30: "the purge gap is an annular radial clearance between an outer surface of the seat ring and a core body bore (22)".

**Attack.** The description gives the illustrated embodiment both features together. [0020]: "In the illustrated embodiment the radial deflection is outward." [0040] and [0053]: the purge gap is "an annular radial clearance between the outer surface of the seat ring 30 and the core body bore 22". [0054] and the FIG. 3 plan and instruction: "seat ring deflected radially outward … purge gap open". [0093] and [0162] say the same of the wiper ring: "deflects the wiper ring (132) radially outward, opening the purge gap (60) … between the outer surface of the wiper ring (132) and the core body bore (22)". Outward radial movement of an outer surface moves it towards a surrounding bore. It narrows that clearance; it cannot open it (A2). The description itself recognises this in [0103], where this gap "opens where the bounding portion of the outer surface … moves away from the core body bore", for example by inward movement. That contradicts [0020], [0053], [0054] and [0093]. The registry's FIG. 1 instruction makes matters worse. It draws the outer surface "with a small clearance to the core body bore (22) even in the closed state", so the drawing would show an open clearance where claim 1 requires "a purge gap … closed". An examiner will call this an insufficiency of the only built embodiment (Art 83 EPC; 35 USC 112(a)) and a clarity objection to claims 29/30/72. An opponent will add that claims 29 and 34 in combination, and claim 9 as described, recite something the text says works but physics says does not. The bench observation in [0159] ("Cured sealant film was present on the outer surface of the seat ring in the region of the purge gap") does not say which way the surface moved.

**Least-narrowing action.** Before filing, obtain the inventor's statement of the mechanism actually built in P-1 to P-3: which surface of the seat ring bounds the purge gap, and which way it moves. Then rewrite [0020], [0040], [0053], [0054], [0093], [0162] and the FIG. 1, 3, 6, 10B instructions to that mechanism. Keep [0103]–[0106] as alternatives. If the gap is at the outer surface, describe the outward deflection at the sealing-edge end together with the inward movement of the bounding portion, as [0103] does, so both are true together. Remove the closed-state clearance from the FIG. 1 instruction. No claim narrowing is needed. Remove the claim 29 dependency from claim 34 (see F-28-5). Requires new disclosure: yes, an inventor fact.

### F-28-2 [high] [enablement] The axial layout stated does not bring the purge shoulder into contact with the seat ring

Claims 3 and 4: "a purge shoulder (44) of the poppet on the chamber side of the sealing face"; "bears axially on the deflectable ring element in over-travel".

**Attack.** The text fixes three relationships. (i) In the opening direction the poppet "moves along the poppet axis towards the chamber side, lifting the sealing face (42) from the sealing edge (34)" ([0046], [0010]), so the sealing edge lies on the supply side of the sealing face. (ii) The purge shoulder is "on the chamber side of the sealing face" ([0021], [0038]), so on opening it moves further chamber-ward, away from the sealing edge. (iii) The seat ring inner diameter "admits the poppet stem (41)" ([0088]), so the poppet head does not sit inside the ring. On those statements the purge shoulder travels away from the seat ring during over-travel, and nothing in the text puts any part of the seat ring on the chamber side of the purge shoulder. The text never states which end of the seat ring carries the sealing edge or which end sits against the internal shoulder. It never states which surface of the ring the purge shoulder bears on. [0116] implies the illustrated contact is at the sealing-edge end ("the contact … is then at the end of the seat ring remote from the sealing edge"). The drawings do not exist yet. They are figure instructions only, and they do not resolve it. An examiner reading the words alone cannot reconstruct a working arrangement. That supports a sufficiency or clarity objection against claims 3–4 and invites a litigator to argue that "chamber side of the sealing face" was a mistake the patentee must live with.

**Least-narrowing action.** Add one closed-state layout paragraph (P4), confirmed by the inventor. State the axial order from supply side to chamber side of the internal shoulder, the seat ring's two ends, the sealing edge, the sealing face, the purge shoulder and the head of the poppet. State which face of the seat ring the purge shoulder meets, and the route of the main passage. If "on the chamber side of the sealing face" does not describe the prototype, correct [0021], [0038], [0112] and claim 3 together. FIG. 1 and FIG. 6A must then be drawn to that paragraph. Requires new disclosure: yes.

### F-28-3 [high] [support] The description confines the dimension and pressure ranges to the superelastic convoluted seat ring, but the claims apply them to any material, geometry and valve format

Independent claim 67: "a seat ring inner diameter of from 2.0 mm to 2.6 mm and a seat ring wall thickness of from 0.15 mm to 0.30 mm" (no material). The same applies to claims 13, 16, 17, 35, 41–43, 69, 70, 76, 77, 82, and to claims 52, 57 read with 13/16/17/35/41–43.

**Attack.** [0088]: the ranges are "those within which the mechanism is expected to operate with a seat ring (30) of that material and wall geometry". [0091]: "The ranges correspond to the superelastic seat ring (30) with a convoluted wall (32). A person skilled in the art will adapt them to … a resilient polymer seat ring, … resilient metal, … another … geometr[y], … a separate wiper ring (132), and will scale the dimensions to the bore available in a larger valve core". [0146] and [0161] repeat this for PEEK. The description therefore tells the reader that a PEEK seat ring of 2.0–2.6 mm inner diameter and 0.15–0.30 mm wall (claims 76, 77) is not the disclosed range for that material. The same goes for a Schrader core with those dimensions (claim 57 with 16) and for a slotted-wall ring with a 0.08–0.20 mm "convolution amplitude". Claim 67 is the sharpest case: an independent claim whose only numerical limitations are expressly tied, in the description, to a material the claim does not recite. The EPO will raise lack of support over the whole claimed scope (Art 84) and require the material to be introduced. In the US, the written description of the range for other materials is at best implicit. "Seat ring wall thickness" is also defined only as "the radial thickness of the material forming the convoluted wall" ([0028]), yet claim 16 hangs on claim 8, which need not have a convoluted wall.

**Least-narrowing action.** First, ask the inventor whether the ranges also hold for a PEEK seat ring, a resilient-metal ring and the other wall geometries. If so, add that statement and remove the self-limiting "correspond to" sentence in [0091] and the equivalents in [0146]/[0161] (P4). Also redefine "seat ring wall thickness" in [0028] as the radial thickness of the wall of the seat ring, convoluted or not. If the ranges do not hold, the only credible route is narrowing. Claim 67 would take "formed from a superelastic alloy" (claim 73), and claims 76/77 would lose the claim 67 ranges. The scope given up is a polymer or plain-metal seat ring of the claimed dimensions. Requires new disclosure: yes (inventor confirmation).

### F-28-4 [medium] [support] A single-rate spring with the ring's stiffness setting the threshold is not shown to keep the ring "in its at-rest form" throughout the normal-open state

Claim 40: "the return spring has a single rate and the over-travel threshold is set by a stiffness of the deflectable ring element", read with claim 1: normal-open state "not exceeding an over-travel threshold … the deflectable ring element is in its at-rest form and the purge gap is closed".

**Attack.** In [0122] the threshold is "the force that the deflecting formation needs to deflect the deflectable ring element". The superelastic ring's loading "follows a stress plateau after an initial elastic rise". So the formation contacts and loads the ring before the threshold is reached, and the ring undergoes the "initial elastic rise" while the pressure differential is still below the threshold. The ring is then not "at rest" in the sense of [0010] ("not deflected by the purge shoulder"), and claim 1's normal-open state is not met throughout. For a polymer or resilient metal ring, [0122] says the threshold "is less distinct", which leaves claim 40 read with claims 51–53 without a defined boundary between states. An examiner will say the combination of claims 1 and 40 is not disclosed as working. A defendant will argue that claim 40's own embodiment falls outside claim 1.

**Least-narrowing action.** Add disclosure (P4), if the inventor agrees, that below the threshold any elastic loading of the ring leaves the purge gap closed. Then define "at rest" in [0010] by reference to the purge gap being closed and the ring being undeflected beyond that elastic pre-load. Alternatively, restrict claim 40 to claim 47 (superelastic plateau). That narrowing gives up polymer and plain-metal rings with a stiffness-set threshold. Requires new disclosure: yes.

### F-28-5 [medium] [support] Multiple dependencies create combinations the description excludes or makes incoherent

**Attack.** Each of these combinations is either technically contradictory or contrary to a stated dependency in [0144]:

### F-28-6 [medium] [support] The inner-surface purge gap is not shown to be a flow path separate from the main passage

Claim 31: "the purge gap is bounded by an inner surface of the seat ring and the poppet on the chamber side of the sealing edge, and the purge flow path passes along a poppet stem (41)".

**Attack.** Claim 1 requires the purge flow path "separate from the main passage". The description never states the route of the main passage beyond "the passage past the sealing face" ([0019], [0045]). The text therefore does not show two distinct paths in this arrangement. "Sufficiently small to carry no significant flow" is also a relative term with no standard.

**Least-narrowing action.** Add, with the inventor, a description of the main-passage route in the illustrated embodiment and in the third arrangement, showing how the two paths differ in the third arrangement (P4). ### F-28-7 [medium] [support] The package itself labels the interference-band arrangement an unconfirmed drafting convention

### F-28-8 [medium] [design-around] [0009] invites US means-plus-function construction of the claim genus terms

Claims 1, 2, 36: "deflectable ring element", "deflecting formation", "threshold-setting arrangement". [0009]: "Each such term refers to any structure capable of performing the recited function, consistent with the corresponding structures described in this specification and their equivalents; this specification describes at least one corresponding structure for each such term".

**Attack.** "Corresponding structures … and their equivalents" is the vocabulary of 35 USC 112(f). "Arrangement" and "formation" are nonce-like words (A4). A US court reading [0009] would likely treat "threshold-setting arrangement" and "deflecting formation", and perhaps "deflectable ring element", as means-plus-function terms. It would limit them to the disclosed species ([0118]–[0123]; [0112]–[0116]) and their equivalents. The genus these terms were meant to secure would be lost to a competitor using an undisclosed structure.

**Least-narrowing action.** Rewrite [0009] (P4) so that each term is stated to be a structural term with the listed species as non-limiting examples, and delete "corresponding structures … and their equivalents". Keep the structural words ("ring", "shoulder", "formation of the poppet") in the claims. Counsel should decide whether any 112(f) treatment is wanted. Requires new disclosure: no.

## Assumptions

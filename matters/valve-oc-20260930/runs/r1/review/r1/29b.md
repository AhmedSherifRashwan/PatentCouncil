## Findings

The paths below assume claim 1 is rejected as filed and use only features expressly in the description ([0001]–[0180]) and the figure instructions; no drawings exist, so figure anchors are to the instructions. Claims 58 and 79 take the same paths with the same basis; where a path fails for them it is said. Risk ratings are for the EPO test of a directly and unambiguously derivable combination; the US written-description test is stated where it differs.

### Amendment Paths

**Path 1. Deflecting formation spaced through the normal stroke (claim 1 + claim 2).**
Amended wording: "... wherein the poppet has a deflecting formation (44) which is spaced from the deflectable ring element in the closed state and throughout the normal stroke and which bears on the deflectable ring element in over-travel so as to produce the radial deflection."
Combination support: [0021], [0038], [0047], [0112] (genus: "any formation that moves with the poppet and that contacts the deflectable ring element only during over-travel"); FIG. 2 instruction (residual clearance at the end of the normal stroke).
Intermediate-generalisation or added-matter risk: none. The genus is defined at [0009], [0021] and [0112] independently of the annular form, and every species ([0113]–[0116]) is stated to share the spacing.
Specification addition: none required.

**Path 2. Seat ring seals and wipes (claim 1 + claim 8 + claim 26).**
Amended wording: "... wherein the deflectable ring element is a seat ring (30) having a sealing edge (34) on which the sealing face rests in the closed state, and wherein, as the seat ring undergoes shape recovery when the pressure differential falls, the seat ring moves across the sealing face and shears sealant film from the sealing face."
Combination support: [0015], [0034], [0060]–[0062], [0092], [0124]; FIG. 4 instruction.
Risk: none. [0061]–[0062] and [0092] describe exactly this combination for the illustrated embodiment, and [0063] and [0076] keep the polymer ring within it.
Specification addition: none required.

**Path 3. Convoluted wall converting axial load (claim 1 + claim 10 + claim 11).**
Amended wording: "... wherein the deflectable ring element has a convoluted wall (32) having one or more convolutions, which converts an axial load applied to the deflectable ring element by the poppet in over-travel into the radial deflection."
Combination support: [0017], [0034], [0052], [0078]–[0080]; FIG. 6A instruction.
Risk: none for the seat ring. Possible if the claim keeps the genus "deflectable ring element" and is read on the wiper ring: [0093] discloses a convoluted wall for the wiper ring in one sentence ("it has a plain wall, a convoluted wall (32) or another of the ... geometries"), which is enough at the EPO but thin.
Specification addition: none required for validity; see F-29-4 for the wiper ring.

**Path 4. Two-stage return spring (claim 1 + claim 36 + claim 37).**
Amended wording: "... wherein the return spring has a first spring stage (52) of lower rate which resists the normal stroke and sets the crack pressure, and a second spring stage (54) of higher rate which comes into action when the normal stroke is complete and sets the over-travel threshold."
Combination support: [0022], [0039], [0046], [0048], [0119]; FIG. 1, 2, 3 instructions (stage 54 "not yet engaged" in FIG. 2, "fully engaged" in FIG. 3).
Risk: none. [0050] adds that the ring stiffness may contribute to the threshold, which does not undermine the combination.
Specification addition: none required.

**Path 5. Superelastic alloy with austenite finish temperature below 5 °C (claim 1 + claim 47 + claim 48).**
Amended wording: "... wherein the deflectable ring element is formed from a superelastic alloy having an austenite finish temperature below 5 °C."
Combination support: [0026], [0035], [0072]–[0073], [0144]–[0145], [0155].
Risk: none for the seat ring; none for the wiper ring ([0093]: "any of the materials described above"). US: the working example is one alloy at one Af limit; a written-description challenge to the genus "superelastic alloy" is answered by [0074] (binary, ternary and copper-based species).
Specification addition: none required.

**Path 6. Retention with a free deflecting portion (claim 1 + claims 18, 19, 21).**
Amended wording: "... wherein the core body has an internal shoulder (24), the deflectable ring element is retained in a core body bore (22) against the internal shoulder by an interference fit provided over an axial band of the deflectable ring element adjacent the internal shoulder, and a remainder of an outer surface of the deflectable ring element, including the portion of the wall which undergoes the radial deflection, is not in interference with the core body bore."
Combination support: [0033], [0036], [0053], [0132]–[0133], [0151]; FIG. 6A instruction.
Risk: possible. The band-plus-free-remainder feature is introduced as "in some embodiments" ([0036]) and the FIG. 6 instruction calls the same convention "a drafting assumption ..., not a confirmed mechanism". More seriously, the description does not say where the purge flow leaves the outer clearance once the band and the internal shoulder close its chamber end, so an examiner asked to accept this as the fallback structure will ask how the claimed purge flow path "extends ... to the chamber side" through it. An opponent will say the applicant has claimed a structure it did not know worked.
Specification addition: F-29-1.

**Path 7. Purge gap as the outer annular clearance (claim 1 + claim 8 + claim 30).**
Amended wording: "... wherein the deflectable ring element is a seat ring (30) having a sealing edge (34) ..., and the purge gap is an annular radial clearance between an outer surface of the seat ring and a core body bore (22) of the core body, which is closed while the seat ring is in its at-rest form and opens when the radial deflection moves the outer surface away from the core body bore."
Combination support: [0018], [0040], [0045], [0053], [0103]; FIG. 3 and 6B instructions.
Risk: possible, for the same reason as Path 6: the clearance is described but its connection to the chamber side is not, and the FIG. 1 instruction draws a clearance at rest. This is the most natural structural fallback and it is the one the text supports least well.
Specification addition: F-29-1 and F-29-2.

**Path 8. Pressure ranges (claim 1 + claim 41 + claim 42).**
Amended wording: "... wherein the crack pressure is from 0.8 bar to 1.5 bar and the over-travel threshold is from 2.5 bar to 5.0 bar."
Combination support: [0090], [0011], [0051].
Risk: likely. [0088] introduces every range as "those of the superelastic seat ring (30) of the worked example, together with the ranges within which the mechanism is expected to operate with a seat ring (30) of that material and wall geometry", and [0091] says the ranges "correspond to the superelastic seat ring (30) with a convoluted wall (32)" and are to be adapted for other materials and geometries. Lifting the pressure ranges into a claim 1 that has no material and no wall is the textbook intermediate generalisation; the examiner will require Path 3 and Path 5 to come with it. The same applies to claim 82 as filed on claim 79.
Specification addition: F-29-3.

**Path 9. Seat ring dimensions with the convoluted wall (claim 1 + claims 8, 11, 16, 17).**
Amended wording: "... wherein the deflectable ring element is a seat ring (30) having a sealing edge (34) ... and a convoluted wall (32), the seat ring having a seat ring inner diameter of from 2.0 mm to 2.6 mm, a seat ring wall thickness of from 0.15 mm to 0.30 mm and a seat ring free length of from 2.5 mm to 4.0 mm."
Combination support: [0028], [0088]–[0089]; FIG. 6A instruction ("These dimensions are called out in FIG. 6A").
Risk: possible. The wall is in the claim, so the [0028] definitions bite, but the material is not, and [0091] ties these numbers to the superelastic ring. If the amendment also takes claim 47 the risk is none; without it the examiner will ask why the polymer ring of [0076] and [0161], for which the numbers are "adapted", is inside the claim.
Specification addition: F-29-3.

**Path 10. Fixed seat with a separate wiper ring (claim 1 + claim 9), as the alternative fallback when the one-piece seat ring is the problem.**
Amended wording: "... further comprising a fixed seat (130) carried by the core body, the fixed seat not deflecting in over-travel and providing a sealing edge on which the sealing face rests in the closed state, wherein the deflectable ring element is a wiper ring (132) separate from the fixed seat and positioned on the chamber side of the fixed seat, the poppet bearing on the wiper ring in over-travel to produce the radial deflection."
Combination support: [0016], [0055], [0093]–[0094], [0125], [0162]; FIG. 10A and 10B instructions.
Risk: possible. The embodiment is prophetic ([0162]) and is described at the level of parts, not geometry: no wall form is fixed ("a plain wall, a convoluted wall (32) or another"), no dimensions, and retention "follow[s] from the material and geometry selected". Any further step down this path (Path 6 or Path 9 applied to the wiper ring) has only [0094] ("applies to both") and [0132] (genus) as basis; the FIG. 10 instruction lists no internal shoulder.
Specification addition: F-29-4.

Paths not recommended: a claim reciting the speed or force of the shape recovery, or the audible snap, has no basis ([0077]: "the term carries no speed or force qualifier"; [0158]: the snap "is not a measurement"); see F-29-5. A claim to over-travel produced by mechanical depression alone ([0066]) or to the deflation purge alone ([0067]) is a different claim, not an amendment of claim 1, and takes the threshold out of it.

### F-29-1 [high] [support] The structural fallbacks (Paths 6 and 7) rest on a retention and flow geometry the description does not complete

Claim 21: "a remainder of an outer surface of the deflectable ring element, including a portion of the wall which undergoes the radial deflection, is not in interference with the core body bore"; claim 30: "an annular radial clearance between an outer surface of the seat ring and a core body bore (22)"; [0036], [0053], [0059]. The description places the interference band and the internal shoulder at the seated end ([0036], [0079]), makes the outer clearance the purge gap, and says the flow reaches "the chamber side of the seat ring (30)" ([0056]) without saying how it passes the band and the shoulder. The FIG. 6 instruction records the convention as an assumption. When claim 1 is rejected and the applicant reaches for these features, the examiner will read them with the flow problem in view and either refuse the amendment for lack of a derivable working combination or grant a claim an opponent attacks for insufficiency.

Least narrowing action: add, before filing, an express description of the illustrated embodiment's retention band, the free portion, and the route by which the purge flow leaves the outer clearance to the chamber side (through the seated end, past the band, or by a port or flute that is then part of the illustrated embodiment), in [0036], [0053] and [0059], with the FIG. 3 and FIG. 6 instructions matched to it. Requires new disclosure: yes. Target P4.

### F-29-2 [medium] [support] No statement that the purge gap is closed to flow at rest despite a drawn clearance

Claim 1: "a purge gap (60) associated with the deflectable ring element is closed"; FIG. 1 instruction: the free portion of the outer surface "is drawn with a small clearance to the core body bore (22) even in the closed state". If Path 7 is taken, the claim says "closed" and the drawing shows a gap. The description at [0040] and [0102] asserts closure but does not say what closes a clearance that is drawn open.

Least narrowing action: either instruct the draftsperson to draw the free portion in contact with the bore at rest, or add to [0040] the feature that closes the clearance to flow at rest (for example the outer surface lying on the lands of [0110], or the clearance being below the size that carries flow, as [0105] already says for the inner-surface arrangement). Requires new disclosure: yes if the second route is taken. Target P4.

### F-29-3 [high] [support] Every numerical fallback (Paths 8 and 9, and claims 13, 16, 17, 35, 41–44, 82) is tied by the text to one material and one wall

[0088]: ranges are those "with a seat ring (30) of that material and wall geometry"; [0091]: "The ranges correspond to the superelastic seat ring (30) with a convoluted wall (32)"; [0146]: "The dimensions and pressure values given in the examples correspond to the embodiment in which the seat ring (30) is formed from a superelastic alloy". No range is stated to hold for any other ring, and the polymer and wiper-ring embodiments are told to adapt them. Any amendment that adds a range without also adding the superelastic alloy and the convoluted wall will be refused as an intermediate generalisation at the EPO, and the ranges are then useless as a graded fallback. The pressure ranges are the clearest case: on the face of [0022] and [0118] the crack pressure and the over-travel threshold are set by the spring, not by the ring material, yet the text ties them to the material.

Least narrowing action: add to [0088] or [0091] which parameters are independent of the ring material and wall form and why: the crack pressure and over-travel threshold as design targets of the threshold-setting arrangement for every embodiment (noting the ring-stiffness contribution of [0050] and [0122]); the seat ring inner diameter, outer diameter and free length as fixed by the poppet stem, the core body bore and the Presta-pattern valve stem ([0088], fourth relationship); and, for the polymer and resilient-metal rings, the wall thickness, convolution amplitude and purge gap opening the inventors conceive, as ranges. Only the inventors can confirm this; if they cannot, the fallback is narrowing to the superelastic convoluted ring, which should be said now. Requires new disclosure: yes. Target P4.

### F-29-4 [medium] [support] The fixed-seat and wiper-ring fallback (Path 10) has no geometry, dimensions or retention of its own

Claim 9: "a wiper ring (132) separate from the fixed seat and positioned on the chamber side of the fixed seat"; [0093]: "the form, thickness and retention of the wiper ring (132) follow from the material and geometry selected as described above"; [0162]: prophetic. The FIG. 10 instruction lists the fixed seat, wiper ring, purge shoulder, purge gap and bore, and no internal shoulder, no interference fit and no wall form. If the one-piece seat ring is the reason claim 1 is rejected, this embodiment is the fallback, and every step past claim 9 (retention per claims 18–19, wall per claims 10–11, dimensions per claims 16–17) is supported only by [0094] "applies to both" and the genus at [0132]. Whether the wiper ring wipes the sealing face (claim 26) is never said; [0093] and [0125] say "a surface of the poppet".

Least narrowing action: add to [0093] the wall form of the disclosed spring-steel wiper ring (plain or convoluted, and whether slotted), its retention (against the internal shoulder or an equivalent formation, by which of the means of [0132]–[0134]), its wall thickness as a range if conceived, and the surface of the poppet it crosses on recovery; and add the internal shoulder and retention to the FIG. 10 instruction. Requires new disclosure: yes. Target P4.

### F-29-5 [low] [support] No basis exists for any amendment reciting the speed, force or audibility of the shape recovery

[0077]: "the term carries no speed or force qualifier"; [0063]: "in the prototype a snap is audible"; [0158]: the audible snap "is not a measurement of the over-travel distance, of the purge gap opening, or of the extent of the snap-back wipe". A distinction over prior-art wipers that rely on the closing stroke alone would naturally be drawn on the rapid, forceful recovery of the superelastic ring, but the text has disclaimed any qualifier and reports no recovery time or force. Counsel should not plan that path.

Least narrowing action: if the inventors have measured or can bound the recovery time or recovery force of the superelastic seat ring, add it to [0063] or [0098] as a reported value with its conditions; otherwise leave as is and rely on Path 5 (material) for that distinction. Requires new disclosure: yes if added. Target P4.

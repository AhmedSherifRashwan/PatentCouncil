### F-33-1 [high] [design-around] Normal-open state requires the ring "in its at-rest form": escaped by light contact or by a ring-stiffness threshold

**Claims 1, 58, 79 (and 40).** Claim 1: "a normal-open state ... in which the poppet is displaced within a normal stroke, the main passage is open, the deflectable ring element is in its at-rest form and the purge gap is closed". "At rest" means "not deflected by the purge shoulder" ([0010]).

**Attack.** A competitor sets the deflecting formation so that it touches, and slightly loads, the ring during the later part of the normal stroke. The main passage is open and the purge gap is still closed, but the ring is no longer "at rest", so the normal-open state as claimed never occurs and claims 1, 58 and 79 are not met. The package's own threshold species does exactly this. In [0122] the over-travel threshold is set "by the force that the deflecting formation needs to deflect the deflectable ring element", and a superelastic ring follows "an initial elastic rise" before its plateau. Below the threshold the formation is therefore already bearing on, and elastically deflecting, the ring. [0050] likewise has the ring contributing to the threshold "once the purge shoulder bears on the seat ring". Claim 40 (single-rate spring, threshold set by ring stiffness) therefore describes a product that arguably falls outside claim 1, the claim it depends on. Defendants will cite [0118] ("below the over-travel threshold ... the deflectable ring element is not touched") to fix that construction. Proof is also hard: showing the ring undeflected at an intermediate pressure needs micro-CT or sectioning under pressure.

**Least narrowing action.** Delete "the deflectable ring element is in its at-rest form and" from the normal-open state in claims 1, 58 and 79. Keep "the purge gap is closed", which is supported by the functional definition of the purge gap in [0102] ("closed in the closed state and in the normal-open state"). Add a sentence to [0118] and [0122] stating that the formation may contact, and elastically load, the ring below the over-travel threshold without opening the purge gap. Then amend [0118] "is not touched" to "is not deflected so as to open the purge gap". This broadens the claims; nothing is given up.

**Requires new disclosure:** yes (the clarifying sentence in [0118] and [0122]). Target P3.

### F-33-2 [high] [design-around] Over-travel by mechanical depression is disclosed but no independent claim covers it

**Claims 1, 58, 79 (46 dependent).** Claim 1: "a purge state, at a pressure differential exceeding the over-travel threshold". Claim 79: "raising the pressure differential above an over-travel threshold ..., whereby the poppet moves into over-travel". Disclosure: [0066] (a depressor in the pump head or an adaptor pushes the poppet into over-travel "whatever the pressure differential"; "the second spring stage (54) may be omitted"); [0067] (a purge reached during deflation only); [0137] (a Schrader-pattern poppet depressed by the head).

**Attack.** A Schrader-pattern competitor, or one supplying a Presta adaptor with a depressor, gives the core a stiff single-rate spring and produces over-travel only by the depressor. Against claim 79 the purge step is never performed by "raising the pressure differential". Against claims 1 and 58 the defendant relies on [0058] and [0088] ("the over-travel threshold lies within the pressure differential that the inflation device applies") to argue that a pressure-driven purge state is not a state of its product in use. To rebut, the patentee must over-pressurise a seized core beyond its rating. Claim 46 ("additionally movable into over-travel by mechanical depression") confirms by claim differentiation that claim 1 requires the pressure-driven purge capability. Where a Schrader pin is depressed by the pump head as a matter of course, this route costs the competitor almost nothing.

**Least narrowing action.** In claims 1 and 58, recite the purge state as reached "when the pressure differential exceeds the over-travel threshold or when the poppet is mechanically depressed beyond the normal stroke", or add a parallel independent claim. Add a method claim in which over-travel is produced by a depressor during coupling, or by pressing the poppet while the chamber is pressurised. Support: [0066], [0067], [0137]. This narrows nothing.

**Requires new disclosure:** no. Target P5.

### F-33-3 [medium] [design-around] Separate purge actuator and inverted arrangement escape "the poppet ... produces a radial deflection" and "carried by the core body"

**Claims 1, 58, 79.** Claim 1: "a deflectable ring element (30) carried by the core body"; "the poppet is in over-travel beyond the normal stroke and produces a radial deflection". Disclosure: [0123] (a separate annular piston or diaphragm carries the deflecting formation; "the poppet (40) performing only its normal stroke"); [0117] (the ring is a skirt on the poppet and a fixed formation in the core body deflects it).

**Attack.** With the purge actuator the poppet has no over-travel, so the purge state of claims 1, 58 and 79 is absent. The package says this form suits Schrader-pattern cores ([0123]). In the inverted arrangement the ring is carried by the poppet, and "carried by the core body" is met only through an argument about indirect carriage ([0005]) that a court may reject, because the poppet is "guided by", not fixed to, the core body.

**Least narrowing action.** Add independent or alternative wording in which the radial deflection is produced "by a deflecting formation moved by the poppet in over-travel or by a purge actuator responsive to the pressure differential exceeding the over-travel threshold" ([0123]). Recite the ring as "carried by the core body or by the poppet" ([0117]).

**Requires new disclosure:** no. Target P5.

### F-33-4 [medium] [design-around] Only radial, in-place deformation is covered: axial flexing or bodily displacement of the ring escapes

**Claims 1, 58, 67, 79.** "radial deflection opening the purge gap"; [0020]: radial deflection "is not a bodily displacement of the element as a whole".

**Attack.** A competitor uses a flat annular diaphragm ring that flexes axially (out of plane) under the poppet and lifts a lip from an axial face. Alternatively, a rigid ring is pushed axially off a sealing land against its own small spring and uncovers flutes. Either way a purge path opens only in over-travel and closes on release, with no radial deflection. The closest disclosed form, the conical annular disc of [0082], still moves its rim radially. The bodily displaced ring sits close to refs. #13 and #16 (sequential opening by movement of separate members), so any claim to it carries novelty exposure. Counsel should weigh that before disclosure is added.

**Least narrowing action.** If within the inventors' conception, add an embodiment in which the ring's elastic deformation opens the purge gap by axial or out-of-plane flexure, and redefine "deflection" in the claims as "elastic deformation of the deflectable ring element" without the word "radial". Do not claim bodily displacement unless counsel is satisfied on refs. #13 and #16.

**Requires new disclosure:** yes. Target P3.

### F-33-5 [medium] [design-around] Seat ring claim 67 is avoided by leaving the dimensional window, or by a wall convoluted along its whole length

**Claim 67.** "a seat ring inner diameter of from 2.0 mm to 2.6 mm and a seat ring wall thickness of from 0.15 mm to 0.30 mm"; "the wall comprising a convoluted wall (32)"; [0028]: diameters are "measured at a portion of the wall free of convolutions"; [0091]: the ranges "correspond to the superelastic seat ring (30) with a convoluted wall".

**Attack.** Several cheap changes escape claim 67:
- a Schrader-scale ring ([0137], [0091] "scale the dimensions to the bore available");
- an inner diameter of 2.65 mm, or a wall of 0.32 mm;
- a slotted, cellular or frustoconical wall (FIG. 12);
- a wall convoluted from end to end, so that there is no "portion of the wall free of convolutions" at which the inner diameter can be measured, and the defendant argues that the limitation cannot be met.

A replacement seat ring sold as a component outside the window is therefore free of claim 67.

**Least narrowing action.**
1. Add a fallback measurement convention to [0028] (for example, where no portion is free of convolutions, the diameter at the sealing edge (34)).
2. Consider an independent seat-ring claim without dimensions, reciting the sealing edge for a poppet, the convoluted wall and a superelastic alloy (claims 73–74), with claim 67's ranges as dependents. Counsel must weigh this against ref. #1, a NiTi ring with a wave-like section giving radial displacement. The breadth given up by the current claim is every non-Presta-scale ring.

**Requires new disclosure:** yes (the measurement convention). Target P3.

### F-33-6 [medium] [design-around] Supply of seat ring, poppet and return spring as a kit, or of core parts to a stem maker, avoids claim 1

**Claims 1, 67.** Claim 1 requires "a core body (20)". [0141] discloses a kit of "the seat ring (30), the poppet (40) and the return spring (50) ... matched so that, when assembled into the core body (20), the purge shoulder (44) is spaced from the seat ring (30) throughout the normal stroke and bears on the seat ring (30) only in over-travel". [0180] discloses the same.

**Attack.** A supplier sells the matched kit, or ships poppets and rings to a valve-stem maker, and never supplies a core body. Claim 1 is not directly infringed. Indirect infringement (US contributory or induced; UK s.60(2)) needs knowledge and is harder to prove. Claim 67 catches only rings inside its dimensional window (F-33-5).

**Least narrowing action.** Add a kit claim reciting the deflectable ring element, a poppet having a sealing face and a deflecting formation, and a return spring, matched so that, when assembled, the three operating states of claim 1 result. Support: [0141], FIG. 5.

**Requires new disclosure:** no. Target P5.

### F-33-7 [medium] [design-around] Manufacturing claim 90 is hidden in the factory and escaped by changing the quench or the cutting route

**Claim 90.** "laser cutting a profile of the seat ring from the tube stock"; "heating the seat ring in the fixture and then water quenching the seat ring".

**Attack.** The process is performed abroad and leaves no trace on the product: [0149] electropolishing removes the heat-affected edge left by laser cutting. Air or oil cooling after shape-setting escapes "water quenching". Photochemical etching or stamping escapes "laser cutting" ([0150]). Product-by-process protection for imports (US 35 USC 271(g); EPC Art. 64(2); UK s.60(1)(c)) presupposes proof of the process, which a defendant need not disclose outside discovery.

**Least narrowing action.** Delete "and then water quenching" from claim 90 (claim 94 keeps it). Make "laser cutting" one of the alternatives for producing the profile, with laser cutting in a dependent claim. Support: [0147], [0148] ("Shape-setting fixes the at-rest geometry"), [0150]. Counsel should note the EPO risk of an intermediate generalisation from the single worked quench. Removing the quench gives up nothing commercially.

**Requires new disclosure:** no. Target P5.

### F-33-8 [low] [design-around] Method of operating claim 79 is performed by the end user

**Claim 79.** "coupling an inflation device to the supply side and raising a pressure differential ... removing the inflation device".

**Attack.** The direct infringer is the cyclist. Against a competitor, claim 79 is enforceable only by inducement or contributory infringement (knowledge and intent), or under UK s.60(2) (knowledge that the means are suited and intended). It is also confined by [0025] to bicycle chambers holding sealant.

**Least narrowing action.** None required. Keep the product claims free of any in-use requirement, so that they carry enforcement, and correct F-33-1 and F-33-2 in the method as well. Counsel should know that claim 79 is a secondary asset.

**Requires new disclosure:** no. Target P5.

### F-33-9 [low] [design-around] "separate from the main passage" invites a construction fight where the flows merge

**Claims 1, 58, 79.** "a purge flow path (62), separate from the main passage".

**Attack.** In the third arrangement ([0105]) the purge flow runs along the poppet stem (41), the capillary path that also receives main-passage flow. The defendant argues that the purge path and the main passage are not "separate".

**Least narrowing action.** Recite that the purge gap is "a flow path distinct from the main passage, opened only by the radial deflection", which locates the separation at the gap. Support: [0019], [0045], [0102].

**Requires new disclosure:** no. Target P5.

### F-33-10 [low] [design-around] Product claim 25 recites a result in use: "shears sealant film"

**Claims 25–27.** "the deflectable ring element moves across a surface of the poppet and shears sealant film from that surface".

**Attack.** A new, unused core has no sealant film, so proof needs used products and evidence of shearing, which the package concedes is not separately measured ([0158], [0167]).

**Least narrowing action.** Recite the movement alone ("moves across a surface of the poppet"), which [0124] makes the definition of the snap-back wipe ("defined by that movement and not by its speed or force"). Keep "shears sealant film" in the method claim 80.

**Requires new disclosure:** no. Target P5.

### F-33-11 [low] [design-around] "return spring" is the species; the package defines the genus "biasing member"

**Claims 1, 58, 79.** "a return spring (50) biasing the poppet".

**Attack.** [0009] names "biasing member (of which the return spring is the species described in detail)". A defendant using a non-spring bias argues that the plain meaning of "spring" prevails over [0022].

**Least narrowing action.** Use "a biasing member (50)" in the independent claims. Support: [0009], [0022].

**Requires new disclosure:** no. Target P5.

## Assumptions

A1. Enforcement is assumed in the US, EPO states and UK, with manufacture probably outside them. US 35 USC 271(b), (c) and (g), EPC Art. 64(2) and UK Patents Act 1977 s.60(1)(c) and s.60(2) are relied on as general knowledge of the statutes, not as checked authority.

A2. The dimensional statements in the table (a part about 2.7 mm across, deflection of about 0.15 mm) are taken from [0089] and [0090]. The view that micro-CT under pressure is the practical way to prove deflection in situ is a general-knowledge premise.

A3. The commercial product is taken to be the replacement Presta core ([0140], [0180]), with Schrader-pattern and OEM stem-integrated forms as follow-on products ([0137], [0138]).

A4. This review is a drafting and analysis aid. It is not legal advice and does not clear anything for filing; qualified patent counsel signs off.

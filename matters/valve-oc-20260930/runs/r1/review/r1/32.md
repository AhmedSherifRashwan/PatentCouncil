### F-32-1 [high] [enablement] Preferred embodiment: outward deflection is said to open a gap at the outer surface

- **Ground.** Enablement and clarity; internal contradiction.
- **Text attacked.**
  - [0020]: "In the illustrated embodiment the radial deflection is outward."
  - [0040]: "the purge gap 60 is an annular radial clearance between the outer surface of the seat ring 30 and the core body bore 22".
  - [0093] and [0162]: the wiper ring is deflected "radially outward, opening the purge gap (60) … between the outer surface of the wiper ring (132) and the core body bore (22)".
  - FIG. 3 instruction: "seat ring (30) shown deflected radially outward … purge gap (60) shown open".
  - Registry FIG. 1: the seat ring's outer surface is "drawn with a small clearance to the core body bore (22) even in the closed state".
  - Registry flag 3: "a drafting assumption … not a confirmed mechanism".
- **Attack.** The claims recite the purge gap as closed in the closed state and opened by the radial deflection (claim 1; claim 30: "an annular radial clearance between an outer surface of the seat ring and a core body bore"; claim 29: "radially outward"). Outward movement of the outer surface closes a clearance to the surrounding bore; it cannot open one. [0053] and [0103] concede that this gap opens only when the surface moves away from the bore.
  - The drawing instruction shows the claimed-closed gap as open at rest.
  - The inspection of Example 1 found cured film exactly where the gap is supposed to open ([0159]). The examiner will say there is no evidence the gap ever opened.
  - The only built embodiment is therefore described by a mechanism that is not credible as written, and FIG. 3, the principal support for claim 1, carries the error. Claim 1 survives on the other gap locations ([0105], [0106]), but only in words.
- **Least narrowing action.**
  - Obtain the prototype's actual gap location and opening sense from the inventor.
  - Rewrite [0020], [0040], [0053], [0093] and [0162] and the FIG. 1, 3, 6 and 10 instructions consistently, e.g. inward flattening of an outwardly convex convolution ([0103]) or a gap between the inner surface and the poppet ([0105]).
  - No claim amendment is needed; claim 29 stays valid for the [0105] arrangement.
- **New disclosure required.** Yes. **Target:** P4.

### F-32-2 [high] [enablement] The test evidence does not show that the pressure differential drives over-travel

- **Ground.** Enablement and support; credibility of the working example.
- **Text attacked.**
  - Claim 1: "a purge state, at a pressure differential exceeding the over-travel threshold … the deflectable ring element undergoing shape recovery … when the pressure differential falls below the over-travel threshold".
  - Claim 83: "the pressure differential exceeding the over-travel threshold is produced by the floor pump".
  - [0158]: "an audible snap was heard on removal of the inflation device".
  - [0044]: recovery "as occurs when the inflation device is removed".
  - [0068]: the core "remains in the purge state while the pressure differential exceeds the over-travel threshold, and returns … when the inflation device is removed".
- **Attack.** The over-travel threshold is a differential across the poppet (3.5 bar, [0048]), not the chamber pressure. A floor pump delivers air in strokes (A4):
  - between strokes the flow stops and the supply side falls towards chamber pressure, so the differential should drop below the threshold, and the ring recover, on every stroke;
  - on removal of the head the supply side vents, and the differential becomes negative.
  A snap heard *on removal*, and only then, is therefore at least as consistent with the poppet being held in over-travel by contact with the pump head, which is the mechanical-depression route of [0066], as with the claimed pressure-driven mechanism. Further:
  - the description never says how a differential of 3.5 bar is developed across an open main passage, or whether the pump head in Example 1 touched the poppet;
  - [0156] says the over-travel distance and gap opening were "not measured";
  - claim 84 inherits the gap: [0179] says compressed-gas devices "may or may not" reach the threshold, and [0100] says chilled recovery was not tested.
  An examiner or opponent will say the working example does not show the claimed mechanism.
- **Least narrowing action.**
  - Obtain from the inventor the pump head type and whether it bears on the poppet, whether the snap also occurred between strokes, and any measurement of the differential or of poppet travel.
  - Add that disclosure, and correct [0044], [0068] and [0158] to describe what was actually observed.
  - If the head depresses the poppet, see F-32-3.
- **New disclosure required.** Yes. **Target:** P4.

### F-32-3 [high] [design-around] Every independent claim ties the purge to over-travel of the poppet caused by the pressure differential

- **Ground.** Claim construction and scope.
- **Text attacked.**
  - Claims 1 and 58: "a purge state, at a pressure differential exceeding the over-travel threshold, in which the poppet is in over-travel … and produces a radial deflection".
  - Claim 79: "raising the pressure differential above an over-travel threshold … whereby the poppet moves into over-travel".
  - Claim 46 covers mechanical depression only "additionally".
  - Claim 57 places claim 1 in a Schrader core.
- **Attack.** The description discloses three routes that fall outside these words:
  - over-travel by a depressor or by hand "whatever the pressure differential" ([0066]);
  - a separate purge actuator with "the poppet (40) performing only its normal stroke" ([0123]);
  - Schrader cores in which "the Schrader-pattern poppet (630) may be depressed by the head of the inflation device" ([0137]).
  In a Schrader core the pump-head pin normally sets the poppet position, so claim 57 describes a combination a competitor would not build. A competitor who purges by depressor or by actuator is outside the literal claims. If F-32-2 shows the prototype was in fact depressed, the claims miss the tested embodiment too. For the US disclosure-dedication risk, see F-30-2.
- **Least narrowing action.**
  - Reformulate claim 1 so that the purge state is defined by the poppet (or a deflecting formation moving with it) being beyond the normal stroke. The pressure-differential trigger goes into a dependent claim, and mechanical depression becomes a sibling dependent claim.
  - Alternatively, add an independent claim reciting a deflecting formation driven by the poppet or by a purge actuator responsive to the pressure differential.
  - Support: [0066], [0112], [0123], [0137].
- **New disclosure required.** No. **Target:** P5.

### F-32-4 [high] [novelty] Claim 67 (seat ring): a known wave ring plus routine dimensions

- **Ground.** Inventive step, with novelty to be checked against the full text.
- **Text attacked.** Claim 67: "A seat ring (30) for a valve core of an inflation valve … a sealing edge (34) at one axial end … a convoluted wall (32) … converting an axial load … into a radial deflection … undergoing shape recovery … inner diameter of from 2.0 mm to 2.6 mm and a seat ring wall thickness of from 0.15 mm to 0.30 mm."
- **Attack.**
  - Claim 67 recites no poppet, no over-travel, no purge gap and no material. "For a valve core" limits only to suitability, and any end edge of a ring is "a sealing edge … for seating". Every elastic ring recovers its shape.
  - Index #1 (US 9,982,818 B2) is summarised as a NiTi ring with a wave-like section whose peaks and troughs give radial displacement. That is the convoluted wall converting load into radial deflection.
  - What remains is the dimensions. [0088] itself says "the relationships between the parameters govern, and the numbers follow from them. … A person skilled in the art who changes one parameter adjusts the others". This is an admission that the dimensions are routine design choices with no stated effect of their own. Scaling to a 6 mm Presta stem is obvious once the ring is chosen.
  - Claims 68–72 add routine options.
  - Claims 73–75 (superelastic, Af below 5 °C, 55.8 wt% Ni) are the first features with a technical rationale: #1 is described as thermally actuated, not superelastic.
- **Least narrowing action.** Narrowing is the only credible route; counsel decides.
  - Option (a): put into claim 67 the superelastic alloy with Af below 5 °C (claims 73–74) and the continuous sealing edge (claim 71). This gives up polymer and resilient-metal rings as standalone components.
  - Option (b): recast claim 67 as the kit of [0141]: seat ring, poppet with purge shoulder and two-stage spring, matched so that the shoulder is clear throughout the normal stroke. This gives up the bare-ring component claim but keeps every material.
  - Check #1's full text for dimensions before choosing.
- **New disclosure required.** No. **Target:** P5.

### F-32-5 [high] [novelty] Claim 90 (manufacture): standard NiTi processing applied to a known ring form

- **Ground.** Inventive step.
- **Text attacked.** Claim 90: "providing tube stock of a superelastic alloy; laser cutting a profile … forming … a convoluted wall … shape-setting … holding the seat ring in a fixture … heating … and then water quenching". Also:
  - claim 94: "at 500 °C for 10 minutes";
  - claim 96: electropolishing;
  - claim 92: a mandrel during laser cutting.
- **Attack.**
  - Laser cutting superelastic NiTi tube on a mandrel, shape-setting it in a fixture at about 450–550 °C for minutes, water quenching and electropolishing is the standard route for NiTi components such as stents (A2).
  - Claim 90's only departure is the product: a seat ring with a sealing edge and a convoluted wall. F-32-4 shows that product is weak.
  - [0150] admits the route is the prototype route, with no process effect claimed.
  - Claims 95 and 97 add routine quality control and a finishing choice.
  - The claim stands or falls with the product, and on this package the product falls.
- **Least narrowing action.** Narrowing is required; counsel decides.
  - Tie the method to a seat ring with the features that survive F-32-4: superelastic with Af below 5 °C, continuous sealing edge, the ranges of claim 67.
  - Alternatively, make it a method of making the valve core of claim 1, combining claims 90 and 100 with claim 1's purge-state features. That gives up protection for seat-ring manufacture outside such a valve core.
- **New disclosure required.** No. **Target:** P5.

### F-32-6 [medium] [novelty] Claim 1: the technical effect is not credibly shown, which opens a combination attack

- **Ground.** Inventive step.
- **Text attacked.**
  - Claim 1 as a whole. The distinguishing feature: a purge gap separate from the main passage, opened by radial deflection of a ring element through poppet over-travel above a threshold higher than the crack pressure, and closed by shape recovery.
  - [0167]: "The data do not separate the cleaning action of the air purge from that of the snap-back wipe".
  - [0159]: cured film "in the region of the purge gap".
- **Attack.** On the index summaries:
  - #16 (US 8,043,186 B2) shows one valve assembly with two spring-set opening pressures, the second opening a second annular orifice;
  - #14 (EP 1137892 A1) shows a tyre valve core in which a second path opens above a set pressure;
  - #1 and #5 show NiTi elements that deform radially and recover.
  Claim 1 does not require the wipe or any material, so its only claimed effect is the air purge. The data neither isolate it nor show it cleaned the purge region. An examiner will reformulate the problem as "an alternative way of opening a second flow path" and argue that replacing #16's rigid second member with a resiliently deflected ring (#1) is a design choice. The motivation is thin and none of the indexed art shows a ring deflected by the poppet, so this is graded medium, not high.
- **Least narrowing action.**
  - Add data isolating the air purge (e.g. a wipe-blocked build) before filing or within the priority year.
  - Describe the objective effect of the separate purge path explicitly in the description.
  - Keep claim 25 (wipe) and claim 47 (superelastic) as the problem-solution fallbacks.
- **New disclosure required.** Yes. **Target:** P4.

### F-32-7 [medium] [enablement] The axial load path of the purge shoulder is not described and reads against the opening direction

- **Ground.** Enablement and clarity.
- **Text attacked.**
  - Claim 3: "a purge shoulder (44) of the poppet on the chamber side of the sealing face".
  - Claim 4: it "bears axially on the deflectable ring element".
  - [0046]: the poppet "moves … towards the chamber side, lifting the sealing face (42) from the sealing edge (34)".
  - [0116]: a spring retainer "bears on the chamber-side end of the seat ring (30) during over-travel".
- **Attack.** Opening moves the poppet chamber-wards, so the sealing edge lies on the supply side of the sealing face. A shoulder on the chamber side of the sealing face moves away from the sealing edge. It can bear only on a supply-facing surface of the ring that lies further chamber-wards than the shoulder, for example an inward lip or an inward convolution. That surface would need a poppet head smaller than the ring bore that still seals on an edge. The description never states:
  - the axial order of the internal shoulder (24), the sealing edge (34), the convolutions (32a, 32b) and the purge shoulder (44);
  - which face of the ring the shoulder meets;
  - which end of the ring is seated on the internal shoulder.
  [0116]'s retainer, bearing on a chamber-facing end while moving chamber-wards, appears kinematically reversed. With no drawings in the package, the skilled reader has to reconstruct the geometry of the illustrated embodiment. If the inventor's sketches FIG-1.png and FIG-2.png are filed they may resolve this; the text should not depend on them.
- **Least narrowing action.** Add a paragraph stating the axial order of these features and the face contacted, taken from the prototype and the inventor's sketches. Correct [0116] if it is reversed.
- **New disclosure required.** Yes. **Target:** P4.

### F-32-8 [medium] [enablement] The worked-example dimensions do not fit together geometrically

- **Ground.** Enablement and clarity.
- **Text attacked.**
  - [0089]: inner diameter 2.3 mm, wall 0.20 mm, "convolution amplitude … 0.12 mm", outer diameter "nominally 2.7 mm".
  - [0036]: interference fit "approximately 0.03 mm on diameter".
  - [0028]: amplitude "measured from the mean radius of the wall to the peak or trough".
  - Claims 13, 69 and 78.
- **Attack.** The mean wall radius is about 1.25 mm.
  - An outward crest of amplitude 0.12 mm puts the outer surface at about 2.94 mm diameter. That will not enter a bore sized for a 0.03 mm interference on 2.7 mm unless the bore is relieved, and no relief is described.
  - An inward trough puts the inner surface at about 2.06 mm, which must clear the poppet stem, whose diameter is not given.
  The description does not say which way the convolutions run or how the bore accommodates them. That also bears on F-32-1: an outward crest would sit in interference, not in a closed clearance.
- **Least narrowing action.** State the direction of the convolutions, the relief of the bore diameter (if any) over the convoluted length, and the diameter of the poppet stem.
- **New disclosure required.** Yes. **Target:** P4.

### F-32-9 [medium] [enablement] "Convoluted wall being defined by the laser cutting"

- **Ground.** Enablement.
- **Text attacked.**
  - Claim 90: "the convoluted wall being defined by the laser cutting, by forming on a mandrel, by shape-setting, or by a combination thereof".
  - [0147]: "the disclosure of the convoluted wall does not depend on which of these steps imparts the convolution".
  - [0150]: none of the alternative routes "has been practised".
- **Attack.**
  - A convolution is a radial excursion of the wall ([0017], [0028]). Laser cutting a tube cuts through the wall along a path and does not by itself displace the wall radially (A5), so the "laser cutting" alternative is not enabled as recited.
  - Example 1 does not say which step actually imparted the 0.12 mm convolution, so the only practised route is not disclosed.
- **Least narrowing action.** Record which step formed the convolution in Example 1. Delete "by the laser cutting" from claim 90, or explain how a cut profile defines the convolution (e.g. a helical cut that forms a wave when the part is shape-set).
- **New disclosure required.** Yes. **Target:** P4.

### F-32-11 [medium] [design-around] Claims 1 and 58: "closing the purge gap when the pressure differential falls below the over-travel threshold" versus hysteresis

- **Ground.** Claim construction.
- **Text attacked.**
  - Claims 1 and 58: "the deflectable ring element undergoing shape recovery to its at-rest form and closing the purge gap when the pressure differential falls below the over-travel threshold".
  - [0074]: "transformation hysteresis".
  - [0122]: threshold set by the stiffness of a superelastic ring.
- **Attack.** Superelastic NiTi unloads on a lower plateau than it loads (A3), and a two-stage spring with friction behaves similarly. The ring may therefore stay deflected until the differential falls well below the threshold at which it deflected. A defendant will argue that its gap does not close "when" the differential falls below the over-travel threshold, only at some lower value, so claim 1 is not met literally. The embodiment of claim 40 is the most exposed.
- **Least narrowing action.** Replace with "… closing the purge gap as the pressure differential falls", as claim 79 already words it. There is no loss of scope.
- **New disclosure required.** No. **Target:** P5.

### F-32-12 [medium] [design-around] "Carried by the core body", read with [0005] and [0117]

- **Ground.** Claim construction.
- **Text attacked.**
  - Claim 1: "a deflectable ring element (30) carried by the core body".
  - [0005]: "carried by" encompasses "an indirect connection or contact through one or more intervening components".
  - [0117]: the inverted arrangement, with a skirt carried by the poppet.
- **Attack.** The same wording cuts two ways:
  - Read with [0005], a ring on a poppet guided by the core body is "indirectly" carried by the core body. The examiner may then read claim 1 on any poppet-mounted resilient ring in the art.
  - [0117] contrasts "held in the core body" with "carried by the poppet". A defendant using the inverted arrangement will argue that the specification itself distinguishes it, so claim 1 does not cover it.
  Either way, scope is uncertain.
- **Least narrowing action.** Recite "a deflectable ring element carried by one of the core body and the poppet, and a deflecting formation carried by the other". Keep the core-body-held arrangement as a dependent claim (support: [0117]).
- **New disclosure required.** No. **Target:** P5.

A3. Common general knowledge: superelastic NiTi shows stress hysteresis, the unloading plateau lying below the loading plateau.

A4. Common general knowledge: a floor pump delivers air in strokes; between strokes flow stops and supply-side pressure approaches chamber pressure; on removal of the head the supply side vents to atmosphere. Some Presta pump heads have an internal boss or pin that can bear on the poppet tip.

A5. Common general knowledge: laser cutting tube stock cuts through the wall along a path on the tube surface and does not by itself displace the wall radially.

A6. Common general knowledge: Af values measured by DSC and by bend and free recovery can differ by several degrees.

A7. The opening direction of the poppet is taken from [0046]. The package contains figure plans, not drawings, so geometry is judged from the text.

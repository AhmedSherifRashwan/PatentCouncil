## Findings

### Jurisdictional Resilience Review

This review raises issues for counsel. It does not reach legal conclusions. The package has no abstract, no background or summary section and no drawings, only figure plans and instructions. The review works on what exists.

#### US

- **Written description and enablement (35 U.S.C. §112(a)).** The mechanism described for the only built embodiment contradicts itself: outward deflection is said to open a gap at the outer surface ([0020], [0040], [0053], [0093]). See F-30-1. The functional genus of claim 1 is broad and rests on a single tested species; full-scope enablement is less acute in the mechanical arts but still arises (F-30-3). Claims 16, 67–70 and 76–77 claim ranges that the description ties to NiTi (F-30-8).
- **Definiteness (§112(b)).** Claims 20, 44, 49, 75, 98 and 101 use "approximately", "about" and "nominally", while [0007] expressly declines to give a tolerance (F-30-5). Claims 48, 74 and 95 use an austenite finish temperature without saying how it is measured (F-30-6). The "purge gap … associated with" wording in claims 1, 58 and 79 is vague (F-30-11).
- **Functional claiming (§112(f)).** [0009] adopts the statutory wording "corresponding structures … and their equivalents" for "deflecting formation", "threshold-setting arrangement", "deflectable ring element" and "biasing member". It invites a means-plus-function construction of claims 1, 2 and 36 (F-30-4).
- **Disclosure-dedication.** Alternatives disclosed but not claimed may be unavailable under the doctrine of equivalents: the separate purge actuator [0123], depressor-only over-travel [0066], the inverted arrangement [0117], deflation-only purge [0067] and the thermal purge [0142] (F-30-2).
- **Restriction.** Claims 67 and 90 do not share claim 1's over-travel/purge feature, so a restriction into at least three groups is likely (F-30-9).
- **Multiple dependencies and fees.** Multiple dependent claims refer to other multiple dependent claims (claim 10 → claims 8 and 9, among many others), which 35 U.S.C. §112(e) and 37 C.F.R. §1.75(c) prohibit. The multiple-dependent-claim fee and excess-claim fees apply to 102 claims (F-30-10).
- **Formal.** No brief description of the drawings (37 C.F.R. §1.74) and no background; [0160] refers to a background that does not exist (F-30-13). The drawing instructions would put text and a drafting-assumption footnote on the sheets (F-30-14).
- **Eligibility (§101).** No issue identified. The claims are to a mechanical valve, a component, and methods of operating and making a physical article.

#### EPO

- **Art. 83.** Same self-contradiction in the preferred embodiment (F-30-1). The technical effect is not credibly shown across the whole scope of claim 1: one tested species, and the purge region was found still fouled ([0159], [0169]). That also weakens the problem formulation under Art. 56 (F-30-3).
- **Art. 84, clarity and support.** Terms of degree with the tolerance disclaimed in [0007] (F-30-5); an undefined measurement method for an unusual parameter (F-30-6); ranges claimed wider than the description allows ([0088], [0091]) (F-30-8); "associated with" (F-30-11); results to be achieved in claim 25 and method claim 80 (F-30-12).
- **Art. 123(2), for later amendment.** [0006] (every sub-range disclosed) and [0143] (every alternative disclosed with every other) are blanket statements. As we understand current practice they are not accepted as a basis for selecting from several lists or for new endpoints (F-30-7).
- **Unity (R. 44, R. 64/164).** Same split as in the US (F-30-9).
- **R. 43(2), claims fees and R. 43(7).** There are three independent product claims (1, 58, 67), plus claims 64 and 66 in the same category. Claims fees are due above 15 claims, at the higher band from the 51st. The reference sign "(30)" is attached to the genus "deflectable ring element" (F-30-10, F-30-14).
- **R. 42, R. 46 and R. 47.** No technical field, background art, problem/solution statement, brief description of drawings or abstract (F-30-13). The drawing instructions put text on the sheets (F-30-14).
- **Art. 52/53.** No exclusion identified.
- **Construction.** Our understanding is that, under current practice after G 1/24, the description is consulted to interpret the claims (counsel to confirm). The definitions in [0001]–[0029] will therefore be read into the claims, including [0005] on "carried by" and [0020] on "radial deflection".

#### UK

- **Sufficiency (s.14(3), s.72(1)(c)).** Breadth insufficiency in the Biogen/Regeneron sense: a functional genus over every material, geometry, gap location and threshold arrangement, against a contribution shown for one NiTi convoluted ring (F-30-3). The preferred embodiment contradicts itself (F-30-1).
- **Clarity and support (s.14(5)(b)–(c)).** As for the EPO (F-30-5, F-30-6, F-30-8, F-30-11, F-30-12).
- **Added matter (s.76).** Follows the EPO gold standard, so the blanket statements in [0006] and [0143] carry the same risk (F-30-7).
- **Unity (s.14(5)(d)) and excess-claims fees above 25 claims** (F-30-9, F-30-10).
- **Excluded matter (s.1(2)).** None identified.
- **Construction.** Actavis v Lilly equivalence softens, but does not remove, the literal gaps in F-30-2. The US disclosure-dedication bar has no direct UK counterpart.

### F-30-1 [high] [enablement] Preferred embodiment: outward deflection is said to open a gap at the outer surface

- **Text attacked.**
  - [0020]: "In the illustrated embodiment the radial deflection is outward."
  - [0040]: "the purge gap 60 is an annular radial clearance between the outer surface of the seat ring 30 and the core body bore 22 … it is opened only by radial deflection of the seat ring 30".
  - [0093]: "the purge shoulder (44) deflects the wiper ring (132) radially outward, opening the purge gap (60), shown in FIG. 10B between the outer surface of the wiper ring (132) and the core body bore (22)". [0162] repeats this.
  - FIG. 3 instruction: "seat ring (30) shown deflected radially outward … purge gap (60) shown open".
  - Registry, FIG. 1: "the remainder of the seat ring's outer surface … is drawn with a small clearance to the core body bore (22) even in the closed state". Compare claim 1, closed state: "a purge gap (60) … is closed".
  - Registry inconsistency flag 3: the convention "rests on a drafting assumption … not a confirmed mechanism".
- **Attack.** Outward movement of the outer surface of the ring brings it towards the core body bore. It narrows, and cannot open, a clearance bounded by those two surfaces. [0053] and [0103] themselves say this gap opens only when the surface moves *away* from the bore, i.e. inwards. The one built and tested embodiment is therefore described by a mechanism that cannot work as written. The drawing instruction then shows the gap that claim 1 says is closed at rest as an open clearance. [0159] and [0169] report fouling in exactly this region and say it is "not purged whenever the valve core is not in the purge state". An examiner may read that as evidence that the gap never opened.
- **Jurisdictions.** US: operability and enablement of the preferred embodiment, and the credibility of Example 1. EPO Art. 83/84: the description contradicts itself. UK: s.14(3).
- **Claims most exposed.** Claims 29, 30 and 72, each read on the illustrated embodiment. The credibility of claim 1 also suffers, because FIG. 3 is its principal support.
- **Least narrowing action.** No claim needs narrowing, because claim 1 is generic as to gap location. Obtain from the inventor the actual location of the purge gap in the prototype and the sense of the movement that opens it. It could be, for example, inward flattening of an outwardly convex convolution ([0103]) or a gap between the inner surface and the poppet ([0105]). Then rewrite [0020], [0040], [0053], [0093], [0162] and the FIG. 1, 3, 6 and 10 instructions to match.
- **New disclosure required.** Yes.

### F-30-2 [high] [design-around] Disclosed but unclaimed alternatives: US dedication risk and literal escape

- **Text attacked.**
  - Claim 1: "a purge state, at a pressure differential exceeding the over-travel threshold, in which the poppet is in over-travel beyond the normal stroke and produces a radial deflection" and "a deflectable ring element (30) carried by the core body".
  - The same limitations appear in claims 58 and 79.
- **Disclosed and not claimed as independent subject matter.**
  - [0123]: a separate purge actuator, "the poppet (40) performing only its normal stroke".
  - [0066]: over-travel by a depressor or by hand "whatever the pressure differential" (claim 46 adds this only "additionally").
  - [0117]: the inverted arrangement, with the ring on the poppet and the deflecting formation in the body.
  - [0067]: purge reached "during deflation only".
  - [0142]: thermally actuated purge.
- **Attack.** A competitor who copies [0123], or depressor-only over-travel (natural in a Schrader core, [0137]), falls outside the literal wording of claims 1, 58 and 79. For counsel: in the US, subject matter disclosed but not claimed risks dedication to the public, which would close off the doctrine of equivalents. At the EPO and in the UK, equivalents doctrine (Art. 69 Protocol, Actavis) reduces but does not close the gap.
- **Least narrowing action.**
  - Reformulate the purge-state limitation of claim 1 as "the poppet being movable beyond the normal stroke into over-travel, in which a deflecting formation produces a radial deflection …", and move the pressure-differential trigger into a dependent claim.
  - Alternatively, add an independent claim in which "a deflecting formation, moved by the poppet or by a purge actuator responsive to the pressure differential, produces the radial deflection" (support: [0066], [0112], [0123]). Add dependent cover for the inverted arrangement of [0117].
  - Decide consciously whether [0142] is to be claimed or deleted.
- **New disclosure required.** No.

### F-30-3 [medium] [enablement] Breadth of the functional genus against the demonstrated contribution

- **Text attacked.**
  - Claim 1 covers any deflectable ring element of any material ([0071]), any conversion geometry ([0084]), any gap location ([0102]–[0107]), any threshold arrangement ([0118]–[0123]) and any valve format ([0136]).
  - [0091]: "only those of the worked example were built and tested".
  - [0167]: "The data do not separate the cleaning action of the air purge from that of the snap-back wipe".
  - [0159]: cured film "present on the outer surface of the seat ring in the region of the purge gap".
- **Attack.**
  - EPO: the air purge (the only cleaning action claim 1 requires) is not credibly shown even for the tested species. The objective problem may be reformulated as "an alternative valve core", which lowers the Art. 56 bar.
  - UK: a Biogen/Regeneron insufficiency attack on breadth.
  - US: full-scope enablement of a functional genus. The risk is lower in a predictable mechanical art, but is raised by the untested polymer and resilient-metal species and by the alternatives noted in [0150] as "None … has been practised".
- **Least narrowing action.**
  - Before filing, or within the priority year, obtain data that separates the purge from the wipe (for example a wipe-blocked or purge-blocked build) and a PEEK build.
  - Make sure the fallback claims (47, 11, 25) are drafted to stand alone as problem-solution positions.
  - Narrowing claim 1 is not proposed.
- **New disclosure required.** Yes (data).

### F-30-4 [medium] [design-around] [0009] invites a §112(f) construction

- **Text attacked.** [0009]: "Each such term refers to any structure capable of performing the recited function, consistent with the corresponding structures described in this specification and their equivalents; this specification describes at least one corresponding structure for each such term so used". This bears on:
  - claim 2, "deflecting formation";
  - claim 36, "a threshold-setting arrangement by which a force resisting movement of the poppet rises";
  - claim 1, "deflectable ring element".
- **Attack.** The paragraph tracks the statutory wording of §112(f). "Arrangement" and "formation" read as nonce terms, so a US examiner or court is likely to confine the terms to the described structures and their equivalents. That undoes the functional genus this package builds. If the link to structure is judged unclear, there is also an indefiniteness exposure. The EPO and UK have no equivalent limitation.
- **Least narrowing action.**
  - Delete from [0009] the words "consistent with the corresponding structures described in this specification and their equivalents; this specification describes at least one corresponding structure for each such term so used".
  - State instead that each term names a class of physical structures, the listed species being examples.
  - Consider structural wording in claim 36, e.g. "a spring stage, auxiliary spring, detent or …".
- **New disclosure required.** No.

### F-30-5 [medium] [formal] Terms of degree whose tolerance [0007] expressly disclaims

- **Text attacked.**
  - Claims 20 and 101: "approximately 0.03 mm".
  - Claim 44: "about 1.0 bar … about 3.5 bar".
  - Claims 49, 75 and 98: "nominally 55.8 wt%".
  - [0007]: "it does not itself define a specific numerical tolerance unless one is separately stated".
- **Attack.** EPO Art. 84: "about" draws objection where it leaves the value without a clear meaning, and the description here disclaims any tolerance. If the word is simply deleted, the claim narrows to the exact value. US: an issue of reasonable certainty after Nautilus. UK: s.14(5)(b).
- **Least narrowing action.**
  - Claim 44: recast on disclosed sub-ranges, e.g. crack pressure 0.91–1.09 bar and over-travel threshold 3.2–3.8 bar ([0090]). No new disclosure is needed.
  - Claims 20 and 101: [0172] says the working range "has not been determined". Either the inventor supplies a tolerance band (new disclosure), or counsel accepts the exact-value risk.
  - Claims 49, 75 and 98: state the specification standard or range the supplier used (new disclosure).
- **New disclosure required.** Yes, in part.

### F-30-6 [medium] [enablement] Austenite finish temperature claimed without a measurement method

- **Text attacked.**
  - Claims 48 and 74: "an austenite finish temperature below 5 °C".
  - Claim 95: "the austenite finish temperature is verified on sampled seat rings".
  - [0026] and [0072] define Af conceptually. [0148] gives no test method.
- **Attack.** Af depends on the method (DSC, or bend and free recovery; see A3). A parameter whose value depends on an unstated method is open to:
  - EPO Art. 84: unusual parameter, method not given;
  - US §112(b): indefiniteness, an issue for counsel;
  - UK s.14(5)(b).
  A competitor can also argue non-infringement by choosing the method.
- **Least narrowing action.** Add the method and standard actually used by the supplier or the inventor. The inventor or supplier must provide it.
- **New disclosure required.** Yes.

### F-30-7 [medium] [support] Blanket statements will not secure an amendment basis at the EPO or in the UK

- **Text attacked.**
  - [0006]: "disclosure of a range constitutes disclosure of every value and every narrower sub-range falling within it".
  - [0143]: "Each of the alternative locations … is disclosed in combination with each of the others and with each of the three principal embodiments".
- **Attack.** Under Art. 123(2) and s.76, amendments that select from several lists, e.g. PEEK ring + flutes (362) + detent (354) + Schrader format, or that create new endpoints, are generally judged on what is directly and unambiguously disclosed. As we understand practice, a blanket statement does not supply that. The risk is an intermediate-generalisation or added-matter objection exactly when a fallback is needed.
- **Least narrowing action.** Add explicit fallback paragraphs for the combinations most likely to be needed in prosecution, each drawn from content already in the description. Examples:
  - superelastic seat ring + convoluted wall + two-stage return spring + defined gap location;
  - PEEK seat ring + convoluted wall + air purge;
  - stainless wiper ring + fixed seat + outward gap.
  Do not rely on [0143].
- **New disclosure required.** Yes: new text, within existing conception.

### F-30-8 [medium] [support] Ranges the description ties to NiTi with a convoluted wall are claimed without that limitation

- **Text attacked.**
  - [0088]: "ranges within which the mechanism is expected to operate with a seat ring (30) of that material and wall geometry".
  - [0091]: "The ranges correspond to the superelastic seat ring (30) with a convoluted wall (32). A person skilled in the art will adapt them to … a resilient polymer seat ring".
  - [0146].
  - Claims in issue:
    - claims 16–17, through claim 8, which is open as to material and geometry;
    - claims 35 and 41–43;
    - claims 67–70, which have no material limitation;
    - claims 76–77: a PEEK seat ring with claim 67's ranges;
    - claim 82.
- **Attack.** Claims 76–77, read with claim 67, claim exactly what the description says must be adapted. That is an inconsistency under EPO Art. 84 and UK s.14(5)(c), and a US written-description issue for polymer or slotted rings at those dimensions.
- **Least narrowing action.**
  - If the inventor confirms that the ranges hold for the other materials, amend [0088] and [0091] (new disclosure).
  - Otherwise, make the range claims depend on claim 47 or 73 and claim 11. This narrows only those dependent claims and gives up range cover for polymer and resilient-metal rings.
- **New disclosure required.** Yes, if the inventor confirms.

### F-30-9 [medium] [formal] Unity: three groups of independent claims

- **Text attacked.**
  - Claims 1, 58 and 79 share the purge-state feature: over-travel beyond the normal stroke producing radial deflection that opens a separate purge gap.
  - Claim 67 ("A seat ring (30) … convoluted wall … inner diameter of from 2.0 mm to 2.6 mm") and claim 90 ("A method of manufacturing a seat ring … tube stock of a superelastic alloy; laser cutting …") require no poppet, no over-travel and no purge gap.
- **Attack.** Claims 67 and 90 share with each other only a seat ring with a sealing edge and a convoluted wall that converts axial load into radial deflection. The index summary of ref #1 (US 9,982,818 B2: NiTi ring with a wave-like section giving radial displacement) suggests that feature may not be special. Consequences:
  - US: restriction likely.
  - EPO: only the first invention searched; further search fees under R. 64 or R. 164.
  - UK: s.14(5)(d).
- **Least narrowing action.** Decide the filing strategy: keep claims 1, 58 and 79 in the parent and plan divisionals for 67 and 90. Wording 67 as "for the valve core of claim 1" does not create unity at the EPO.
- **New disclosure required.** No.

### F-30-10 [medium] [formal] Claim count, multiple dependencies and independent claims in one category

- **Text attacked.**
  - 102 claims in total.
  - Claim 10: "The valve core of any one of claims 1 to 9", where claims 8 and 9 are themselves multiple dependent. The same pattern runs through claims 18, 25, 28, 32–36, 41–47, 54–57, 61, 89 and 99.
  - Claims 1, 58 and 67 are independent product claims, with 64 and 66 also in the product category.
- **Attack.**
  - US §112(e) and 37 C.F.R. §1.75(c): a multiple dependent claim may not serve as the basis for another. There is a multiple-dependent-claim fee and fees for excess total and independent claims.
  - EPO R. 43(2): an objection to three independent product claims is likely unless they are justified as interrelated products. Claims fees apply from the 16th claim, with the higher band from the 51st.
  - UK: excess-claims fees above 25 claims.
- **Least narrowing action.**
  - US entry: restructure into singly dependent chains.
  - EPO: justify claims 1, 58 and 67 as interrelated products (component and assembly), or fold claim 1 into claim 58 through claims 59–61.
  - Prune to the fallback ladder counsel actually wants.
- **New disclosure required.** No.

### F-30-11 [medium] [formal] "Purge gap … associated with the deflectable ring element" is vague

- **Text attacked.** Claims 1, 58 and 79: "a purge gap (60) associated with the deflectable ring element". Compare:
  - [0018] and [0102]: "a clearance bounded at least in part by a surface of the deflectable ring element";
  - [0106]: "openings through the wall of the deflectable ring element".
- **Attack.** "Associated with" states no physical relationship. EPO Art. 84, UK s.14(5)(b) and US §112(b) all allow an attack. It also leaves the examiner free to read the claim on a gap anywhere in the valve that opens when some ring moves.
- **Least narrowing action.** Replace with "a purge gap (60) bounded at least in part by, or formed through, the deflectable ring element". This is co-extensive with [0102]–[0107] and gives up no disclosed species.
- **New disclosure required.** No.

### F-30-12 [medium] [support] Claims 25 and 80 are defined by a result on sealant

- **Text attacked.**
  - Claim 25: "the deflectable ring element moves across a surface of the poppet and shears sealant film from that surface". Claim 80 is the method counterpart.
  - [0077]: "the term carries no speed or force qualifier".
  - [0063]: a PEEK ring gives a weaker wipe.
  - [0158]: the snap "is not a measurement … of the extent of the snap-back wipe".
- **Attack.**
  - EPO: claim 25 is an apparatus claim defined by a result on a material (sealant film) that is not part of the claim. This is a result-to-be-achieved objection under Art. 84.
  - All three jurisdictions: whether a recovering ring that "rides over" the film ([0063], [0072]) infringes is uncertain. The shearing result itself is not measured.
- **Least narrowing action.**
  - Recast claim 25 kinematically: "the deflectable ring element is arranged so that, during shape recovery, a portion of it moves across and in contact with a surface of the poppet".
  - Keep the shearing effect in the description.
  - Claim 80 may keep "shears" as the purpose of a method step, or be recast the same way.
- **New disclosure required.** No.

### F-30-13 [medium] [formal] Required description sections are missing; [0160] refers to a background that does not exist

- **Text attacked.**
  - The description opens at [0001] "Definitions and conventions". There is no title, technical field, background art, statement of problem or summary, or brief description of drawings, and no abstract is in the package.
  - [0160]: "exhibited both of the failure modes described in the background".
- **Attack.**
  - EPO R. 42(1)(a)–(d) and R. 47.
  - US 37 C.F.R. §1.74 (brief description of drawings) and §1.72(b) (abstract).
  - A dangling cross-reference in the passage reporting the comparative example.
  - Without an acknowledged background, the problem-solution framing is left entirely to the examiner.
- **Least narrowing action.** Add the missing sections, including an acknowledgment of background art from the saved references. Either supply the background that [0160] relies on or reword [0160] to describe the failure modes directly.
- **New disclosure required.** Yes.

### F-30-14 [low] [formal] Figure instructions carry drafting annotations and text for the drawing sheets; reference-sign use

- **Text attacked.**
  - figures.md and the registry cite internal working ids: "01 Q22", "DA-7, DA-23, DA-24", "`14.md`", "Gate 2", "Assumption A2 in Prompt 20".
  - FIG. 6: "mark with a footnote symbol on the sheet referencing the description's treatment of this point".
  - FIG. 9 callout: "Alternative embodiment: seat ring (30) formed from PEEK. Recovery is slower …".
  - Claims 1 and 58: "a deflectable ring element (30)", where 30 is the numeral of the seat ring species.
  - [0093]: "the sealing edge (34)" of the fixed seat (130), where 34 is registered only for the seat ring.
- **Attack.**
  - EPO R. 46(2)(f) and US 37 C.F.R. §1.84 limit text on drawings.
  - A sheet footnote flagging an unconfirmed assumption would be a damaging admission.
  - Reference signs tied to a species in genus language invite a narrow reading (weak at the EPO, R. 43(7); relevant in litigation).
- **Least narrowing action.**
  - Strip every internal annotation from the instructions and limit sheet text to short labels (e.g. "PEEK").
  - Drop "(30)" after "deflectable ring element" in claims 1 and 58, or use "(30, 132)".
  - Give the fixed seat's sealing edge its own numeral (e.g. 134).
- **New disclosure required.** No.

## Assumptions

A1. The content of the prior art is taken from the summaries in `prior-art/INDEX.md`; the full texts in `prior-art/refs/` have not been quoted. Unity observations that rely on ref #1 are provisional.

A2. We understand that, under current EPO practice following G 1/24, the description and drawings are consulted when interpreting the claims. Counsel to confirm.

A3. Common general knowledge: the austenite finish temperature of NiTi measured by differential scanning calorimetry and by bend and free recovery can differ by several degrees, so a stated Af value needs its method.

A4. Common general knowledge: outward radial movement of a ring's outer surface reduces its clearance to a surrounding bore.

A5. The figures are plans and instructions only; no drawing was reviewed. Where the text and the instructions differ, both are reported.

A6. The fee thresholds (EPO claims fees above 15 claims with a higher band from 51; UK excess-claims fees above 25; US excess-claims and multiple-dependent-claim fees) are stated as general practice, for counsel to confirm against the current fee schedules.

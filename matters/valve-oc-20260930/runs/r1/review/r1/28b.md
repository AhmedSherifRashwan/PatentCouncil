### F-28-1 [high] [enablement] The load path and the purge exit of the illustrated embodiment cannot be reconstructed from the text

Claim 1: "the poppet is in over-travel beyond the normal stroke and produces a radial deflection of the deflectable ring element, the radial deflection opening the purge gap so that a purge flow path (62), separate from the main passage, extends from the supply side through the purge gap to the chamber side"; claim 4: "bears axially on the deflectable ring element in over-travel"; claims 21, 30, 31.

The attack, as an examiner or opponent would put it, is that the description fixes four facts that do not close into one geometry, and never states the two facts that would close them. (a) The poppet opens by moving towards the chamber side ([0010], [0046]). (b) The purge shoulder is on the chamber side of the sealing face ([0021], [0038]) and contacts the seat ring only in over-travel. (c) The seat ring is seated against the internal shoulder at the end opposite the sealing edge ([0079]), and [0116] places that seated end on the chamber side ("bears on the chamber-side end of the seat ring ... the end ... remote from the sealing edge"). (d) The purge gap of FIGS. 1 to 4 is the outer annular clearance, with the interference band at the internal shoulder ([0036], [0053]) and the purge flow leaving "to the chamber side of the seat ring" ([0056], [0059]). From (a) to (c) a shoulder moving chamber-ward can bear on the ring only at a feature of the ring's inner surface between the sealing edge and the seated end, and the description never says so; nothing in [0038], [0052] or [0112] names the surface of the ring that receives the axial load. From (c) and (d) the outer clearance is closed at its chamber end by the interference band and the internal shoulder, and no exit to the chamber side is described for the illustrated embodiment; purge ports, flutes and windows are described only as alternatives ([0104], [0109], [0110]). [0105] and claim 31 make it worse: they place a purge gap "on the chamber side of the sealing edge" with the flow "along the poppet stem (41)", although [0088] says the ring bore admits the stem, so the stem is on the supply side of the sealing edge under (c). [0116] itself is incoherent on its own terms: a formation moving chamber-ward cannot bear on the chamber-side end face of a ring that is seated on the internal shoulder at that end. The figure instruction for FIG. 6 states that the interference-fit and purge-gap convention "rests on a drafting assumption ..., not a confirmed mechanism", which an opponent will read as the applicant not knowing how the claimed purge flow path is formed. No drawing exists to resolve any of this.

Least narrowing action: add disclosure, in [0038] and [0052], of the surface of the seat ring on which the purge shoulder bears (for the illustrated ring, the feature of the inner surface, or of an end, that receives the axial load) and of its axial position relative to the sealing edge and the seated end; add, in [0056] or [0059], where the purge flow leaves the outer clearance in the embodiment of FIGS. 1 to 4 (past the seated end, through the interference band, or by a port or flute that is then part of the illustrated embodiment); reconcile [0079], [0105] and [0116] with that layout; and give the FIG. 3 and FIG. 6B instructions the same contact location and exit. Requires new disclosure from the inventor: yes. Target P4. No narrowing of any claim is proposed.

Claims 13, 16, 17, 35, 41, 42, 43, 44, 67, 69, 70, 78, 82, each of which recites a range or value from [0089]–[0090], and which through "any one of claims" dependencies is claimed together with claim 9 (wiper ring), claim 24 (integral flexure), claim 51 (resilient metal), claims 52–53 and 76–77 (polymer), claim 57 (Schrader) and claim 79 (no material or wall recited at all).

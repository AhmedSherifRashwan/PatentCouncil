### F-30-1 [high] [formal] US multiple-dependency structure is improper and the claim set is fee-heavy in all three offices

Claim language: claim 14 "any one of claims 11 to 13"; claim 18 "any one of claims 1 to 17"; claim 61 "a valve core according to any one of claims 1 to 57"; claim 89 "a valve core according to any one of claims 1 to 57 or is an inflation valve according to any one of claims 58 to 63"; claim 99 "a seat ring according to any one of claims 67 to 78". Attack: in the US a multiple dependent claim may not serve as a basis for another multiple dependent claim; most "any one of" claims from claim 14 onward are objectionable as filed, and the set carries five independent claims and 102 claims in total. At the EPO claim fees run from claim 16 and at the higher rate from claim 51; at the UK IPO excess-claims fees run above 25. Least narrowing action: prepare a US-format claim set with single dependencies (no scope is lost; only the dependency wording changes), and a fee-budgeted filing set for EPO/UK with the remaining dependents kept in the description as fallback text. No new disclosure required.

### F-30-2 [high] [support] [0009] invites means-plus-function construction of the functional genus terms in claim 1 and claim 36

Paragraph cited: [0009] "Each such term refers to any structure capable of performing the recited function, consistent with the corresponding structures described in this specification and their equivalents". Claim language: claim 1 "a deflectable ring element (30) carried by the core body"; claim 36 "a threshold-setting arrangement by which a force resisting movement of the poppet rises"; claim 2 "a deflecting formation (44)"; claim 62 "a locating formation". Attack (US): "element", "arrangement" and "formation" are nonce words coupled to function; [0009] supplies the "corresponding structures ... and their equivalents" formula, so a court can hold §112(f) invoked and confine claim 1 to the seat ring/wiper ring species and equivalents, and claim 36 to the second spring stage, auxiliary spring, detent and ring stiffness. If §112(f) is not invoked, the same sentence ("any structure capable of performing the recited function") supports an Ariad-type written-description challenge to a functional genus with one built species. Least narrowing action: redraft [0009] to state that the terms are structural terms defined at [0014], [0021] and [0022] and are not intended to invoke means-plus-function treatment, delete the "corresponding structures ... and their equivalents" formula, and keep the structural definitions (annular element, carried by the valve core, deflected radially by the poppet, recovers). Counsel to decide whether a deliberate §112(f) fallback claim is wanted. No new disclosure required.

### F-30-3 [high] [formal] Rule 43(2) and unity: claims 67 and 90 do not share the purge-gap feature with claim 1

Claim language: claim 67 "A seat ring (30) for a valve core of an inflation valve, the seat ring being an annular element having a sealing edge (34) ... a convoluted wall (32) ... a seat ring inner diameter of from 2.0 mm to 2.6 mm and a seat ring wall thickness of from 0.15 mm to 0.30 mm"; claim 90 "A method of manufacturing a seat ring ... laser cutting ... shape-setting". Attack: neither claim recites a purge gap, over-travel, a threshold or a poppet; the EPO will ask under Rule 43(2) why three product independents are needed and, a posteriori, whether claims 67–78 and 90–102 share any special technical feature with claims 1–66 and 79–89 once a convoluted thin-walled ring, or the standard laser-cut/shape-set route, is found in the art. The UK IPO applies the same test under s.14(5)(d). Least narrowing action: narrowing is the only route within one application. Either (a) amend claim 67 to add "the seat ring being formed from a superelastic alloy" (claim 73) and a link to the sealing-and-purging function, and amend claim 90 to recite the seat ring of amended claim 67, or (b) file claims 67–78 and 90–102 as a divisional. The scope given up under (a) is the resilient-polymer seat ring per se (claims 76, 77) and the generic manufacturing route. No new disclosure required.

### F-30-4 [high] [enablement] The illustrated embodiment's purge gap is described as opening by outward deflection against the core body bore, which the description itself elsewhere says cannot open such a gap

Paragraphs cited: [0020] "In the illustrated embodiment the radial deflection is outward"; [0040] "the purge gap 60 is an annular radial clearance between the outer surface of the seat ring 30 and the core body bore 22"; FIG. 3 instructions "seat ring (30) shown deflected radially outward ... purge gap (60) shown open"; [0103] "Such a purge gap (60) opens where the bounding portion of the outer surface of the seat ring (30) moves away from the core body bore (22). That movement may be an inward movement ... or it may be the non-uniform deflection"; registry flag 3 "rests on a drafting assumption (Prompt 20, Assumption A2), not a confirmed mechanism"; FIG. 1 instructions both "purge gap (60), shown as a closed (zero-width) annular line" and "drawn with a small clearance to the core body bore (22) even in the closed state". Attack: an outward-moving surface cannot move away from a rigid bore that surrounds it; the only built embodiment is therefore described in a way that the skilled person cannot reconcile, and the figure instructions are self-contradictory on whether the gap is closed at rest. An opponent frames this as insufficiency of the worked example (Art 83, §112(a), s.14(3)) and as a clarity attack on claim 29 read with [0020]. Claims 29 and 30 cannot be combined as drafted (30 depends on 8 only), but the description asserts both for the illustrated embodiment. Least narrowing action: added disclosure, not narrowing. State for the illustrated embodiment which portion of the seat ring surface bounds the purge gap and in which sense it moves (inward flattening of a convolution, ovalising, or outward flaring of the sealing-edge end away from a bore that is relieved there), align [0020], [0053], FIG. 3 and FIG. 6B with it, and correct the FIG. 1 instruction to one statement of the at-rest condition. Requires new disclosure from the inventor as to what the prototype actually did.

### F-30-5 [high] [design-around] Four disclosed alternatives fall outside claim 1 and, in the US, are dedicated to the public

Paragraphs cited: [0051] "In some embodiments the over-travel threshold is set close to the crack pressure, so that the purge state is reached on substantially every opening ... the second spring stage (54) may be omitted"; [0123] "the deflecting formation is carried not by the poppet (40) but by a separate purge actuator ... the poppet (40) performing only its normal stroke"; [0117] "the deflectable ring element is a thin skirt carried by the poppet (40), a fixed formation in the core body (20) deflects the skirt"; [0067] "in other embodiments it is reached during deflation only". Claim language excluding them: claim 1 "a normal-open state, at a pressure differential at or above the crack pressure and not exceeding an over-travel threshold higher than the crack pressure ... the purge gap is closed"; "the poppet is in over-travel beyond the normal stroke and produces a radial deflection"; "a deflectable ring element (30) carried by the core body". Attack: a competitor builds a single-rate spring with the shoulder engaging the ring from the start of the stroke (no normal-open state), or an annular piston purge actuator in a Schrader core, and cites the applicant's own description to show the variant was disclosed and not claimed. Least narrowing action: add an independent claim (or an alternative form of claim 1) in which the purge gap is opened by displacement of the poppet beyond a first displacement, without requiring a normal-open state in which the gap is closed, supported by [0051]; add a dependent or independent claim to the separate purge actuator on [0123]; counsel to decide whether [0117] and [0067] merit claims or deletion. Unity and fee consequences apply. No new disclosure required.

### F-30-6 [medium] [support] Numerical dependents are claimed in combinations the description says were not derived for

Claim language: claim 13 "convolution amplitude of from 0.08 mm to 0.20 mm" depending through claims 10–12 on claims 1 to 9 (including claim 9, wiper ring); claim 35 "purge gap opening of from 0.10 mm to 0.25 mm" on claims 1 to 34; claims 41–43 on claims 1 to 40; claim 16 on claim 8 read with claims 51, 52. Attack: a PEEK seat ring or a slotted stainless wiper ring with a 0.12 mm amplitude and a 0.15 mm gap opening is claimed but the description says only that the skilled person will adapt the numbers; support (Art 84, s.14(5)(c)), enablement across the combination (§112(a)) and, on later amendment, added matter (Art 123(2), s.76) are all exposed. Least narrowing action: added disclosure. State in [0091] that the same ranges apply to the resilient-metal, polymer and wiper-ring species as starting values, and how the amplitude and wall thickness are adjusted for a lower recoverable strain; if the inventor cannot say so, make claims 13, 16, 17, 35, 43 depend only on claim 11 or claim 47 (narrowing; the scope given up is the numerical dependents for the non-superelastic species). Requires new disclosure.

### F-30-7 [medium] [support] Claim 46 and claim 31 each contradict a limitation of claim 1

Claim language: claim 1 "the deflectable ring element undergoing shape recovery to its at-rest form and closing the purge gap when the pressure differential falls below the over-travel threshold"; claim 46 "additionally movable into over-travel by mechanical depression of the poppet by a depressor in a head of an inflation device"; [0066] "the purge state lasts for as long as the inflation device is coupled ... whatever the pressure differential". Claim 1 "a purge flow path (62), separate from the main passage, extends from the supply side through the purge gap to the chamber side"; claim 31 "the purge gap is bounded by an inner surface of the seat ring and the poppet on the chamber side of the sealing edge, and the purge flow path passes along a poppet stem (41)". Attack: with a depressor holding the poppet in over-travel the gap does not close when the pressure falls, so claim 46 lacks the closing feature of claim 1 (Art 84, §112(d)); in claim 31 the supply side reaches the purge gap only through the lifted sealing face, so the path is not "separate from the main passage ... from the supply side". Least narrowing action: reformulate claim 1's closing clause as "when the load applied by the poppet to the deflectable ring element is removed" (supported by [0060], [0098]) and reformulate claim 31 to state that the purge gap communicates with the supply side through the open main passage and is distinct from it (supported by [0105]). No new disclosure required.

### F-30-8 [medium] [enablement] Claims 47, 48, 74 and 95 depend on an austenite finish temperature with no measurement method and on an undefined service temperature

Claim language: claim 48 "the superelastic alloy has an austenite finish temperature below 5 °C"; claim 95 "the austenite finish temperature is verified on sampled seat rings". Paragraph cited: [0026] "an alloy that recovers large elastic strains on unloading, at the service temperature of the inflation valve". Attack: the austenite finish temperature of a shape-set NiTi part differs by several degrees between calorimetric and bend-and-free-recovery methods, and the description gives neither a method nor a specimen state (tube stock, cut ring, after electropolishing). "Service temperature" is undefined, so whether an alloy with an austenite finish temperature of, say, 20 °C is a "superelastic alloy" within claim 47 cannot be determined (§112(b), Art 84). Least narrowing action: added disclosure stating the test method and specimen state used for the "below 5 °C" specification, and replacing "service temperature" in [0026] with a stated range of ambient temperatures. Requires new disclosure.

### F-30-10 [medium] [novelty] The evidenced technical effect belongs to the wipe, not to the purge gap that distinguishes claim 1

Paragraphs cited: [0159] "Cured sealant film was present on the outer surface of the seat ring in the region of the purge gap (60)"; [0167] "The data do not separate the cleaning action of the air purge from that of the snap-back wipe, and no attribution between them is made herein". Attack (EPO problem-solution, UK plausibility, US obviousness): the only data show clean surfaces where the recovering ring wipes and fouled surfaces where the purge flows; the examiner treats the purge gap as an unproven bypass, reformulates the problem as providing an alternative flow path, and asks why a secondary passage opened later in the stroke is not an obvious design choice. Least narrowing action: added disclosure. State the surfaces along the purge flow path that were clean at inspection, if any, or add purge-only and wipe-only comparative data if it exists; otherwise explain in [0169] why film in a region that is closed for 24 hours per cycle and purged for seconds is consistent with the purge acting on the flow path. Do not narrow claim 1 to the wipe; keep claims 25 and 26 as the fallback. Requires new disclosure.

### F-30-11 [medium] [enablement] No relationship is given between over-travel distance, convolution amplitude and recoverable strain, and the two key values were never measured

Least narrowing action: added disclosure of the design relationship used (the axial shortening absorbed by each convolution at the over-travel distance and the resulting wall strain), or a statement that the over-travel stop of [0049] limits the axial load on the ring in the worked example. Requires new disclosure.

## Assumptions

A1. The package is the whole filing text: no abstract, no drawings (figure plan and instructions only), no priority document and no title were supplied, and the review proceeds on that basis.
A2. The figure plan and figure instructions are treated as part of the package as filed; if they are internal documents that will not be filed, F-30-9 reduces to the missing sections and the FIG. 1 contradiction.
A3. Common general knowledge premise: a conventional Presta or Schrader valve core comprises a poppet, a seat, a single-rate return spring and a crack pressure, as the description itself admits at [0047] and [0160].
A4. Common general knowledge premise: thin-walled convoluted (bellows-type) metal rings that shorten axially and deflect radially under axial load, and recover elastically, are known mechanical elements.
A5. Common general knowledge premise: laser cutting of nickel-titanium tube stock, shape-setting by heating in a constraining fixture followed by quenching, and electropolishing are the standard processing route for superelastic nickel-titanium tubular components.
A6. Fee thresholds are stated at the level of principle; counsel confirms current amounts.
A7. The default jurisdictions are US, EPO and UK; no other jurisdiction was assessed.

## Open Questions

Q1. Which sense of radial deflection actually opened the purge gap in prototypes P-1 to P-3, and at which surface: only the inventor can answer, and F-30-4 depends on it.
Q2. By what method and on what specimen was the austenite finish temperature specified below 5 °C (F-30-8).
Q3. Does any purge-only or wipe-only observation exist, or any inspection of the purge flow path surfaces other than the outer surface of the seat ring, to answer F-30-10.
Q4. Are claims 67–78 and 90–102 wanted in this application, accepting Rule 43(2) and unity risk, or is a divisional intended (F-30-3).
Q5. Is a purge-on-every-opening product, or a separate purge actuator product, within the applicant's commercial plan, such that claims on [0051] and [0123] are worth their unity and fee cost (F-30-5).
Q6. No priority document was supplied; if an earlier filing exists, the priority and new-matter control of Prompt 31 has not been run against it.

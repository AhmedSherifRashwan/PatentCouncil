## Deliverable

### Enablement Audit

Audited text: `runs/r1/description.md` (stitched, paragraphs [0001] to [0350]). Checked against `CONFIRMED.md`, `P1/01`, `P1/03`, `P2/09`, `P4/16` and `intake/data/bench-test.md`. The intended breadth is taken from the claim-support statements at [0298] to [0348] and from the support map of `P4/16`. This is a drafting and analysis aid, not legal advice or a filing decision; qualified patent counsel signs off before anything is filed.

**Result.** One configuration is built and tested: the valve core of Example 1, with a seat ring of superelastic NiTi alloy. Even for that configuration the text does not yet tell the skilled person how the purge gap opens, where the air purge enters and leaves, or on which surface of the seat ring the purge shoulder bears (EN-1 to EN-5). Those five rows gate the independent statements 1.1, 3.1 and 4.1 and come first. Everything broader than Example 1 rests on forms that are described in one or two sentences each and have not been built.

**Jurisdictions.** In the US (35 U.S.C. 112(a)), at the EPO (Art. 83 EPC) and in the UK (Patents Act 1977, s.14(3)) the text must enable the whole breadth claimed, so the rows on functional breadth apply in all three. At the EPO and in the UK an effect that is recited in a claim, for example that the air purge carries sealant film into the tyre chamber, is part of what must be sufficiently disclosed; rows EN-16 and EN-17 weigh most there.

**Severity.** Critical: the built embodiment cannot be practised from the text without the missing information. High: a claim family or a principal alternative is not enabled across its breadth. Medium: an optional embodiment or a dependent set. Low: routine for the skilled person, recorded for completeness.

## Rationale

The audit is of the stitched text, so every row cites paragraphs the fix pass can open. I added a Paragraphs column and a Gap column to the five columns the prompt lists: the task requires one line per gap with its paragraph, and a cure is unreadable without the gap it cures.

EN-1 to EN-5 are ranked above the breadth rows because they affect the built embodiment. `P1/01` Q2, Q4 and Q15 and `P2/09` Q2 raised them before drafting; the description answered them with embodiments from `CONFIRMED.md` that it expressly does not attribute to the prototype, which is honest and leaves the gap open.

EN-2 and EN-5 rest on geometry, not on data. As a rough check only, not a measurement: a wall of 3.0 mm free length carrying one wave of 0.12 mm amplitude has about 0.05 mm of excess arc length. If the seat ring shortened by the whole over-travel distance of 0.5 mm, the wave would have to grow to roughly 0.37 mm and not flatten, and the change of curvature in a wall 0.20 mm thick would imply a bending strain of well over 10%, above the recoverable strain usually cited for superelastic NiTi alloy. Either the seat ring does not take the whole over-travel distance, or the load is not compressive, or the wall is cut. The inventors must say which. I kept the numbers out of the Deliverable because they are my approximation.

EN-3: the fit band arrangement closes one end of the annular purge gap, and a purge gap that ran from the pump side to the tyre chamber round the sealing edge would need to be gas-tight at rest, which [0067] disclaims. I recorded it as a missing statement and not as a defect of the invention, since the sketches may resolve it.

EN-16: the timing point comes from the text's own sequence in [0137].

EN-18: the dates and the cycle count are both in the record; the description reports the dates with "is recorded as", which flags the point without resolving it.

Declined: I did not propose a depth for the core body bore relief, a rate for the return spring or a value of the martensite start temperature. Each would be an invented fact.

Self-curable items are limited to wording that brings the text into line with what the record supports. Whether a specification should carry statements of what is untested is for counsel; the alternative in each case is deletion.

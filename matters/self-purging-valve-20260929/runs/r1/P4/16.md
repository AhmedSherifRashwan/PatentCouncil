**Source classes.** Every node carries one or more tags, and the part drafters keep the wording discipline of the tag.

- **[I]** stated by the inventor in the intake disclosure (cited §n through 01). Mandatory language only for what 03 classifies essential.
- **[D]** measured or observed in the bench data. Reported as measured, with the record's columns and limits.
- **[C]** carried by `CONFIRMED.md` but not stated in the disclosure and not built. Drafted as an embodiment with "in some embodiments", with no result, test or preference asserted, in a paragraph of its own so that it can be removed without touching its neighbours. ## Rationale

**Treatment of the automatic Gate 2.** `CONFIRMED.md` was confirmed by the Orchestrator in unattended mode with no inventor answers, and most of its rows still read "needs inventor confirmation". The risk report (its A3, PF-12) treats those rows as still conditional. I did not want either extreme: drafting everything as though conceived, or refusing everything not in the disclosure, which would leave the principal embodiment unable to explain its own purge gap. The four source classes resolve it. Class C is drafted in its own paragraphs in non-testing wording so it can be cut cleanly; class H follows the risk report's "only if the inventor confirms" instructions item by item (PF-6, PF-7, PF-8, PF-11, PF-15, PF-16) and the record's own "not worth disclosing" column.

**Where the risk report and the record pull apart.** The relieved core body bore, axial clamping and through-wall flow each carry "requires inventor confirmation" in the record, but PF-3 directs that they be described, as embodiments and not as the tested construction, because without them the lead distinction has no enabled form. I followed PF-3 and classed them C. Example 1 describes the prototype only in the inventor's words, so no fit arrangement is attributed to the tested cores.

**Four parts.** The stitch builds the whole description from the four parts, so the front matter had to sit in one of them. Part 1 takes front matter and definitions, which depend on the claim families and the lock rather than on drafting detail, and can be written from this blueprint alone. Parts 2 and 3 split at the line between the inventor's structure and operation (with threshold and load path) and the forms of the seat and purge gap. Part 4 takes everything quantitative, procedural and evidential. Tyre valve patterns moved forward into Part 2 to balance sizes. Cross-references by section title keep the parts independent of paragraph numbering.

**Title and problem.** The title avoids result words and materials. The lock's avoid list includes a result word for the air purge and a short form for the sealant; both are kept out (PF-17). The technical problem is written for the EPO problem-solution approach; for the US the background will describe documents by number and content without labelling anything admitted prior art. UK practice follows the EPO on both points.

**Claim families.** "System", "kit" and "product-by-process" are included because the prompt asks for them as justified; the map says plainly that the kit and product-by-process rows have thin support. Composition and software are recorded as not applicable rather than omitted.

**Figures and parallel outputs.** Prompts 18, 19 and 20 finished while this blueprint was being written, so the figure numbering, the S7 groups and the list of prophetic examples follow them instead of a provisional scheme of my own. Two points of the figure plan conflict with the risk report (the purge path numbered in FIG. 9, and FIG. 13A); I recorded them in the Deliverable and did not change the figure plan, which is a separate output.

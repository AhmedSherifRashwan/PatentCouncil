## Rationale

The plan gives one figure or figure group to each disclosure-value category the prompt asks for: system overview and use sequence (FIGS. 1, 2, 6), exploded/assembly (FIG. 3), a claim-critical detail view (FIG. 4), a construction-agnostic schematic for an underspecified feature (FIG. 5), manufacture (FIG. 7), the inventor's own three named alternatives (FIGS. 8–10), and the design-around genus alternatives the Phase 3 analysis flagged as the cheapest competitor escapes (FIGS. 11–13A).

FIGS. 11, 12 and 13A depict constructions that came from the design-around and equivalent-architecture analysis, not from the inventor's own disclosure, and every one of them still carries a "candidate"/"needs inventor confirmation" tag in that analysis. They are drawn in prophetic, conditional form — mechanically credible options, not asserted results — precisely so the specification can close these escape routes with genus language without representing that the inventor conceived a specific untested geometry as fact. Whether to carry any of them through into claim scope, or keep them as description-only fallback disclosure, is left as an open question for the Claims Architect and for Gate 3, not decided here.

I have not cited the internal Phase 3 identifiers (design-around numbers, nucleus-ladder rungs, source document names) inside the Deliverable itself, because Prompt 20's Figure Plan is one of the inputs the review-package assembles verbatim for the blind reviewers, and those identifiers are dossier-internal working-record references the reviewers must not see. The same mapping, with identifiers, is kept here in the Rationale, which is stripped before the package reaches review: FIG. 1/2 ↔ disclosure.md §2, §3, §7; FIG. 4 ↔ disclosure.md §3 item 3, §4, §7, and the purge-gap/interference-fit reconciliation proposed in the equivalent-architecture library's "Relieved core body bore" item; FIG. 5 ↔ disclosure.md §3 item 5, §4; FIG. 6 ↔ disclosure.md §2; FIG. 7 ↔ disclosure.md §7; FIG. 8 ↔ disclosure.md §6 Alt-A; FIG. 9 ↔ disclosure.md §6 Alt-B; FIG. 10 ↔ disclosure.md §6 Alt-C; FIG. 11 ↔ the design-around register's seat-ring-geometry alternatives (slotted sleeve, split ring, tapered ring); FIG. 12 ↔ the design-around register's purge-gap-location alternatives; FIG. 13A ↔ the design-around register's tyre-valve-pattern alternative; FIG. 13B ↔ the locked terminology's own "valve core integral with the valve stem" species.

The blueprint (Prompt 16) has not yet been produced in this run, so claim-concept support is stated functionally rather than by blueprint section id, as the prompt's fallback allows.

## Assumptions

- A1 — The purge gap is bounded by the core body bore, not the valve stem bore, consistent with the disclosure's own assumption A1 (§3 item 6 read against §2's looser wording). All figures are drawn on this basis. Not blocking; if a later inventor answer places the boundary at the valve stem bore instead, FIGS. 1, 2, 4 and 12 need revision.
- A2 — The pump head is shown only schematically, as a conventional external device that applies the pressure differential; it is not itself a claimed component of the valve core. Not blocking.
- A3 — FIGS. 11, 12 and 13A depict candidate design-around geometries in prophetic/conditional form rather than as confirmed embodiments, because they originate from Phase 3 competitive analysis rather than the inventor's disclosure. Not blocking for this figure plan; blocks the decision on whether to draft claims reading on them.
- A4 — Reference-numeral assignment is deferred entirely to Prompt 20a; no numerals appear in this plan.

## Open Questions

- Q1 — Where exactly the purge gap sits and how an interference fit and a radially opening clearance coexist on the same seat-ring outer surface is unresolved in the disclosure; FIG. 4/FIG. 12A propose one drawable resolution (a fit band near the locating shoulder and a free portion beyond it) as a candidate, not a confirmed construction. Blocks P4 (finalising which resolution the description commits to), P5.
- Q2 — Convolution count and how the wave is formed (shape-set wall vs laser-cut pattern) for the actual prototype is undisclosed; FIG. 4 shows both convolution counts as alternative species rather than picking one. Blocks P4, P5.
- Q3 — The two-rate return spring's internal construction is undisclosed; FIG. 5 is deliberately construction-agnostic. Blocks the worked-example detail in P4 and any dependent claim reciting a specific construction in P5.
- Q4 — Whether to keep the thermal variant (FIG. 10) as a claimed embodiment or retain it only as disclosed-but-unclaimed subject matter; the inventor states uncertainty and no data exists. Blocks P4, P5.
- Q5 — Whether the candidate design-around geometries and the alternative valve pattern (FIGS. 11, 12, 13A) should be carried into claim scope as genus fallbacks, or held as description-only disclosure pending Gate 3 counsel review, since none is confirmed as the inventor's own conception. Blocks P4's final claim-support mapping, P5, REVIEW.

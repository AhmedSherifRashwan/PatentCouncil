## Deliverable

### Description

#### Title: valve core for a tyre valve, seat ring, and methods of use and manufacture

#### Technical field

The present disclosure relates to tyre valves. It relates in particular to a valve core for a tyre valve that admits air into a tyre chamber containing sealant, to a tyre valve and a tyre chamber fitted with such a valve core, to a seat ring for such a valve core, to a method of cleaning a tyre valve during an inflation cycle, and to methods of making the seat ring and of assembling the valve core. The tyre chamber of a bicycle is described by way of example.

#### Background

A liquid sealant is commonly introduced into the tyre chamber of a bicycle, whether a bicycle inner tube or a tubeless tyre and rim cavity, to seal punctures. The sealant is typically a latex emulsion containing ammonia and suspended particles, and it is introduced through the valve stem of the tyre valve. On each inflation a small quantity of sealant is drawn into the valve core by the pressure surge and by capillary action along the stem of the poppet. Between rides the sealant dries, and a sealant film forms on surfaces of the valve core.

After about ten to twenty inflation cycles the seat of a conventional valve core is coated with cured sealant. The tyre valve then either leaks slowly, because the poppet no longer seats, or cannot be opened by the pump. In the bench test reported under "Examples and data", three conventional valve cores reached their first failure after twelve, fourteen and eighteen inflation cycles respectively, two because the pump could not open them and one by loss of pressure. The usual remedy is manual: the valve core is removed with a tool and is scraped or replaced, and sealant is introduced again.

US11179979B2 describes a valve stem system in which a first valve is arranged at the end of the valve stem body that lies in the volume enclosed by the tyre and the rim. The first valve is described as a self-sealing one-way valve that is operable although a sealant is present, and the document states that the first valve usually prevents sealant from entering the valve stem.

US11148485B2 describes a valve stem assembly in which a plunger of the valve may be used as a tool against clogging when sealant accumulates inside the body of the valve stem.

US20210323363A1 states that valves used with tubeless bicycle tyres are often clogged by sealant due to their small diameter. It describes a valve having a larger internal diameter than typical bicycle valves, which is said to result in higher air flow and reduced clogging.

US2019/0123456A1 describes a valve core having a bore that is enlarged to reduce capillary rise of sealant along the stem of the poppet, a sealing face coated with a fluoropolymer layer that is described as reducing adhesion of cured sealant, a rigid seat, and a removable insert that captures deposits of sealant and can be pulled out and cleaned or replaced without removal of the whole valve core.

Valves in which further travel of a poppet opens a further flow path are described in other fields. US5918628A describes a check valve having a spring-biased poppet with a first stage opening and second stage openings spaced axially along the poppet. Flow through the second stage openings is restricted when the poppet first moves from its closed position, and increases after a predetermined axial movement of the poppet. US4044791A describes a valve assembly having a floating valve seat that is yieldably biased into engagement with a poppet. After the floating valve seat meets an abutment, the fluid unseats the poppet from the floating valve seat and flows through secondary passageways in the poppet.

Valves in which a flow of fluid cleans a seat, or deforms a ring, are also described in other fields. US3570510A describes a valve device in which a clean fluid such as gas or air is supplied to the valve seat from a circular space through an annular slit defined between two concentric seat rings, so as to keep the valve seat clean. US3595265A describes a check valve having a groove in the valve face that acts as an orifice when the valve is closed; when the valve opens, the stream of fluid flowing past the valve face and the groove flushes away foreign particles lodged there. US5924444A describes a valve in which, in the fully opened position, fluid leaving an orifice radially deforms an elastomeric ring outwardly and forms a gap between a side wall and the elastomeric ring, the gap enhancing the egress of fluid, and the elastomeric ring returns to its non-deformed shape as the valve closes.

#### Technical problem

The technical problem addressed is the removal, on each inflation cycle and without a tool, of a sealant film that has already formed on surfaces of the valve core.

Air that enters the tyre chamber through the main passage flows between the sealing face of the poppet and the sealing edge. A longer travel of the poppet along the main passage, or a main passage of larger section, enlarges that same flow path. The stream of air remains within the main passage, and a surface of the valve core that lies outside the main passage, for example an outer surface of the seat ring, is not brought into the stream by either measure.

In the conventional valve cores of the bench test reported under "Examples and data", the air admitted on every inflation cycle passed through the main passage, and those valve cores nevertheless reached their first failure within eighteen inflation cycles.

The valve core described herein has a purge gap, which is a secondary flow path distinct from the main passage, and which is opened by a load that the poppet applies to a radially deflectable element when the poppet travels beyond its normal stroke. In the documents referred to above, further travel of a poppet increases the flow through the valve, and the gap formed at the elastomeric ring enhances the egress of fluid. The purge gap described herein instead directs air, on its way into the tyre chamber, across a surface of the valve core on which sealant film accumulates.

#### Summary

According to a first aspect there is provided a valve core for a tyre valve, the valve core comprising a core body, a radially deflectable element, and a poppet that is movable along an axis of the valve core. The poppet has a sealing face that closes against a sealing edge at rest. The poppet is movable away from the sealing edge through a normal stroke, in which a main passage is open between the sealing face and the sealing edge, and is movable beyond the normal stroke through an over-travel. A load-applying surface that is moved by the poppet is spaced from the radially deflectable element throughout the normal stroke and bears on the radially deflectable element in the over-travel. The axial load so applied produces a radial deflection of the radially deflectable element, and the radial deflection opens a purge gap. The purge gap is a secondary flow path that is distinct from the main passage and that is closed at rest and throughout the normal stroke. When the axial load is removed the radially deflectable element undergoes shape recovery and the purge gap closes.

Two features of the first aspect act together. The first is that the normal stroke and the over-travel are two travel ranges of the same poppet, and the load-applying surface bears on the radially deflectable element only in the second of them, so that the radially deflectable element is not deflected by the load-applying surface during ordinary opening of the tyre valve. The second is that the purge gap is a flow path of its own. The purge gap does not run between the sealing face and the sealing edge, and a main passage that is enlarged, by whatever cause, is not a purge gap.

The purge gap is opened by the mechanical load of the poppet. The radial deflection is produced by the load-applying surface bearing on the radially deflectable element, and not by a flow of fluid acting on the radially deflectable element. When the purge gap is open, air at pump pressure flows through the purge gap, across a surface of the valve core on which sealant film accumulates, and into the tyre chamber. This flow is the air purge.

In embodiments the radially deflectable element is a seat ring that carries the sealing edge, the load-applying surface is a purge shoulder on the poppet, the radial deflection is a radial expansion of the seat ring, and the purge gap is bounded by an outer surface of the seat ring and by a bore surrounding the seat ring.

According to a second aspect there is provided a tyre valve comprising a valve stem and a valve core according to the first aspect. In some embodiments the valve core is a removable threaded valve core received in the valve stem. There is also provided a tyre chamber fitted with the tyre valve, for example a bicycle inner tube or a tubeless tyre and rim cavity, which in use contains sealant.

In some embodiments of the second aspect the valve core is integral with the valve stem.

According to a third aspect there is provided a seat ring for a valve core of a tyre valve. The seat ring is tubular and comprises a sealing edge, on which a sealing face of a poppet of the valve core seats, and a wall having a load-converting geometry. The load-converting geometry is such that an axial load, applied to the seat ring by a load-applying surface that is moved by the poppet in an over-travel of the poppet beyond a normal stroke, produces a radial expansion of the wall that opens a purge gap distinct from a main passage of the valve core. The seat ring undergoes shape recovery, which closes the purge gap, when the axial load is removed. The seat ring may be supplied as a replacement part for a valve core, and the valve core may be supplied as a replacement part for a tyre valve.

According to a fourth aspect there is provided a method of cleaning a tyre valve during an inflation cycle, the tyre valve having a valve core according to the first aspect and being fitted to a tyre chamber that contains sealant. The method comprises coupling a pump head to the tyre valve; applying with the pump head a pressure differential that moves the poppet through the normal stroke; moving the poppet into the over-travel, so that the load-applying surface bears on the radially deflectable element and the purge gap opens; passing air through the open purge gap into the tyre chamber; and removing the pump head, whereupon the pressure differential collapses, the poppet returns, the radially deflectable element undergoes shape recovery and the purge gap closes. The method is performed with the valve core in place in the tyre valve.

According to a fifth aspect there is provided a method of making a seat ring according to the third aspect, the method comprising laser cutting a tube of NiTi alloy, shape-setting the cut tube in a fixture that holds the load-converting geometry, and quenching. In some embodiments the method further comprises electropolishing. There is also provided a method of assembling a valve core, comprising press-fitting the seat ring into a core body, against a locating shoulder of the core body, with an interference fit.

In some embodiments the valve core comprises a return bias that urges the poppet towards the sealing edge and that has a two-rate characteristic, with a first spring stage that is overcome at a crack pressure and a second spring stage that is overcome at an over-travel differential greater than the crack pressure. The poppet then moves through the normal stroke at a pressure differential between the crack pressure and the over-travel differential, and reaches the over-travel only when the higher pressure differential is applied. In embodiments the return bias is a return spring.

In some embodiments the purge gap is annular and lies between an outer surface of the seat ring and a bore surrounding the seat ring, the bore being the core body bore or a bore of the valve stem. The air purge then passes over the outer surface of the seat ring, which lies outside the main passage.

In some embodiments the purge gap runs through the wall of the seat ring, so that the outer surface of the seat ring can be retained along its whole length.

In some embodiments the load-applying surface is a purge shoulder on the poppet, below the sealing face, that is spaced from the seat ring throughout the normal stroke. The axial load is then applied by the poppet itself, and the position of the poppet at which the purge shoulder meets the seat ring marks the end of the normal stroke.

In some embodiments the load-converting geometry is a convoluted wall having at least one convolution. Under the axial load the shape of the convolution changes by bending of the convoluted wall, and the change of shape produces the radial expansion.

In some embodiments the seat ring is made of a NiTi alloy that is superelastic and that has an austenite finish temperature lower than 5 °C, so that the seat ring is superelastic at riding temperatures.

In some embodiments the shape recovery of the seat ring produces a snap-back wipe, in which the seat ring moves across the sealing face of the poppet as the seat ring recovers. The snap-back wipe is a cleaning action additional to the air purge and is not present in every embodiment.

In some embodiments the seat ring is a polymer seat ring, for example of PEEK, which has radial compliance and which undergoes shape recovery more slowly and with lower force than a seat ring of superelastic NiTi alloy.

In some embodiments the valve core has a two-piece seat, in which a rigid seat provides the sealing edge and a separate wiper ring below the rigid seat is the radially deflectable element on which the purge shoulder bears. Sealing then takes place on an element that is not deflected in the over-travel.

In some embodiments the core body is made of brass. In other embodiments the core body is made of stainless steel, for resistance to corrosion where the sealant contains ammonia. In some embodiments the sealing face is of hard-anodised aluminium, which gives the sealing face a hard surface.

In some embodiments the dimensions of the seat ring, the over-travel distance, the purge gap width, the crack pressure and the over-travel differential have the values and ranges set out under "Parameters and ranges".

A thermal variant, in which the seat ring opens the purge gap on warming, is described under "Materials and shape recovery".

Features described in connection with one aspect apply to the other aspects, and the optional features are combinable with one another as set out under "Combinations".

##### Component and structural terms

As used herein, **"seat ring"** means a tubular element, retained in the core body and having radial compliance, that has a sealing edge against which the sealing face of the poppet closes at rest, and a wall that converts an axial load applied by the purge shoulder in the over-travel into a radial expansion that opens the purge gap, the seat ring undergoing shape recovery when the axial load is removed. The seat ring is not limited to a material. A seat ring of superelastic NiTi alloy is preferred, and a polymer seat ring is another example.

As used herein, **"sealing edge"** means the surface on which the sealing face of the poppet seats to close the tyre valve at rest. In embodiments having a seat ring the sealing edge is an edge of the seat ring. In the two-piece seat the sealing edge is provided by the rigid seat.

As used herein, **"core body bore"** means the internal surface of the core body that faces the outer surface of the seat ring. In embodiments the core body bore is one boundary of the purge gap.

As used herein, **"convoluted wall"** means a wall of the seat ring that is not straight in section, so that an axial load on the seat ring translates into a radial expansion of the convoluted wall. A convoluted wall having one convolution and a convoluted wall having two convolutions are both within this definition.

As used herein, **"convolution"** means a single wave in the section of a convoluted wall.

As used herein, **"purge shoulder"** means a surface of the poppet, below the sealing face, that is spaced from the seat ring throughout the normal stroke, that contacts the seat ring only in the over-travel, and that applies the axial load which produces the radial expansion. The purge shoulder is the preferred load-applying surface.

As used herein, **"main passage"** means the flow path by which air flows into the tyre chamber past the sealing edge when the sealing face of the poppet is lifted off the sealing edge. A flow path that runs between the sealing face and the sealing edge is part of the main passage, whatever the size of that flow path, and whether or not that flow path is enlarged by a deflection of the element on which the poppet seats.

As used herein, **"purge gap"** means a secondary flow path into the tyre chamber that is distinct from the main passage, that is closed at rest and throughout the normal stroke, and that is opened in the over-travel by the deflection that the load-applying surface produces in the radially deflectable element. The purge gap is distinguished from the main passage by where the purge gap runs and by the surfaces that the purge gap sweeps, and not by its size: the purge gap does not run between the sealing face and the sealing edge, and the purge gap is bounded by a surface of the radially deflectable element that lies outside the main passage. In the over-travel, air flows into the tyre chamber through the purge gap in addition to the main passage. In the preferred form the purge gap is an annular clearance between the outer surface of the seat ring and a bore surrounding the seat ring, the bore being the core body bore, which is preferred, or a bore of the valve stem.

In some embodiments the purge gap is a flow path through the wall of the seat ring, or is a flow path at one or more locations around the seat ring that does not extend around the whole circumference, as described under "Purge gap arrangements and retention of the seat ring".

As used herein, the purge gap is **"closed at rest"** when the surfaces that bound the purge gap are in the position that those surfaces take when no load is applied to the radially deflectable element by the load-applying surface. The expression does not require closure that is gas-tight to a stated degree; the tyre valve is closed at rest by the sealing face of the poppet on the sealing edge.

##### Threshold, pressure and travel terms

As used herein, **"pressure differential"** means the difference between the pressure upstream of the poppet, applied by a pump head, and the pressure in the tyre chamber.

As used herein, **"crack pressure"** means the pressure differential across the poppet at which the sealing face of the poppet first lifts off the sealing edge. In embodiments having a return spring with a two-rate characteristic, the crack pressure is the pressure differential that overcomes the first spring stage.

As used herein, **"over-travel differential"** means the pressure differential across the poppet, greater than the crack pressure, that is exceeded for the poppet to reach the over-travel. In embodiments having a return spring with a two-rate characteristic, the over-travel differential is the pressure differential that overcomes the second spring stage.

As used herein, **"normal stroke"** means the range of travel of the poppet that begins when the sealing face lifts off the sealing edge and that ends at the position of the poppet at which the load-applying surface first meets the radially deflectable element. Throughout the normal stroke the load-applying surface is spaced from the radially deflectable element. In embodiments in which the poppet is moved by the pressure differential, the poppet is in the normal stroke when the pressure differential is between the crack pressure and the over-travel differential.

As used herein, **"over-travel"** means the range of travel of the same poppet beyond the normal stroke, in which, and only in which, the load-applying surface bears on the radially deflectable element. The normal stroke and the over-travel are two ranges of travel of one poppet, and the position at which the load-applying surface first meets the radially deflectable element is the boundary between them. In the embodiments described the poppet reaches the over-travel when the pressure differential exceeds the over-travel differential, which is the preferred actuation.

An element that carries the sealing edge is loaded by the sealing face of the poppet at rest. A statement herein that the radially deflectable element is loaded only in the over-travel refers to the axial load applied by the load-applying surface.

As used herein, **"over-travel distance"** means the distance that the poppet moves beyond the normal stroke.

As used herein, **"two-rate characteristic"** means a force–travel behaviour of the return bias that has a lower rate through the normal stroke and a higher rate that is overcome for the poppet to reach the over-travel. Constructions that give a two-rate characteristic are described under "Return bias and load-applying surface".

As used herein, **"first spring stage"** means the part of the two-rate characteristic that the crack pressure overcomes during the normal stroke, and **"second spring stage"** means the part of the two-rate characteristic that the over-travel differential overcomes to produce the over-travel.

##### Material and property terms

As used herein, **"superelastic"** means the property of a material that recovers a large strain on unloading at a temperature above the austenite finish temperature of the material, as distinct from a thermally triggered response in which a change of temperature produces the recovery. A material that recovers its shape on unloading by another mechanism is not superelastic as the term is used herein, and is within the definition of shape recovery given below.

As used herein, **"NiTi alloy"** means an alloy based on nickel and titanium. Examples are given under "Materials and shape recovery".

As used herein, **"austenite finish temperature"** means the temperature above which an alloy such as NiTi alloy is fully austenitic and is superelastic.

As used herein, **"martensite start temperature"** means the temperature below which an alloy such as NiTi alloy begins to transform to martensite and correspondingly loses superelastic recovery.

As used herein, **"radial expansion"** means an outward movement of the wall of the seat ring, or of a wall of another radially deflectable element, under an axial load, that opens the purge gap. The term does not require that the wall stretches uniformly around its circumference. In embodiments described herein the radial expansion arises from a change, under the axial load, in the shape of a load-converting geometry such as a convoluted wall.

As used herein, **"radial compliance"** means the capacity of the radially deflectable element to expand radially under the axial load applied by the load-applying surface and to return substantially to its radial dimension at rest when the axial load is removed.

As used herein, **"shape recovery"** means the return of the radially deflectable element towards its shape at rest when the axial load applied by the load-applying surface is removed, which closes the purge gap. The term is not limited to a mechanism of recovery. The shape recovery is superelastic in a seat ring of superelastic NiTi alloy, and is elastic recovery of another kind in other embodiments, for example in a polymer seat ring. The speed and the force of the shape recovery differ between embodiments.

##### Cleaning-action terms

As used herein, **"air purge"** means a first cleaning action, being the flow of air at pump pressure through the open purge gap, across a surface of the valve core on which sealant film accumulates, and into the tyre chamber.

As used herein, **"snap-back wipe"** means a second, mechanical cleaning action that is present in some embodiments, in which the seat ring, on shape recovery, moves across the sealing face of the poppet. In the preferred embodiment the snap-back wipe is produced by the superelastic recovery of a seat ring of NiTi alloy. An embodiment in which the shape recovery is slower or weaker, for example an embodiment having a polymer seat ring, provides the air purge whether or not that embodiment also provides a snap-back wipe of comparable effect.

##### Fluid, chamber and cycle terms

As used herein, **"sealant"** means a liquid, for example a latex emulsion containing ammonia and suspended particles, that is introduced into the tyre chamber through the tyre valve to seal punctures.

As used herein, **"sealant film"** means a layer of sealant, drawn into the valve core, that dries and cures on a surface of the valve core between uses. The term covers a sealant film that is partially cured and a sealant film that is cured.

As used herein, **"tyre chamber"** means the pressurised volume into which the tyre valve admits air, for example a bicycle inner tube or a tubeless tyre and rim cavity.

As used herein, **"pump head"** means a device that is coupled to the tyre valve and that applies the pressure differential, for example the head of a floor pump.

As used herein, **"inflation cycle"** means one application and removal of the pressure differential by a pump head.

##### Alternative-embodiment terms

As used herein, **"polymer seat ring"** means a seat ring made of a polymer that has radial compliance, for example PEEK.

As used herein, **"two-piece seat"** means an arrangement in which the sealing edge is provided by a rigid seat, and a separate wiper ring below the rigid seat is the radially deflectable element on which the purge shoulder bears in the over-travel.

As used herein, **"rigid seat"** means, in the two-piece seat, the element that provides the sealing edge.

As used herein, **"wiper ring"** means, in the two-piece seat, a thin ring having radial compliance that is positioned below the rigid seat, that is deflected outwards by the purge shoulder in the over-travel, and that undergoes shape recovery and wipes when the axial load is removed.

As used herein, **"thermal variant"** means an embodiment in which the seat ring is of a NiTi alloy trained with two-way shape memory and has an austenite finish temperature of approximately 35 °C to 40 °C, so that warming of the seat ring, and not the over-travel of the poppet, opens the purge gap.

##### Dimensional and process terms

As used herein, **"convolution amplitude"** means the height of a convolution of the convoluted wall, measured from the mean line of the convoluted wall.

As used herein, **"purge gap width"** means the radial size of the purge gap when the purge gap is fully open.

As used herein, **"shape-setting"** means a heat treatment that is applied to a seat ring held in a fixture, and that fixes in the material of the seat ring, for example NiTi alloy, the convoluted wall or other load-converting geometry held by the fixture.

##### Genus terms

As used herein, **"radially deflectable element"** means an element of the valve core that is deflected radially by the axial load which the load-applying surface applies in the over-travel, that thereby opens the purge gap, and that undergoes shape recovery when the axial load is removed. The term covers the seat ring, including the seat ring of superelastic NiTi alloy and the polymer seat ring, and covers the wiper ring of the two-piece seat. The expression "radially compliant seat element" has the same meaning.

In some embodiments the radially deflectable element is a slotted sleeve, a split seat ring or a tapered seat ring, as described under "Forms of the radially deflectable element".

As used herein, **"return bias"** means an element, or a combination of elements, that urges the poppet towards the sealing edge. The return spring is the preferred return bias.

As used herein, **"load-applying surface"** means a surface that is moved by the poppet, that is spaced from the radially deflectable element throughout the normal stroke, and that bears on the radially deflectable element in the over-travel. The purge shoulder on the poppet is the preferred load-applying surface.

In some embodiments the load-applying surface is a surface of a separate collar carried by the poppet, is conical, or is a surface of a member through which the return spring acts, as described under "Return bias and load-applying surface".

As used herein, **"load-converting geometry"** means a shape of the radially deflectable element by which an axial load on the radially deflectable element is converted into a radial deflection. The convoluted wall is the preferred load-converting geometry. Other forms are described under "Forms of the radially deflectable element".

As used herein, **"secondary flow path"** means a flow path into the tyre chamber other than the main passage. The purge gap is a secondary flow path that is closed at rest and that is opened in the over-travel.

A term that is defined by the function performed, including "radially deflectable element", "return bias", "load-applying surface", "load-converting geometry" and "secondary flow path", covers each structure described herein that performs the function, and is not limited to the preferred structure, unless the passage or the claim concerned recites the preferred structure expressly.

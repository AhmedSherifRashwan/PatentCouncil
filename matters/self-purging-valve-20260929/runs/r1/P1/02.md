## Rationale

Ranking follows the prompt's instruction to rank by what a competitor must copy to reach the same commercial outcome, not by technical novelty. Row 1 and row 2 are ranked above the component- and process-level rows because a competitor who avoids the assembly or the method avoids the whole benefit, whereas a competitor who avoids only the seat-ring material (row 7) or the body material (row 8) still delivers a materially similar product. Rows 5 and 6 sit in the middle because the disclosure itself, through 01's open questions (M6, M2), identifies them as the most exposed sub-mechanisms — exactly where a competitor would look first for a cheap design-around, which is also why Invention Analyst's Phase 3 design-around register should treat them as priority targets.

The consumable/aftermarket framing for row 3 is drawn directly from §1 ("cores are sold in multipacks for this reason"), which is the only commercial fact the disclosure states; everything past that sentence about channels, licensing or customer segments is reasoned, not disclosed, and is carried as an assumption rather than presented as fact.

Component names throughout reuse 01's terms verbatim (seat ring, poppet, purge shoulder, purge gap, over-travel, two-rate return spring, core body) since no terminology lock exists yet; no new invention-specific terms were introduced. "Consumable", "aftermarket", "upstream", "independent claim", "dependent claim" and "separate claim family" are patent-commercial vocabulary, not invention terminology, and are not new_terms.

## Assumptions

- A1 — The competitor set (valve-core manufacturers, bicycle/wheel OEMs, tubeless-system suppliers, aftermarket parts sellers) is reasoned from the product category (bicycle valves, §1) and from §1's own statement that cores are sold as replaceable multipacks; the disclosure names no actual competitor, customer or sales channel. Not blocking.
- A2 — Row 2's method claim is assumed to be commercially and legally valuable as a separate family; whether it can be drafted broadly (independent of the seat-ring mechanism) depends on Q1, Q7 and Q9 in `01` being answered, since those questions determine whether "self-purging" can be defined by air-purge alone, wipe alone, or both. Blocks P5 claim drafting for this row until answered.
- A3 — Row 3's premise, that a fatigue-limited seat ring (01 M10) creates a replacement-parts market analogous to conventional cores, assumes the ring is in fact a wear item; if Gate 1/testing shows it lasts the life of the core, the separate component claim family is still valid defensively but the "consumable" commercial rationale weakens. Not blocking.
- A4 — Row 4's premise, that manufacture could be licensed upstream to existing core brands rather than sold direct, is a plausible but undisclosed business model. Not blocking.
- A5 — Rows 7–9 are ranked using the inventor's own characterisation in §6 (lower-cost line for Alt-A; unsure for Alt-C) rather than any market data. Not blocking.

## Open Questions

- Q2 — Is a corrosion-resistant (stainless, Alt-D) or thermal (Alt-C) variant an actual planned product, or purely exploratory? Affects whether rows 8 and 9 warrant dependent claims now or should wait for Gate 2 confirmation. Blocks P3 (embodiment prioritisation), P5.

## D1 — Purge gap as a secondary flow path

**D1.1 US5924444A (new, INDEX 15) — strong against the functional claim, weak against the structural one.** A tyre-mounted valve in which a ring retained on an interior surface of the housing, against which the poppet seals, is deformed radially outward to open a gap that does not exist when the valve is closed.

- Claim 1: "in said fully opened position, said orifice is disposed relative to said elastomeric ring such that fluid radially exiting said orifice deforms said elastomeric ring outwardly forming a gap between said side wall and said elastomeric ring, said gap enhancing the egress of fluid from said first cavity to said second cavity".
- Description: "As the flow develops, the elastomeric ring 303 deforms radially outward thereby increasing the gap 314 available for fluid flow." and "As the valve closes, radial fluid flow against the elastomeric ring 303 decreases allowing it to return to its non-deformed shape."
- FIG. 14 embodiment, gap opened only on excess pressure: "Excess pressure will force poppet 194 upwardly from the shown position. Flow exiting out of channel 234 at the sealing surface 208 in valving region 204 will move upwardly so as to produce a gap between sealing surface 208 and O-ring 210 ... Consequently, fluid flow (relief flow) from the tire will pass through passage 230, through the gap, into cavity 200".
- In the fill-through embodiments the tyre is inflated along a different path ("Inflation of the tire can now be performed through inlet stem 64"), so the gap is a second path, separate from the inflation passage.

09 proposes to claim the purge gap functionally in the independent claim: "a secondary flow path opened by radial expansion of the seat ring". That wording reads on US5924444A if "seat ring" covers a ring on which the poppet seals. What US5924444A lacks: the gap is on the inside of the ring, against the poppet side wall; the ring is elastomeric; it is deformed by the fluid, not by the poppet; flow is out of the tyre; there is no cleaning purpose.

**D1.2 US3570510A (INDEX 3) — moderate, and missing from the 09 matrix.** Air purge of the sealing surfaces through an annular gap at the seat rings, for sticky contamination, is published.

- Abstract: "clean fluid such as gas, air, water and the like, is supplied to the valve seat of the valve device from a circular space having an annular slit so as to keep the valve seat clean."
- Problem: "in instances where the fluid flowing through the valve contains sticky dust particles or the like, the valve or rather the valve seat starts to collect such dust particles, which prevents proper closing of the valve."
- "Two concentric seat rings 6 and 7 ... said seal rings defining an annular slit 8".
- The purge is stepped up at a chosen point in the valve cycle: gas "escapes through the slit at a considerably higher velocity ... to sweep the seal rings 6 and 7 and the rubber seal 9 so that the sealing surfaces are free from contamination at the time when they join to form the seal".

Lacks: a dedicated gas supply and control valves; the slit is permanently open; it lies between two seat rings, not between a seat ring and the bore. 09's technical effect for this row ("air ... directed across the surfaces where the sealant film forms") is this document's effect. 09 does not chart it in any row.

**D1.3 US3595265A (INDEX 10) — moderate on the effect, also missing from the 09 matrix.** Flushing deposits at the seat with the working fluid itself on each opening, no separate supply: "This large stream of fluid flowing past the face of the valve, and past the groove ... flushes away any foreign particles of material that may have lodged therein when the valve was closed." Object: "an orifice check valve that will periodically clean its orifice".

**D1.4 US4044791A (INDEX 2) — moderate as a combination piece.** A second path that is closed while the poppet sits on its seat and opens on further poppet travel at higher pressure: claim 11, "said main fluid passageway and said volumetric space being blocked from communication with each other when said valve elements are disposed in engagement with each other"; description, the fluid "will unseat the poppet from the floating valve seat thus causing a displacement between the floating valve seat and poppet permitting fluid to flow past the protruding portion of the poppet and through secondary passageways 54". Opened by axial separation, not by expansion of the seat.

**D1.5 The distinction as worded in 09 is kinematically incoherent — strong, from 09's own text.** 09 places the purge gap "between an outer surface of the seat ring and the core body bore", "closed at rest and opened by radial expansion of the seat ring". A ring that expands radially outward moves its outer surface towards the bore. Expansion closes a gap on the outside of the ring; it cannot open one. 09 A1 shows the location is the analyst's assumption, not the inventor's statement: the disclosure says "stem bore" and 09 calls that "loose wording". 09 Q2 treats the point as a missing enablement detail. It is more than that:

- If the gap is outside the ring, the examiner and any opponent will say the claimed mechanism is inoperative as described (insufficiency at the EPO and UK, enablement and written description in the US), and an unenabled feature cannot carry inventive step.
- If the gap is in fact on the inside of the ring, between the ring and the poppet, the mechanism is coherent, and US5924444A claim 1 and ref-02 [0019] are then far closer than 09 rates them: both open a gap at the inner side of a seat ring by deflecting it outward.

This must be resolved with the inventor before any claim is drafted. Nobody here should choose the answer for him.

**D1.6 The technical effect is not shown to come from the purge gap — moderate, from 09's own text.** 09 relies on one bench observation, quoted as cured film "on the ring outer surface in the purge-gap region; sealing edge and poppet face clean". A hostile reader takes that the other way: the only place film remained is the purge gap. The surfaces that seal were clean, but 09 itself says the purge air is directed "behind the sealing edge", so the purge gap does not sweep them. Their cleanliness is equally explained by the longer stroke alone, which US5918628A already teaches ("to stroke long enough such that particles will pass easily between the valve head and the valve seat"). At the EPO the objective problem is then reformulated as providing an alternative, and D1.2 to D1.4 become enough. It also suggests the purge gap is where the prototype fouls.

**Survives on novelty (structural form):** no passage found with a gap between a seat ring and the bore that carries it, opened by deformation of that ring. Searched L2, L6, L8, L10 and the Prior-Art Analyst's queries 4, 9, 12.

## D2 — Over-travel above a second, higher differential

**D2.1 09 mischaracterises US5918628A (INDEX 8) — strong against the proposed claim language.** 09 lists it under "one bias and one opening regime". Its title is "Multi-stage check valve" and claim 1 recites a single spring-biased poppet whose further travel opens a second flow path: "said valve body and poppet valve cooperating to provide a flow path through the first stage opening and to restrict the flow path through the second stage opening when the poppet valve initially moves in said second direction from said closed position, and then after a predetermined axial movement of the poppet valve relative to the valve body toward said open position, increasing the flow path to the second stage opening." Abstract: "When the cracking force of the valve is exceeded and the valve head moves away from the valve seat, fill flow occurs through the first stage opening ... The flow increases through the second stage openings as the poppet valve moves toward its open position."

**D2.2 The proposed claim language reads on any spring-biased poppet — strong.** 09 proposes: "movable through a normal stroke when the pressure differential exceeds a crack pressure and through an over-travel beyond the normal stroke only when the pressure differential exceeds an over-travel differential greater than the crack pressure". With any return spring the force rises with travel, so every additional increment of travel needs a higher differential than the one that cracked the valve. Without a structural or measurable definition of where the normal stroke ends (a step in the force–travel characteristic, a stop, first contact with the seat ring), the limitation distinguishes nothing, and "normal stroke" will be construed against the patentee. This is a common-general-knowledge premise, labelled as such (A2 below), but US5918628A and US4044791A each show it in a document.

**D2.3 US4044791A (INDEX 2) — moderate.** Staged travel of the same poppet, with a second path opening only in the later part: "Initially, as poppet 14 moves to the right, it will remain seated on the floating valve seat ... As the poppet continues to move to the right, however, end surface 40 of the floating valve seat eventually will engage abutment surface 22 ... the force of the fluid, continuing to act on end surface 49 of the poppet, will unseat the poppet from the floating valve seat". 09 distinguishes it by its two springs and rigid seat; neither is in the proposed claim language. Lacks: a check function (primary passageway 50 is always open) and any cleaning purpose.

**D2.4 US1934572A (INDEX 9) — weak to moderate.** A second, higher pressure threshold inside a tyre valve core: "As the pressure within the tire increases the pressure on the valve 31 likewise increases until a predetermined pressure is reached at which the valve 31 is moved slightly upward against the bias of the spring 32". A separate valve and a relief function, but it puts staged thresholds in the field.

**D2.5 US5924444A (INDEX 15) — moderate against the fallback 09 wants to keep open.** 09's design-around cell asks whether the seat ring's own stiffness can set the second threshold (Q3). That a seal's resistance to deformation adds to the opening pressure is published: "since the fluid pressure must be high enough not only to move the poppet, but also to deform the seal, a certain amount of hysteresis is present in the system which increases its stability."

**D2.6 That "the seat ring is loaded only in over-travel" is not true as written — moderate, from 09's own text.** The seat ring carries the sealing edge on which the poppet closes, so it is loaded at rest. If the limitation is meant as "loaded by the purge shoulder only in over-travel", it must say so.

**Survives:** a two-rate return spring in a tyre valve core. Searched L4, L10 and queries 6, 12. 09 rightly keeps it out of the independent claim; as a dependent it is a real fallback in this set.

## D3 — Expansion caused mechanically by the poppet

**D3.1 ref-02 claim 4 (supplied, fictional) — strong against the independent-claim form.** 09 charts ref-02 in this row only as "lip deflected by inflation pressure". Its claim 4 recites "the seat being a lip seal deflectable by the poppet during opening", and [0024] allows "a spring steel lip seal". 09's proposed independent-claim wording, "the poppet bears on the seat ring during the over-travel", differs from that only by when the contact happens, which is D2. So D3 adds nothing to D2 at independent-claim level.

**D3.2 US3473561A (INDEX 1) — moderate.** Axial load against a rigid abutment converted into radial movement of a resilient sealing element by flattening a circumferential feature of its wall: "The wall contains a circumferential recess 231" and "Under back pressure conditions, recess 231 flattens against the downstream faces of the vanes, causing downstream elongation of the closure member, wiping lip 234 against the valve seat surface." The element is the closure member, in elastomer, loaded by back pressure.

**D3.3 The stated effect contradicts D2 — moderate, from 09's own text.** 09 claims the gap opening is "independent of how fast the pump delivers air". Over-travel is triggered by a differential across the open valve (09 quotes 3.5 bar against a 1.0 bar crack pressure). The pressure drop across an open valve is set by flow rate. A slow hand pump may never reach the over-travel differential; a compressor will reach it at once. The opening therefore depends on exactly what the effect says it does not. 09 quotes "over-travel confirmed on every prototype inflation" without the pump or the flow; that condition has to be in the description, and the effect has to be restated.

**D3.4 Which surface the purge shoulder engages is not stated — moderate.** 09 has the purge shoulder "below the sealing face" bearing on the seat ring (D3) and the seat ring converting "axial load on its sealing edge" (D4). If the shoulder lands on the sealing edge, it closes the main passage again during over-travel and the purge gap becomes the only flow path. Then it is not a secondary path, and ref-02 [0019] ("deflects outward to enlarge the flow passage") is on point.

**Survives on novelty:** a shoulder on the poppet, separate from its sealing face, that loads a seat ring after a first part of the stroke. Searched L6, L8 and query 12.

## D4 — Convoluted tube wall

**D4.1 09 mischaracterises EP3171056A1 (INDEX 4) — moderate.** 09 calls it a "flat, static SMA ring". The document discloses the geometry principle and the material together:

- Claim 1: a shape memory alloy body "in a wave-like shape comprising at least one peak (26, 26') and at least one trough (28, 28')"; "the body may comprise only one peak and only one trough", which is 09's "one or two convolutions".
- Axial height traded for radial width: "heating may cause the ring to flatten out so that its extension (or height) in the axial direction is reduced (i.e. the peak(s) and/or trough(s) become less pronounced), and its width in the radial direction ... is increased."
- Superelastic behaviour and fatigue: "when above the transformation temperature, the SMA may exhibit pseudoelastic behaviour. This advantageously allows the SMA to absorb high strains whilst maintaining a certain stress or sealing force, which gives such sealing members a high fatigue resistance".
- Any alloy including "Ni-Ti".

Lacks: a valve, a poppet, mechanical loading; the wave runs radially across a washer, not along a tube wall; the change is thermal.

**D4.2 US11549591B1 (new, INDEX 17) — moderate.** A metal seat in a poppet check valve that is a tube with undulating portions: "The seat 810 for the valve element 804 is formed from a resilient metal element 812. The resilient metal element 812 has a cylindrical sidewall 814 ... The cylindrical sidewall 814 extends in an axial direction along the bore 806"; the flange that meets the poppet "may be planar, undulating, or curved"; and "a wavy or undulating shape 420, which facilitates the resiliency". Lacks: radial expansion, a second flow path, undulation in the sidewall itself.

**D4.3 US3473561A (INDEX 1) — weak.** "A bellows section 181 connects the core with a central portion 182 of the closure member cone", with the circumferential recess of D3.2.

**D4.4 The prototype numbers invite a technical challenge — moderate, from 09's own text.** 09 quotes a 0.15 mm radial expansion from a convolution amplitude of 0.12 mm in a 0.20 mm wall under 0.5 mm of axial travel. An examiner's technical specialist will ask how a convolution smaller than the expansion produces it, and whether axial compression of a convoluted tube flattens or deepens the convolution. The description needs the load path and the deformed shape, from the inventor.

**Design-around, as 09 says:** slotted collet, conical washer, the C-spring of US11549591B1 FIG. 11 ("formed as a spring clamp or C spring member"). Generic language (a seat ring with a compliant wall that converts axial load into radial deflection) is needed above the convoluted wall, if the inventor conceived it.

**Survives on novelty:** a convoluted tube wall as a valve seat that expands radially under poppet load. Searched L2 and query 10.

## D5 — Snap-back wipe

**D5.1 US3473561A (INDEX 1) — strong against the wipe as a concept.** A sealing element cleaning the sealing pair by wiping under its own elastic deformation and recovery, every cycle, in both directions: "Sealing edge 134 of the closure member tends to wipe against valving surfaces 133 of the valve body, both when the member displaces from hub 114 and when back pressure compresses the closure member ... The wiping motion dislodges any particles residing on the valving surface or the sealing surface of the closure member, thus effecting better sealing." The valve is described as "self-cleaning". The only structural difference is which member of the pair is resilient, which is a reversal of parts.

**D5.2 US4050473A (INDEX 5) — moderate.** It records that wiping by the primary sealing member was already known before 1975, of one earlier patent: "While a cleaning action is described in the patent, the disk serves as the primary valve sealing member", and of another, "a flexible disk which provides a valve cleaning action. That disk, however, forms the principal seal in the valve." The two earlier patents are not saved and are not relied on beyond what this document says of them.

**D5.3 ref-02 (supplied, fictional) — strong against the proposed language by inherency.** A lip "deflectable by the poppet during opening" (claim 4) that "returns under its own elasticity" ([0020]) slides across the poppet as it returns. 09's proposed language, "on shape recovery the seat ring wipes across the sealing face of the poppet", states a result that ref-02's structure produces whether or not ref-02 says so. 09 notes this; it should treat it as decisive for any claim without the material.

**D5.4 The effect is unsupported — strong, conceded in 09.** The bench test does not separate the wipe from the air purge (09 Q4). "Snap" is also doubtful as a property of the seat ring: the ring recovers only as fast as the purge shoulder retreats, which is set by the collapse of the differential and the return spring, not by the alloy.

Film against particles is a difference in the fouling, not in the structure. Every wiping reference found cleans particles; none addresses a cured film. That is an argument for inventive step, not a claim feature.

## D6 — Superelastic NiTi seat in moving duty

**D6.1 US6019118A (new, INDEX 16) — strong.** 09 says the superelastic seals in the set are static. This one is a valve seat and tappet that open and close:

- Claim 4: "at least one pair of sealing elements including a seat and a tappet, whose seat surfaces are in sealing contact with one another in the shutoff position, at least one of said sealing elements of the pair consist of a linearly superelastic shape memory alloy".
- Claim 5: "selected from a group consisting of NiTi and Cu".
- Purpose: "The seat surfaces (sealing surfaces) are thus able to compensate surface roughnesses caused by machining, wear and contamination."
- "A linear elastic deformation of up to 4.5% in the temperature range of about -150° C. to +150° C. is displayed especially by the NiTi-based alloys."

What is left is the austenite finish temperature. US6019118A's alloy is cold worked so that it "no longer displays a temperature-dependent structural transformation", so it has none.

**D6.2 Choosing the transformation temperature below the service temperature is routine — strong.** EP3171056A1: pseudoelastic behaviour "when above the transformation temperature". US9611937B2 pairs "an Austenite finish temperature (Af) of about 57° C." with "superelastic behavior within a temperature range of from about 120° C. to about 205° C." Below 5 °C for a bicycle is the same choice for a different climate.

**D6.3 Design-around opened by US6019118A — moderate.** A claim to "an austenite finish temperature below 5 °C" is avoided by a cold-worked, linearly superelastic NiTi seat ring, which has no austenite finish temperature and recovers up to about 4 % strain at any riding temperature. Whether "superelastic" without the temperature covers that material is a construction question the description must settle by definition.

D6 is a dependent fallback only, as 09 says. It should not appear in any statement of what distinguishes the invention.

## D7 — Tool-free removal on each inflation cycle

**Strong; 09 concedes it is result language. Three further points.**

- Automatic cleaning on every cycle by the working fluid or by the valve's own motion is old: US3595265A (D1.3), US3473561A (D5.1), US3570510A (D1.2).
- Tool-free answers to sealant fouling in a bicycle valve exist by exclusion rather than removal. US11179979B2 (new, INDEX 19): a first valve at the tyre end that is "a self-sealing one-way valve ... which is operable although a sealant is present", and "the first valve usually will prevent sealant from entering into the valve stern. In the valve stem systems known from the prior art, such pollution of the valve stem regularly causes malfunctions of the valves due to so-called valve clogging." US20210323363A1: "seals the valve body, preventing air from passing from the tire into the valve. This prevents sealant in the tire from clogging the valve." These are also the commercial alternatives a competitor would sell instead.
- The comparison 09 cites does not support an effect over the closest prior art. It compares prototypes carrying every feature with conventional cores carrying none, three of each. It shows nothing against an elastomer-seat valve (ref-02) or a coated, large-bore core (ref-01), and it cannot attribute the result to any one of D1 to D6.

## D8 — Manufacture of the seat ring

**D8.1 US9126251B2 (new, INDEX 18) — strong for the chain, moderate for the claim 09 proposes.** 09 says no process is charted beyond US9611937B2. The standard NiTi tube chain is: "laser cutting a tubular member or tubing of suitable material to create the intricate strut patterns"; "a laser cut Nitinol work piece must be carefully processed by a number of different cycles of chemical treatment, radial expansion, and heat stabilization to achieve the final stent size"; "Any remaining oxidized wall material and other adhered oxide debris must first be removed in order to attain an acceptably smooth surface later during electropolishing." Removal of the oxide and the heat-affected edge, which 09 gives as the technical effect of D8, is this document's stated purpose.

**D8.2 US9611937B2 (INDEX 11) — weak.** Superelasticity "imparted ... through a thermal treatment process", as 09 notes.

**Survives in this set:** shape-setting in a fixture that holds the convoluted wall, and the press fit against a locating shoulder. Searched L9; the two heat-treatment documents returned (mould restraint of wire, heat treatment of cold-worked alloy) were read and not saved because neither shows a tube held in a fixture. Shape-setting NiTi in a fixture followed by a quench is, on my understanding, common general knowledge (A3); a reference for it should be expected from any examiner. The process claim is novel only through the shape of the product it makes.

## The combination an examiner will run against D1 + D2 + D3

Closest prior art: ref-02, a tyre valve with a seat "deflectable by the poppet during opening", in elastomer or "spring steel". If the fictional references are set aside, US4044791A or US5924444A. Add staged travel from US5918628A or US4044791A (D2), air purge of the seat through an annular gap from US3570510A (D1.2), and the fouling problem in the field from US20210323363A1 ("often clogged by sealant due to their small diameter").

Rating: **moderate**. Three documents from three fields; none shows a seat deformed by the poppet so as to open a path. In the US an obviousness rejection on this combination is credible. At the EPO it depends on the objective technical problem, and therefore on D1.6: if no effect is attributable to the purge gap, the problem becomes an alternative and the combination is much easier.

## What the text must answer

1. **Where the purge gap is, and why expansion opens it (D1.5).** Inventor question, before drafting. It decides which references are closest.
2. **A measurable boundary between normal stroke and over-travel (D2.2).** Otherwise the second independent-claim feature reads on any sprung poppet.
3. **Independent claim wording (D1.1, D3.1).** The wordings "a secondary flow path opened by radial expansion of the seat ring" and "the poppet bears on the seat ring" are each anticipated in functional terms. The claim has to tie them together: the poppet, in over-travel, deforming the seat ring so as to open a path that bypasses the sealing edge.
4. **The effect statements (D1.6, D3.3, D5.4).** Each is contradicted or unsupported on 09's own account. State only what the data show, with the inflation conditions.
5. **The charting errors in 09.** US5918628A is multi-stage, not single-regime. EP3171056A1 is a wave-section ring with stated pseudoelastic behaviour, not a flat ring. ref-02 claim 4 belongs in D3. US3570510A and US3595265A, the two closest air-purge documents, appear in no row.
6. **A definition of "superelastic" (D6.3)** that says whether linearly superelastic, cold-worked NiTi is inside or outside.

## Assumptions

A1. The two requester-supplied references are fictional (09 A4) and are treated as published. Attacks D3.1 and D5.3 rest on them; with those set aside, D3 at independent-claim level falls from strong to moderate (US3473561A) and D5 stays strong on US3473561A alone.
A2. Common general knowledge: the force of a return spring rises with its deflection, so further poppet travel needs a higher differential. No reference is cited for it beyond the staged valves in D2.
A3. Common general knowledge: NiTi parts are shape-set by heating while held in a fixture and then quenching. No saved reference shows it for a tube.
A4. All saved references are taken to be prior art by date. No priority or filing date for this matter was checked.
A5. Statements about the disclosure and the bench data are taken from 09's quotations only.

## Open Questions

Q1. Is the purge gap outside the seat ring, against the core body bore, or inside it, against the poppet? If outside, what opens it? Blocks D1, and the choice of closest prior art.
Q2. At what pump flow was over-travel observed, and does it occur with a hand pump? Blocks D2 and D3.3.
Q3. Does the purge shoulder land on the sealing edge, and does the main passage stay open during over-travel? Blocks D3.4.
Q4. The search remains thin in the same places the Prior-Art Analyst named: CN, TW, DE and JP utility models on bicycle valve cores, and product literature for sealant-resistant cores. None of the ten new queries reached them. A professional search is still warranted before filing.

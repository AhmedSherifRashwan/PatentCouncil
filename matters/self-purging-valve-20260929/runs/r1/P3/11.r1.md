**What the register shows about the nucleus.** DA-1, DA-3, DA-6 and DA-7 confirm 03's placement of the two-rate return spring, the convoluted wall and the purge shoulder below N0: each is avoidable cheaply, and N0 must not recite them. DA-2 attacks N0 itself: if the inventor did not conceive of purge on every opening, a single-threshold core escapes the core limitation. DA-10 and DA-14 escape N0 entirely by removing the deflectable element; they can only be reached through a function-level method claim, and only if air purge alone gives the effect. Those three (DA-2, DA-10, DA-14) are the routes Gate 2 must resolve first.

## Rationale

The attack starts from the three N0 features, because a design-around that escapes N0 escapes the whole family, and then works down the ladder. For each essential-looking limitation I tried omission, substitution, relocation, splitting across two parts, and a different actuation. Several attractive routes (DA-10, DA-14, DA-17) cannot be closed by genus language without adding matter the inventor has not conceived; they are recorded as escape routes and turned into questions rather than padded into embodiments.

Ranking puts the cheapest high-plausibility escapes first: DA-1 and DA-2 cost the competitor nothing but a stiffer seat ring or shorter travel, and DA-3 and DA-4 remove the two expensive processes (shape-setting and NiTi). Rigid-part routes rank lower only because their effect is unproven, not because they are hard to make.

Material species named in DA-4 are generic engineering materials used to show the attack, not proposed embodiments; only PEEK and stainless spring steel have any footing in the disclosure. Elastomers are flagged medium because creep could hold the purge gap open, which is the leak-down failure the invention addresses.

Prior-art differentiation (09) and white space (10) were absent, so no route was ranked by whether its genus would read on the references; that check falls to the Litigator's attack and to 09.

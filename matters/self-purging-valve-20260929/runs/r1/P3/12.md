## Deliverable

### Equivalent Architecture Library

**Status: candidate.** Nothing below has been built or tested unless it is marked "inventor's own" with its disclosure section; every other item is a candidate for Human Gate 2, which decides whether it is technically real and within the inventor's conception. Sources: `intake/disclosure.md` (§n), `runs/r1/P1/01.md` (01), `runs/r1/P1/03.md` (03 and its rungs N0–N6), `runs/r1/P3/11.r1.md` (design-around ids DA-n). No inventor answers (`intake/answers.md`) exist yet, and Prompts 09 and 10 had not been written, so no alternative here is checked against the differentiation or the white space. Coverage follows the features 03 classifies essential or advantageous. This is a drafting and analysis aid; qualified patent counsel signs off before anything is filed.

Each alternative gives: structure or mechanism; how it achieves the same purpose; what changes; the genus term that embraces it and the disclosed form; credibility; the DA ids it closes.

#### over-travel

Purpose (03 N0 item 1): a second poppet travel range, beyond the normal stroke, that brings the load-applying surface onto the seat ring.

1. **Spring-loaded stop at the end of the normal stroke.** Structure: the normal stroke ends when the poppet meets a stop washer that is itself held by a stiffer spring; over-travel is the poppet pushing that stop back. Purpose: the stop defines the end of the normal stroke and the stiffer spring defines the over-travel differential, so the poppet reaches over-travel only above it. Changes: adds a washer and a second spring; the return spring can be single-rate. Genus: extended poppet travel. Credibility: medium–high; ordinary stop-and-spring construction, but it adds axial length the 6 mm valve stem may not have (01 Q15). Closes: DA-6. Status: candidate.
2. **Displacement-actuated over-travel.** Structure: a push rod in the pump head, or an adapter threaded on the valve stem, drives the poppet into over-travel directly, with or without the over-travel differential. Purpose: the purge shoulder reaches the seat ring by displacement rather than pressure; the purge gap and shape recovery work as disclosed. Changes: the trigger is mechanical; the second threshold becomes a force at the poppet tip, not a pressure differential. Genus: extended poppet travel, however actuated. Credibility: medium–high as mechanics; the disclosure says nothing about mechanical actuation, so whether it is within the conception is for the inventor. Closes: DA-15. Status: candidate.

#### return spring

Purpose (03 row 2, essential as a function; N3): separate the over-travel differential from the crack pressure by a two-rate characteristic. The disclosure gives only "a compression spring … with a two-rate characteristic" and not how the two rates arise (01 (j), M6).

1. **Variable-pitch single spring.** Structure: one compression spring whose closely pitched coils close solid during the normal stroke, leaving the widely pitched coils, now fewer active coils and so a higher rate, as the second spring stage. Purpose: first spring stage and second spring stage from one part. Changes: none at the interfaces. Genus: return bias with a two-rate characteristic. Credibility: high; variable pitch is an ordinary way to obtain a rising rate. Closes: DA-6 (single-spring species). Status: candidate.
2. **Two springs in series.** Structure: a soft spring and a stiff spring stacked, with a stop that takes the soft spring out of the load path at the end of the normal stroke. Purpose: the soft spring sets the crack pressure; the stiff spring sets the over-travel differential. Changes: two parts plus a stop; more axial length. Genus: return bias. Credibility: high. Closes: DA-6. Status: candidate.
3. **Nested springs in parallel, staggered engagement.** Structure: an outer spring acts through the whole travel; a shorter inner spring engages only at the end of the normal stroke, so the rate in over-travel is the sum of both. Purpose and changes: as item 2, shorter axially. Genus: return bias. Credibility: high. Closes: DA-6. Status: candidate.
4. **Spring plus elastomeric bumper.** Structure: a single-rate return spring and an elastomeric bumper that the poppet reaches at the end of the normal stroke. Purpose: the bumper supplies the second spring stage. Changes: an elastomer inside a sealant-wetted core. Genus: return bias. Credibility: medium; the bumper must tolerate ammonia-bearing sealant and not take a compression set that shifts the over-travel differential; no data. Closes: DA-6. Status: candidate.
5. **Seat ring stiffness as the second spring stage.** Structure: a single-rate return spring; once the purge shoulder meets the seat ring, the axial stiffness of the seat ring adds to the return spring, so the poppet needs the higher over-travel differential to continue. Purpose: the threshold is set by the part that opens the purge gap, removing a part. Changes: the seat ring geometry now sets a pressure threshold as well as the purge gap width. Genus: return bias comprising the return spring and the seat ring. Credibility: medium; physically sound, but whether a seat ring of the §4 dimensions has an axial stiffness in the right range is unknown. Closes: DA-1. Status: candidate; requires inventor confirmation (Q2).
6. **Disc spring engaged at the end of the normal stroke.** Structure: a disc spring stacked with a soft coil spring, meeting the poppet only in over-travel. Purpose: a stiff, short second spring stage. Changes: a stock part; small axial length. Genus: return bias. Credibility: high. Closes: DA-6, part of DA-9. Status: candidate.
7. **Detent.** Structure: a spring-loaded ball, or a magnet and keeper, that holds the poppet at the end of the normal stroke until the over-travel differential overcomes it. Purpose: a force step rather than a rate change separates the two thresholds, which also makes over-travel abrupt. Changes: a detent in the sealant path. Genus: means for setting the over-travel differential. Credibility: medium; sealant fouling of a ball detent is a real risk, and the magnet option depends on poppet material (brass is non-magnetic). Closes: DA-6. Status: candidate.

#### seat ring

Purpose (03 N0 item 2, N1): a radially deflectable element that the purge shoulder deflects in over-travel to open the purge gap, and that recovers to close it. The genus used throughout is "radially deflectable element" (03), with "deflectable element" for non-radial forms (11.r1).

1. **Two-piece seat (inventor's own, Alt-B, §6).** Structure: rigid seat carrying the sealing edge; a thin stainless wiper ring below it deflected outward by the purge shoulder. Purpose: sealing on the rigid seat; purge and wipe on the wiper ring. Changes: one more part, more axial length; the purge path is not described (03 Q4). Genus: radially deflectable element separate from the sealing edge. Credibility: stated by the inventor; not built. Closes: DA-8. Status: candidate.
2. **Slotted sleeve.** Structure: a tube with axial slots from one end forming fingers; a conical purge shoulder entering the fingered end spreads them. Purpose: radial expansion by finger bending rather than hoop strain, so the strain in the material is low. Changes: the purge shoulder becomes conical; the slots are themselves a flow route when the fingers spread (see purge gap item 3). Genus: radially deflectable element. Credibility: high; collet-type spreading is ordinary, and it suits spring-tempered metals as well as NiTi alloy. Closes: DA-3(a). Status: candidate.
3. **Split seat ring.** Structure: a C-section ring with one axial split that opens circumferentially when a cone on the poppet enters it. Purpose: large diameter change for small strain. Changes: the split must be closed at rest, or must lie outside the sealing edge, or the valve leaks at rest. Genus: radially deflectable element. Credibility: medium; the split interrupts the sealing edge unless the ring seals on a face beside it. Closes: DA-3(b). Status: candidate.
4. **Tapered seat ring.** Structure: a seat ring with a conical inner surface wedged outward by a matching taper on the poppet in over-travel. Purpose: the taper converts axial load into radial expansion without a convolution. Changes: taper angles on two parts; friction governs recovery. Genus: radially deflectable element. Credibility: medium–high for a compliant material; a stiff wedge may lock if the taper is below the friction angle. Closes: DA-3(c). Status: candidate.
5. **Disc spring as the deflectable element.** Structure: a conical disc spring whose rim seals on a face at rest; the purge shoulder flattens it and its rim lifts, opening a face purge gap. Purpose: purge gap opened by axial rather than radial deflection; the disc spring also supplies a second spring stage. Changes: the purge gap becomes axial; the radial expansion rows of the lock no longer describe it. Genus: deflectable element. Credibility: medium–high; stock parts, but the rim must seal against sealant at rest. Closes: DA-9. Status: candidate.
6. **No deformable element: groove uncovered by over-travel.** Structure: in over-travel the poppet carries a sealing band past a groove or flat in the core body bore, uncovering a secondary flow path; it re-seals on return. Purpose: air purge without deformation. Changes: no shape recovery and no snap-back wipe; the cleaned surfaces differ. Genus: secondary flow path opened by over-travel. Credibility: medium; machinable, but it falls outside N0 items 2 and 3 and is a candidate only if the inventor confirms conceiving it. Closes: DA-10. Status: candidate; requires inventor confirmation (Q4).

#### convoluted wall

Purpose (03 N2): convert axial load on the sealing edge into radial expansion. The disclosure does not say whether the convolution is formed in the wall section or cut as a pattern (01 M3).

1. **Section convolution formed by shape-setting.** Structure: a plain tube wall pressed into one or two circumferential waves in a shape-setting fixture; no pattern is cut. Purpose: axial load flattens the waves outward. Changes: laser cutting only trims the ends. Genus: load-converting geometry. Credibility: high as a reading of §7 ("fixture holding the wave geometry"); which reading the prototype used is Q1. Closes: none; a species of the disclosed wall. Status: candidate.
2. **Laser-cut strut pattern.** Structure: the wall cut into a zig-zag or diamond-cell strut pattern that shortens axially and bulges radially under axial load. Purpose: radial expansion by strut bending at low strain. Changes: the wall is open at rest unless the cells are covered at the sealing edge and the fit band; the cut region becomes the purge route. Genus: load-converting geometry. Credibility: medium–high; this is established laser-cut NiTi tube practice, but the cells must be closed at rest (Q3). Closes: DA-3, DA-5. Status: candidate.
3. **Pre-bowed wall.** Structure: a single outward bow along the free length, which increases under axial load as a pre-buckled column. Purpose: a monotonic, repeatable radial expansion from one wave. Changes: none at interfaces; it is the one-convolution case written as a bow. Genus: load-converting geometry. Credibility: high. Closes: DA-3. Status: candidate.
4. **Helical slot.** Structure: a helical slot through the wall along part of the free length; axial compression unwinds the helix slightly and increases the diameter. Purpose: radial expansion with a built-in axial compliance. Changes: the slot is a flow route when open. Genus: load-converting geometry. Credibility: medium; the diameter change per unit travel is small and must be shown to reach the purge gap width. Closes: DA-3, DA-5. Status: candidate.

#### purge shoulder

Purpose (03 row 12, advantageous): the load-applying surface that meets the seat ring only in over-travel.

1. **Separate collar on the poppet.** Structure: a collar pressed, crimped or threaded onto the poppet below the sealing face. Purpose: as the purge shoulder; the over-travel distance can be set at assembly by the collar position. Changes: one more part; adjustable. Genus: load-applying surface. Credibility: high. Closes: DA-7. Status: candidate.
2. **Conical purge shoulder.** Structure: a cone rather than a flat annulus. Purpose: needed to spread a slotted sleeve, split seat ring or tapered seat ring (seat ring items 2–4). Changes: shape only. Genus: load-applying surface. Credibility: high. Closes: DA-3. Status: candidate.
3. **Load applied through the return spring.** Structure: a spring seat washer between the return spring and the poppet, which bears on the seat ring once the first spring stage is taken up. Purpose: the load path to the seat ring runs through the return bias, not a shoulder on the poppet. Changes: the washer, not the poppet, touches the seat ring. Genus: load-applying surface. Credibility: medium; depends on the core layout, which the disclosure does not dimension (01 Q15). Closes: DA-7. Status: candidate.

#### purge gap

Purpose (03 N0 item 2, N2): a secondary flow path beside the main passage, closed at rest and opened only in over-travel. 01 M2 records that an interference fit and an opening clearance on the same outer surface are not reconciled.

1. **Relieved core body bore.** Structure: the interference fit acts on a short band of the seat ring next to the locating shoulder; the core body bore is relieved (a counterbore or undercut) along the free portion, and the seat ring closes the purge gap at rest by line contact at one end of the relief. Purpose: the free portion expands into the relief and opens the purge gap while the fit band stays retained. Changes: one more machined diameter in the core body. Genus: purge gap bounded by the core body bore. Credibility: high as machining; it is one way to resolve M2, not the inventor's stated way. Closes: DA-5 (in part). Status: candidate; requires inventor confirmation (Q3).
2. **Purge gap bounded by the valve stem bore (§2 wording).** Structure: the seat ring sits at the end of the core body and expands against the valve stem bore. Purpose: as disclosed in §2. Changes: the valve stem, not the core body, is a boundary; removing the core body leaves the boundary in place. Genus: purge gap bounded by a bore surrounding the seat ring. Credibility: high geometrically; §2 and §3 item 6 disagree (01 A1). Closes: none. Status: candidate.
3. **Purge flow through the seat ring wall.** Structure: slots or perforations in the free portion of the wall, closed at rest because they lie against the bore or because their edges abut, and opened when the wall deforms. Purpose: the purge flow sweeps the inner and outer seat ring surfaces; the outer surface can be retained along its whole length. Changes: removes the fit/clearance conflict; the purge gap is no longer annular. Genus: secondary flow path through or past the radially deflectable element. Credibility: medium–high; closing a slot tightly at rest is the open point. Closes: DA-5. Status: candidate.
4. **Purge channel in the poppet.** Structure: an internal channel with ports that pass a seal and open only in over-travel. Purpose: an air purge with no deformable element. Changes: sweeps the poppet interior, not the sealing edge; no snap-back wipe. Genus: secondary flow path opened by over-travel. Credibility: medium; channels of a few tenths of a millimetre in the poppet can themselves fill with sealant. Closes: DA-14. Status: candidate; requires inventor confirmation (Q4).

Credibility check on the disclosed dimensions: 0.15 mm radial opening at a seat ring outer radius of 1.35 mm is about 11 % hoop strain if produced by hoop expansion alone, above the recoverable strain usually cited for superelastic NiTi alloy. Bending-dominated geometries (convolution flattening, fingers, struts) avoid this. Whether 0.15 mm is radial or diametral, and which mechanism produces it, is Q5.

#### shape recovery

Purpose (03 N0 item 3): the radially deflectable element returns to its at-rest shape and closes the purge gap when the pressure differential collapses.

1. **Polymer recovery (inventor's own, Alt-A, §6).** Structure: PEEK seat ring with the same convoluted wall. Purpose: closes the purge gap; weaker snap-back wipe. Changes: material; slower recovery. Genus: elastic recovery. Credibility: stated by the inventor; not built. Closes: DA-4, DA-13. Status: candidate.
2. **Spring-temper metal recovery.** Structure: a slotted sleeve or strut pattern in spring-tempered stainless steel or copper–beryllium, kept within its elastic limit by bending-dominated deflection. Purpose: elastic recovery without a superelastic material. Changes: needs the low-strain geometries above; a continuous convoluted ring in spring steel would likely yield. Genus: elastic recovery. Credibility: high for fingered forms, low for a continuous ring. Closes: DA-4. Status: candidate.
3. **Garter-spring-assisted recovery.** Structure: a circumferential coil spring around a slotted sleeve, split seat ring or polymer seat ring supplies the closing force. Purpose: recovery independent of the element's own stiffness, so a low-recovery material can be used. Changes: the garter spring sits in the purge gap region and must pass purge flow between its coils. Genus: recovery assisted by a biasing element. Credibility: medium; common in lip seals, unproven here. Closes: DA-16. Status: candidate.
4. **Elastomer recovery.** Structure: an elastomeric ring with a load-converting section. Purpose: elastic recovery. Changes: the inventor contrasts the invention with rubber seats that embed sealant (§2), so this sits against the stated rationale. Genus: elastic recovery. Credibility: medium mechanically; weak on the effect. Closes: DA-4. Status: candidate; requires inventor confirmation (Q6).

#### NiTi alloy

Purpose (03 N4): the material of the seat ring in the prototype, giving superelastic recovery at riding temperatures.

1. **Ternary superelastic NiTi alloys.** Structure: Ni–Ti–Cr or Ni–Ti–Cu tube in place of binary NiTi alloy. Purpose: superelastic recovery with a shifted transformation temperature (Cr) or narrower hysteresis (Cu). Changes: supplier and shape-setting parameters. Genus: superelastic alloy. Credibility: high; both are commercial superelastic grades. Closes: DA-4 (in part). Status: candidate.
2. **Copper-based shape-memory alloys.** Structure: Cu–Al–Ni or Cu–Zn–Al seat ring. Purpose: superelastic recovery at lower material cost. Changes: lower recoverable strain and poorer fatigue; ammonia compatibility of copper alloys is doubtful. Genus: superelastic alloy. Credibility: low–medium; flagged mainly for the ammonia point. Closes: DA-4. Status: candidate.
3. **Other high-modulus thermoplastics.** Structure: PPS or PEI seat ring in place of PEEK. Purpose: as Alt-A. Changes: chemical compatibility with ammonia-bearing sealant to be confirmed per polymer. Genus: polymer seat ring. Credibility: medium. Closes: DA-4. Status: candidate.

#### austenite finish temperature

Purpose (03 row 13): keeps the NiTi alloy superelastic at riding temperatures.

1. **Specification by martensite start temperature or by a service temperature margin.** Structure: the material is specified by its martensite start temperature below the lowest expected service temperature, or by Af a stated margin below it, instead of by Af below 5 °C. Purpose: the same property, stated in the terms that govern cold behaviour, including the CO₂ chill of §9 item 2. Changes: definition only; the value must come from the inventor. Genus: transformation temperature. Credibility: high as a definition; no value is offered here. Closes: none; supports 01 Q11. Status: candidate; requires inventor confirmation (Q7).
2. **Thermal variant (inventor's own, Alt-C, §6).** Structure: two-way trained NiTi alloy with Af of 35 °C to 40 °C, opened by warming. Purpose: a pump-free purge. Changes: no over-travel; outside N0. Genus: thermally actuated purge. Credibility: the inventor is unsure (§6). Closes: DA-19. Status: candidate.

#### snap-back wipe

Purpose (03 row 11, advantageous provisional): the second cleaning action on shape recovery. Which surfaces meet is not described (01 M5).

1. **Wiping lip on the seat ring.** Structure: a thin inward lip at the sealing edge that sweeps the sealing face as the seat ring recovers. Purpose: concentrates the wipe on a defined edge. Changes: one more feature in the laser-cut or shape-set profile. Genus: mechanical cleaning action. Credibility: medium; depends on the wipe mechanism the inventor describes. Closes: DA-13 (in part). Status: candidate; requires inventor confirmation (Q8).
2. **Wipe by the wiper ring (inventor's own, Alt-B, §6).** Structure and purpose: the wiper ring of the two-piece seat wipes on return. Changes: the wipe acts below the rigid seat, not across the sealing face. Genus: mechanical cleaning action. Credibility: stated by the inventor. Closes: DA-8. Status: candidate.

#### locating shoulder

Purpose (03 row 15, conventional, but tied to the purge gap conflict): axial location and retention of the seat ring.

1. **Axial clamping instead of an interference fit.** Structure: the seat ring is clamped between the locating shoulder and a pressed or threaded retainer sleeve, or held by staking the core body rim over its end. Purpose: retention without radial preload on the free portion, which removes the fit/clearance conflict of 01 M2. Changes: one more part (retainer sleeve) or one more operation (staking). Genus: retention of the seat ring against the locating shoulder. Credibility: high; welding NiTi alloy to brass is not credible and is left out. Closes: DA-5 (in part). Status: candidate.

#### tyre valve

Purpose (03 rows 16–17, conventional/exemplary): the valve pattern carrying the valve core.

1. **Schrader-pattern valve core.** Structure: seat ring, purge shoulder and two-rate return spring scaled into a Schrader-pattern core body. Purpose: the same over-travel purge in the other common tyre valve pattern. Changes: dimensions and the actuating geometry of the core. Genus: tyre valve. Credibility: medium–high mechanically; the disclosure mentions no other pattern (01 M13). Closes: DA-11. Status: candidate; requires inventor confirmation (Q9).
2. **Valve core integral with the valve stem.** Structure: core body formed in the valve stem, not threaded into it. Purpose: as disclosed, without a removable valve core. Changes: no core removal for service. Genus: valve core. Credibility: high. Closes: DA-11. Status: candidate.

#### laser cutting and shape-setting

Purpose (03 row 19, exemplary): making the seat ring.

1. **Machined polymer seat ring.** Structure: PEEK seat ring turned from rod. Purpose: makes Alt-A without tooling. Changes: a 0.20 mm wall turned in polymer needs support during cutting. Genus: seat ring manufacture. Credibility: medium–high. Closes: DA-4. Status: candidate.
2. **Formed metal sleeve.** Structure: a slotted sleeve stamped, deep-drawn or rolled from spring-temper strip. Purpose: a low-cost radially deflectable element. Changes: no shape-setting. Genus: seat ring manufacture. Credibility: high for strip-formed parts of this size. Closes: DA-3, DA-4. Status: candidate.
3. **Alternatives to electropolishing.** Structure: chemical etching or abrasive-flow finishing to remove the oxide and heat-affected edge. Purpose: the stated purpose of electropolishing (§7). Changes: finish quality; whether finish matters to the effect is 01 Q16. Genus: surface finishing. Credibility: high. Closes: none. Status: candidate.

## Rationale

Features were taken from 03's essential and advantageous rows; conventional rows were included only where they bear on an unresolved essential point (the locating shoulder on the purge gap conflict, the tyre valve pattern on DA-11). Manufacturing is included because the prompt asks for it and because two structural alternatives (slotted sleeve, formed sleeve) depend on a process the disclosure does not describe.

Left out as not credible: reverse-direction purge on deflation (DA-18), which would draw sealant through the purge gap and out; plastic-deforming elements reset mechanically (DA-16), except the garter-spring form; welding NiTi alloy to brass; a thin brass convoluted wall integral with the core body, which would yield.

The 11 % hoop-strain arithmetic is included because it changes which alternatives are credible: it favours bending-dominated geometries and makes spring steel viable only in fingered forms. It is arithmetic on disclosed numbers, not a test result, and is put to the inventor.

Where the inventor's own alternatives (Alt-A, Alt-B, Alt-C) already cover a component, they are listed first and marked, so Gate 2 can confirm them quickly.

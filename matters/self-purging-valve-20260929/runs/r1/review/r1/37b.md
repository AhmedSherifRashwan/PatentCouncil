### F-37-1 [high] [formal] Multiple dependent claims depend, directly or indirectly, on other multiple dependent claims

Claim 11 ("any one of claims 5 to 10") is a valid multiple dependent claim (MDC): none of claims 5–10 is itself an MDC. From claim 13 onward the pattern breaks down. Claim 13 recites "The valve core of any one of claims 1 and 5 to 12" — that range includes claim 11, itself an MDC. Claim 15 repeats the same range. Claim 16 recites "any one of claims 1 and 5 to 15," pulling in claims 11, 13 and 15, all MDCs. Claim 18 ("claim 16 or claim 17") cites claim 16, itself an MDC. Claim 19 ("any one of claims 16 to 18") compounds it further. Claim 20 ("any one of claims 1 and 5 to 19") inherits the whole chain. The same pattern recurs at claims 25, 30, 33, 35–38, 43, 44, 46–50, 54–60, 65 ("any one of claims 3 and 62 to 64"), 67, 68 ("any one of claims 3 and 62 to 67," which includes MDC 65 and 67), 69, 74–77, 84 and 86.

Under 37 CFR 1.75(c) and MPEP 608.01(n), "a claim in multiple dependent form shall not serve as a basis for any other multiple dependent claim, either directly or indirectly." As drafted, essentially the entire dependent-claim tree from claim 13 onward violates this rule for a US filing. This does not affect claim validity on the merits — an examiner will object to claim form and require amendment, with USPTO fee calculation recomputed on the corrected structure — but it blocks acceptance of the claim set as filed in the US and requires renumbering that touches most of claims 13–86. EPO and UK practice do not carry the same prohibition, so this is jurisdiction-specific (see the companion Prompt 40a report).

Least narrowing action: restructure the dependency tree so that every MDC's range contains only singly-dependent claims — for example, by converting the offending "any one of claims 1 and 5 to N" ranges into a set of parallel singly-dependent claims reciting the added limitation against each valid base, or by re-ordering the claim set so species-adding claims (13, 15, 16...) do not themselves need to be multiply dependent. This is a renumbering exercise, not a scope change, and does not require new disclosure.

### F-37-2 [high] [support] Claim 9's alternatives omit the feature the specification says produces the required two-rate characteristic

Claim 9 depends from claim 6, which requires "a two-rate characteristic, the two-rate characteristic having a first spring stage (52), which the crack pressure overcomes, and a second spring stage (54), which the over-travel differential overcomes." Claim 9 reads: "wherein the return bias comprises a soft spring and a stiff spring arranged in series, or comprises nested springs that act in parallel."

The description's own account of each alternative gives it a feature that claim 9 drops. [0144]: "the return bias comprises a soft spring and a stiff spring arranged in series, with a stop that takes the soft spring out of the load path at the end of the normal stroke." Two springs in series without a stop present a single combined (softer) rate for the whole travel, not two rates — the stop is what removes the soft spring from the load path so the stiff spring alone sets the second stage. [0145]: "the return bias comprises nested springs that act in parallel with staggered engagement. An outer spring acts over the whole travel... A shorter inner spring engages only at the end of the normal stroke." Two nested springs acting in parallel without staggered engagement likewise present a single, constant combined rate throughout, not two rates — staggered engagement is what makes the inner spring's contribution begin only at the boundary.

As literally claimed, neither alternative in claim 9 is disclosed as capable of delivering the two-rate characteristic claim 6 requires; both descriptions in the specification tie the two-rate result to a feature ("with a stop..."; "with staggered engagement") that claim 9 does not recite. This is an unsupported/enablement gap inherited from a broader claim, not a drafting nicety.

Least narrowing action: reinsert the enabling language already in the description — "a soft spring and a stiff spring arranged in series, with a stop that takes the soft spring out of the load path at the end of the normal stroke, or nested springs that act in parallel with staggered engagement." No new disclosure is required; the text already exists at [0144]–[0145].

### F-37-3 [high] [support] Claim 1's numeral 146 has no antecedent anywhere in the specification text

Claim 1 recites "the radial expansion opening a purge gap (60, 146)." Numeral 60 is introduced and used extensively in the description (e.g. [0124]–[0126]). Numeral 146 is registered in the package's figure instructions ("registry.md," Reference-Numeral Registry, entry 146: purge gap, first figure FIG. 9) and appears in the figure-drawing instruction for FIG. 9A/9B ("Draw the purge gap (146) as a closed clearance between the wiper ring (144) and the core body bore (22)"), but it does not appear anywhere in description.md's prose. The two-piece-seat paragraphs ([0181]–[0184]) describe the rigid seat (140), sealing edge (142) and wiper ring (144) in detail, and state that the purge shoulder "deflects it outward" and that the wiper ring "wipes on its return," but never name or number the gap that opens between the wiper ring and the core body bore. A claim term's reference numeral, standing alone in the broadest independent claim with no corresponding description passage, is a textbook unsupported-claim-term objection: an examiner or opponent reading claim 1 against the specification will find nothing that defines what "146" refers to.

Least narrowing action: add the numeral to the existing two-piece-seat description passage — for example, appending to [0182] a sentence such as "...and deflects it outward, so that a purge gap 146, closed at rest, opens between the wiper ring 144 and the core body bore 22." The concept is already disclosed; only the numeral is missing from the text.

### F-37-4 [medium] [formal] Claim cross-references embedded in the claim body rather than in the dependency clause

Claims 47, 77 and 86 are drafted in preamble form ("A tyre valve (1) comprising a valve stem (10) and the valve core (5) of any one of claims 1 and 5 to 44..."; "A valve core (5) comprising a core body (20)... and the seat ring (30) of any one of claims 3 and 62 to 76..."; "The method of claim 4, wherein the seat ring is the seat ring of any one of claims 62 to 69") rather than as a standard dependent-claim preamble ("The tyre valve of any one of claims..."). Any claim that refers to another claim for limitations is, by definition, a dependent claim regardless of its surface phrasing; drafting it as if independent while incorporating a multiple-claim range in the body obscures the claim's true category and its multiple-dependency status for a reader and for USPTO fee calculation.

Claim 61 is the most serious instance: "The method of claim 2, wherein the valve core is the valve core of any one of claims 1 and 5 to 46." The dependency clause names only claim 2 (a method claim), but the body incorporates the entire valve-core claim tree (claims 1, 5–46) — including every one of the multiple-dependent claims identified in F-37-1 — as an alternative definition of "the valve core" inside a "wherein" clause. This both compounds the multiple-dependency defect and creates a claim whose actual scope (method of claim 2, combined with any of ~40 alternative valve-core claims) is not apparent from its own dependency statement.

Least narrowing action: rewrite each as a standard dependent claim naming the full base range in the introductory clause (e.g. "The tyre valve of any one of claims 1 and 5 to 44, comprising a valve stem..."), and for claim 61, restructure as a proper multiple dependent claim on the combined ranges rather than embedding the cross-reference in a "wherein."

### F-37-5 [medium] [formal] "Load-applying surface" and "radially deflectable element" risk means-plus-function construction

Claim 1's two broadest structural recitations — "a radially deflectable element" and "a load-applying surface that is moved by the poppet" — are defined in the specification exclusively by the function performed. [0102]: "'load-applying surface' means a surface that is moved by the poppet, that is spaced from the radially deflectable element throughout the normal stroke, and that bears on the radially deflectable element in the over-travel." [0099]: "'radially deflectable element' means an element of the valve core that is deflected radially by the axial load... and that undergoes shape recovery when the axial load is removed." [0106] states expressly that a function-defined term "covers each structure described herein that performs the function... and is not limited to the preferred structure." Under Williamson v. Citrix and its progeny, a generic placeholder ("surface," "element") coupled with purely functional recitation, without further structural qualification, is open to a competitor or examiner argument for 35 U.S.C. 112(f) construction, which would confine claim 1 to the disclosed corresponding structures (purge shoulder, collar, spring-seat washer; seat ring, polymer seat ring, wiper ring, slotted sleeve, split seat ring, tapered seat ring) and their statutory equivalents rather than the full functional genus the applicant intends. This is precisely the kind of construction trap litigation counsel would raise at claim construction, not an examiner clarity rejection, and it bears on the breadth of the broadest claim in the set.

Least narrowing action: none of the disclosed structural relationships needs to be lost; the claim already recites that the load-applying surface "is moved by the poppet" and is "spaced from"/"bears on" the radially deflectable element, which is some structural context. A defensive amendment would add minimal further structural anchoring already used elsewhere in the specification (for example, tying the load-applying surface to an axial-load-transmitting surface of the poppet) without narrowing to a single species. This is presented as a construction risk for counsel to weigh, not asserted as a certainty.

### F-37-6 [medium] [formal] Numeral scope is inconsistent between claim 1 and claim 2

Claim 1 recites "a sealing edge (32, 132, 142)" and "a purge gap (60, 146)" — reference signs spanning three (respectively two) structurally different embodiments without "or." Claim 2, the corresponding method claim, recites only "a sealing edge (32)" in its preamble and "a purge gap (60)" / "the purge gap" in its body — the single principal-embodiment numerals only. Claim 61 then states "the valve core is the valve core of any one of claims 1 and 5 to 46," which brings the polymer, two-piece-seat-adjacent and other species numerals (132, 142, 146, 130, 140, 144, 170, 180, 190...) into combination with claim 2's method steps despite claim 2's preamble reciting only the narrower numerals (32, 60). A reader cannot tell from claim 2 alone whether its "sealing edge (32)" is meant generically (as in claim 1) or specifically to the principal embodiment.

Least narrowing action: align claim 2's reference numerals with claim 1's generic set, or state expressly (as claim 1 does) that the numerals are illustrative of alternative species.

### F-37-7 [low] [support] Claim 47 excludes the disclosed valve-stem-bore purge gap species

Claim 47 ("A tyre valve (1) comprising a valve stem (10) and the valve core (5) of any one of claims 1 and 5 to 44...") does not include claim 45 ("The valve core of claim 16, wherein the bore is a bore of a valve stem (10) in which the valve core is fitted") in its base range, nor claim 46. Claim 45 corresponds to a disclosed species at [0125] and [0189] — the purge gap bounded by the valve stem bore rather than the core body bore. Because claim 47 is the only route by which a valve stem is positively recited together with the valve core, and its range stops at claim 44, the valve-stem-bore purge-gap embodiment cannot be reached through the tyre valve / tyre chamber claim family (claims 47–50), even though it is disclosed and separately claimed at the valve-core level.

Least narrowing action: extend claim 47's range to include claim 45 (and, if intended, claim 46), or add a claim in the 47–50 family reciting the valve-stem-bore species directly.

### F-37-8 [low] [formal] Relative term "about" in claim 85

Claim 85: "wherein the interference fit is about 0.03 mm on diameter." "About" is defined at [0056] as covering "the ordinary manufacturing tolerance and measurement tolerance for the parameter concerned," which supplies a construction anchor, but the claim itself states no numeric tolerance and is open to a 35 U.S.C. 112(b) clarity objection on that basis alone, independent of the specification's definition.

Least narrowing action: none required for filing; flagged for counsel's judgement on whether to state a numeric tolerance (e.g. "0.03 mm ± 0.01 mm on diameter") to remove the ambiguity outright.

## Assumptions

A1. "Radially deflectable element" and "load-applying surface" are read as intended to be genus/functional claim terms (consistent with their treatment at [0099]–[0106]), not as inadvertent placeholders; F-37-5 is raised as a construction risk on that basis, not as an assertion that the drafters intended narrow means-plus-function claiming.

A2. The multiple-dependency analysis (F-37-1) applies 37 CFR 1.75(c) and MPEP 608.01(n) as common general knowledge of US claim-drafting practice; no US-practice reference was supplied in the package.

A3. Claim 61's incorporation of "any one of claims 1 and 5 to 46" is read as intended to make the cleaning method claim combinable with every disclosed valve-core species, consistent with the breadth used elsewhere in the claim set.

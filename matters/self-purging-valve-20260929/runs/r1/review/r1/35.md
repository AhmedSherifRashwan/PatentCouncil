Claims 47 (tyre valve) and 77 (valve core with the claim 3 ring) are product claims in dependent form. They take claim 1's and claim 3's profiles, with detectability unchanged.

**Claim 1 (54).** "a load-applying surface that is moved by the poppet … spaced from the radially deflectable element throughout the normal stroke … in the over-travel, the load-applying surface bears on the radially deflectable element and applies an axial load that produces a radial expansion … opening a purge gap … distinct from the main passage". This is the product as sold: a replacement valve core. That gives the high CR, and BR is high because the claim is not limited to material, seat-ring form, valve pattern or return-bias construction.

- NR 7: no saved reference combines two-stage poppet travel with a deformable element opening a second path. The nearest single references are US4044791A (#2: floating seat, secondary passageways) and US5924444A (#15: ring deformed radially to open a gap).
- IR 5: an obviousness case can be assembled from staged travel (#2, #8), a ring that opens a gap (#15), air cleaning of a seat (#3, #10) and the tyre-sealant problem (#6, #7).
- DR 5: the package itself teaches two literal escapes, the axial disc-spring element of [0179]–[0180] and contact from lift-off defeating the spacing clause ([0071]). Both are reported as findings F-33-1 and F-33-2 in Prompt 33 and are not repeated here.
- ID 7 and EE 6: every element can be established by teardown and a pressure–travel bench on a bought core, but the spacing and the separate flow path need sectioning or CT at stroke.
- SS 7: the principal embodiment is built and tested ([0279]–[0289]), but the genus "radially deflectable element" rests partly on untested forms ([0184], [0202]).

**Claim 2 (42).** "moving the poppet through an over-travel (116) beyond the normal stroke … passing air through the open purge gap into the tyre chamber". The claim is as broad as claim 1 in substance, but the cyclist performs it, so the maker is reachable only as an indirect infringer. Proof also needs evidence that users reach the over-travel differential ([0273]), hence ID 4 and EE 3. The enforcement weakness is reported as F-33-3 in Prompt 33.

**Claim 3 (48).** "a wall having a load-converting geometry; wherein an axial load, applied to the seat ring by a load-applying surface that is moved by the poppet in an over-travel (116) … produces … a radial expansion of the wall". The ring is the heart of the product and is sold as a spare ([0162]), so CR 7. ID 8 because the ring itself is the evidence.

- NR 5 and IR 4: at the EPO the poppet and over-travel clauses are likely to be read as "suitable for". The claim then reduces to a tubular ring with a sealing edge and a geometry that expands radially under axial load and recovers. That sits close to EP3171056A1 (#4: SMA ring with a wave section), US20040194970A1 (#13: folded seal expanded radially) and US11549591B1 (#17: undulating tubular metal seat engaged by a poppet).
- DR 5: the functional term "load-converting geometry" is at risk of US § 112(f) construction, as F-33-4 of Prompt 33 reports.

**Claim 4 (29).** "shape-setting (512) the seat ring in a fixture that holds the load-converting geometry". It is limited to NiTi and to a single conventional operation (the saved US9126251B2, #18, and US9611937B2, #11, show NiTi tube shaping and heat treatment as standard), so BR 3 and IR 3. The claim is carried out in a factory, probably abroad, and leaves no reliable signature in the product, hence ID 2 and EE 2. SS 8 because the process is fully described with values ([0252]–[0259]). Its commercial value is as a product-by-process route in the EPO and UK (Art. 64(2) EPC; UK s60(1)(c)) and on import into the US (35 U.S.C. § 271(g)), all of which still need proof of the process.

Which claims protect the commercially successful architecture: claim 1, and claim 3 through its structural dependents (62–69, 71–73). Claims 2 and 4 are supplementary. The design-around and detectability findings on claims 1 to 3 were raised in Prompt 33. The one new low-score finding is on claim 4.

### F-35-1 [low] [design-around] The manufacturing method claim is performed out of sight and avoided by a different process

Claim 4: "A method of manufacturing the seat ring (30) of claim 3, the seat ring being of a NiTi alloy, the method comprising shape-setting (512) the seat ring in a fixture that holds the load-converting geometry."

Attack. A competitor can avoid the claim in three ways: cold-form the convolution without a shape-setting fixture, use a polymer ring ([0207]), or use a slotted sleeve stamped from spring-temper strip ([0262]). If the competitor does shape-set, it does so at a supplier's plant, and the finished ring carries no reliable evidence of the step. Discovery or a product-by-process presumption is the only route. Design-around resistance 3; detectability 2.

Least narrowing action. No amendment. The claim costs little to keep, but counsel should not rely on it for enforcement. The protective weight belongs on claim 3's structural dependents (62–69, 71–73). No new disclosure. Target P5 only if counsel wants to drop or restructure the claim.

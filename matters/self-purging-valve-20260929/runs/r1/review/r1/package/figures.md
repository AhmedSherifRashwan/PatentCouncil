### Figure Plan

#### FIG. 1

View: axial cross-section, system overview, main embodiment, valve at rest.
Shows: the tyre valve assembly (valve stem carrying a valve core) with the poppet seated on the sealing edge of the seat ring, the purge gap closed, and a pump head shown schematically (broken outline) approaching the valve stem tip.
Embodiment depicted: the main embodiment as built and bench-tested — superelastic NiTi seat ring with a convoluted wall, brass core body, brass poppet, two-rate stainless return spring, lock nut. Corresponds to the inventor's sketch `FIG-1.png`.
Components to be numbered (locked terms): tyre valve; valve stem; core body; core body bore; locating shoulder; main passage; seat ring; sealing edge; convoluted wall; poppet; sealing face; purge shoulder; return spring; first spring stage; second spring stage; purge gap; lock nut; tyre chamber; pump head.
Supports: the base structural combination of the independent valve-core claim (core body, seat ring, poppet, return spring) and the closed-at-rest limitation of the purge gap.

#### FIG. 2

View: axial cross-section, same section as FIG. 1, main embodiment, valve in over-travel.
Shows: the purge shoulder bearing on the seat ring, the seat ring expanded radially, the purge gap open, and arrows showing air flow through the main passage and through the purge gap into the tyre chamber; the pump head shown engaged.
Embodiment depicted: main embodiment. Corresponds to the inventor's sketch `FIG-2.png`.
Components to be numbered: as FIG. 1, plus flow-path arrows (unnumbered graphic elements, not components).
Supports: the over-travel limitation, the radial-expansion limitation, and the air-purge cleaning-action limitation of the independent claim.

#### FIG. 3

View: exploded perspective, main embodiment.
Shows: the core body, seat ring, poppet, return spring and lock nut in axial alignment, separated along the valve axis.
Embodiment depicted: main embodiment.
Components to be numbered: core body; seat ring; poppet; return spring; lock nut.
Supports: the "assembly of core body, seat ring, poppet and return spring" antecedent for the valve core, and enablement of the assembly sequence.

#### FIG. 4

View: enlarged detail cross-sections of the seat ring alone; two panels, FIG. 4A (wall with one convolution) and FIG. 4B (wall with two convolutions).
Shows: the sealing edge, the convoluted wall, the convolution(s), an axial region of the outer surface adjacent to the locating shoulder over which the interference fit acts, and the remaining free portion of the wall that is unconstrained and expands radially to open the purge gap. Wall thickness, free length and convolution amplitude are shown as dimension lines without separate reference numerals.
Embodiment depicted: main embodiment, both disclosed convolution-count species.
Components to be numbered: seat ring; sealing edge; convoluted wall; convolution.
Supports: dependent-claim fallbacks on convolution count (one vs two) and on the dimensional ranges; gives a concrete, drawable resolution of how an interference fit and a radially opening clearance coexist on the same outer surface, without asserting this is the only possible resolution.

#### FIG. 5

View: schematic force–travel diagram (not a mechanical section).
Shows: poppet travel on the horizontal axis and force on the vertical axis; a lower-slope segment over the normal stroke (first spring stage) rising to the crack pressure; a steeper-slope segment over over-travel (second spring stage) rising to the over-travel differential; the normal-stroke and over-travel ranges marked on the travel axis.
Embodiment depicted: main embodiment; deliberately drawn without specifying how the two-rate characteristic is physically produced, because the disclosure describes only "a compression spring … with a two-rate characteristic" and not its internal construction.
Components/features to be numbered: travel axis; force axis; first spring stage (as a plotted segment); second spring stage (as a plotted segment); crack pressure; over-travel differential; normal stroke; over-travel.
Supports: the two-rate-characteristic and threshold-pressure limitations, in a form that does not tie the claim to one particular spring construction.

#### FIG. 6

View: four-panel process-flow diagram, use sequence.
Shows: (a) at rest — sealing face seated, purge gap closed; (b) normal stroke — pressure differential above the crack pressure, poppet lifted, purge gap still closed; (c) over-travel — pressure differential above the over-travel differential, purge shoulder on the seat ring, purge gap open, air purge; (d) return — pressure differential collapsed, poppet returned, seat ring recovering, snap-back wipe, purge gap closing.
Embodiment depicted: main embodiment.
Components to be numbered: reuses the FIG. 1/FIG. 2 component numerals in each panel; the four panels themselves are numbered as method steps.
Supports: the steps of a method claim directed to purging sealant from the valve core over an inflation cycle.

#### FIG. 7

View: process-flow diagram, manufacturing sequence.
Shows: laser cutting of the seat ring from NiTi tube stock on a mandrel; shape-setting in a fixture; water-quenching; electropolishing; press-fitting the seat ring into the core body against the locating shoulder.
Embodiment depicted: main embodiment manufacturing route as disclosed.
Components/steps to be numbered: the five process steps.
Supports: enablement of the manufacturing embodiment and any manufacturing-method claim or dependent process limitation.

#### FIG. 8

View: axial cross-section, alternative embodiment A.
Shows: the same valve core architecture as FIG. 1 with the seat ring replaced by a polymer seat ring of the same convoluted-wall geometry.
Embodiment depicted: the inventor's disclosed polymer (PEEK) alternative; not built or bench-tested.
Components to be numbered: polymer seat ring, with its own sealing edge and convoluted wall; other components reuse the FIG. 1 numerals.
Supports: genus claim language directed to a seat ring having radial compliance and shape recovery generally, of which superelastic NiTi and the polymer are disclosed species; a fallback position if a claim limited to superelastic recovery is narrowed.

#### FIG. 9

View: axial cross-section, alternative embodiment B; two panels, FIG. 9A (at rest) and FIG. 9B (in over-travel).
Shows: a rigid seat carrying the sealing edge, with a separate wiper ring below it that the purge shoulder deflects outward in over-travel to open a purge path and that wipes on return.
Embodiment depicted: the inventor's disclosed two-piece-seat alternative; not built or bench-tested.
Components to be numbered: rigid seat; wiper ring; purge path.
Supports: genus claim language in which the sealing function and the purge/wipe function are provided by separate elements, as an alternative structure to the one-piece seat ring.

#### FIG. 10

View: schematic axial cross-section, alternative embodiment C.
Shows: a two-way-trained seat ring that opens the purge gap on warming, without poppet over-travel; the poppet and return spring are shown unchanged and not displaced.
Embodiment depicted: the inventor's disclosed thermal variant; discussed only, no data, and the inventor states uncertainty about pursuing it.
Components to be numbered: thermally actuated seat ring; purge gap.
Supports: a separate, thermally triggered claim family distinct from the pressure/over-travel family, retained as disclosed subject matter pending a decision on whether to pursue it as a claimed embodiment.

#### FIG. 11

View: axial cross-sections, candidate alternative seat-ring wall geometries; three panels, FIG. 11A (slotted sleeve with axial fingers spread by a conical purge shoulder), FIG. 11B (split ring opened circumferentially by a conical portion of the poppet), FIG. 11C (tapered seat ring wedged outward by a matching taper on the poppet).
Shows: each geometry converting the poppet's axial load into radial expansion of the seat ring by a mechanism other than wall convolution.
Embodiment depicted: candidate alternative constructions identified during design-around analysis; not built, not tested, and not stated in the inventor's disclosure. Shown in prophetic/conditional form only, pending inventor confirmation.
Components to be numbered: slotted sleeve and its fingers; split ring and its axial split; tapered seat ring and the matching poppet taper.
Supports: genus claim language describing the seat ring's radial-expansion mechanism functionally, rather than limited to a convoluted wall, so that this class of substitution does not fall outside the claims if confirmed at a later gate.

#### FIG. 12

View: axial cross-sections, candidate alternative purge-gap locations; two panels, FIG. 12A (purge gap formed by a relief in the core body bore, with the seat ring retained by an interference fit only over the fit band adjacent to the locating shoulder) and FIG. 12B (purge flow through slots in the seat ring wall, closed at rest, opened when the wall deforms).
Embodiment depicted: candidate alternative constructions identified during design-around analysis; not built, not tested, and not stated in the inventor's disclosure. Shown in prophetic/conditional form only, pending inventor confirmation.
Components to be numbered: core body bore relief; wall slots.
Supports: genus claim language for the purge gap's location and form, and a drawable proposal for how the same seat ring can be interference-fitted over part of its length and free to expand over the rest.

#### FIG. 13

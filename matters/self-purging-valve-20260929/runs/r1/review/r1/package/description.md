#### Title: valve core for a tyre valve, seat ring, and methods of use and manufacture

#### Technical field

[0001] The present disclosure relates to tyre valves. It relates in particular to a valve core for a tyre valve that admits air into a tyre chamber containing sealant, to a tyre valve and a tyre chamber fitted with such a valve core, to a seat ring for such a valve core, to a method of cleaning a tyre valve during an inflation cycle, and to methods of making the seat ring and of assembling the valve core. The tyre chamber of a bicycle is described by way of example.

#### Background

[0002] A liquid sealant is commonly introduced into the tyre chamber of a bicycle, whether a bicycle inner tube or a tubeless tyre and rim cavity, to seal punctures. The sealant is typically a latex emulsion containing ammonia and suspended particles, and it is introduced through the valve stem of the tyre valve. On each inflation a small quantity of sealant is drawn into the valve core by the pressure surge and by capillary action along the stem of the poppet. Between rides the sealant dries, and a sealant film forms on surfaces of the valve core.

[0003] After about ten to twenty inflation cycles the seat of a conventional valve core is coated with cured sealant. The tyre valve then either leaks slowly, because the poppet no longer seats, or cannot be opened by the pump. In the bench test reported under "Examples and data", three conventional valve cores reached their first failure after twelve, fourteen and eighteen inflation cycles respectively, two because the pump could not open them and one by loss of pressure. The usual remedy is manual: the valve core is removed with a tool and is scraped or replaced, and sealant is introduced again.

[0004] US11179979B2 describes a valve stem system in which a first valve is arranged at the end of the valve stem body that lies in the volume enclosed by the tyre and the rim. The first valve is described as a self-sealing one-way valve that is operable although a sealant is present, and the document states that the first valve usually prevents sealant from entering the valve stem.

[0005] US11148485B2 describes a valve stem assembly in which a plunger of the valve may be used as a tool against clogging when sealant accumulates inside the body of the valve stem.

[0006] US20210323363A1 states that valves used with tubeless bicycle tyres are often clogged by sealant due to their small diameter. It describes a valve having a larger internal diameter than typical bicycle valves, which is said to result in higher air flow and reduced clogging.

[0007] US2019/0123456A1 describes a valve core having a bore that is enlarged to reduce capillary rise of sealant along the stem of the poppet, a sealing face coated with a fluoropolymer layer that is described as reducing adhesion of cured sealant, a rigid seat, and a removable insert that captures deposits of sealant and can be pulled out and cleaned or replaced without removal of the whole valve core.

[0008] Valves in which further travel of a poppet opens a further flow path are described in other fields. US5918628A describes a check valve having a spring-biased poppet with a first stage opening and second stage openings spaced axially along the poppet. Flow through the second stage openings is restricted when the poppet first moves from its closed position, and increases after a predetermined axial movement of the poppet. US4044791A describes a valve assembly having a floating valve seat that is yieldably biased into engagement with a poppet. After the floating valve seat meets an abutment, the fluid unseats the poppet from the floating valve seat and flows through secondary passageways in the poppet.

[0009] Valves in which a flow of fluid cleans a seat, or deforms a ring, are also described in other fields. US3570510A describes a valve device in which a clean fluid such as gas or air is supplied to the valve seat from a circular space through an annular slit defined between two concentric seat rings, so as to keep the valve seat clean. US3595265A describes a check valve having a groove in the valve face that acts as an orifice when the valve is closed; when the valve opens, the stream of fluid flowing past the valve face and the groove flushes away foreign particles lodged there. US5924444A describes a valve in which, in the fully opened position, fluid leaving an orifice radially deforms an elastomeric ring outwardly and forms a gap between a side wall and the elastomeric ring, the gap enhancing the egress of fluid, and the elastomeric ring returns to its non-deformed shape as the valve closes.

#### Technical problem

[0010] The technical problem addressed is the removal, on each inflation cycle and without a tool, of a sealant film that has already formed on surfaces of the valve core.

[0011] Air that enters the tyre chamber through the main passage flows between the sealing face of the poppet and the sealing edge. A longer travel of the poppet along the main passage, or a main passage of larger section, enlarges that same flow path. The stream of air remains within the main passage, and a surface of the valve core that lies outside the main passage, for example an outer surface of the seat ring, is not brought into the stream by either measure.

[0012] In the conventional valve cores of the bench test reported under "Examples and data", the air admitted on every inflation cycle passed through the main passage, and those valve cores nevertheless reached their first failure within eighteen inflation cycles.

[0013] The valve core described herein has a purge gap, which is a secondary flow path distinct from the main passage, and which is opened by a load that the poppet applies to a radially deflectable element when the poppet travels beyond its normal stroke. In the documents referred to above, further travel of a poppet increases the flow through the valve, and the gap formed at the elastomeric ring enhances the egress of fluid. The purge gap described herein instead directs air, on its way into the tyre chamber, across a surface of the valve core on which sealant film accumulates.

#### Summary

[0014] According to a first aspect there is provided a valve core for a tyre valve, the valve core comprising a core body, a radially deflectable element, and a poppet that is movable along an axis of the valve core. The poppet has a sealing face that closes against a sealing edge at rest. The poppet is movable away from the sealing edge through a normal stroke, in which a main passage is open between the sealing face and the sealing edge, and is movable beyond the normal stroke through an over-travel. A load-applying surface that is moved by the poppet is spaced from the radially deflectable element throughout the normal stroke and bears on the radially deflectable element in the over-travel. The axial load so applied produces a radial deflection of the radially deflectable element, and the radial deflection opens a purge gap. The purge gap is a secondary flow path that is distinct from the main passage and that is closed at rest and throughout the normal stroke. When the axial load is removed the radially deflectable element undergoes shape recovery and the purge gap closes.

[0015] Two features of the first aspect act together. The first is that the normal stroke and the over-travel are two travel ranges of the same poppet, and the load-applying surface bears on the radially deflectable element only in the second of them, so that the radially deflectable element is not deflected by the load-applying surface during ordinary opening of the tyre valve. The second is that the purge gap is a flow path of its own. The purge gap does not run between the sealing face and the sealing edge, and a main passage that is enlarged, by whatever cause, is not a purge gap.

[0016] The purge gap is opened by the mechanical load of the poppet. The radial deflection is produced by the load-applying surface bearing on the radially deflectable element, and not by a flow of fluid acting on the radially deflectable element. When the purge gap is open, air at pump pressure flows through the purge gap, across a surface of the valve core on which sealant film accumulates, and into the tyre chamber. This flow is the air purge.

[0017] In embodiments the radially deflectable element is a seat ring that carries the sealing edge, the load-applying surface is a purge shoulder on the poppet, the radial deflection is a radial expansion of the seat ring, and the purge gap is bounded by an outer surface of the seat ring and by a bore surrounding the seat ring.

[0018] According to a second aspect there is provided a tyre valve comprising a valve stem and a valve core according to the first aspect. In some embodiments the valve core is a removable threaded valve core received in the valve stem. There is also provided a tyre chamber fitted with the tyre valve, for example a bicycle inner tube or a tubeless tyre and rim cavity, which in use contains sealant.

[0019] In some embodiments of the second aspect the valve core is integral with the valve stem.

[0020] According to a third aspect there is provided a seat ring for a valve core of a tyre valve. The seat ring is tubular and comprises a sealing edge, on which a sealing face of a poppet of the valve core seats, and a wall having a load-converting geometry. The load-converting geometry is such that an axial load, applied to the seat ring by a load-applying surface that is moved by the poppet in an over-travel of the poppet beyond a normal stroke, produces a radial expansion of the wall that opens a purge gap distinct from a main passage of the valve core. The seat ring undergoes shape recovery, which closes the purge gap, when the axial load is removed. The seat ring may be supplied as a replacement part for a valve core, and the valve core may be supplied as a replacement part for a tyre valve.

[0021] According to a fourth aspect there is provided a method of cleaning a tyre valve during an inflation cycle, the tyre valve having a valve core according to the first aspect and being fitted to a tyre chamber that contains sealant. The method comprises coupling a pump head to the tyre valve; applying with the pump head a pressure differential that moves the poppet through the normal stroke; moving the poppet into the over-travel, so that the load-applying surface bears on the radially deflectable element and the purge gap opens; passing air through the open purge gap into the tyre chamber; and removing the pump head, whereupon the pressure differential collapses, the poppet returns, the radially deflectable element undergoes shape recovery and the purge gap closes. The method is performed with the valve core in place in the tyre valve.

[0022] According to a fifth aspect there is provided a method of making a seat ring according to the third aspect, the method comprising laser cutting a tube of NiTi alloy, shape-setting the cut tube in a fixture that holds the load-converting geometry, and quenching. In some embodiments the method further comprises electropolishing. There is also provided a method of assembling a valve core, comprising press-fitting the seat ring into a core body, against a locating shoulder of the core body, with an interference fit.

[0023] In some embodiments the valve core comprises a return bias that urges the poppet towards the sealing edge and that has a two-rate characteristic, with a first spring stage that is overcome at a crack pressure and a second spring stage that is overcome at an over-travel differential greater than the crack pressure. The poppet then moves through the normal stroke at a pressure differential between the crack pressure and the over-travel differential, and reaches the over-travel only when the higher pressure differential is applied. In embodiments the return bias is a return spring.

[0024] In some embodiments the purge gap is annular and lies between an outer surface of the seat ring and a bore surrounding the seat ring, the bore being the core body bore or a bore of the valve stem. The air purge then passes over the outer surface of the seat ring, which lies outside the main passage.

[0025] In some embodiments the purge gap runs through the wall of the seat ring, so that the outer surface of the seat ring can be retained along its whole length.

[0026] In some embodiments the load-applying surface is a purge shoulder on the poppet, below the sealing face, that is spaced from the seat ring throughout the normal stroke. The axial load is then applied by the poppet itself, and the position of the poppet at which the purge shoulder meets the seat ring marks the end of the normal stroke.

[0027] In some embodiments the load-converting geometry is a convoluted wall having at least one convolution. Under the axial load the shape of the convolution changes by bending of the convoluted wall, and the change of shape produces the radial expansion.

[0028] In some embodiments the seat ring is made of a NiTi alloy that is superelastic and that has an austenite finish temperature lower than 5 °C, so that the seat ring is superelastic at riding temperatures.

[0029] In some embodiments the shape recovery of the seat ring produces a snap-back wipe, in which the seat ring moves across the sealing face of the poppet as the seat ring recovers. The snap-back wipe is a cleaning action additional to the air purge and is not present in every embodiment.

[0030] In some embodiments the seat ring is a polymer seat ring, for example of PEEK, which has radial compliance and which undergoes shape recovery more slowly and with lower force than a seat ring of superelastic NiTi alloy.

[0031] In some embodiments the valve core has a two-piece seat, in which a rigid seat provides the sealing edge and a separate wiper ring below the rigid seat is the radially deflectable element on which the purge shoulder bears. Sealing then takes place on an element that is not deflected in the over-travel.

[0032] In some embodiments the core body is made of brass. In other embodiments the core body is made of stainless steel, for resistance to corrosion where the sealant contains ammonia. In some embodiments the sealing face is of hard-anodised aluminium, which gives the sealing face a hard surface.

[0033] In some embodiments the dimensions of the seat ring, the over-travel distance, the purge gap width, the crack pressure and the over-travel differential have the values and ranges set out under "Parameters and ranges".

[0034] A thermal variant, in which the seat ring opens the purge gap on warming, is described under "Materials and shape recovery".

[0035] Features described in connection with one aspect apply to the other aspects, and the optional features are combinable with one another as set out under "Combinations".

##### Component and structural terms

[0058] As used herein, **"seat ring"** means a tubular element, retained in the core body and having radial compliance, that has a sealing edge against which the sealing face of the poppet closes at rest, and a wall that converts an axial load applied by the purge shoulder in the over-travel into a radial expansion that opens the purge gap, the seat ring undergoing shape recovery when the axial load is removed. The seat ring is not limited to a material. A seat ring of superelastic NiTi alloy is preferred, and a polymer seat ring is another example.

[0059] As used herein, **"sealing edge"** means the surface on which the sealing face of the poppet seats to close the tyre valve at rest. In embodiments having a seat ring the sealing edge is an edge of the seat ring. In the two-piece seat the sealing edge is provided by the rigid seat.

[0060] As used herein, **"core body bore"** means the internal surface of the core body that faces the outer surface of the seat ring. In embodiments the core body bore is one boundary of the purge gap.

[0061] As used herein, **"convoluted wall"** means a wall of the seat ring that is not straight in section, so that an axial load on the seat ring translates into a radial expansion of the convoluted wall. A convoluted wall having one convolution and a convoluted wall having two convolutions are both within this definition.

[0062] As used herein, **"convolution"** means a single wave in the section of a convoluted wall.

[0063] As used herein, **"purge shoulder"** means a surface of the poppet, below the sealing face, that is spaced from the seat ring throughout the normal stroke, that contacts the seat ring only in the over-travel, and that applies the axial load which produces the radial expansion. The purge shoulder is the preferred load-applying surface.

[0064] As used herein, **"main passage"** means the flow path by which air flows into the tyre chamber past the sealing edge when the sealing face of the poppet is lifted off the sealing edge. A flow path that runs between the sealing face and the sealing edge is part of the main passage, whatever the size of that flow path, and whether or not that flow path is enlarged by a deflection of the element on which the poppet seats.

[0065] As used herein, **"purge gap"** means a secondary flow path into the tyre chamber that is distinct from the main passage, that is closed at rest and throughout the normal stroke, and that is opened in the over-travel by the deflection that the load-applying surface produces in the radially deflectable element. The purge gap is distinguished from the main passage by where the purge gap runs and by the surfaces that the purge gap sweeps, and not by its size: the purge gap does not run between the sealing face and the sealing edge, and the purge gap is bounded by a surface of the radially deflectable element that lies outside the main passage. In the over-travel, air flows into the tyre chamber through the purge gap in addition to the main passage. In the preferred form the purge gap is an annular clearance between the outer surface of the seat ring and a bore surrounding the seat ring, the bore being the core body bore, which is preferred, or a bore of the valve stem.

[0066] In some embodiments the purge gap is a flow path through the wall of the seat ring, or is a flow path at one or more locations around the seat ring that does not extend around the whole circumference, as described under "Purge gap arrangements and retention of the seat ring".

[0067] As used herein, the purge gap is **"closed at rest"** when the surfaces that bound the purge gap are in the position that those surfaces take when no load is applied to the radially deflectable element by the load-applying surface. The expression does not require closure that is gas-tight to a stated degree; the tyre valve is closed at rest by the sealing face of the poppet on the sealing edge.

##### Threshold, pressure and travel terms

[0068] As used herein, **"pressure differential"** means the difference between the pressure upstream of the poppet, applied by a pump head, and the pressure in the tyre chamber.

[0069] As used herein, **"crack pressure"** means the pressure differential across the poppet at which the sealing face of the poppet first lifts off the sealing edge. In embodiments having a return spring with a two-rate characteristic, the crack pressure is the pressure differential that overcomes the first spring stage.

[0070] As used herein, **"over-travel differential"** means the pressure differential across the poppet, greater than the crack pressure, that is exceeded for the poppet to reach the over-travel. In embodiments having a return spring with a two-rate characteristic, the over-travel differential is the pressure differential that overcomes the second spring stage.

[0071] As used herein, **"normal stroke"** means the range of travel of the poppet that begins when the sealing face lifts off the sealing edge and that ends at the position of the poppet at which the load-applying surface first meets the radially deflectable element. Throughout the normal stroke the load-applying surface is spaced from the radially deflectable element. In embodiments in which the poppet is moved by the pressure differential, the poppet is in the normal stroke when the pressure differential is between the crack pressure and the over-travel differential.

[0072] As used herein, **"over-travel"** means the range of travel of the same poppet beyond the normal stroke, in which, and only in which, the load-applying surface bears on the radially deflectable element. The normal stroke and the over-travel are two ranges of travel of one poppet, and the position at which the load-applying surface first meets the radially deflectable element is the boundary between them. In the embodiments described the poppet reaches the over-travel when the pressure differential exceeds the over-travel differential, which is the preferred actuation.

[0073] An element that carries the sealing edge is loaded by the sealing face of the poppet at rest. A statement herein that the radially deflectable element is loaded only in the over-travel refers to the axial load applied by the load-applying surface.

[0074] As used herein, **"over-travel distance"** means the distance that the poppet moves beyond the normal stroke.

[0075] As used herein, **"two-rate characteristic"** means a force–travel behaviour of the return bias that has a lower rate through the normal stroke and a higher rate that is overcome for the poppet to reach the over-travel. Constructions that give a two-rate characteristic are described under "Return bias and load-applying surface".

[0076] As used herein, **"first spring stage"** means the part of the two-rate characteristic that the crack pressure overcomes during the normal stroke, and **"second spring stage"** means the part of the two-rate characteristic that the over-travel differential overcomes to produce the over-travel.

##### Material and property terms

[0077] As used herein, **"superelastic"** means the property of a material that recovers a large strain on unloading at a temperature above the austenite finish temperature of the material, as distinct from a thermally triggered response in which a change of temperature produces the recovery. A material that recovers its shape on unloading by another mechanism is not superelastic as the term is used herein, and is within the definition of shape recovery given below.

[0078] As used herein, **"NiTi alloy"** means an alloy based on nickel and titanium. Examples are given under "Materials and shape recovery".

[0079] As used herein, **"austenite finish temperature"** means the temperature above which an alloy such as NiTi alloy is fully austenitic and is superelastic.

[0080] As used herein, **"martensite start temperature"** means the temperature below which an alloy such as NiTi alloy begins to transform to martensite and correspondingly loses superelastic recovery.

[0081] As used herein, **"radial expansion"** means an outward movement of the wall of the seat ring, or of a wall of another radially deflectable element, under an axial load, that opens the purge gap. The term does not require that the wall stretches uniformly around its circumference. In embodiments described herein the radial expansion arises from a change, under the axial load, in the shape of a load-converting geometry such as a convoluted wall.

[0082] As used herein, **"radial compliance"** means the capacity of the radially deflectable element to expand radially under the axial load applied by the load-applying surface and to return substantially to its radial dimension at rest when the axial load is removed.

[0083] As used herein, **"shape recovery"** means the return of the radially deflectable element towards its shape at rest when the axial load applied by the load-applying surface is removed, which closes the purge gap. The term is not limited to a mechanism of recovery. The shape recovery is superelastic in a seat ring of superelastic NiTi alloy, and is elastic recovery of another kind in other embodiments, for example in a polymer seat ring. The speed and the force of the shape recovery differ between embodiments.

##### Cleaning-action terms

[0084] As used herein, **"air purge"** means a first cleaning action, being the flow of air at pump pressure through the open purge gap, across a surface of the valve core on which sealant film accumulates, and into the tyre chamber.

[0085] As used herein, **"snap-back wipe"** means a second, mechanical cleaning action that is present in some embodiments, in which the seat ring, on shape recovery, moves across the sealing face of the poppet. In the preferred embodiment the snap-back wipe is produced by the superelastic recovery of a seat ring of NiTi alloy. An embodiment in which the shape recovery is slower or weaker, for example an embodiment having a polymer seat ring, provides the air purge whether or not that embodiment also provides a snap-back wipe of comparable effect.

#### Principal embodiment: structure

[0107] A principal embodiment of the tyre valve 1 is described with reference to FIGS. 1 to 3 and FIG. 5. FIG. 1 is an axial section of the tyre valve 1 at rest. [0108] The tyre valve 1 comprises a valve stem 10 and a valve core 5. The valve stem 10 is a tubular member that is fixed to a tyre chamber 80 and has a bore that receives the valve core 5. The tyre valve 1 admits air into the tyre chamber 80 and retains it there. The tyre chamber 80 is indicated in FIG. 1 by a boundary in broken lines and contains sealant in use.

[0109] A pump head 90 is a device that is coupled to the tyre valve 1 and applies a pressure differential across the poppet 40. The pressure differential is the difference between the pressure upstream of the poppet 40 and the pressure in the tyre chamber 80. The pump head 90 is shown in broken outline in FIG. 1, where it is not engaged, and in solid outline in FIG. 2, where it is engaged on the valve stem 10. The pump head 90 is a conventional external device and is not a part of the valve core 5.

[0110] In the principal embodiment the valve core 5 is an assembly of a core body 20, a seat ring 30, a poppet 40 and a return spring 50. In some embodiments the valve core 5 also carries a lock nut 70. FIG. 3 shows these parts separated along the valve axis in their order of assembly: the core body 20, the seat ring 30, the poppet 40 with the return spring 50 concentric on the stem of the poppet 40, and the lock nut 70.

[0111] The core body 20 is fixed in the valve stem 10. In the principal embodiment the core body 20 has a thread by which it is screwed into the bore of the valve stem 10. The core body 20 carries the seat ring 30 and guides the poppet 40 along the valve axis. The core body 20 has a core body bore 22, which is the internal surface of the core body 20 that faces the outer surface of the seat ring 30. The core body 20 also has a locating shoulder 24, which is an internal step of the core body 20 against which the seat ring 30 is seated axially. Materials of the core body 20 are described under "Materials and shape recovery".

[0112] The seat ring 30 is a short tubular element. An end edge of the seat ring 30, also referred to as its upper edge, forms a sealing edge 32 on which the sealing face 42 of the poppet 40 seats to close the tyre valve 1. The seat ring 30 is pressed into the core body 20 with a light interference fit and is located against the locating shoulder 24.

[0113] The seat ring 30 has a convoluted wall 34, that is, a wall that is not straight in section. The convoluted wall 34 has at least one convolution 36, and each convolution 36 is shallow. In some embodiments the convoluted wall 34 has one convolution 36, as shown in FIG. 1. In other embodiments the convoluted wall 34 has two convolutions. The convoluted wall 34 has the effect that an axial load applied to the seat ring 30, for example at its upper edge, translates into radial expansion of the seat ring 30.

[0114] In the principal embodiment the seat ring 30 is a single part that both carries the sealing edge 32 and undergoes the radial expansion. The closing of the tyre valve 1 and the opening of the purge gap 60 are thereby provided by one part, without the additional part and the additional axial length of the two-piece seat described under "Forms of the radially deflectable element".

[0115] The seat ring 30 is an example of a radially deflectable element. Other forms of the radially deflectable element are described under "Forms of the radially deflectable element". Materials of the seat ring 30, including the superelastic NiTi alloy of the valve cores of Example 1, are described under "Materials and shape recovery".

[0116] The poppet 40 is a member that is movable along the valve axis. The poppet 40 has a sealing face 42, which in the principal embodiment is conical and which seats on the sealing edge 32 to close the tyre valve 1. From the closed position the poppet 40 travels away from the sealing edge 32 through a normal stroke 114 and, beyond the normal stroke 114, through an over-travel 116.

[0117] The poppet 40 has a purge shoulder 44. The purge shoulder 44 is a surface of the poppet 40 that is separate from the sealing face 42. In the principal embodiment the purge shoulder 44 is located below the sealing face 42, that is, it is spaced from the sealing face 42 along the valve axis. In some embodiments the purge shoulder 44 is annular. The purge shoulder 44 is spaced from the seat ring 30 when the poppet 40 is at rest and throughout the normal stroke 114. The purge shoulder 44 contacts the seat ring 30 only during the over-travel 116, in which it applies an axial load to the seat ring 30.

[0118] Because the purge shoulder 44 is spaced from the seat ring 30 up to the end of the normal stroke 114, the seat ring 30 is loaded by the purge shoulder 44 only in the over-travel 116. At rest the seat ring 30 carries on its sealing edge 32 the load with which the sealing face 42 is held closed. That load is distinct from the axial load applied by the purge shoulder 44.

[0119] The return spring 50 biases the poppet 40 towards the closed position. In the principal embodiment the return spring 50 is a compression spring arranged under the poppet 40. The return spring 50 has a two-rate characteristic: a first spring stage 52 of lower rate acts through the normal stroke 114, and a second spring stage 54 of higher rate must be overcome for the poppet 40 to reach the over-travel 116. The return spring 50 thereby sets two values of the pressure differential. The first is a crack pressure 110, at which the poppet 40 first lifts off the sealing edge 32 against the first spring stage 52. The second is an over-travel differential 112, greater than the crack pressure 110, which overcomes the second spring stage 54 and produces the over-travel 116.

[0120] The two-rate characteristic separates the over-travel differential 112 from the crack pressure 110. Inflation at a pressure differential between the two takes place through the normal stroke 114 alone, and the over-travel 116 is reached only at the higher pressure differential.

[0121] FIG. 5 shows the two-rate characteristic qualitatively. The horizontal axis represents the travel of the poppet 40 from the closed position and the vertical axis represents the force on the poppet 40. The first spring stage 52 appears as a segment of lower slope extending over the normal stroke 114, and the second spring stage 54 as a segment of higher slope extending over the over-travel 116. The crack pressure 110 and the over-travel differential 112 are marked on the curve. FIG. 5 is schematic: it carries no scale and represents no measured values. FIG. 5 does not depend on how the two-rate characteristic is produced, which may be by a single spring or by more than one spring or biasing element, as described under "Return bias and load-applying surface".

[0122] The normal stroke 114 and the over-travel 116 are two successive ranges of the travel of the same poppet 40. In the principal embodiment the end of the normal stroke 114 can be identified in the valve core 5 in two ways. It is the travel at which the first spring stage 52 gives way to the second spring stage 54, which appears in FIG. 5 as the change of slope. It is also the limit up to which the purge shoulder 44 remains spaced from the seat ring 30; beyond that limit, in the over-travel 116, the purge shoulder 44 bears on the seat ring 30.

[0123] A main passage 26 is the path by which inflation air flows into the tyre chamber 80 when the poppet 40 is lifted off the sealing edge 32. The main passage 26 runs past the sealing edge 32, between the sealing edge 32 and the sealing face 42. In FIG. 1 the poppet 40 is seated, so that the main passage 26 is closed and is indicated as a path.

[0124] A purge gap 60 is a secondary flow path into the tyre chamber 80 that is separate from the main passage 26. The purge gap 60 is bounded by the outer surface of the seat ring 30 and by a bore surrounding the seat ring 30. The purge gap 60 is closed at rest and is open when the seat ring 30 has undergone the radial expansion. In the principal embodiment the bore surrounding the seat ring 30 is the core body bore 22, and the purge gap 60 is an annular clearance between the outer surface of the seat ring 30 and the core body bore 22. In FIG. 1 the purge gap 60 is closed and is drawn as a single line.

[0125] In some embodiments the bore surrounding the seat ring 30 is the bore of the valve stem 10, so that the purge gap 60 lies between the seat ring 30 and the bore of the valve stem 10. This arrangement is described further under "Purge gap arrangements and retention of the seat ring".

[0126] The purge gap 60 differs from the main passage 26 in where it runs and in which surfaces it sweeps. The main passage 26 runs between the poppet 40 and the seat ring 30, past the sealing edge 32. The purge gap 60 runs along the outer surface of the seat ring 30, between the seat ring 30 and the bore surrounding it. Air flowing through the purge gap 60 passes over the outer surface of the seat ring 30 and over the surrounding bore. The poppet 40 is not one of the boundaries of the purge gap 60. A main passage 26 that is made larger, whether by further travel of the poppet 40 or by deflection of the seat ring 30, remains the main passage 26 and is not a purge gap 60.

[0127] Arrangements for retaining the seat ring 30 in the core body 20 while leaving the seat ring 30 free to undergo the radial expansion, and the exit of the air purge into the tyre chamber 80, are described under "Purge gap arrangements and retention of the seat ring".

[0128] In some embodiments a lock nut 70 is threaded onto the stem of the poppet 40 outside the core body 20. The lock nut 70 is conventional. The lock nut 70 is used to close the poppet 40 manually for storage and transport, and it plays no part in the opening of the purge gap 60.

#### Principal embodiment: operation

[0129] The operation of the principal embodiment over one inflation cycle is described with reference to FIGS. 1, 2 and 5. An inflation cycle is one application and removal of the pressure differential by the pump head 90. The steps of use are set out under "Use: cleaning during an inflation cycle".

[0130] At rest, as shown in FIG. 1, the return spring 50 holds the sealing face 42 of the poppet 40 on the sealing edge 32 of the seat ring 30, and the main passage 26 is closed. The purge shoulder 44 is spaced from the seat ring 30. The seat ring 30 is in its at-rest shape and the purge gap 60 is closed, so that no air purge passes through the purge gap 60 and the tyre valve 1 retains the air in the tyre chamber 80.

[0131] When the pump head 90 is coupled to the tyre valve 1 and the pressure differential exceeds the crack pressure 110, the poppet 40 lifts off the sealing edge 32 against the first spring stage 52 and travels through the normal stroke 114. Air flows through the main passage 26 into the tyre chamber 80.

[0132] Throughout the normal stroke 114 the purge shoulder 44 remains spaced from the seat ring 30. The seat ring 30 is not loaded by the purge shoulder 44 and keeps its at-rest shape, and the purge gap 60 remains closed. The geometry of the sealing edge 32 is therefore the same after a normal stroke 114 as before it. At a pressure differential below the over-travel differential 112 the tyre chamber 80 is inflated through the main passage 26 alone.

[0133] When the pressure differential exceeds the over-travel differential 112, the poppet 40 overcomes the second spring stage 54 and travels beyond the normal stroke 114 into the over-travel 116, as shown in FIG. 2. In the over-travel 116 the purge shoulder 44 bears on the seat ring 30 and applies an axial load to it. Under that axial load the seat ring 30 undergoes radial expansion, and the purge gap 60 opens.

[0134] The convoluted wall 34 converts the axial load into the radial expansion. In some embodiments the convolution 36 flattens under the axial load, and FIG. 2 shows the convolution 36 flattened in comparison with FIG. 1. The convoluted wall 34 then deforms by bending at the convolution 36. The radial expansion is obtained from this change in the shape of the convoluted wall 34 and not from a uniform circumferential stretching of the convoluted wall 34.

[0135] The purge gap 60 is opened by the mechanical load that the poppet 40 applies through the purge shoulder 44. The purge gap 60 is not opened by the flow of air acting on the seat ring 30. Whether the poppet 40 reaches the over-travel 116 depends on the pressure differential applied by the pump head 90. While the poppet 40 is in the over-travel 116, the purge shoulder 44 holds the seat ring 30 in its expanded shape whatever the rate at which air passes through the purge gap 60.

[0136] With the purge gap 60 open, air at the pressure delivered by the pump head 90 flows through the purge gap 60 into the tyre chamber 80. This flow is the air purge. The main passage 26 remains open during the over-travel 116, so that air flows into the tyre chamber 80 through the main passage 26 and through the purge gap 60 at the same time, as indicated by the two flow arrows in FIG. 2. The air purge passes over the outer surface of the seat ring 30 and over the bore surrounding it, and acts on sealant film present on those surfaces so as to carry it into the tyre chamber 80.

[0137] When the pump head 90 is removed, the pressure differential collapses and the return spring 50 returns the poppet 40. As the poppet 40 leaves the over-travel 116 the purge shoulder 44 lifts from the seat ring 30 and the axial load is removed. The seat ring 30 undergoes shape recovery, returning to its at-rest shape, and the purge gap 60 closes. The poppet 40 then returns through the normal stroke 114 and the sealing face 42 seats again on the sealing edge 32.

[0138] In some embodiments, in particular those in which the seat ring 30 is of a superelastic material, the shape recovery gives a second cleaning action, the snap-back wipe. The shape recovery of a superelastic seat ring 30 is fast and stiff. It is believed that on shape recovery the seat ring 30 moves across the sealing face 42 of the poppet 40 and shears partially cured sealant film from it. The surfaces concerned in the snap-back wipe are the seat ring 30 and the sealing face 42.

[0139] An inflation cycle in which the poppet 40 reaches the over-travel 116 therefore provides the air purge while the purge gap 60 is open and, in the embodiments that have it, the snap-back wipe when the pump head 90 is removed. Both cleaning actions take place in the course of the inflation cycle, without a tool and without removal of the valve core 5 from the valve stem 10. The air purge takes place before the shape recovery and does not require the snap-back wipe. The snap-back wipe is an additional cleaning action that is present in some embodiments.

[0140] The test reported in Example 1 was carried out on valve cores according to the principal embodiment having a seat ring 30 of superelastic NiTi alloy. That test does not isolate the air purge from the snap-back wipe, and no statement is made as to the share of each cleaning action in the result reported there. When those valve cores were inspected at the end of that test, the sealing edge 32 and the sealing face 42 were clean, and cured sealant film was present on the outer surface of the seat ring 30 in the region of the purge gap 60.

[0141] In the test reported in Example 1, a snap was heard on removal of the pump head 90 after each inflation of the valve cores according to the principal embodiment. The snap was taken in that test as an indication that the poppet 40 had reached the over-travel 116. The snap is recorded as an observation, and no embodiment requires it.

#### Return bias and load-applying surface

[0142] The return spring 50 is an example of a return bias, that is, of an arrangement that biases the poppet 40 towards the closed position. A return bias having a two-rate characteristic can be constructed in several ways, which are described in the following paragraphs. Each construction provides a first spring stage 52 that the crack pressure 110 overcomes and a second spring stage 54 that the over-travel differential 112 overcomes. The rates of the first spring stage 52 and of the second spring stage 54 are selected so that the crack pressure 110 and the over-travel differential 112 have the values described under "Parameters and ranges".

[0143] In some embodiments the return spring 50 is a single compression spring of variable pitch. The closely pitched coils of the variable-pitch spring close solid during the normal stroke 114. The widely pitched coils that remain active are fewer and so give a higher rate, and they form the second spring stage 54. The first spring stage 52 and the second spring stage 54 are thereby obtained from one part, and the parts that adjoin the return spring 50 are unchanged. FIGS. 1 and 2 show a return spring 50 of this construction by way of example.

[0144] In some embodiments the return bias comprises a soft spring and a stiff spring arranged in series, with a stop that takes the soft spring out of the load path at the end of the normal stroke 114. The soft spring provides the first spring stage 52 and sets the crack pressure 110. The stiff spring provides the second spring stage 54 and sets the over-travel differential 112.

[0145] In some embodiments the return bias comprises nested springs that act in parallel with staggered engagement. An outer spring acts over the whole travel of the poppet 40. A shorter inner spring engages only at the end of the normal stroke 114, so that the rate in the over-travel 116 is the sum of the rates of the two springs. This construction occupies less axial length than two springs in series.

[0146] In some embodiments the return bias comprises a return spring 50 of a single rate and an elastomeric bumper that the poppet 40 reaches at the end of the normal stroke 114. The elastomeric bumper provides the second spring stage 54. The material of the elastomeric bumper is selected to withstand the sealant, including sealant that contains ammonia, and to resist compression set, since compression set would shift the over-travel differential 112.

[0147] In some embodiments the return bias comprises a soft coil spring stacked with a disc spring, the disc spring being engaged at the end of the normal stroke 114. The disc spring provides a second spring stage 54 that is stiff and occupies little axial length.

[0148] In some embodiments a detent holds the poppet 40 at the end of the normal stroke 114 until the over-travel differential 112 overcomes the detent. The detent is, for example, a spring-loaded ball, or a magnet and a keeper. In these embodiments the crack pressure 110 and the over-travel differential 112 are separated by a step in force in place of a change of rate, and the entry of the poppet 40 into the over-travel 116 is abrupt.

[0149] Where the two-rate characteristic is provided by a single spring, the over-travel differential 112 does not depend on the seating of two springs relative to one another. In some embodiments a single variable-pitch spring is used for that reason in preference to a construction having two springs.

[0150] In some embodiments the return spring 50 is subjected to a pre-setting step, that is, a stabilising step, before it is assembled into the valve core 5. The pre-setting step is directed to limiting relaxation of the return spring 50 under repeated compression, since relaxation would cause the crack pressure 110 and the over-travel differential 112 to drift in service.

[0151] In some embodiments the normal stroke 114 ends at a spring-loaded stop. The poppet 40 meets a stop washer at the end of the normal stroke 114, and the stop washer is itself held by a spring that is stiffer than the return spring 50. The over-travel 116 is the travel in which the poppet 40 pushes the stop washer back against the stiffer spring. The stop washer defines the end of the normal stroke 114 and the stiffer spring sets the over-travel differential 112, and the return spring 50 can then have a single rate.

[0152] The purge shoulder 44 is an example of a load-applying surface, that is, of a surface that is moved by the poppet 40 in the over-travel 116 and that applies the axial load to the seat ring 30 or to another radially deflectable element. In the principal embodiment the load-applying surface is the purge shoulder 44 formed on the poppet 40 itself.

[0153] In some embodiments the load-applying surface is provided on a collar that is a separate part carried by the poppet 40. The collar is pressed, crimped or threaded onto the poppet 40 below the sealing face 42. The position of the collar on the poppet 40 sets the over-travel distance, so that the over-travel distance can be set when the valve core 5 is assembled.

[0154] In some embodiments the purge shoulder 44 is conical in place of a flat annulus. A conical purge shoulder 44 is used with forms of the radially deflectable element that are spread by a cone, namely the slotted sleeve, the split seat ring and the tapered seat ring described under "Forms of the radially deflectable element".

[0155] In some embodiments the axial load is applied to the seat ring 30 through the return bias. A spring seat washer is arranged between the return spring 50 and the poppet 40 and bears on the seat ring 30 once the first spring stage 52 has been taken up. In these embodiments the load-applying surface is a surface of the spring seat washer, and the spring seat washer, not the poppet 40, touches the seat ring 30.

[0156] In each of these embodiments the load-applying surface is moved by the poppet 40, and the radially deflectable element is loaded by the load-applying surface only in the over-travel 116.

#### Forms of the radially deflectable element

[0163] The seat ring 30 described under "Principal embodiment: structure" is one form of a radially deflectable element, as that term is defined under "Definitions and interpretation". The radially deflectable element is also embodied by the polymer seat ring 130 shown in FIG. 8 and by the wiper ring 144 of the two-piece seat shown in FIG. 9A and FIG. 9B, and in some embodiments by the further forms shown in FIG. 11A to FIG. 11C. In each form, a load-applying surface moved by the poppet 40, for example the purge shoulder 44, bears on the radially deflectable element only in over-travel, the axial load so applied deflects the element radially, and the element returns by shape recovery when the load is removed.

[0164] In some embodiments the radially deflectable element carries the sealing edge, as the seat ring 30 carries the sealing edge 32 and the polymer seat ring 130 carries the sealing edge 132. In other embodiments the radially deflectable element is separate from the element that carries the sealing edge, as in the two-piece seat, in which the rigid seat 140 carries the sealing edge 142 and the wiper ring 144 is the radially deflectable element.

[0165] In some embodiments the radially deflectable element has a load-converting geometry, that is, a geometry by which the axial load applied in over-travel is converted into radial expansion. The convoluted wall 34 of the seat ring 30 is the load-converting geometry of the principal embodiment. In some embodiments the conversion is made by the shape of the radially deflectable element, by the shape of the load-applying surface that bears on it, or by both.

[0166] The effect of the load-converting geometry is the opening of a flow path. The axial load is converted into radial expansion, and the radial expansion opens the purge gap 60, which is a secondary flow path distinct from the main passage 26 and closed at rest. A wavy or undulating shape of a resilient metal seal is described in US11549591B1, where the shape pre-loads a seat interface to provide a seating and sealing force. The load-converting geometry described here has a different effect: it acts when the poppet 40 is in over-travel, and its deformation opens the purge gap 60 through which the air purge passes.

[0167] In some embodiments the convoluted wall 34 has one convolution 36, as shown in FIG. 4A. The convolution 36 is a single shallow wave in the section of the convoluted wall 34, between the sealing edge 32 and the end of the seat ring 30 that is seated against the locating shoulder 24. Axial load applied to the seat ring 30 in over-travel is translated by the convolution 36 into radial expansion of the convoluted wall 34.

[0168] In other embodiments the convoluted wall 34 has two convolutions, as shown in FIG. 4B: a convolution 36 nearer the sealing edge 32 and a convolution 38 nearer the fit band 35. The two convolutions lie in series along the convoluted wall 34 and together translate the axial load into radial expansion.

[0169] In FIG. 4A and FIG. 4B the wall thickness is marked t, the free length is marked L and the convolution amplitude is marked a. Values of these dimensions are given under "Parameters and ranges".

[0170] In some embodiments the convolution 36 is formed in the section of the wall by shape-setting. A plain tube wall is pressed into one or two circumferential waves in a shape-setting fixture, no pattern is cut in the wall, and laser cutting serves only to trim the ends of the seat ring 30. Under axial load the waves flatten and the wall moves outward.

[0171] In some embodiments the load-converting geometry is a strut pattern cut in the wall by laser cutting, for example a zig-zag pattern or a pattern of diamond-shaped cells. Under axial load the strut pattern shortens axially and bulges radially, so that the radial expansion is obtained by bending of the struts. In such embodiments the cells of the strut pattern are closed at rest, and the cut portion of the wall provides a route for the purge flow when the wall deforms, as described under "Purge gap arrangements and retention of the seat ring".

[0172] In some embodiments the load-converting geometry is a pre-bowed wall, that is, a wall having a single outward bow along the free length. The bow increases under axial load in the manner of a pre-buckled column. The pre-bowed wall corresponds to the convoluted wall 34 with one convolution 36, written as a bow, and its purpose is a radial expansion that increases progressively with the axial load.

[0173] In some embodiments the load-converting geometry is a helical slot through the wall along part of the free length. Axial compression unwinds the helix slightly and increases the diameter of the wall, and the helical slot gives the wall an axial compliance. The helical slot is itself a route for the purge flow when it is open.

[0174] In some embodiments the convolution 36 has a root radius sized to reduce the concentration of strain at the root of the convolution 36, where repeated radial expansion and shape recovery concentrate the strain.

[0175] In some embodiments the convolution amplitude is chosen towards the lower end of the range given under "Parameters and ranges", to reduce the peak strain in the convoluted wall 34. No service life is stated for any form of the radially deflectable element; the extent of the test carried out is stated under "Examples and data".

[0176] In some embodiments the radially deflectable element is a slotted sleeve 170, shown in FIG. 11A. The slotted sleeve 170 is a thin-walled cylinder in which axial slots running from one end form fingers 172. The purge shoulder 44 is conical in these embodiments and, in over-travel, enters between the fingers 172 and spreads them outward towards the core body bore 22. The radial expansion is obtained by bending of the fingers 172 and not by hoop strain of a continuous wall. The axial slots between the fingers 172 are a route for the purge flow when the fingers 172 are spread.

[0177] In some embodiments the radially deflectable element is a split seat ring 180, shown in FIG. 11B. The split seat ring 180 has a C-shaped section with one axial split 182, and opens circumferentially when a cone on the poppet 40 enters it in over-travel. The purpose of the axial split 182 is a change of diameter obtained with little strain of the material. In such embodiments the axial split 182 is closed at rest, or the seal at rest is made on a face beside the axial split 182, so that the axial split 182 does not interrupt the seal at rest.

[0178] In some embodiments the radially deflectable element is a tapered seat ring 190, shown in FIG. 11C. The tapered seat ring 190 has a conical inner surface, the taper 192, and the poppet 40 has a matching taper 194 in place of the purge shoulder 44. In over-travel the matching taper 194 wedges the tapered seat ring 190 outward, so that the axial load is converted into radial expansion without a convolution. In some such embodiments the angle of the taper 192 is chosen greater than the friction angle between the taper 192 and the matching taper 194, so that the tapered seat ring 190 is released when the poppet 40 returns.

[0179] In some embodiments the element deflected by the load-applying surface is a disc spring of conical form, the rim of which seals on a face at rest. In over-travel the load-applying surface flattens the disc spring, the rim lifts from the face, and a purge gap opens at the face. In these embodiments the deflection is axial and the purge gap is a gap at a face, so that the element is referred to as a deflectable element and its deflection is not a radial expansion. The disc spring also provides a second spring stage. No figure shows this form and no test of it is reported.

[0180] In some embodiments the deformation of the deflectable element that opens the purge gap is radial, axial, tilting or a combination of these. Radial expansion is the deformation of the principal embodiment.

[0181] In some embodiments the valve core 5 has a two-piece seat, shown at rest in FIG. 9A and in over-travel in FIG. 9B. The two-piece seat comprises a rigid seat 140 and a wiper ring 144. #### Purge gap arrangements and retention of the seat ring

[0185] The purge gap 60 is bounded by an outer surface of the seat ring 30 and by a bore surrounding the seat ring 30, as described under "Principal embodiment: structure". This section describes embodiments in which the seat ring 30 is retained in the core body 20 while a portion of it remains free to deflect, and embodiments of the route taken by the purge flow.

[0186] In some embodiments the interference fit acts on a fit band 35 of the seat ring 30 and not on the whole of its outer surface. [0187] In these embodiments the seat ring 30 closes the purge gap 60 at rest by line contact at one end of the core body bore relief 202. [0188] The effect of the fit band 35 and the free portion 37 is that the retention of the seat ring 30 and the radial expansion are carried by different portions of the same outer surface. The core body 20 has one more machined diameter than a core body having a plain core body bore 22.

#### Materials and shape recovery

[0203] In the principal embodiment the seat ring 30 is made of superelastic NiTi alloy of nominally 55.8 wt% Ni, in the form of drawn tube which is shaped by shape-setting and finished by electropolishing. The austenite finish temperature of the NiTi alloy is below 5 °C, so that the seat ring 30 is superelastic at riding temperatures.

[0204] In some embodiments the radially deflectable element is made of an elastically recovering material having radial compliance. Superelastic NiTi alloy, a polymer and a spring-tempered metal are examples of such a material. Shape recovery of the radially deflectable element does not require a transformation of the material.

[0205] In some embodiments the seat ring 30 is made of a ternary superelastic alloy, for example a Ni–Ti–Cr alloy or a Ni–Ti–Cu alloy, in place of the binary NiTi alloy. The addition of chromium shifts the transformation temperature, and the addition of copper narrows the hysteresis. The parameters of shape-setting are adapted to the alloy.

[0206] In some embodiments the seat ring 30 is made of a copper-based alloy that is superelastic, for example a Cu–Al–Ni alloy or a Cu–Zn–Al alloy. The recoverable strain of such alloys is lower than that of NiTi alloy, and their compatibility with an ammonia-bearing sealant is not established.

[0207] In some embodiments the valve core 5 has a polymer seat ring 130 in place of the seat ring 30, as shown in FIG. 8. The polymer seat ring 130 has a sealing edge 132 and a convoluted wall 134, the convoluted wall 134 being the same as the convoluted wall 34 of the seat ring 30. In some embodiments the polymer seat ring 130 is made of polyether ether ketone (PEEK). The core body 20, the core body bore 22, the locating shoulder 24, the poppet 40 with the sealing face 42 and the purge shoulder 44, the return spring 50 and the purge gap 60 are as in the principal embodiment.

[0208] The polymer seat ring 130 has some radial compliance and a lower cost than the seat ring 30 of superelastic NiTi alloy. Its shape recovery is slower and the force of its return is lower, so that the snap-back wipe is weaker. No polymer seat ring 130 has been built or tested.

[0209] In some embodiments the polymer seat ring 130 is made of another high-modulus thermoplastic, for example polyphenylene sulphide (PPS) or polyetherimide (PEI), in place of PEEK. The chemical compatibility of each such polymer with an ammonia-bearing sealant is to be confirmed for that polymer.

[0210] In some embodiments the radially deflectable element is made of a spring-tempered metal, for example spring-tempered stainless steel or copper–beryllium, and has a form in which the deflection is dominated by bending, such as the slotted sleeve 170 or the strut pattern. The bending-dominated deflection keeps the material within its elastic limit, so that the shape recovery is the elastic recovery of a metal that is not superelastic. A spring-tempered metal is not proposed for a continuous convoluted wall 34.

[0211] In some embodiments the shape recovery is assisted by a garter spring, that is, a circumferential coil spring placed around the slotted sleeve 170, the split seat ring 180 or the polymer seat ring 130. The garter spring supplies a closing force, so that the closing of the purge gap does not depend only on the stiffness of the radially deflectable element. The garter spring lies in the region of the purge gap and passes the purge flow between its coils.

#### Use: cleaning during an inflation cycle

[0269] FIG. 6 shows, in four panels, the states through which the valve core 5 passes in one inflation cycle: at rest 500, normal stroke 502, over-travel 504 and return 506. Each panel shows the poppet 40 with the sealing face 42 and the purge shoulder 44, the seat ring 30 and the purge gap 60. The behaviour of the valve core 5 in each state is described under "Principal embodiment: operation". The present section describes the inflation cycle as a method of cleaning the tyre valve 1.

[0270] The method starts with the valve core 5 at rest 500: the sealing face 42 is seated on the sealing edge 32 and the purge gap 60 is closed. A pump head 90 is coupled to the tyre valve 1 and applies a pressure differential across the poppet 40. When the pressure differential exceeds the crack pressure 110, the poppet 40 lifts through the normal stroke 114, as shown in the normal stroke panel 502, and air flows through the main passage 26 into the tyre chamber 80. The purge gap 60 remains closed.

[0271] When the pressure differential exceeds the over-travel differential 112, the poppet 40 moves through the over-travel 116, as shown in the over-travel panel 504. The purge shoulder 44 bears on the seat ring 30, the seat ring 30 undergoes radial expansion and the purge gap 60 opens. Air at pump pressure flows through the purge gap 60, alongside the air flowing through the main passage 26, across surfaces of the valve core 5 on which sealant film accumulates. This air purge carries sealant film into the tyre chamber 80.

[0272] When the pump head 90 is removed, the pressure differential collapses and the poppet 40 returns, as shown in the return panel 506. The axial load on the seat ring 30 is removed and the seat ring 30 undergoes shape recovery, which closes the purge gap 60. In embodiments in which the seat ring 30 is superelastic, the shape recovery also produces the snap-back wipe, as described under "Principal embodiment: operation".

[0273] The cleaning is part of the inflation cycle. The user couples the pump head 90, inflates the tyre chamber 80 and removes the pump head 90. The valve core 5 stays in place in the valve stem 10 and is cleaned without a tool. The cleaning takes place in each inflation cycle in which the pressure differential exceeds the over-travel differential 112.

[0274] The method can be carried out with any pump head 90 that applies a pressure differential exceeding the over-travel differential 112. In Example 1 the pump head 90 was that of a floor pump, the tyre chamber 80 was inflated to 6 bar, and over-travel was confirmed on every inflation of a prototype valve core. The rate at which the floor pump delivered air was not recorded.

[0275] Inflation from a CO₂ cartridge has not been tested. A CO₂ cartridge inflator chills the tyre valve 1 to well below 0 °C for a few seconds. If the temperature of a seat ring 30 of NiTi alloy falls below the martensite start temperature of the NiTi alloy, the seat ring 30 loses its superelastic recovery until it warms.

[0276] In some embodiments the valve core 5 is supplied with instructions for use that recommend inflation with a floor pump. The choice of the austenite finish temperature for cold conditions is described under "Materials and shape recovery".

[0277] The sealant with which the method has been tested is a latex emulsion sealant. Sealant of this kind typically contains ammonia and suspended particles. No ammonia-free sealant has been tested.

[0282] Three conventional valve cores, designated C-1, C-2 and C-3, were tested for comparison. They were standard Presta-pattern valve cores of a single manufacturer.

[0288] Over-travel was confirmed on every inflation of a prototype valve core by an audible snap on removal of the pump head 90.

[0289] The evidence of Example 1 has the following limits. The test covered a total of 40 inflation cycles, one sealant, one floor pump, one temperature, one size of tyre chamber 80 and three valve cores of each type. No fatigue test and no cold test was carried out. The comparison was between the prototype valve cores and conventional valve cores. No valve core having the air purge alone or the snap-back wipe alone was tested, so the test does not show the separate contribution of each cleaning action. The behaviour of the seat ring 30 beyond the inflation cycles tested, including whether the purge gap 60 remains closed at rest after longer service, has not been established, and no statement is made as to service life.

#### Prophetic example 2: polymer seat ring

[0290] This example has not been built or tested. The valve core 5 would be as in Example 1, except that the polymer seat ring 130, of PEEK, would take the place of the seat ring 30 of NiTi alloy. The polymer seat ring 130 would have a sealing edge 132 and a convoluted wall 134 of the same geometry as in Example 1, with a seat ring inner diameter of 2.3 mm, a wall thickness of 0.20 mm, a free length of 3.0 mm and a convolution amplitude of 0.12 mm. No value is given for the radial expansion or for the purge gap width that these dimensions would give in PEEK.

[0291] The polymer seat ring 130 would be turned from rod as described under "Manufacture and assembly" and press-fitted against the locating shoulder 24. In use the poppet 40 would lift through the normal stroke 114 and, above the over-travel differential 112, would reach the over-travel 116 as in Example 1. The polymer seat ring 130 has some radial compliance. Its shape recovery on removal of the pump head 90 would be slower, and of lower force, than that of the seat ring 30 of NiTi alloy.

[0292] The air purge is expected to operate as in Example 1, because it depends on the opening of the purge gap 60 and not on the material of the seat ring. The snap-back wipe is expected to be weaker than in Example 1.

#### Prophetic example 3: two-piece seat

[0293] This example has not been built or tested. The valve core 5 would have a two-piece seat in place of the seat ring 30 of Example 1. A rigid seat 140 of brass would provide the sealing edge 142 on which the sealing face 42 of the poppet 40 seats. A wiper ring 144, being a thin ring of stainless steel of spring character, would lie below the rigid seat 140. The poppet 40, the return spring 50 and the remaining components would be as in Example 1. No dimensions are given for the rigid seat 140 or for the wiper ring 144.

[0294] At rest the seal would be made on the rigid seat 140 and would not depend on the wiper ring 144. During the over-travel 116 the purge shoulder 44 would deflect the wiper ring 144 outward. On removal of the pump head 90 the wiper ring 144 would undergo shape recovery and would wipe on its return.

[0295] Sealing on the rigid seat 140 is expected to be more predictable than sealing on an element that also deflects, and the two-piece seat may be easier to make. The two-piece seat would have one more part and a greater axial length than the seat ring 30 of Example 1.

#### Prophetic example 4: thermal variant

[0296] This example has not been built or tested, and there are no data for it. The seat ring would be the thermal variant 150, being a seat ring of NiTi alloy trained with two-way shape memory and having an austenite finish temperature of about 35 °C to 40 °C. Warming the valve core 5, for example by hand, would take the thermal variant 150 through that temperature range, whereupon the thermal variant 150 would change shape and open the purge gap 60 without a pump. On cooling, the thermal variant 150 would return to its earlier shape. Opening by warming would not use the over-travel 116 of the poppet 40. The thermal variant 150 is further described under "Materials and shape recovery".

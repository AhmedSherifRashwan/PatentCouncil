## Findings

### Enforcement Analysis

Independent claims analysed: claim 1 (valve core), claim 2 (method of cleaning), claim 3 (seat ring) and claim 4 (method of manufacturing, a separate category although it refers back to claim 3). Claims 47 and 77 are product claims in dependent form and inherit the claim 1 and claim 3 analysis.

Summary for the human. Claim 1 is the enforcement workhorse: every element can be found by teardown and a pressure bench on a bought part, and nothing in it is intent-dependent. Its two literal-avoidance routes are the ones the package itself teaches: an element that deflects axially rather than radially ([0179]–[0180]), and a load-applying surface that touches the element from lift-off instead of being spaced from it. Claim 2 is performed by end users, and claim 4 is performed in a factory; neither is a practical primary enforcement vehicle.

### F-33-1 [high] [design-around] An axially deflecting element, taught in the package, avoids "radially deflectable element" and "radial expansion"

Claim 1: "a radially deflectable element" … "applies an axial load that produces a radial expansion of the radially deflectable element, the radial expansion opening a purge gap". Claim 2 has the same words.

Attack (competitor's engineer). [0179] describes a conical disc spring whose rim seals on a face at rest. In over-travel the load-applying surface flattens it, the rim lifts, and a purge gap opens at the face. The paragraph says outright that "the deflection is axial … and its deflection is not a radial expansion", and [0180] adds deformations that are "axial, tilting or a combination". The disc spring also provides the second spring stage ([0179]), so one part replaces two. That makes it cheaper than the principal embodiment, and the specification hands it to the competitor while claims 1 and 2 exclude it. Under US law the disclosed-but-unclaimed alternative is dedicated to the public. Under the EPO and UK approach it is at best an equivalents argument against the applicant's own statement that it "is not a radial expansion".

Least narrowing action. Add a separate independent claim (valve core, with a matching method claim) that recites "a deflectable element" whose deflection by the load-applying surface in the over-travel opens the purge gap, keeping every other claim 1 limitation. Support: [0179], [0180], [0081], [0065]. Keep claim 1 as it is, because the broader claim sits closer to the floating seat and secondary passageways of US4044791A (INDEX #2), and counsel should weigh that exposure separately rather than put claim 1 at risk. A figure of the disc-spring form would help (see F-36-3), but the claim is supported by the existing text. No new disclosure is needed for the claim.

### F-33-2 [high] [design-around] Contact from lift-off avoids "spaced … throughout the normal stroke"

Claim 1: "wherein the load-applying surface is spaced from the radially deflectable element throughout the normal stroke". Claim 2 carries the same boundary through "a normal stroke (114) … while a purge gap (60) remains closed" read with [0071].

Attack. [0071] defines the normal stroke as ending "at the position of the poppet at which the load-applying surface first meets the radially deflectable element", and [0072] defines the over-travel as the travel in which, "and only in which", that surface bears on the element. A competitor sets the collar or shoulder at zero clearance, or with light contact from lift-off, and makes the element's opening threshold-dependent instead of contact-dependent: for example a stiffer convolution that does not open the gap until the load passes a value, or a detent of the kind in [0148]. The purge gap still stays shut at ordinary inflation and still opens at a higher pressure, which is the whole commercial effect. But on the package's own definitions there is no travel in which the surface is spaced from the element, so no "normal stroke" and no "over-travel" exist as claimed. Litigation counsel would run the definitions against us. The same product is hard to catch even on the equivalents doctrine, because the spacing is presented in [0015] as one of the "two features … [that] act together".

Least narrowing action. Add a claim, parallel to claim 1, that bounds the two travel ranges by the pressure thresholds and the state of the purge gap rather than by first contact: "the purge gap remains closed while the pressure differential is between the crack pressure and the over-travel differential, and opens when the pressure differential exceeds the over-travel differential". The pressure-defined part is supported by [0071] (last sentence), [0120], [0132] and [0239]. However, every existing embodiment has the surface spaced from the element, and [0071]/[0072] tie the boundary to contact. A claim that covers contact from lift-off therefore needs a disclosed embodiment in which the load-applying surface is in contact with, or preloads, the element through the normal stroke and the purge gap opens only above a load threshold. Only the inventor can say whether that is within their conception (Q1). New disclosure is required. Target P3.

### F-33-3 [medium] [design-around] The cleaning method is performed by the end user, not the manufacturer

Claim 2: "moving the poppet from the closed position through a normal stroke … moving the poppet through an over-travel … passing air through the open purge gap … returning the poppet to the closed position".

Attack. Every step is performed by the cyclist with a pump. The competitor who makes and sells the valve core performs none of them, so it is at most an indirect infringer. In the US that means inducement (knowledge and specific intent) or contributory infringement (a component especially adapted, with no substantial non-infringing use). In the UK it means s60(2) supply of means essential, with knowledge. At the EPO level, indirect infringement is national, for example § 10 PatG in Germany. The steps also occur only when the user actually reaches the over-travel differential ([0273]: "in each inflation cycle in which the pressure differential exceeds the over-travel differential"). Proving that users do so, and that the competitor knew it, needs survey or marketing evidence. Damages would run against individual users, which no one will pursue.

Least narrowing action. No amendment. The product claims (1, 3, 47, 50, 77) already capture the maker and seller directly, so claim 2 should be kept as a supplementary claim and not relied on commercially. Counsel may consider whether claim 50 ("the tyre chamber contains sealant") should be mirrored by a claim to a tyre chamber fitted with the valve core, which [0018] and [0160] support, to catch rim or tube sellers who fit the core at the factory. No new disclosure.

### F-33-4 [medium] [design-around] "Load-converting geometry" is at risk of a structure-limited construction in the US

Claim 3: "a wall having a load-converting geometry; wherein an axial load … produces through the load-converting geometry a radial expansion of the wall".

Attack. "Load-converting geometry" is defined only by the function it performs ([0104]), and [0106] asks that it cover "each structure described herein that performs the function". A US court may treat "geometry" as a nonce word that recites no definite structure and construe it under 35 U.S.C. § 112(f). That construction confines the claim to the structures disclosed (convoluted wall [0113], strut pattern [0171], pre-bowed wall [0172], helical slot [0173], slotted and split and tapered forms [0176]–[0178]) and their equivalents. If the specification were found not to link a structure clearly enough, the claim could be indefinite. EPO and UK practice read the term functionally, so the risk is US-specific. The dependent ladder names only the convoluted wall (62–64) and wall slots (67), so a strut-pattern or helical-slot ring has no named dependent fallback in the seat ring family.

Least narrowing action. Add dependent claims to claim 3 (and to claim 21's family for claim 1): "wherein the load-converting geometry is a strut pattern cut in the wall" ([0171]) and "wherein the load-converting geometry is a helical slot through the wall" ([0173]). These give each disclosed structure its own claim, whatever construction a court adopts. No new disclosure. Target P5.

### F-33-5 [medium] [design-around] Flow-actuated expansion and main-passage enlargement are excluded by the package's own definitions

Claim 1: "the load-applying surface bears on the radially deflectable element and applies an axial load that produces a radial expansion" and "the purge gap is a secondary flow path that is distinct from the main passage". [0016]: "The radial deflection is produced by the load-applying surface bearing on the radially deflectable element, and not by a flow of fluid acting on the radially deflectable element." [0064] and [0126]: a main passage "enlarged by a deflection of the element on which the poppet seats … remains the main passage".

Attack. Two cheap competitor routes fall outside claim 1 on the package's express words. One is a thin sleeve that bulges under inflation pressure above a threshold. The other is a compliant seat that swings its sealing edge away from the poppet cone at high flow, sweeping the sealing edge but opening no second path. Either can be marketed as "self-cleaning". The package's own [0011] argues that the second route cleans less well, but that is a performance argument, not an infringement argument.

Least narrowing action. None that is free. Both exclusions sit where the prior art is (US5924444A, INDEX #15, deforms a ring by flow; US4044791A, INDEX #2, stages seat movement), so widening claim 1 would trade away novelty. Record this as a residual gap in scope for the human. No new disclosure; no change proposed. Target P5 if counsel decides otherwise.

### F-33-6 [low] [design-around] The seat ring claim is proved only through the core it is fitted to

Claim 3: "wherein an axial load, applied to the seat ring by a load-applying surface that is moved by the poppet in an over-travel (116) of the poppet beyond a normal stroke (114)" … "wherein, when the seat ring is installed in the valve core, the radial expansion opens a purge gap (60)".

Attack. The claimed object is the ring alone, but its limitations are defined by a poppet, a stroke and a purge gap that are not part of it. A seller of bare replacement rings argues that its ring is for a core without over-travel, or that the ring does not open a gap in the seller's recommended core. The EPO reads the clauses as "suitable for", which favours us. A US court may require proof of the ring's behaviour in a specific core.

Least narrowing action. No amendment. Dependent claims 62–69 and 71–73 add structure (convolutions, dimensions, material) that can be proved on the ring alone and should be the enforcement claims for spare parts. No new disclosure.

## Assumptions

A1. The package is the whole application as it would be filed. No abstract is present, and none has been reviewed.
A2. Rules on indirect infringement are stated at the level of general patent practice (US 35 U.S.C. § 271(b), (c); UK Patents Act 1977 s60(2); national indirect-infringement provisions in EPC states). This is common general knowledge of patent practice, not a legal opinion.
A3. US doctrine that disclosed but unclaimed subject matter is dedicated to the public, and US means-plus-function construction of nonce terms under § 112(f), are assumed as general practice.
A4. The definition of "closed at rest" in [0067] is positional. It is assumed that a court would give it effect as lexicography, but a court might instead hold that it departs from the plain meaning of "closed". That construction risk is reported here for the human and belongs to the clarity review, not to this enforcement prompt.
A5. Detectability ratings assume that the accused product can be bought and sectioned, and that a pressure/travel bench can be built for a valve core of this size.

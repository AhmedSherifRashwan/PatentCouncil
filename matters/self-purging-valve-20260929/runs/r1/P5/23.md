### Dependent Claim Ladders

Drafted under the four claims of `P5/22.r1.md` (claim 1, the valve core; claim 2, the method of use; claim 3, the seat ring; claim 4, the method of manufacture, which refers to claim 3). Dependent claims are numbered continuously from 5. Claim numbers are provisional until Prompt 25.

Support. No stitched description existed when this was written, so no paragraph number can be cited. The support entry of every note cites what the description is being drafted from: "§n" is a section of the inventor's disclosure as relayed by `P2/09` and `CONFIRMED.md`; "C-11", "C-12", "C-13", "C-14" and "C-15" are the parts of `CONFIRMED.md` taken from `11.r1`, `12.r1`, `13`, `14` and `15`; "DA-n" and "FM-n" are its design-around and failure-mode rows; "19" is a definition in `P4/19`; "reg" is `registry.json`, with the figure that shows the numeral. Paragraph anchors are mapped against the stitched description at Prompt 25. A claim marked "candidate" rests on a row of `CONFIRMED.md` that the inventor has not stated in the disclosure; it stays in the set only if the description carries it.

Reference signs are those of `registry.json`. The radially deflectable element is a genus and has no numeral of its own. Ranges are the inventor's §4 ranges and prototype values only. This is a drafting and analysis aid; qualified patent counsel signs off on anything filed.

Each note reads: added limitation | technical effect | support | prior-art or design-around risk answered.

## Rationale

**Order of the first ladder.** The threshold claims come first, the load path second, the form of the element third, and the purge gap, material and window after them, so that each later group can depend on "any one of" the earlier ones and every combination that the prototype embodies is actually claimed (claims 5, 10, 12, 15, 16, 22 to 24, 27 to 29, 33 to 37). The two-piece seat is placed after all of them and depends only on claims 1 and 5 to 14, because a wiper ring that is superelastic, polymer or convoluted is not disclosed (`14`, footnotes 6, 9, 13).

**Claims 5 to 9 before anything else.** `P1/02` row 5 and DA-1 and DA-6 name the two-rate characteristic as the first place a competitor looks. Claim 5 gives the structure; claims 6 to 8 give three constructions; claim 9 takes the DA-1 route itself. I declined the detent (sealant fouling, brass is non-magnetic) and the spring-loaded stop (it is claim 7 with a washer, and the axial length is not shown to exist).

**"Only" restated positively.** Claim 11 says the purge shoulder is spaced from the element in the closed position and throughout the normal stroke. It is a dependent claim, so lesson L1 does not forbid a negative form, but the positive form is observable on a sectioned sample and the negative is not.

**Claim 12.** "Below the sealing face" is the inventor's wording. I did not convert it into "towards the tyre chamber": the dossier does not say which way the poppet moves relative to the seat ring, and choosing one would be inventing a fact.

**Candidates drafted.** The slotted sleeve, the split seat ring, the tapered seat ring, the conical purge shoulder, the core body bore relief and the wall slots all have registry numerals and figures (FIG. 11A to 12B), so the Specification Drafter is describing them. They close DA-3 and DA-5, which `P2/09` rates medium to high. They are drafted and marked candidate. If the inventor rejects one, its claim goes with it and nothing above it changes.

**Ranges.** Only the §4 ranges and the prototype values are claimed. `13` proposes intermediate and preferred bands; they were constructed by analysis and are marked "needs confirmation", so claiming them would put numbers in the inventor's mouth. `P2/08` rates every range as routine optimisation in any case. The seat ring dimensions are in one claim in the "at least one of" form, which gives each range as an amendment basis without four claims.

**Snap-back wipe.** Claims 33, 52 and 69 do not depend on the two-piece seat, because there the wipe acts below the rigid seat, not across the sealing face. They are allowed to depend on the polymer seat ring because the inventor says its wipe is weaker, not absent.

**Claim 50.** Claim 2 of `22` does not recite that the purge gap stays closed through the normal stroke, which claim 1 does. It is the first fallback of the method against ref-02 claim 4.

**Claims 57 and 79.** One referring claim each, instead of repeating a ladder in a second category.

**Not drafted.** Named valve patterns (the names are trade designations, a clarity risk at the EPO, and the mechanism does not depend on the pattern); the spring seat washer load path (layout not dimensioned); retention by a retainer sleeve or staking; the laser-cut strut pattern and the helical slot (cells must be shown closed at rest); the wiping lip and the garter spring; every FM mitigation generated by analysis (spring pre-set, anti-rotation key, alignment chamfer, batch verification, positive stop); mechanical actuation (DA-15); the thermal variant. Each is a disclosure request or is left to the description.

**Terms.** The lock's avoid list is not used anywhere in the Deliverable. Registry names that are not locked terms are declared in `new_terms`.

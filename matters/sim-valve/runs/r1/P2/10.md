## Deliverable

### White-Space Opportunities

Each item points to the disclosure passage (`intake/disclosure.md`, §n) that already supports it; no inventor answers exist yet, so no Gate answer is relied on. "Clear of the charted references" means clear of the sixteen references in `P2/07` only; the set is thin (07 Q1) and a professional search is warranted before filing. Nothing here is a confirmed embodiment: every item is a candidate until a gate confirms it. This is a drafting and analysis aid; qualified patent counsel signs off on anything filed.

1. **Method of cleaning a tyre valve during an inflation cycle (use protocol).** Coupling a pump head, applying a pressure differential above the crack pressure to move the poppet through its normal stroke and above the over-travel differential to drive it into over-travel, the poppet bearing on the seat ring so that radial expansion opens the purge gap and air purge carries the sealant film into the tyre chamber; removing the pump head so that shape recovery closes the purge gap and produces the snap-back wipe.
   - Clear because: no charted reference opens a secondary flow path by deflecting a seat during a second travel range (07 combination analysis; 09 rows 1–3). US3570510A cleans a seat with air only from a separate stored supply (07 chart row N0.3).
   - Support: §2 (the full sequence, "cleans itself twice per inflation"); §3 items 4–6; §8 (inflate to 6 bar with a floor pump, deflate, repeat); bench data notes.
   - Commercial note: a method claim reaches the act of inflating with any floor pump, useful against an imported valve core where the apparatus claim is contested; direct infringement by riders is of limited commercial use, so it is a supporting claim, not the lead.

2. **Replacement valve core as a separately sold unit (disposable/reusable division).** The core body, poppet, return spring and seat ring as a removable threaded valve core fitted into an existing valve stem.
   - Clear because: US1934572A shows the conventional removable core without any deflectable seat (07 chart row F); 09 rows 1–3 distinguish the inner structure.
   - Support: §1 ("cores are sold in multipacks"); §3 items 1–2 (core body threaded to the stem).
   - Commercial note: the core is the consumable riders buy repeatedly and fits stems already on the market, so this is the claim that reads on the product line; it also avoids limiting claims to a whole tyre valve.

3. **The seat ring as a component (sub-combination).** A tubular seat ring with a convoluted wall of one or two convolutions, a sealing edge on its upper end, radial compliance converting axial load into radial expansion, and shape recovery; with the §4 dimensions as dependent ranges.
   - Clear because: EP3171056A1's wave runs radially in a flat static ring and US20040194970A1 expands by wire stimulus (07 charts row N2; 09 row 4).
   - Support: §3 item 3; §4 (seat ring inner diameter, wall thickness, free length, convolution amplitude); §5; §7.
   - Commercial note: lets the company license or supply the seat ring to other valve core makers; weakest against a design-around by another compliant geometry (09 row 4; 09 Q5).

4. **Poppet with a purge shoulder (interface).** A poppet carrying a conical sealing face and, below it, a purge shoulder positioned to engage the seat ring only during over-travel.
   - Clear because: no charted poppet carries a second surface that loads the seat during a later travel range (07 charts row N3).
   - Support: §3 item 4.
   - Commercial note: the poppet–seat ring interface is where a competitor copying the function must copy the geometry; worth a dependent claim set, not an independent one, because the load path could be moved (09 row 3).

5. **Return spring with a two-rate characteristic in a tyre valve core (control scheme).** A first spring stage overcome at the crack pressure for the normal stroke and a second spring stage overcome at the over-travel differential.
   - Clear because: US4044791A reaches two pressure regimes with two separate springs and a rigid floating seat; US1934572A uses a second valve with its own spring (07 charts row N0.1; 09 row 2).
   - Support: §3 item 5; §4 (crack pressure 1.0 bar, range 0.8–1.5 bar; over-travel differential 3.5 bar, range 2.5–5.0 bar).
   - Commercial note: useful as a dependent; its construction and rates are not disclosed (03 feature table, enablement flag), so it cannot stand alone.

6. **Polymer seat ring (Alternative A, lower-cost line).** The same convoluted-wall seat ring in PEEK, giving radial compliance and some snap-back wipe.
   - Clear because: 09 rows 1–4 do not depend on superelasticity; ref-02's elastomer lip enlarges the main passage, not a purge gap (07 chart ref-02 row N0.3).
   - Support: §6 Alternative A.
   - Commercial note: keeps the independent claim above the material, so a cheaper polymer copy is covered; not built, so disclose as an alternative, not as tested.

7. **Two-piece seat (Alternative B).** A rigid seat providing the sealing edge and a separate wiper ring below it, deflected outward by the purge shoulder.
   - Clear because: no charted reference has a second, deflectable ring loaded by the poppet beneath a rigid seat (07 charts rows N0.2, N1).
   - Support: §6 Alternative B ("separates the sealing function from the purge/wipe function").
   - Commercial note: blocks the most obvious design-around of a one-piece seat ring. Its purge path is not described (03 Q4), so it is white space only for the wipe and deflection; the purge path is a question (Q2 below).

8. **Superelastic seat ring with a specified transformation temperature (material window).** Superelastic NiTi alloy with an austenite finish temperature below 5 °C, so the seat ring is superelastic at riding temperatures.
   - Clear because: US9611937B2 and US8100410B2 are static seals with no transformation temperature for a moving-duty valve seat (07 charts row N4; 09 row 6).
   - Support: §2; §4; §5.
   - Commercial note: dependent fallback only; material substitution is an obvious-alternative attack (08 N4).

9. **Manufacture of the seat ring (manufacturing steps).** Laser cutting NiTi tube to the convoluted profile on a mandrel, shape-setting at 500 °C for 10 minutes in a fixture holding the convolution geometry, quenching, electropolishing, then press-fitting against the locating shoulder with an interference fit of about 0.03 mm on diameter.
   - Clear because: the only charted process is generic thermal treatment for superelasticity in US9611937B2 (07 chart row N6; 09 row 8).
   - Support: §7; §5.
   - Commercial note: narrow and easy to design around (09 row 8); worth a short process claim for a supplier-level dispute, and it supports enablement of items 3 and 8.

10. **Corrosion-resistant core body for ammonia-bearing sealant.** A stainless 303 core body variant.
    - Clear because: no charted reference addresses core body material against sealant (07 charts row F).
    - Support: §5 ("a stainless 303 variant was also machined for corrosion resistance with ammonia-bearing sealant").
    - Commercial note: minor dependent; the material itself is conventional.

11. **Thermal variant (hand-warm purge).** A seat ring trained with two-way shape memory so that warming it by hand (austenite finish temperature about 35–40 °C) opens the purge gap without a pump.
    - Clear because: EP3171056A1 uses heating to set a static seal once, not to open a purge gap in a valve (07 chart EP3171056A1 row N4).
    - Support: §6, final paragraph only ("We are not sure this is worth pursuing and have no data").
    - Commercial note: thin support; disclose briefly as an alternative so it is not left to competitors, and do not claim it independently unless the inventor develops it.

## Rationale

Items 1–3 carry most of the commercial value: the method reaches the act of inflating, the replacement core is the consumable, and the seat ring is the part other makers would buy. They are listed first for that reason, not because they are the strongest over the art; items 1 and 2 inherit the 09 rows 1–3 distinctions, while item 3 rests on the geometry distinction that 09 rated medium–high for design-around.

Alternatives A and B are listed because they keep the independent claim above the seat ring material and the one-piece construction. Alt-B's purge path is not described, so only its deflection and wipe are white space; the purge path is raised as a question rather than filled in.

The audible snap on pump-head removal (bench data notes) was considered as an indicator feature and excluded: it is an observation, not a feature the inventor intends, so it would be new matter to claim it as an indicator. It is raised as a question.

CO₂ inflation and fatigue (§9) are known unknowns, not white space.

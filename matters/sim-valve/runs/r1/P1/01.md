### Structured Invention Disclosure

Source of every fact below: `intake/disclosure.md` (cited as §n). `intake/data/bench-test.md` (cited as "bench data") and `intake/drawings/README.md` (cited as "drawings README", file names and captions only; the images themselves are not present) are used as corroboration only. Anything inferred rather than read is labelled *inferred* and listed under Assumptions. This is a drafting and analysis aid; qualified patent counsel signs off on anything filed.

#### (a) Problem being solved

- Liquid sealant (typically a latex emulsion with ammonia and suspended particles) is used in bicycle inner tubes and tubeless set-ups and is injected through the valve stem (§1).
- On each inflation a small quantity of sealant is drawn into the valve core bore by the pressure surge and by capillary action along the poppet stem; between rides the film dries (§1).
- After ten to twenty inflation cycles the core seat is coated with cured latex and the valve either leaks slowly (the poppet no longer seats) or blocks (the pump cannot open it) (§1). Bench data corroborates: conventional cores failed at cycles 12, 14 and 18, two by blocking and one by leak-down.
- The current remedy is manual: remove the core with a tool, scrape or replace it, re-inject sealant (§1).
- Existing approaches named by the inventor — low-adhesion polymer coating on the poppet, an enlarged bore, a removable insert — slow the build-up but do not remove a film once formed (§1). The inventor gives no references for these; they are the inventor's characterisation only.

#### (b) Core inventive concept

A valve core in which the poppet, when driven by a pressure differential above a threshold, travels beyond its normal opening stroke ("over-travel"); during over-travel a purge shoulder on the poppet bears on a radially compliant seat ring, which expands radially and opens a secondary annular purge gap, so that air at pump pressure flows across the surfaces on which sealant accumulates and carries the sealant film into the tube (§2). On removal of the pump the differential collapses, the poppet returns and the seat ring recovers its shape; in the superelastic NiTi embodiment the recovery is fast and stiff, and the inventor states that the recovering ring wipes across the poppet face and shears off partially cured latex (§2).

The inventor frames this as two cleaning actions per inflation: (1) air flow through the purge gap; (2) a mechanical wipe on snap-back (§2).

Three features carry the concept as disclosed:
1. a second, pressure-triggered poppet travel range beyond the normal stroke (§2, §3 item 5);
2. a seat element that converts axial load from the poppet into radial expansion that opens a flow path (§2, §3 item 3);
3. a seat element that recovers its shape when the load is removed (§2).

Whether feature 3 must be *superelastic* recovery, or whether any elastic recovery suffices, is not settled by the disclosure: §6 Alternative A (PEEK) says polymer recovery gives "some" radial compliance and a "weaker" wipe, and Alternative B separates the sealing function from the purge/wipe function entirely. See (m) M1.

#### (c) Technical effect

- Stated: the valve keeps sealing and keeps opening after far more sealant-loaded inflation cycles than a conventional core, with no tool and no rider action (§2, last paragraph).
- Measured (§8, bench data): three prototype cores completed 40 cycles without failure; three conventional cores failed between cycles 12 and 18. Prototype mean inflation time at cycle 40 was within 15 % of cycle 1 (bench data: 43→47 s, 41→46 s, 44→50 s). Leak-down at cycle 40: 0.1, 0.1, 0.2 bar/24 h against a failure threshold of 0.5 bar.
- Observed on disassembly (§8, bench data): sealing edge and poppet face clean at cycle 40; cured film present on the outside of the seat ring in the purge-gap region.
- The attribution of the effect to the two separate cleaning actions (air purge; snap-back wipe) is the inventor's explanation (§2). The test does not separate them: no variant with purge only or wipe only was tested. See (m) M9.

Operating sequence (§2):
1. At rest: sealing face (42) seated on the upper edge of the seat ring (30); purge gap (60) closed.
2. Normal inflation: differential exceeds the crack pressure; poppet lifts through the normal stroke against the first spring stage.
3. Over-travel: differential exceeds a higher threshold; poppet overcomes the second spring stage and travels beyond the normal stroke; purge shoulder (44) bears on the seat ring (30); seat ring expands radially; purge gap (60) opens; air flows through the purge gap into the tube, alongside the main passage (drawings README, FIG-2 caption).
4. Pump removal: differential collapses; poppet returns; seat ring recovers its shape and, per the inventor, wipes across the poppet face; purge gap closes.

#### (g) Inputs / outputs

- Input: pressure differential applied by a pump head across the valve (§2). Two thresholds: crack pressure (first spring stage) and over-travel differential (second spring stage) (§3 item 5, §4).
- Input: pump-head removal, collapsing the differential (§2).
- Output: air flow into the tube through the main passage, and during over-travel also through the purge gap (§2; drawings README FIG-2 caption).
- Output: sealant film displaced from the sealing surfaces into the tube (§2).
- Output: audible snap on pump-head removal, used in the bench test to confirm over-travel occurred (bench data, notes). Whether the snap is intended as a user-facing indicator is not stated.
- The inventor states that every floor pump reaches the over-travel threshold (§2). No pump data is given. See (m) M7.

#### (i) Control logic / software

None. The device is purely mechanical; the "logic" is the two-threshold behaviour set by the two-rate return spring (first stage: normal opening at the crack pressure; second stage: over-travel at the higher differential) (§3 item 5, §4). No electronics, sensing or software are disclosed.

#### (j) Manufacturing methods

Seat ring (§7):
1. Laser-cut from NiTi tube (2.7 mm OD, 0.20 mm wall) to the convoluted profile, on a mandrel.
2. Shape-set at 500 °C for 10 minutes in a fixture holding the wave geometry.
3. Water-quench.
4. Electropolish to remove the oxide and the heat-affected edge.

Assembly (§7): seat ring pressed into the core body with approx. 0.03 mm interference on diameter and seated against the internal shoulder.

Other parts (§7): ordinary turning for core body and poppet; "no other special process". Spring manufacture and how the two-rate characteristic is produced (variable pitch, two springs in series, or otherwise) are not described.

The phrase "laser-cut … to the convoluted profile" (§7) does not say whether the convolution is formed by cutting (a slotted or patterned wall) or by the shape-setting fixture deforming a plain tube wall into a wave. See (m) M3.

#### (k) Use cases

- Bicycle inner tubes charged with liquid sealant (§1, §8).
- Tubeless tyre set-ups run with sealant (§1).
- Inflation with a floor pump (§2, §8); CO₂ cartridge inflation is a raised concern, not a tested use (§9).
- Lower-cost product line using Alt-A (§6).
- No non-bicycle application (other tyres, other sealant-exposed valves) is mentioned.

#### (m) Unresolved technical questions

Each is repeated in Open Questions with the phases it blocks.

- **M1 — Is superelasticity essential?** The disclosure presents NiTi superelastic recovery as the source of the snap-back wipe (§2), yet describes Alt-A (PEEK, weaker wipe) and Alt-B (stainless spring wiper, sealing separated) as working options. Is the effect achieved, in the inventor's view, by (i) the air purge alone, (ii) the wipe alone, or (iii) only both together? This decides whether the seat ring material is part of the core concept.
- **M2 — Purge gap location and the interference fit.** §3 item 6 places the purge gap between the seat ring outer surface and the core body bore; §2 says "between the ring and the stem bore". §3 item 3 and §7 say the ring is pressed in with a light interference fit. How can the outer surface both carry an interference fit and open a clearance on expansion? Is the fit at one end (at the shoulder) only, with a free portion that expands toward a relief in the bore? Which bore — core body (20) or stem (10)?
- **M3 — Convolution geometry.** One or two convolutions — which did the prototype have? Is the wave formed in the wall section by shape-setting, or cut as a pattern by laser? Does the wave run circumferentially (a bellows-like section) or axially? Is the ring continuous or slotted?
- **M4 — Where the air flows during purge.** Where does air enter the purge gap and where does it exit into the tube? Does it pass over the sealing edge, the poppet face, the poppet stem, or only the ring outer surface? The cured film found on the ring outer surface at cycle 40 (§8) suggests the purge gap region itself accumulates sealant.
- **M5 — Wipe mechanism.** Which surface of the seat ring contacts which surface of the poppet during recovery, and in which direction does it move? Is there evidence of the wipe beyond the clean sealing edge and poppet face at cycle 40?
- **M6 — Two-rate spring construction.** How is the two-rate characteristic produced (variable pitch, two springs, nested springs, a spring plus a stop)? Spring rates for each stage? Could the over-travel threshold be set by something other than a two-rate spring (for example the seat ring's own stiffness)?
- **M7 — Over-travel threshold versus pumps.** Evidence for "every floor pump reaches" 2.5–5.0 bar differential across the valve at the point of use? Do mini-pumps, compressors or CO₂ heads reach it? Is over-travel expected on every inflation, or only above a certain tyre pressure?
- **M8 — Basis for the §4 ranges.** The ranges are "the ranges we believe would work". Is any range endpoint supported by a build, a calculation or a failed sample? Normal poppet stroke has no range.
- **M9 — Separating the two cleaning actions.** No test isolates the air purge from the wipe. Is any such data available, or planned?
- **M10 — Fatigue life** (§9 item 1): behaviour beyond 40 over-travel cycles; residual strain; whether the purge gap stays closed at rest after long service. Target life ("several thousand") — is there a specific number?
- **M11 — Cold behaviour** (§9 item 2): whether Af below 5 °C is sufficient for CO₂ inflation; whether a cold-shocked ring stays deformed until it warms; martensite start temperature of the material used is not given.
- **M12 — Ammonia-free sealants** (§9): untested.
- **M13 — Valve standards.** Is the concept limited to Presta-pattern cores? Is a Schrader-pattern or other core contemplated? Is a removable core essential, or could the elements be integral with the stem?
- **M14 — Alt-C thermal variant.** Two-way shape memory, hand-warm purge: include as an embodiment or drop? No data.
- **M15 — Missing dimensions and tolerances.** Poppet dimensions, sealing face cone angle, purge shoulder geometry, core body bore dimensions, spring dimensions, and any tolerances are not given.
- **M16 — Electropolishing role.** Is electropolishing only for removing oxide and the heat-affected edge, or does surface finish contribute to the clean sealing edge?
- **M17 — Stainless 303 variant.** Was it tested with sealant, or only machined?

## Rationale

The prompt makes `disclosure.md` the sole source of technical fact, so every row cites a section, and the bench data is used only to corroborate §8 and to add the per-core figures that §8 summarises. The drawings README captions are cited only for reference numerals and for the flow arrows in FIG-2; the images are absent from the fixture and nothing is said about them beyond the captions.

Component names follow the inventor's own terms (seat ring, poppet, purge shoulder, purge gap, over-travel) because they are functional and neutral enough for Prompt 04 to adopt or re-label; no new names were coined for the components. The two terms I introduced are "over-travel differential" (to distinguish the second threshold from the crack pressure) and "snap-back wipe" (the inventor's own phrases "snap-back" and "wiping action" combined); both are declared in `new_terms`.

The core concept is stated in functional terms (radially compliant seat ring, shape recovery) with NiTi as the prototype, because the disclosure itself describes a polymer and a two-piece alternative. Whether material-level superelasticity is essential is the single most scope-relevant question and is put first (M1), not resolved.

The §2/§3 discrepancy on the purge-gap boundary and the apparent conflict between an interference fit and an outer-surface clearance (M2) are raised rather than reconciled; a smooth reading would have required inventing geometry.

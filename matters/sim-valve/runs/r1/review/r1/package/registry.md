### Figure Instructions

General drafting notes applying to all figures: line drawings in patent-drawing style (black and white, uniform line weight, no shading except standard section hatching); axis of the tyre valve horizontal in all axial-section views; lead lines end in an arrowhead at the feature and a plain terminus at the numeral; hatching direction for the core body (20) differs from that for the seat ring (30) and the poppet (40) so the three parts read as separate components in section; flow arrows are open arrows, unnumbered, distinguished from lead lines by a broken shaft.

#### FIG. 1 — axial section, at rest

Full longitudinal section through the tyre valve axis. Draw, left to right: valve stem (10) as a stepped tube; core body (20) threaded into the valve stem's bore; the core body bore (22) as the internal cylindrical surface of the core body; the locating shoulder (24) as an internal step near the tyre-chamber end of the core body; the main passage (26) as the annular flow path between the poppet (40) and the core body bore (22) when the poppet is lifted (draw the poppet seated for this figure, so the main passage is shown as a labelled path, not an open gap); the seat ring (30) pressed against the locating shoulder (24), its sealing edge (32) at the tyre-chamber end and its convoluted wall (34) with one convolution (36) between the sealing edge and the fit band; the poppet (40) with its sealing face (42) seated on the sealing edge (32) and its purge shoulder (44) spaced away from the seat ring; the return spring (50) coiled around the poppet stem between a spring seat on the poppet and a spring seat in the core body, drawn with visibly closer coils at the pump-head end (first spring stage, 52) and visibly wider coils at the tyre-chamber end (second spring stage, 54); the purge gap (60) as a thin closed line (zero width, or a single hairline) between the seat ring outer surface and the core body bore; the lock nut (70) threaded onto the poppet stem outside the core body; the tyre chamber (80) as a broken-line boundary at the right of the section; the pump head (90) drawn in broken outline, not engaged, above the valve stem's outer end, labelled as the source of the pressure differential across the poppet. Every numeral above gets its own lead line; 42 and 44 lead to the two distinct faces of the poppet, not to the poppet body generally (which carries 40).

#### FIG. 2 — axial section, in over-travel

Same section and same numerals as FIG. 1, redrawn with: the poppet (40) displaced further from the sealing edge (32) than in FIG. 1, past the normal stroke; the purge shoulder (44) touching the seat ring (30) at the fit-band end of the convoluted wall (34); the seat ring's convolution (36) shown flattened/opened relative to FIG. 1, its outer surface displaced outward so the purge gap (60) is drawn as an open annular clearance with visible width; the return spring (50) compressed further, its second spring stage (54) coils shown closed up; the pump head (90) drawn in solid outline, engaged on the valve stem (10). Add two broken-shaft flow arrows: one through the main passage (26) into the tyre chamber (80), one through the open purge gap (60) into the tyre chamber (80) carrying the air purge. No new numerals.

#### FIG. 3 — exploded perspective

Isometric view, components separated along the valve axis in assembly order, left to right: core body (20), seat ring (30), poppet (40) with its return spring (50) already concentric on the poppet stem, lock nut (70). Each component gets one lead line to its numeral; do not repeat 32, 34, 42 or 44 in this view unless legible at this scale — if shown, letter them as in FIG. 1.

#### FIG. 4 — seat ring detail, two panels

FIG. 4A: enlarged axial half-section of the seat ring (30) with one convolution. Show the sealing edge (32) at the top; the convoluted wall (34) with its single convolution (36); the fit band (35) as a shaded/cross-hatched short band of the outer surface adjacent to the locating-shoulder end, drawn at a slightly larger outer diameter than the rest of the wall to indicate the interference fit; the free portion (37) as the remaining length of the outer surface, drawn with a small radial clearance already shown to the (phantom, broken-line) core body bore. Dimension lines (unnumbered) for wall thickness, free length and convolution amplitude, each with a lower-case letter callout (t, L, a) rather than a reference numeral.
FIG. 4B: identical to 4A but with two convolutions in series along the wall (36 for the convolution nearer the sealing edge, 38 for the second, nearer the fit band). Reuse 30, 32, 34, 35, 37 unchanged.

#### FIG. 5 — return-spring characteristic (schematic graph)

Not a mechanical section; a simple x–y plot. Horizontal axis: travel axis (402), labelled in poppet travel from zero (sealing face seated) increasing to the right; mark and bracket the normal stroke (114) as the first segment of the axis and the over-travel (116) as the following segment. Vertical axis: force axis (404), labelled in force on the poppet. Plot a single curve: a shallow-slope line from the origin, labelled as the first spring stage (52), rising to a point marked crack pressure (110) at the end of the normal-stroke bracket; then a steeper-slope line, labelled as the second spring stage (54), continuing to a point marked over-travel differential (112) at the end of the over-travel bracket. Add a caption note: "schematic; the two-rate characteristic may be produced by a single spring or by more than one spring or biasing element."

#### FIG. 6 — use sequence, four panels

Four small axial half-sections (or a single figure in four labelled quadrants), each a simplified version of the FIG. 1/FIG. 2 section showing only the poppet (40), sealing face (42), purge shoulder (44), seat ring (30) and purge gap (60), captioned and numbered as sequential steps: at rest (500) — sealing face (42) on the seat ring (30), purge gap (60) closed; normal stroke (502) — poppet (40) lifted off the sealing edge, purge gap (60) still closed; over-travel (504) — purge shoulder (44) on the seat ring (30), purge gap (60) open, flow arrow shown; return (506) — poppet (40) returning, seat ring (30) recovering (draw a short curved arrow at the seat ring indicating its shape recovery and snap-back wipe), purge gap (60) closing. Arrows between the four panels show the sequence order.

#### FIG. 7 — manufacturing sequence, flow diagram

Five boxes in series, connected by arrows, each captioned and numbered: laser cutting (510) — a schematic tube with the convoluted profile being cut; shape-setting (512) — the cut tube held in a fixture at the convolution geometry; quenching (514) — the fixture/part shown entering a liquid bath; electropolishing (516) — the part shown in an electrolytic bath; press-fitting (518) — the finished seat ring (30) being pressed into the core body (20) against the locating shoulder (24). Use 30, 20, 24 as already registered; no new numerals besides the five step numbers.

**Inconsistency flags**

1. `description.md` and `claims.md` do not yet exist in this run (Prompt 21 onward has not run), so the cross-check of this registry against the description text and the claims is deferred; it must be re-run with `pc numerals` once both exist.
2. Deliberately unnumbered: sealant and sealant film (substances acted upon, not structure); inflation cycle (a temporal event, not structure). Numbering them would misrepresent a material or an event as a drawn part.
3. Numeral 146 (purge gap, FIG. 9) and numeral 60 (purge gap, FIGS. 1, 2, 8, 10) name the same locked term for two structurally different arrangements — an annular clearance around the seat ring outer surface (60) versus a clearance below a separate wiper ring in the two-piece-seat alternative (146). This is a deliberate choice, not a numeral collision: the two-piece seat is a different embodiment with a different purge-gap location, so it is given its own numeral rather than reusing 60 for a physically different clearance.
4. FIGS. 11, 12 and 13A number candidate constructions that are not in the inventor's disclosure and are not built or tested (Phase 3 design-around analysis only). Their numerals and captions say so; whether any of them is carried into the claimed embodiments, or held as description-only disclosure, is Open Question Q5 of Prompt 20 and remains open here.
5. FIG. 5's plotted elements (110, 112, 114, 116, and the segments 52/54) label a schematic graph, not drawn hardware; flagged so the draftsperson does not attempt to render them as physical parts on a mechanical sheet.
6. No numeral is registered for the pump head's own internal structure (90 numbers only its outline); it is drawn as a conventional external device and is not part of the claimed valve core.

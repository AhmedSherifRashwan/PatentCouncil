## Findings

### Moat Scores

These scores are a drafting and analysis aid, not legal advice and not a valuation. Qualified patent counsel reviews them before any filing decision.

Scale: 1 is weakest and 10 is strongest. Each score is a judgement on the text of the package and on the saved references listed in `prior-art/INDEX.md`. The index states that its search was thin and that nothing in it reads on the whole nucleus, so the two resilience scores are provisional in both directions. The commercially successful architecture is taken to be the one form that was built and tested: a removable threaded valve core with a one-piece seat ring of superelastic NiTi alloy, a purge shoulder on the poppet and a return spring with a two-rate characteristic ([0279] to [0281]).

**Claim 1.** Commercial relevance is high because the claim is to the article sold, "A valve core (5) for a tyre valve (1)", and is not limited to a valve pattern, a material or a means of actuation. Breadth is held at 6 by three words and one condition: "radially deflectable", "radial expansion", "secondary flow path", and "spaced from the radially deflectable element throughout the normal stroke". Novelty resilience is 6: no saved reference shows a load applied by the poppet to an element that opens a second path, but the search was thin. Inventive-step resilience is 4: saved references 2, 8 and 15 show staged opening and a gap opened at a ring, saved references 6, 7 and 19 show the problem, and the package does not show that the purge gap contributes to the result, since [0287] reports cured sealant film "on the outer surface of the seat ring 30 in the region of the purge gap 60" and [0289] says no test isolates either cleaning action. Design-around resistance is 3: three routes leave the claim, each cheaper than the claimed form (F-35-1, F-35-2, F-35-3), and saved references 6 and 19 show that the commercial result can also be reached by a larger bore or by keeping sealant out. Detectability is 5: the article is sold openly and can be sectioned, but "the radial expansion opening a purge gap" happens only under load inside the core body. Evidentiary ease is 4 for the same reason and because absence of contact must be proved. Support strength is 6: one form was built and tested; every range is untested ([0230]) and every other form is prophetic ([0184]).

**Claim 2.** The steps "moving the poppet through an over-travel (116)" and "passing air through the open purge gap into the tyre chamber" are performed by a rider at each inflation. Commercial relevance is 4 because the claim reaches a supplier only indirectly. Detectability and evidentiary ease are 2: each act is private, whether it occurs depends on the pump ([0273], [0274]), and flow through the gap cannot be observed in use. The claim shares the features of claim 1, so its breadth and resilience scores follow claim 1.

**Claim 3.** The claim reaches the component maker and the replacement part ([0020], [0162]), which claim 1 does not. It is defined by what happens in a valve core that the claim does not define: "when the seat ring is installed in the valve core, the radial expansion opens a purge gap (60)". Novelty resilience is 4 and inventive-step resilience is 3 because, read as a statement of suitability, the claim may be met by a tubular resilient ring with a wall that is not straight; saved references 4, 12 and 17 show rings and seats with wave-shaped or undulating sections and rings of superelastic alloy under axial compression. Design-around resistance is 3: "a sealing edge (32) at one end of the seat ring" and "a wall having a load-converting geometry" are both avoided by parts the description itself describes (F-35-5). Detectability is 6 because the convoluted wall is visible at rest.

**Claim 4.** The claim is "A method of manufacturing the seat ring (30) of claim 3, the seat ring being of a NiTi alloy, the method comprising shape-setting (512) the seat ring in a fixture that holds the load-converting geometry". It depends on claim 3, so it reaches no maker whom claim 3 does not already reach. The step is a factory step that leaves no distinguishing mark in the product. Saved references 11 and 18 show the process chain for NiTi alloy in other fields, so the claim adds little resilience if claim 3 falls. Support strength is the highest of the four, 7, because the process values are those actually used ([0250], [0255]).

**Which claims protect the commercially successful architecture.** Claim 1 does, and within it the dependent claims that a competitor's copy of the prototype would infringe and that can be proved from a product at rest: claims 20 to 24 (seat ring with a convoluted wall), 26 (fit band, free portion and core body bore relief), 27 to 29 (superelastic NiTi alloy), 13 and 14 (purge shoulder) and 6 to 8 (two-rate characteristic, variable pitch spring). Claim 3 protects the seat ring of that architecture as a part. Claims 2 and 4 and their dependants are technically interesting and carry little enforcement weight. No claim protects a form in which the seat deflects and recovers without a secondary flow path, which is the form a competitor would choose after reading [0287].

The findings below arise from the low scores for design-around resistance and for detectability. Each is the same objection as the finding of the same title in 33b, restated here against the score, and carries the same title, category and claim references.

### F-35-1 [high] [design-around] No independent claim covers cleaning by deflection of the seat without a secondary flow path

Score concerned: design-around resistance, 3, for claims 1, 2 and 3.

Claim language attacked. Claim 1: "the purge gap is a secondary flow path that is distinct from the main passage". The snap-back wipe is recited only in dependent claims 32, 56 and 76.

Attack. [0064] and [0126] state that a flow path enlarged by deflection of the seat remains the main passage. [0287] reports clean sealing surfaces and cured sealant film on the purge gap surface. A competitor keeps the over-travel and a seat that deflects and recovers, removes the purge gap, and is outside every claim.

Least narrowing action. Add an independent claim to the snap-back wipe with no purge gap recited. Basis for the two-piece seat: [0182], [0294], [0347]. The one-piece seat ring needs a statement from the inventors.

New disclosure required: yes. Target P3.

### F-35-2 [high] [design-around] Axially deflecting and tilting elements are described but lie outside every claim

Score concerned: design-around resistance, 3, for claims 1, 2 and 3.

Claim language attacked. Claim 1: "an axial load that produces a radial expansion of the radially deflectable element".

Attack. [0179] describes a disc spring whose rim lifts from a face and states that "its deflection is not a radial expansion". The form is disclosed, unclaimed and expressly distinguished from the claims.

Least narrowing action. Claim "a deflectable element" and "a deflection", with radial expansion in a dependent claim. Basis: [0179], [0180].

New disclosure required: no. Target P5.

### F-35-3 [high] [design-around] Contact from lift-off avoids "spaced from" and the normal stroke

Score concerned: design-around resistance, 3, and evidentiary ease, 4, for claims 1, 2 and 3.

Claim language attacked. Claim 1: "wherein the load-applying surface is spaced from the radially deflectable element throughout the normal stroke".

Attack. [0071] and [0072] define the two ranges of travel by first contact alone. A surface in light contact from the closed position leaves no normal stroke and no over-travel as defined. The claimant must prove an absence of contact.

Least narrowing action. Add a formulation that defines the ranges by the state of the purge gap and by the load. The inventors must confirm that contact below the opening load is within their conception.

New disclosure required: yes. Target P3.

### F-35-4 [medium] [design-around] Claim 2 is performed by the rider and reaches a supplier only indirectly

Score concerned: infringement detectability, 2, for claim 2.

Claim language attacked. Claim 2: "passing air through the open purge gap into the tyre chamber".

Attack. Every step is a private act of a user ([0273]). Whether the over-travel is reached depends on the pump ([0274]). A supplier is liable only indirectly.

Least narrowing action. Keep claim 2 and rely on the product claims. [0265] supports a claim to the manufacturer's test of each valve core.

New disclosure required: no. Target P5.

### F-35-5 [medium] [design-around] Claim 3 is avoided by a part that has no sealing edge or no load-converting geometry of its own

Score concerned: design-around resistance, 3, for claim 3.

Claim language attacked. Claim 3: "a sealing edge (32) at one end of the seat ring" and "a wall having a load-converting geometry".

Attack. The wiper ring 144 has no sealing edge ([0164]). A plain ring spread by a conical surface has no load-converting geometry of its own ([0165]). Neither part is reached by claim 3.

Least narrowing action. Add an independent claim to a radially deflectable element as a part. Basis: [0099], [0164], [0165].

New disclosure required: no. Target P5.

### F-35-6 [low] [design-around] The shape-setting step of claim 4 cannot be seen in the product and is avoided by another forming route

Score concerned: infringement detectability, 2, and design-around resistance, 2, for claim 4.

Claim language attacked. Claim 4: "shape-setting (512) the seat ring in a fixture that holds the load-converting geometry".

Attack. The step leaves no distinguishing mark in the ring and is avoided by cutting the geometry into tube that is already superelastic ([0171], [0254]).

Least narrowing action. Add a method claim in which the geometry is formed by laser cutting a pattern in the wall. Treat the manufacturing claims as fallback positions.

New disclosure required: no. Target P5.

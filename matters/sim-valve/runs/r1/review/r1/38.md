### F-38-1 [medium] [novelty] Description admits a structurally similar prior-art feature and distinguishes only on effect

**Paragraph attacked:** [0166], "A wavy or undulating shape of a resilient metal seal is described in US11549591B1, where the shape pre-loads a seat interface to provide a seating and sealing force. The load-converting geometry described here has a different effect: it acts when the poppet is in over-travel, and its deformation opens the purge gap through which the air purge passes."

**Attack.** This paragraph is a direct admission that a wavy/undulating resilient-metal-seal geometry — structurally the same genus of feature as the claimed convoluted wall (claims 1, 3, 21, 62) — is already known from US11549591B1. The distinction offered is entirely functional (what the deformation is used *for*), not structural (what the wall *is*). An examiner combining US11549591B1 with any of the valve documents already discussed in the Background (for example US5918628A or US4044791A, both showing further poppet travel opening a further flow path, or US5924444A, showing a flow-induced radial deformation opening a gap) has, in the applicant's own words, a structural teaching for the load-converting wall and only needs a motivation to apply it to a poppet-actuated purge function — a combination an examiner will treat as obvious to try, precisely because the specification itself frames the difference as one of application rather than construction. This is exactly the kind of self-inflicted admission (MPEP 2129) that strengthens an obviousness rejection the applicant would otherwise have to be argued into.

**Least narrowing action.** No narrowing. Rewrite [0166] to add a structural distinction, not only a functional one — for example, noting that US11549591B1's wavy shape maintains continuous contact/pre-load at a seat interface at all times, whereas the convoluted wall here is spaced from the load-applying surface throughout the normal stroke and is undeformed at rest, or noting any other structural difference the inventor can confirm (e.g., the seal of US11549591B1 is not itself the sealing element against which a separate poppet seats). This adds clarifying disclosure rather than conceding a common genus.

**Category / severity.** `novelty`; `medium` — this does not defeat any claim on its own (the claims recite the over-travel/purge-gap combination that the paragraph does distinguish), but it is a citable admission an examiner or opponent will quote verbatim, and the fix is cheap relative to the risk.

**Requires new disclosure:** Yes (a structural distinction must come from the inventor's actual knowledge of US11549591B1's construction). **Suggested target:** `P4`.

### F-38-2 [medium] [support] Enablement caveat for a disclosed species undercuts the breadth of a claim reciting the genus

**Paragraph attacked:** [0206], "In some embodiments the seat ring 30 is made of a copper-based alloy that is superelastic, for example a Cu–Al–Ni alloy or a Cu–Zn–Al alloy. The recoverable strain of such alloys is lower than that of NiTi alloy, and their compatibility with an ammonia-bearing sealant is not established."

**Attack.** Claim 27 ("wherein the seat ring is of a superelastic material") and claim 70 (its seat-ring-claim counterpart) claim the material genus without restriction to NiTi. [0206] discloses copper-based superelastic alloys as a species within that genus, then states in the same breath that this species' compatibility with the sealant the invention is expressly for ([0002]: "a liquid sealant is commonly introduced... typically a latex emulsion containing ammonia") "is not established." An examiner reviewing enablement/written description for the full breadth of "a superelastic material" can point to the applicant's own words as evidence that at least one disclosed species may not work for its stated purpose, undermining enablement across the genus as claimed (Wands factors; the same concern would arise under Article 83/84 EPC for sufficiency across the claimed breadth).

**Least narrowing action.** No narrowing required. Add a sentence to [0206] (or to the genus definition at [0058]/[0099]) making clear that the load-conversion function claimed does not depend on sealant compatibility — sealant compatibility is a material-selection question separate from the mechanical superelastic-recovery function the claim actually requires — so the untested compatibility does not, on the specification's own terms, bear on whether the claimed function is enabled. This is a clarifying addition, not new subject matter.

**Category / severity.** `support`; `medium` — a genuine breadth-versus-disclosure tension worth fixing before an examiner raises it, not a fatal defect since the core NiTi and PEEK species are separately, fully supported.

**Requires new disclosure:** No (clarification of existing disclosure). **Suggested target:** `P4`.

### F-38-3 [medium] [formal] FIG. 13A and its registered numeral (220) are absent from the filed specification

**Paragraph attacked:** [0049], "FIG. 13B is an axial section of an embodiment in which the valve core is integral with the valve stem." (No corresponding sentence for FIG. 13A appears anywhere in the Brief Description of the Drawings, [0036]–[0049], and no paragraph in the Detailed Description ([0107]–[0296]) mentions FIG. 13A or numeral 220; a search of the whole description for "220" and "Schrader" returns no hits outside this audit.)

**Attack.** The reference-numeral registry supplied with this package registers numeral 220 ("Schrader-pattern core body") as first appearing in FIG. 13A, and the figure-instructions document describes FIG. 13A in detail (a Schrader-pattern candidate core body). Neither the figure nor the numeral is mentioned anywhere in the actual specification text. 37 CFR 1.74 / MPEP 608.01(f) require the brief description to describe every figure of the drawings; a filed application with a drawing sheet showing FIG. 13A and no corresponding text would draw an objection for an undescribed figure, and conversely, if FIG. 13A is not to be filed at all, the registry and figure-instructions documents are simply wrong to still carry it forward as a live figure. Either way, the package as it stands is internally inconsistent between what the drawings package proposes to show and what the specification actually describes.

**Least narrowing action.** If FIG. 13A (the Schrader-pattern candidate) is to be retained in the drawings: add a Brief Description sentence for it and a short Detailed Description passage describing the Schrader-pattern core body at numeral 220, parallel to the treatment already given to FIG. 13B at [0159]. If it is not to be retained: remove FIG. 13A and numeral 220 from the figure instructions and the reference-numeral registry so the drawings package matches the specification. Either correction is mechanical; no claim is affected since numeral 220 does not appear in any claim.

**Category / severity.** `formal`; `medium` — not a claim defect, but a real drawing/description mismatch that must be resolved one way or the other before the application can be filed as a consistent package.

**Requires new disclosure:** Only if FIG. 13A is retained (`P3`/`P4`); otherwise no (a registry/figure-plan edit, `in-place`). **Suggested target:** `P4`.

### F-38-4 [low] [formal] The locked terminology table has no row for several claimed terms; the deterministic terms check's one warning is a false positive that masks the real gap

**Paragraph attacked:** `terminology/LOCKED.md`, Terminology Table (no row for "slotted sleeve", "split seat ring", "tapered seat ring", "matching taper" or "Schrader-pattern core body"), against description [0176]–[0178] and claims 39–44, which use exactly these terms.

**Attack.** Running the terminology check against the claims (`pc terms --locked terminology/LOCKED.md --reply claims.md --kind analysis`) returns one warning: an undeclared-synonym hit on "split seat" in claim 42, matched against the "two-piece seat" row's synonym column ("split seat; two-part seat"). On inspection this is a false positive: claim 42's "split seat ring (180)" is the FIG. 11B candidate genus species (an axially-split ring opened by a poppet cone), a structurally different thing from the locked "two-piece seat" (a separate rigid seat (140) plus wiper ring (144), FIG. 9), and the two are independently registered under different numerals (180 vs. 140/144) and never conflated in the text. The checker's clean result on this one term should not be read as clearing the terminology of these claims: "slotted sleeve", "split seat ring", "tapered seat ring" and "matching taper" are used in claims 39–44 and description [0176]–[0178] without ever having been locked at Gate 1 at all. `LOCKED.md`'s own Note 1 records that the table was prepared before Prompt 03 and that "any 03 feature without a row must be added before the table is locked" — these candidate-geometry terms, introduced during design-around analysis after the seat-ring family was first described, are exactly the kind of feature that note anticipates and the table does not yet cover.

**Least narrowing action.** Add rows to `terminology/LOCKED.md` for "slotted sleeve", "split seat ring", "tapered seat ring", "matching taper" (all claimed) and "Schrader-pattern core body" (disclosed, unclaimed, but present in the registry) with their preferred terms, synonyms and functional descriptions, so the lock is complete for every term actually in the filed text. This is a lock-table correction; it does not itself require the description or claims to change wording, since neither drifts from a locked preferred term (there is no locked term yet to drift from).

**Category / severity.** `formal`; `low` — the lock itself, not the specification text, is incomplete; the text is internally consistent in how it uses these terms, so the risk is process (an un-locked term could drift silently in a later revision), not present-tense ambiguity.

**Requires new disclosure:** No. **Suggested target:** `in-place` (terminology table maintained by the agent that owns Gate 1).

### F-38-5 [low] [formal] The reviewed registry file carries a stale drafting-process note inconsistent with the package's actual state

**Paragraph attacked:** `registry.md`, "Inconsistency flags", item 1: "`description.md` and `claims.md` do not yet exist in this run (Prompt 21 onward has not run), so the cross-check of this registry against the description text and the claims is deferred; it must be re-run with `pc numerals` once both exist."

**Attack.** This note is factually stale: both `description.md` and `claims.md` are present in this package and have in fact been cross-checked against the registry (cleanly — no unregistered numerals, no orphaned registry numerals against the figures, no unnumbered claim components). A document handed to a blind reviewer should not carry forward an internal statement about the drafting timeline that is no longer true and that, if taken at face value, would incorrectly suggest the numeral cross-check has never been performed on the current text.

**Least narrowing action.** Remove or update this flag in the registry document once description and claims exist and the cross-check has been run, so the copy that reaches review reflects the current state of the package rather than an earlier drafting-phase snapshot.

**Category / severity.** `formal`; `low` — a package-hygiene item, not a defect in the specification or claims themselves.

**Requires new disclosure:** No. **Suggested target:** `in-place`.

## Assumptions

A1. The "novelty" admission at [0166] is assessed only as an admission the specification itself makes; no independent view is taken on whether US11549591B1 in fact anticipates or renders obvious any claim, which is outside a specification-consistency audit and belongs to Prompt 32.

A3. Common general knowledge is relied on only for the standard content of 37 CFR 1.74/MPEP 608.01(f) (every figure must be described) and MPEP 2129 (admissions as prior art), both cited above.

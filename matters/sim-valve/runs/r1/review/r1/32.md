### F-32-1 [high] [novelty] Inventive step: the only purpose-specific effect is not shown by the application's own data

Claims 1 and 2 ("passing air through the open purge gap into the tyre chamber"), read with [0016], [0136] and [0271].

**Attack.**
- The description attributes the cleaning to the air purge. Yet [0287] reports: "Cured sealant film was present on the outer surface of the seat ring 30 in the region of the purge gap 60."
- The surfaces found clean are the sealing edge 32 and the sealing face 42. The main passage and the snap-back wipe act on those, not the air purge.
- [0140] and [0289] state that the test "does not isolate the air purge from the snap-back wipe".
- Claim 1 recites neither sealant nor cleaning. It also covers the polymer and two-piece forms, whose snap-back wipe is weak or absent ([0208], [0294]).

The examiner will therefore not accept a cleaning effect across the scope of claim 1. The problem becomes "provide a second flow path opened at a higher pressure differential", and the combination is:
- **Closest art:** US4044791A. A floating seat is biased against the poppet; further movement against a second spring opens "circumferentially spaced secondary passageways". Alternatively US5918628A: second-stage openings that follow a predetermined axial movement of the poppet.
- **Combined with:** US5924444A, a tyre-mounted valve in some embodiments. Its elastomeric ring "deforms radially outward thereby increasing the gap" and "return[s] to its non-deformed shape" as the valve closes.
- **Bridge:** using a surface moved by the poppet to deform the ring, in place of the flow, is argued as common general knowledge (A2).

**Least narrowing action.**
- First, add disclosure within the inventor's knowledge: the route of the purge flow over the seat-ring outer surface; why film remains in the purge-gap region (confined, thinner, or loosened but not expelled); and any observation that separates the two cleaning actions.
- Second, if nothing of that kind exists, restate the effect in [0013], [0016] and [0139] around what Example 1 shows. The over-travel together with the shape recovery keeps the sealing surfaces clean: none of the prototypes failed in 40 cycles, against conventional cores failing at 12, 14 and 18. The air purge would then be offered as a contributing mechanism, not as the proven cause.

No claim narrowing is proposed. New disclosure required: yes. Target: P4.

### F-32-2 [high] [novelty] Claim 3 seat ring is distinguished from undulating tubular metal seats only by its intended use

Claim 3: "A seat ring (30) for a valve core (5) of a tyre valve (1), the seat ring being tubular and comprising: a sealing edge (32) at one end … and a wall having a load-converting geometry; wherein an axial load, applied … by a load-applying surface that is moved by the poppet in an over-travel … produces … a radial expansion of the wall; wherein, when the seat ring is installed in the valve core, the radial expansion opens a purge gap …".

**Attack.** The poppet, the over-travel, the valve core and the purge gap are not part of the claimed article. What claim 3 actually requires of the ring is this:
- it is tubular;
- it has a sealing edge at one end;
- its wall shape makes it grow radially under axial load;
- it recovers when the load is removed.

US11549591B1, FIG. 10, discloses a "resilient metal element 812" with "a cylindrical sidewall 814" that "extends in an axial direction", and a "downstream flanged portion 820" forming the seat for the poppet 804. The flanges "may be planar, undulating, or curved". Under [0061], a wall that is "not straight in section" is a convoluted wall, and the flanged sidewall is not straight in section.

- **US:** intended-use and "when installed" language does not distinguish a prior structure capable of the function. An axially loaded resilient undulating metal seat is argued to expand radially and to recover (A1).
- **EPO and UK:** "for a valve core" means only "suitable for". Even if suitability is contested, EP3171056A1 (a wave-section ring whose flattening moves its inner and outer portions radially) supplies the load-to-radial-motion conversion. Obviousness of claim 3 is then credible.
- Claims 4 and 86 (manufacture by NiTi shape-setting, standard per US9126251B2 and US9611937B2) and claims 62–64 fall with claim 3.
- Unity with claim 1 is then lost (F-32-12).

**Least narrowing action.** Give claim 3 structural features that the art does not show and that the description supports:
- the wall has an outer surface that, with a bore surrounding the seat ring, bounds the purge gap (claim 65, [0124], [0334] statement 4.1); and
- the load-converting geometry lies between the sealing edge and the opposite end of the seat ring ([0167]).

Scope given up: the through-wall purge path of claim 67 ([0190]) would need its own dependent route or a parallel independent claim. Claim 3's function-only form would no longer be available.

New disclosure required: no. Target: P5.

### F-32-3 [high] [support] "Closed at rest" is defined so that it has no limiting content

Claim 1: "that is closed at rest and throughout the normal stroke". Claim 2: "while a purge gap (60) remains closed". Claim 67.

**Attack.**
- [0067] says the gap is "closed at rest" when the bounding surfaces "are in the position that those surfaces take when no load is applied", and adds that closure need not be gas-tight. Every gap is therefore "closed" at rest.
- The FIG. 4A instruction draws the free portion 37 "with a small radial clearance already shown" to the bore at rest. [0187] says the ring closes the purge gap at rest "by line contact".
- **US:** the claim fails the reasonable-certainty test (112(b)).
- **EPO and UK:** under G 1/24 the definition is applied. Claim 1 then reads on a valve in which the deflection only widens an existing clearance, which is US5924444A's gap 314 by analogy. That dissolves the distinction the description draws in [0013] and [0065].

**Least narrowing action.** Redefine the term by what it does: the bounding surfaces are in contact, or within a stated clearance, so that no substantial flow passes at rest. Exclude a clearance that already passes flow at rest. Correct the FIG. 4A instruction to show the contact of [0187]. This needs the inventor's confirmation of the prototype's at-rest state.

New disclosure required: yes. Target: P4.

### F-32-6 [medium] [novelty] Normal stroke and over-travel are defined by the contact itself, so claim 1 has no independent travel limitation

Claim 1: "wherein the load-applying surface is spaced from the radially deflectable element throughout the normal stroke; wherein, in the over-travel, the load-applying surface bears on the radially deflectable element".

**Attack.**
- [0071] ends the normal stroke "at the position of the poppet at which the load-applying surface first meets the radially deflectable element".
- [0072] defines the over-travel as the range "in which, and only in which, the load-applying surface bears".
- The spacing limitation is therefore true of any poppet that meets a deflectable element somewhere in its travel.
- Claim 1 does not require the two pressure thresholds of claim 5. It reads on a valve whose poppet, at the end of an ordinary single-rate lift, pushes a compliant element that opens a bypass.
- That makes the combination in F-32-1 easier. Any staged valve with a late-contact deformable element meets the travel features.

**Least narrowing action.** In [0071], define the end of the normal stroke by an independent structural marker as well: for example, the change of rate of the return bias ([0122]) or a stop ([0151]). Keep the contact definition as a consequence. If the examiner persists, folding claim 5 into claim 1 is the fallback. It is narrowing: it gives up poppet travel produced other than by a pressure differential above a separate over-travel differential, although the detent ([0148]) and spring-stop ([0151]) variants remain within it.

New disclosure required: no. Target: P5.

### F-32-7 [medium] [enablement] Snap-back wipe cannot occur in the sequence the description gives

Claim 32: "on the shape recovery, the seat ring moves across the sealing face of the poppet in a snap-back wipe". Claims 56 and 76 are to the same effect.

**Attack.**
- [0137] sets out the sequence. As the poppet leaves the over-travel, "the purge shoulder 44 lifts from the seat ring 30 and the axial load is removed. The seat ring 30 undergoes shape recovery … The poppet 40 then returns through the normal stroke 114 and the sealing face 42 seats again."
- At the moment of shape recovery, the sealing face is therefore still a full normal stroke (0.6 mm, [0235]) away from the sealing edge.
- The recovery is a radial return of at most 0.15 mm ([0237]). It cannot carry the seat ring "across the sealing face".
- [0138] offers the mechanism only as a belief ("It is believed").
- **US:** the claims are exposed to 112(a).
- **EPO:** Art 83 for these dependent claims, and a clarity point on what "moves across" means.

**Least narrowing action.** Have the inventor state the actual geometry:
- which part of the seat ring is near the sealing face at recovery, and at what spacing; or
- whether the wipe acts on the purge shoulder 44 rather than on the sealing face 42.

Then reword [0085], [0138] and the three claims to match. If the geometry cannot be stated, delete the wipe from the claims and keep it in the description as an observation.

New disclosure required: yes. Target: P4.

New disclosure required: yes. Target: P4.

### F-32-9 [medium] [enablement] Principal-embodiment geometry: axial load path and purge-flow route are not stated consistently

[0112]: "An end edge of the seat ring 30, also referred to as its upper edge, forms a sealing edge 32". [0113]: "an axial load applied to the seat ring 30, for example at its upper edge". [0117]: "the purge shoulder 44 is located below the sealing face 42". [0167]: the convolution is "between the sealing edge 32 and the end of the seat ring 30 that is seated against the locating shoulder 24". FIG. 2 instruction: "the purge shoulder (44) touching the seat ring (30) at the fit-band end". [0186]–[0187]: the fit band 35 lies next to the locating shoulder, and the purge gap is closed at rest by line contact.

**Attack.**
- **Where the load is applied.** The sealing face lifts away from the sealing edge in the direction of poppet travel. A purge shoulder on the same poppet can therefore only reach the seat ring at its other end, which the text places at the locating shoulder and fit band. The FIG. 2 instruction agrees with that; [0113] ("at its upper edge", which is the sealing edge) does not.
- **What reacts the load.** A load applied at the retained end is reacted by the locating shoulder or the fit band directly. The text does not say what reacts it so that it passes through the convoluted wall.
- **How air enters the purge gap.** The annular purge gap is bounded on one side by the interference-fitted fit band. The text does not say how pump-side air enters it without passing the sealing edge. [0194] describes only its exit into the tyre chamber.
- The drawings were not in the package (A4). If they resolve these points, the objection falls to clarity only. On the text alone, an examiner can raise 112(a) or Art 83 doubts about how the claimed mechanism works in the only built embodiment, and a 112(b) or Art 84 point on "upper" and "below".

**Least narrowing action.** Add one paragraph, confirmed by the inventor, stating:
- the orientation of the seat ring (which end faces the tyre chamber);
- on which side of the seat ring the purge shoulder lies, and which end it contacts;
- what reacts the axial load;
- the inlet and outlet of the purge gap 60.

Align the FIG. 2 instruction and [0113] with that paragraph.

New disclosure required: yes. Target: P4.

## Assumptions

A1. Common general knowledge: a thin resilient undulating or bowed wall under axial compression changes its diameter and recovers when unloaded. This is stated as the examiner's premise, not proved.

A2. Common general knowledge (the examiner's likely position): actuating a seal element mechanically, by a part moved with the valve member, in place of by the flow, is an ordinary design alternative.

A3. The package is the complete filing text. No abstract and no priority document were supplied. The drawings themselves were not in the package; the figure instructions were read as their content (A4 of Prompt 30).

A4. Patent eligibility (35 USC 101; Art 52–53 EPC; UK s.1(2)) raises no objection: all claims are to mechanical articles, their manufacture or their physical operation. It is listed here so its absence from the twenty is not read as an omission.

A5. The saved prior art (19 references) is thin by its own index. Objections 1 and 2 are combinations built on it. A fuller search, especially of CN, TW and DE utility models, could produce a stronger reference than any cited here.

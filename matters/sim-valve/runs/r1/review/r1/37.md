### F-37-1 [medium] [formal] Multiple dependent claims refer to their antecedents conjunctively, not in the alternative

**Claim language attacked:** claim 13, "The valve core of any one of claims 1 and 5 to 12, ..." (representative; the identical pattern recurs in claims 15, 16, 20, 33, 35–38, 43, 44, 46, 47, 54, 55, 57–61, 65, 67–70, 74, 76, 77, 81, 82 and 84).

**Attack.** A multiple dependent claim "shall refer to such other claims in the alternative only" (MPEP 608.01(n); mirrored by EPO Rule 43(4) EPC and UKIPO practice, which likewise require an "or" between alternatives). "Any one of claims 1 and 5 to 12" reads, on its face, as a claim incorporating claim 1 **and** the range 5–12 together, not as one alternative selected from the set {1, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12}. The "any one of" preamble signals the drafter's intent, but the conjunction inside it is the wrong word for that intent, and an examiner is entitled to object to the form under 37 CFR 1.75(c) and require correction before allowance, or (at the EPO/UKIPO) to raise a clarity objection under Article 84 EPC / section 14(5)(b) Patents Act 1977 to the scope actually referred to. Because the defect is systemic across roughly a third of the claim set, it is a real objection an examiner will raise on the first action, not a single stray error.

**Least narrowing action.** Purely mechanical: replace "and" with a comma in each affected dependency clause (e.g., "any one of claims 1, 5 to 12"), which reads unambiguously as an alternative list and changes no claim scope. No disclosure is required.

**Category / severity.** `formal`; `medium` — not fatal to any claim on the merits, but a first-action objection across most of the dependent claims, correctable only by amendment, and the discipline calls for recognising a systemic pattern once rather than treating each instance as a fresh oversight.

**Requires new disclosure:** No. **Suggested target:** `in-place`.

### F-37-2 [low] [formal] Claimed dimension "about 0.03 mm" relies entirely on a specification-level tolerance definition

**Claim language attacked:** claim 85, "wherein the interference fit is about 0.03 mm on diameter."

**Attack.** "About" in a claimed numeric limitation is a classic 35 U.S.C. 112(b) target: absent a stated tolerance, an examiner will ask what range of interference fits the term covers and whether a competitor's part at, say, 0.05 mm is inside or outside it. [0056] supplies a definition ("the value covers the ordinary manufacturing tolerance and measurement tolerance for the parameter concerned"), and the description itself never states what that tolerance is numerically for this parameter, so the claim's outer boundary is fixed only by reference to an undefined manufacturing tolerance. This is workable but is exactly the kind of claim term litigation counsel would target for a definiteness challenge, because "ordinary manufacturing tolerance" is itself description-dependent rather than a stated number.

**Least narrowing action.** No narrowing needed: either (a) leave the claim as drafted and rely on [0056] (acceptable but carries the residual definiteness risk above), or (b) if a numeric tolerance is later confirmed by the inventor, add it to [0250]/[0056] so the claim term has an explicit numeric anchor. This does not require new disclosure beyond confirming a number the inventor may already hold.

**Category / severity.** `formal`; `low` — the term is defined, if indirectly; this is a tidy-up flag, not a claim-breaking defect.

**Requires new disclosure:** Only if a numeric tolerance is to be added (`P4`); otherwise no. **Suggested target:** `in-place` (no action) or `P4` (if a numeric tolerance is confirmed).

## Assumptions

A1. "Any one of claims X and Y to Z" is treated throughout as intended to mean "any one of {X, Y, Y+1, ..., Z}" (an alternative reference), consistent with the surrounding "any one of" language and with the claims as a whole never reciting two ranges conjunctively in a way that would make sense structurally; the defect is treated as one of form, not of the drafter's underlying intent.

A2. US practice is used as the primary reference for the multiple-dependency rule (MPEP 608.01(n)) because it states the alternative-only requirement most explicitly; the same corrective action also cures the equivalent EPO (Rule 43(4) EPC) and UK clarity concern, so one correction serves all three default jurisdictions.

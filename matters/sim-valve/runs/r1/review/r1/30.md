## Findings

AI drafting and review are drafting and analysis aids. Everything below is an issue for qualified patent counsel to weigh. None of it is a legal conclusion, and none of it clears the application for filing.

### Jurisdictional Resilience Review

Package reviewed: `description.md` ([0001]–[0350]), `claims.md` (claims 1–86), `figures.md` (figure plan, figure instructions, numeral registry) and `registry.md`. No abstract is in the package, and neither is a priority document, so there is no priority or new-matter baseline. The saved prior art is `prior-art/INDEX.md` and `prior-art/refs/` (19 references). The index itself calls that search thin.

#### US

- **Definiteness (35 USC 112(b)).** Claim 1 says "closed at rest and throughout the normal stroke". [0067] defines "closed at rest" as the rest position of the bounding surfaces, and says gas-tight closure is not required. The figure instructions for FIG. 4A draw the free portion 37 "with a small radial clearance already shown" to the bore at rest. So the text does not tell a reader whether a clearance that exists at rest is "closed" (F-30-1).
- **Definiteness of the stroke boundary.** "Normal stroke" and "over-travel" ([0071], [0072]) are defined by the first contact of the load-applying surface. Claim 1's "spaced … throughout the normal stroke" is therefore true by definition. This is taken up in Prompt 32 as a construction issue.
- **Means-plus-function (35 USC 112(f)).** "Radially deflectable element" (claim 1), "return bias" (claim 6) and "load-converting geometry" (claim 3) are each a generic noun joined to a function. [0106] says these terms cover "each structure described herein that performs the function". That wording invites a 112(f) reading, which would limit each term to the structures disclosed and their equivalents (F-30-6).
- **Written description and enablement (35 USC 112(a)).** Only one configuration has been built: a NiTi seat ring with a convoluted wall ([0184], [0230], [0289]). The genus in claim 1 also covers the polymer seat ring, the wiper ring, the slotted sleeve, the split seat ring and the tapered seat ring, all of them prophetic. [0210] says a spring-tempered metal "is not proposed for a continuous convoluted wall 34", yet claims 20–26 place no limit on the material (F-30-7).
- **Inventorship.** `figures.md` describes FIGS. 11, 12 and 13A as "candidate alternative constructions … not stated in the inventor's disclosure … pending inventor confirmation". Claims 26, 40–44, 66 and 67 are directed to that subject matter (F-30-3).
- **Duty of disclosure / accuracy of the background.** [0007] attributes specific content to "US2019/0123456A1". The saved prior-art index lists a reference of that number only as a requester-supplied item marked in its own file as fictional (F-30-4).
- **Multiple dependency and fees (37 CFR 1.75(c)).** Many claims are multiple dependent claims that rest on other multiple dependent claims, for example claims 13, 16, 20, 33, 47, 61, 69, 77 and 86. There are 86 claims in all. For fees, claims 1, 2 and 3 are independent, and claims 4, 47 and 77 are cross-category or cross-referencing (F-30-5).
- **Restriction.** The claims cover a valve core (1), a method of use (2), a seat ring as a subcombination (3) and a method of making (4). A restriction requirement is likely, which would split the prosecution cost (F-30-8).
- **Claim form.** Claim 45 bounds the purge gap by the valve stem although claim 45 is a valve-core claim. Claim 49 contradicts claim 47 ("received in a bore" versus "formed in the valve stem"). Claim 50 claims a tyre valve by reference to sealant in the tyre chamber (F-30-9).
- **Drawings (37 CFR 1.74, 1.84).** The figure plan includes FIG. 13A, but the Brief description of the drawings ([0036]–[0049]) never mentions it. Numerals 146 (used in claim 1) and 220 appear nowhere in the description. The figure captions carry "candidate … not built or tested" legends (F-30-10).
- **Patent eligibility (35 USC 101).** No issue seen. Every claim is directed to a mechanical article, its manufacture or its physical operation.

#### EPO

- **Clarity (Art 84) and claim interpretation.** After G 1/24 (18 June 2025), the description and drawings are always consulted when interpreting claims. The definitions in [0064], [0065], [0067] and [0106] will therefore shape how claim 1 is read for novelty and inventive step. The "closed at rest" definition in [0067] departs from the ordinary meaning of "closed" and enlarges the prior art that reads on claim 1 (F-30-1).
- **Inventive step and the credibility of the effect (Art 56).** Claim 1 recites no sealant and no cleaning. The only purpose-specific effect in the text is that the air purge removes sealant film ([0016], [0136]). The application's own data in [0287] report cured sealant film on the outer surface of the seat ring "in the region of the purge gap 60". [0140] and [0289] add that the test does not isolate the air purge from the snap-back wipe. An examiner will likely recast the problem as providing an alternative second flow path at a higher differential (F-30-2).
- **Sufficiency across the claim's breadth (Art 83).** This is the same point as under 112(a): one built configuration, a genus of prophetic forms, and [0210] saying one material is not proposed for the claimed wall form. Prophetic example 2 ([0290]) gives no purge gap width for PEEK (F-30-7).
- **Rule 43(2) and unity (Art 82).** Claim 77 is a second independent valve-core claim, obtained by reference to the seat ring of claim 3, and it does not fall within claim 1: it lacks the "spaced … throughout the normal stroke" and "closed … throughout the normal stroke" limitations. Claims 1, 2 and 3 share a special technical feature only through the over-travel purge-gap mechanism. If claim 3 falls to a purely structural reading (Prompt 32), unity between claims 1–2 and 3–4 is at risk a posteriori (F-30-8).
- **Claims defined by an entity that is not claimed.** Claim 45 (valve stem bore) and claim 50 (tyre chamber containing sealant) are the examples (F-30-9).
- **Drawings (Rule 46(2)(f)).** The drawings must not contain text except where it is indispensable, and the "candidate / not built or tested / pending confirmation" captions are not. FIG. 13A is not described (Rule 42(1)(d)) (F-30-10).
- **Adaptation of the description.** After G 1/25, the thermal variant ([0095], [0216], FIG. 10), the axially deflecting disc spring ([0179]) and the axial or tilting deflections ([0180]) need not be removed for formal concordance alone. The same decision allows adaptation to be demanded where an inconsistency with the claims causes non-compliance. [0180], which treats radial, axial and tilting deflection as interchangeable, sits beside a claim 1 that requires "radial expansion". No finding is raised; counsel should weigh this.
- **Claims fees and form (Rule 45 and Rule 43(1)).** 86 claims attract claims fees for each claim over 15, at a higher rate over 50. The two-part form may be requested with US1934572A (a conventional tyre valve core) as the closest prior art (F-30-5).
- **Relative and approximate terms.** "Below" in claim 14 is used where the drawings are drawn with the valve axis horizontal ([0112], [0117]; figure instructions, general note). "About" appears in claim 85 and "nominal" in claim 73 (F-30-11, F-30-12).
- **Added matter (Art 123(2)).** This is not engaged before filing. The "Combinations" section ([0217]–[0229]), the range-combination rule ([0249]) and the claim-support statements ([0297]–[0348]) give amendment bases. Prompt 29 tests them.
- **Excluded subject matter (Art 52–53).** No issue seen.

#### UK

- **Sufficiency (s.14(3), s.72(1)(c)).** UK courts require the invention to be enabled across the full breadth of the claim. Claim 1 is a mechanism claimed as a principle, and only one species of it has been made. This is the same point as under EPO Art 83, with higher stakes in UK revocation proceedings (F-30-7).
- **Clarity (s.14(5)(c)).** The same issue as the EPO point on the [0067] definition. A UK court construing claim 1 purposively will read "closed at rest" with [0067] in mind, and under the equivalents approach a permanent-bypass variant may fall inside or outside the claim unpredictably (F-30-1).
- **Inventive step (s.3).** The same concern as under EPO Art 56, including the application's own data (F-30-2).
- **Inventorship (s.7, s.13).** The same issue as under US inventorship. Any subject matter that no named person devised cannot support a claim (F-30-3).
- **Excess claims fee.** A fee is payable for each claim over 25; 86 claims are filed (F-30-5).
- **Excluded matter (s.1(2)).** No issue seen.

### F-30-1 [high] [support] "Closed at rest" is defined so that it has no limiting content

Claim 1: "the purge gap is a secondary flow path that is distinct from the main passage and that is closed at rest and throughout the normal stroke". Claim 2: "while a purge gap (60) remains closed". Claim 67: "the wall slots being closed at rest".

**Attack.** [0067] reads: the purge gap is "closed at rest" when "the surfaces that bound the purge gap are in the position that those surfaces take when no load is applied … The expression does not require closure that is gas-tight to a stated degree". On that definition every gap is "closed" at rest, because every gap is in its rest position at rest. The FIG. 4A instruction draws the free portion 37 with "a small radial clearance already shown" to the bore at rest. [0124] and [0187], by contrast, describe the purge gap as closed at rest, "by line contact" in [0187].

- **US:** a person skilled in the art cannot tell with reasonable certainty whether a device with a permanent bypass clearance that widens in over-travel infringes. That is a 112(b) rejection.
- **EPO and UK:** the definition departs from the ordinary meaning of "closed" and will be applied under G 1/24. That widens claim 1 to read on arrangements where a deflection merely enlarges a clearance that already exists. US5924444A ("the elastomeric ring 303 deforms radially outward thereby increasing the gap 314") becomes relevant art under that reading.

**Least narrowing action.** Redefine "closed at rest" in [0067] by what it does. For example: the bounding surfaces are in contact, or are spaced by no more than a stated clearance, so that the purge gap passes no substantial flow at rest. State in [0067] that a clearance which already passes flow at rest is not a closed purge gap. Redraw the FIG. 4A instruction so the free portion 37 contacts the bore at rest, or name the contact line of [0187]. This needs the inventor to confirm what the prototype actually does at rest. It narrows the definition's reach only, not the claim wording.

New disclosure required: yes (the inventor's confirmation of the at-rest condition). Target: P4.

### F-30-2 [high] [novelty] Inventive step: the only purpose-specific effect is not shown by the application's own data

Claim 1 as a whole, and claim 2: "passing air through the open purge gap into the tyre chamber".

**Attack (EPO and UK problem-solution; US 103 secondary considerations).**

- The text attributes the cleaning to the air purge ([0016], [0084], [0136], [0271]).
- [0287] reports: "Cured sealant film was present on the outer surface of the seat ring 30 in the region of the purge gap 60". That is the very surface the air purge is said to sweep. The surfaces found clean, the sealing edge 32 and the sealing face 42, are those swept by the main passage and by the snap-back wipe.
- [0140] and [0289] state that the test "does not isolate the air purge from the snap-back wipe".
- An examiner will therefore treat the cleaning effect as not credibly achieved by the distinguishing features across the scope of claim 1. That includes the polymer and two-piece forms, which have weak or no snap-back wipe.
- The problem will be recast as "provide a second flow path opened at a higher pressure differential". Against that problem the examiner will combine two things:
  - a staged-opening poppet valve: US4044791A (second passageways opened by further travel against a second spring) or US5918628A (second-stage openings after predetermined poppet travel);
  - a seal element that is deformed radially outward to open a gap and then returns: US5924444A, which is a tyre-mounted valve in some embodiments. A mechanical actuator in place of fluid actuation is argued as common general knowledge.

**Least narrowing action.** First, add disclosure explaining how the air purge acts on the purge-gap surfaces, why film remains in the purge-gap region, and whether it is loose, thinner or confined there. Add any further data that separates the air purge from the snap-back wipe, if the inventor has it. Second, if no such data exists, reframe the stated effect around what the data do show: clean sealing surfaces after 40 cycles against failures at 12–18. The mechanism of the over-travel and the shape recovery together would then be presented as the cause, without singling out the air purge. No claim narrowing is proposed at this stage.

New disclosure required: yes. Target: P4.

### F-30-3 [high] [support] Subject matter recorded as unconfirmed candidates is drawn and claimed

The following are claimed: claim 26 (core body bore relief 202), claims 40–41 (slotted sleeve 170), claim 42 (split seat ring 180), claim 43 (tapered seat ring 190), claim 44 and claim 67 (wall slots 204), claim 66 (fit band 35 and free portion 37), and the Schrader form of FIG. 13A.

**Attack.** `figures.md` states for FIGS. 11 and 12: "candidate alternative constructions identified during design-around analysis; not built, not tested, and not stated in the inventor's disclosure. Shown in prophetic/conditional form only, pending inventor confirmation". It says the same for FIG. 13A. For FIG. 4 it says the fit-band drawing is a "drawable resolution … without asserting this is the only possible resolution". The package therefore records on its face that some drawn and claimed subject matter may not have been conceived by a named inventor.

- **US:** inventorship requires conception by a natural person (Thaler v. Vidal, Fed. Cir. 2022).
- **UK:** the same requirement applies (Thaler v Comptroller-General [2023] UKSC 49).
- **EPO:** the same requirement applies (J 8/20).
- A claim whose subject matter no named inventor conceived is exposed in all three jurisdictions. An opponent reading the figure legends will ask the question.

**Least narrowing action.** Put each candidate to the inventor at the embodiment gate. Keep what the inventor confirms as their own conception. For anything not confirmed, either delete it from the drawings, description and claims, or record the actual contributor and assess inventorship. Remove the provenance legends from the captions in every case (F-30-10). Nothing needs narrowing if the inventor confirms.

New disclosure required: no (confirmation, not text). Target: P3.

### F-30-4 [high] [formal] Background attributes content to a publication number that the record marks as fictional

New disclosure required: no. Target: in-place.

### F-30-5 [medium] [formal] US multiple-dependency form and claims fees in all three offices

The claims affected include claim 13 ("any one of claims 1 and 5 to 12", which includes claim 11, itself multiple dependent), claims 16, 20, 33, 47, 61, 69 and 77, and claim 86 ("any one of claims 62 to 69").

**Attack.**
- **US:** a multiple dependent claim may not serve as a basis for another multiple dependent claim (35 USC 112(e); 37 CFR 1.75(c)). Improper ones are objected to and not examined on the merits. Proper ones carry a surcharge and count as the number of claims they refer to.
- **EPO:** claims fees are due for claims 16–50, and at a higher rate from claim 51 (Rule 45).
- **UK:** an excess-claims fee is due for each claim over 25.

**Least narrowing action.** Prepare a US claim set with single dependencies and no loss of fallback positions: select the dependent chains to keep and re-express the alternatives as separate dependent claims. For the EPO and UK, consolidate range and material fallbacks, for example claims 68–69 and 81–85, where the description already provides amendment basis ([0240]–[0249]) so they need not stand as claims.

New disclosure required: no. Target: in-place.

### F-30-6 [medium] [support] Functional genus terms invite a US 112(f) construction

Claim 1 "a radially deflectable element", claim 6 "a return bias that biases the poppet", claim 3 "a wall having a load-converting geometry".

**Attack.** "Element", "bias" and "geometry" are generic nouns defined by what they do. [0106] reinforces this: the terms cover "each structure described herein that performs the function".

- **US:** a court may apply 112(f) and limit each term to the disclosed structures (seat ring 30, polymer seat ring 130, wiper ring 144, slotted sleeve 170, split seat ring 180, tapered seat ring 190) and their equivalents. If the corresponding structure is not clearly linked, the claim is exposed to indefiniteness.
- **EPO and UK:** the terms are acceptable if the skilled person can find structures that perform the function without undue burden. That links back to F-30-7.

**Least narrowing action.** Give each term structural content that avoids 112(f) without narrowing the genus. For example: "a radially deflectable element having a wall that bounds the purge gap", and "a return bias comprising at least one spring". Add to [0106] an express statement of the structures linked to each function. For the US file, counsel may prefer to accept the 112(f) reading, since the disclosed structures are numerous.

New disclosure required: no. Target: P5.

### F-30-7 [medium] [enablement] Sufficiency across the breadth of the genus claims

Claim 1 ("a radially deflectable element"), claims 20–26 (seat ring with convoluted wall, no material limit), claims 30–31 and 74–75 (PEEK).

**Attack.**
- Only the superelastic NiTi seat ring with a convoluted wall has been built ([0184]). The ranges are untested ([0230]).
- [0210] states that a spring-tempered metal "is not proposed for a continuous convoluted wall 34". The description thus concedes that a material within claims 20–26 is not expected to work in the claimed form.
- For PEEK, [0290] gives "no value … for the radial expansion or for the purge gap width".
- [0208] says the shape recovery is slower and the snap-back wipe weaker.
- **UK:** revocation for insufficiency, where the claim must be enabled across its whole scope.
- **EPO:** Art 83 objection on serious doubts substantiated by the application's own statements.
- **US:** an enablement challenge on the Wands factors, pointing to the limited working examples.

**Least narrowing action.** First, add disclosure within the inventor's conception: why a PEEK wall of the stated geometry would flatten elastically and recover, with an estimated strain; which materials suit which forms of the element. Second, re-express the [0210] exclusion as guidance ("for a continuous convoluted wall, a material recovering a strain of at least X is used") and not as a disclaimer of a claimed species. Narrowing claims 20–26 by material is the last resort, and it would give up non-NiTi metal seat rings.

New disclosure required: yes. Target: P4.

### F-30-8 [medium] [formal] Second independent valve-core claim (claim 77), unity a posteriori, and US restriction

Claim 77: "A valve core (5) comprising a core body (20), a poppet (40) having a sealing face (42), and the seat ring (30) of any one of claims 3 and 62 to 76".

**Attack.**
- **EPO Rule 43(2):** claim 77 is a second independent claim in the same category as claim 1 and does not fall within claim 1's scope.
- **EPO Art 82:** unity between claims 1–2 and 3–4 rests on the over-travel purge-gap mechanism. If claim 3 is read as a bare tubular undulating ring (Prompt 32, F-32-2), no special technical feature is shared.
- **US:** claims 1, 2, 3 and 4 fall in distinct statutory and combination/subcombination classes, so restriction is likely.

**Least narrowing action.** Recast claim 77 as dependent on claim 1 ("The valve core of claim 20, wherein the seat ring is the seat ring of claim 3"), or delete it. Keep claims 3–4 in a form whose special technical feature matches claim 1 (see F-32-2). Budget for a possible divisional in the US.

New disclosure required: no. Target: P5.

### F-30-9 [medium] [formal] Claims 45, 49 and 50 are defined by, or contradict, entities outside the claimed category

- Claim 45: "the bore is a bore of a valve stem (10) in which the valve core is fitted". This is a valve-core claim bounded by a part the valve core does not contain.
- Claim 49 depends on claim 47, which requires "the valve core being received in a bore of the valve stem", yet claim 49 says "the core body being formed in the valve stem".
- Claim 50 is a tyre-valve claim reciting "the tyre chamber contains sealant".

**Attack.**
- Claim 45 is unclear as to what article infringes. A valve core sold alone does not have the purge gap of claim 45.
- Claim 49 is internally inconsistent with its parent, which is a 112(b) and 112(d) issue in the US and an Art 84 issue at the EPO.
- Claim 50 mixes categories: a tyre valve is not changed by what the tube contains.

**Least narrowing action.**
- Claim 45: move it under claim 47, as "The tyre valve of claim 47, wherein the purge gap is bounded by an outer surface of the radially deflectable element and by the bore of the valve stem".
- Claim 47: amend to "the valve core being received in, or formed in, the valve stem".
- Claim 50: recast as "A tyre chamber (80) fitted with the tyre valve of any one of claims 47 to 49 and containing sealant", which follows statement 2.5, [0327].

New disclosure required: no. Target: in-place.

## Assumptions

A1. Common general knowledge: a thin undulating or bowed tubular wall under axial compression changes its diameter. The examiner is assumed to know this without a citation.

A2. Common general knowledge: mechanical actuation of a seal element in place of fluid actuation is an ordinary design alternative. This premise is relied on in F-30-2 and is stated as an examiner's likely position, not as a fact.

A3. The package is the complete filing text. No abstract exists yet, and no priority document is supplied, so no priority or new-matter baseline was assessed.

A4. The drawings will follow the figure instructions in `figures.md`, including the captions quoted. The drawings themselves were not in the package.

A5. The legal points cited (G 1/24 of 18 June 2025; G 1/25 on adaptation of the description; the UK excess-claims fee over 25 claims) were checked against public commentary on 2026-09-29: [A&O Shearman on G 1/24](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/decision-g-1-24-freshly-issued-the-epo-adopts-the-upc-stance-on-claim-interpretation), [Withers & Rogers on G 1/25](https://withersrogers.com/news/ip-case-law/g-1-25-enlarged-board-clarifies-when-the-description-must-be-adapted/), [Hindles on UKIPO fees](https://www.hindles.co.uk/content/fee-increases-at-the-ukipo-from-1-april-2026).

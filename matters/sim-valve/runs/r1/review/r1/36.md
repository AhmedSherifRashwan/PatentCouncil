### F-36-1 [high] [support] Claims recite constructions that the package records as not yet confirmed by the inventor

Claims 40–42 ("a slotted sleeve (170) having fingers (172)", "a split seat ring (180) having an axial split (182)"), claim 43 ("a tapered seat ring (190) having a taper (192)"), claims 44 and 67 ("wall slots (204)"), and claim 26 ("a core body bore relief (202)"). Description [0176]–[0178] and [0186]–[0191].

Attack. The numeral registry in the package (note 4) and the FIG. 11, 12 and 13A captions state that these constructions "are not in the inventor's disclosure and are not built or tested", with confirmation still open. The description, by contrast, presents them without qualification as "embodiments". If they are not the inventor's conception, then in the US a claim to them needs a natural-person inventor who contributed significantly to that claim, and a claim no named inventor conceived is open to an inventorship challenge and to invalidity. At the EPO and in the UK, inventorship is not examined, but a mis-designation is still correctable only at a cost, and entitlement disputes remain. The same package note also makes the description's positive statements hard to defend if they are questioned.

Least narrowing action. The inventor confirms, construction by construction, whether each form is within their conception. Confirmed forms stay as claimed. Unconfirmed forms are deleted from the claims (26, 40–45, 67, and the corresponding parts of 44 and 67), and counsel decides whether to keep them in the description as defensive disclosure. Nothing needs adding to the text. The decision is a human one, routed through the embodiment owner.

### F-36-2 [medium] [formal] The Background cites a publication that the matter's own index records as fictional

[0007]: "US2019/0123456A1 describes a valve core having a bore that is enlarged to reduce capillary rise … a sealing face coated with a fluoropolymer layer … and a removable insert that captures deposits of sealant". [0013] relies on it among "the documents referred to above".

Attack. `prior-art/INDEX.md` lists the source as requester-supplied reference S1, marked in its own file "FICTIONAL US 2019/0123456 A1" and "not a real publication". A filed application that characterises a non-existent publication would be queried by the examiner at the first search. In the US it would also sit uneasily with the duty of candour, and it undermines the credibility of the Background the application relies on in [0013] to frame the technical problem.

Least narrowing action. Delete [0007], or replace it with a real, fetched reference if counsel wants the same technical point made. Check [0013] still reads correctly without it. No new disclosure of the invention is needed. In-place, owned by the Specification Drafter.

### F-36-3 [medium] [enablement] The evidence of over-travel does not match the stated mechanism

[0137]: "When the pump head 90 is removed, the pressure differential collapses and the return spring 50 returns the poppet 40." [0141] and [0288]: a snap was heard "on removal of the pump head 90 after each inflation" and "Over-travel was confirmed on every inflation … by an audible snap on removal of the pump head 90". [0274]: "The rate at which the floor pump delivered air was not recorded."

Attack (examiner or opponent, with a technical specialist). The poppet is moved by the pressure differential across it ([0068], [0133]). When pumping stops with the head still coupled, air flows through the open poppet until the hose and the tyre chamber equalise. The differential then falls below the crack pressure and the poppet closes, before the head is removed (A3). A seat-ring snap at the moment of removal therefore suggests one of two things: the over-travel was held by something other than the stated differential, for example the pump head bearing on the poppet or lock nut, or the snap is not the over-travel event at all. Either way, Example 1, the only actual example and the evidence for the pressure-threshold claims (5, 11, 12, 51–53), does not show that the over-travel differential of 3.5 bar was reached by pressure. With a tyre chamber at 6 bar, that differential would need about 9.5 bar upstream.

Least narrowing action. Add, from the inventor's test record, when in the inflation cycle over-travel occurred, how the pump head engaged the valve, and whether any part of the head bore on the poppet or lock nut. If the snap has another explanation, correct [0141] and [0288] to match what was observed. This is new disclosure and may affect the pressure-defined claims. Target P4.

### F-36-4 [medium] [support] Mechanical actuation of the over-travel is implied but not described

[0072]: "In the embodiments described the poppet reaches the over-travel when the pressure differential exceeds the over-travel differential, which is the preferred actuation."

Attack. "Preferred" signals an alternative, but none is disclosed: a pump head with a depressor pin, a user pressing the poppet or lock nut, or a dedicated purge button. A competitor who sells a core purged by a pin-type head, possibly the very mechanism behind F-36-3, practises claim 1 but not the pressure dependents 5–12 or method claims 51–53. There is also no text on which to base a method claim covering mechanical actuation.

Least narrowing action. If within the inventor's conception, add a paragraph describing over-travel produced by a force applied mechanically to the poppet (by a pump-head pin, by hand through the lock nut 70, or by a push-button), and a matching dependent method claim. New disclosure. Target P3.

### F-36-5 [medium] [support] The disc-spring deflectable element has no drawing and no claim

[0179]: "In some embodiments the element deflected by the load-applying surface is a disc spring of conical form, the rim of which seals on a face at rest … No figure shows this form and no test of it is reported."

Attack. This is the cheapest one-part alternative (it also provides the second spring stage). F-33-1 recommends claiming it. Without a figure, the claim rests on a single paragraph, and a competitor's disc-spring core is the first product an opponent would use to test that paragraph's sufficiency.

Least narrowing action. Add a figure (axial section, at rest and in over-travel) and, if the inventor has them, indicative dimensions or a force–deflection relation for the disc spring. New disclosure (drawing). Target P4.

### F-36-6 [medium] [support] No range is stated for the normal stroke

[0235]: "In the prototype valve cores the normal stroke 114 of the poppet 40 was 0.6 mm. This is the only value of the normal stroke 114 that has been built, and no range is stated for it." Claim 34: "the normal stroke is 0.6 mm and the over-travel distance is 0.5 mm".

Attack. Every other travel and dimension has a range with nested fallbacks ([0231]–[0248]). The normal stroke has only a point value, so the only claim to it is claim 34, which a competitor avoids by any other stroke. After filing, a range cannot be added without an added-matter objection.

Least narrowing action. If the inventor has an operating window for the normal stroke, state it before filing, with nested narrower ranges containing 0.6 mm, in the same form as [0240]–[0248]. New disclosure. Target P4.

### F-36-7 [medium] [support] The two-piece seat has no fallback claim

[0181]: "The two-piece seat comprises a rigid seat 140 and a wiper ring 144. The rigid seat 140 carries the sealing edge 142 …". Statement 7.1 ([0347]) sets out a valve core with the two-piece seat. Claims 1–86 contain no claim reciting the rigid seat or the wiper ring.

Attack. Claim 1's genus covers the two-piece seat, but if claim 1 is narrowed to a seat ring carrying the sealing edge (as in claim 20) to overcome art, the two-piece seat drops out with no fallback. It is also the form a competitor can present as sealing "on a rigid seat" ([0182]).

Least narrowing action. Add dependent claims to claim 1: "wherein the sealing edge is provided by a rigid seat (140) and the radially deflectable element is a wiper ring (144) below the rigid seat" and "wherein the wiper ring undergoes shape recovery and wipes on its return", with support from [0092]–[0094], [0181]–[0183] and [0347]–[0348]. This is claim text only; the supporting description already exists. Target P5.

### F-36-8 [medium] [enablement] The polymer seat ring and the two-piece seat are claimed or described without operative dimensions

[0261]: "a wall thickness of 0.20 mm being thin for a turned polymer part". [0290]: "No value is given for the radial expansion or for the purge gap width that these dimensions would give in PEEK." [0293]: "No dimensions are given for the rigid seat 140 or for the wiper ring 144." Claims 30–31 and 74–75 recite the PEEK ring.

Attack (examiner, EPO sufficiency and plausibility; US enablement). The only PEEK geometry given is the NiTi geometry copied across, which the text itself flags as hard to make. No indication is given that it yields a purge gap in PEEK within the elastic range of the material. A skilled person must experiment to find a working polymer wall. Claims 30–31 and 74–75 are exposed; so is the genus, if the polymer is relied on as a second species.

Least narrowing action. If the inventor has them, add a polymer wall-thickness and convolution-amplitude window, the expected radial expansion or purge gap width in PEEK, and indicative dimensions for the rigid seat and the wiper ring. Otherwise, counsel weighs keeping claims 30–31 and 74–75. New disclosure. Target P4.

### F-36-9 [medium] [support] No use case beyond bicycle tyres is named

[0001]: "The tyre chamber of a bicycle is described by way of example." [0088] defines "tyre chamber" as "for example a bicycle inner tube or a tubeless tyre and rim cavity". [0160] names only a bicycle inner tube and a tubeless bicycle tyre.

Attack. Sealants are used in motorcycle, moped, e-scooter, wheelchair, off-road and automotive tyres. The claims are not limited to bicycles, but the dimensions, pressures and all examples are bicycle-specific. A competitor in another vehicle market will argue that the disclosure does not support the claims there. Dependent fallbacks naming those uses cannot be added later.

Least narrowing action. If within the inventor's conception, add a paragraph naming the tyre chambers of other vehicles and of non-vehicle inflatables that take liquid sealant through a valve, and state which parameters scale and how. New disclosure. Target P4.

### F-36-10 [medium] [support] No valve pattern other than Presta is described

[0157]: "In the principal embodiment the tyre valve 1 is a Presta-pattern tyre valve … The Presta pattern is an example of a pattern of the tyre valve 1." The registry numbers a Schrader-pattern core body (220) for FIG. 13A, but no paragraph describes it.

Attack. Schrader (and Dunlop/Woods) cores differ in body, seal and poppet construction. The claims are pattern-neutral, but without text there is no fallback claim to a Schrader core and no guidance on scaling the stroke, pressures and seat-ring dimensions to it. A competitor launching a Schrader version will say it was not enabled.

Least narrowing action. If within the inventor's conception, describe the architecture in a Schrader-pattern core (and any other pattern contemplated), with any dimensional differences, and keep FIG. 13A only if so. New disclosure. Target P3, because the registry records this construction as a candidate awaiting confirmation.

### F-36-11 [low] [formal] FIG. 13A is numbered but not described

The figure plan and the registry define FIG. 13A with the Schrader-pattern core body 220. [0049] reads: "FIG. 13B is an axial section of an embodiment in which the valve core is integral with the valve stem." No brief description or detailed description refers to FIG. 13A or to numeral 220.

Attack. Filing FIG. 13A without a brief description, or with numeral 220 unused in the description, draws a formal objection under 37 CFR 1.74 and 1.84(p)(5) in the US, and the equivalent requirements of Rule 42(1)(d) and Rule 46(2)(i) EPC in Europe.

Least narrowing action. Once F-36-10 is decided, either add FIG. 13A to [0049] and describe it with numeral 220, or drop FIG. 13A and numeral 220 from the drawings and registry. In-place.

### F-36-12 [low] [enablement] No value is given for cold-service and CO₂ margins

[0212]: "No value of the margin is stated, and no test at low temperature is reported." [0275]: "A CO₂ cartridge inflator chills the tyre valve 1 to well below 0 °C for a few seconds."

Attack. Claims 29 and 72 recite an austenite finish temperature "below 5 °C". With no lower value or margin, the cold-robust design, which is the one most likely to be commercialised for winter and CO₂ use, is left as an unclaimed improvement for a competitor.

Least narrowing action. If the inventor has a target (for example a specified austenite finish temperature for CO₂ compatibility), state it with a range before filing. New disclosure. Target P4.

### F-36-13 [low] [support] The strut-pattern and helical-slot geometries have no drawing

[0171]: "the load-converting geometry is a strut pattern cut in the wall … a zig-zag pattern or a pattern of diamond-shaped cells"; [0173]: "a helical slot through the wall". No figure shows either.

Attack. F-33-4 proposes dependent claims to both. A drawing makes each a credible, structurally defined species, and it helps the § 112(f) linking of "load-converting geometry" to structure.

Least narrowing action. Add a figure with two panels (strut pattern, helical slot), in the form of FIG. 11. New disclosure (drawing). Target P4.

### F-36-14 [low] [support] No dimensionless fallback survives scaling

[0238]–[0239] and [0236] state the crack pressure, the over-travel differential and the over-travel distance in absolute units only. The prototype values are 1.0 bar / 3.5 bar and 0.6 mm / 0.5 mm.

Attack. If the core is scaled (Schrader, larger bore, other vehicles), absolute ranges no longer read. A ratio fallback, such as an over-travel differential of at least a stated multiple of the crack pressure, cannot be derived after filing from the endpoints alone without an added-matter argument.

Least narrowing action. If the inventor regards the ratio as the design rule, state it and its range explicitly before filing. New disclosure. Target P4.

## Assumptions

A1. The package is the whole application. No abstract is present and none has been reviewed.
A2. The statement in the package's numeral registry (note 4) and figure captions that FIGS. 11, 12 and 13A show constructions not in the inventor's disclosure is taken at face value. I have not looked behind it.
A3. Common general knowledge (fluid mechanics): when flow stops with a pump head still coupled, the pressure upstream and downstream of an open, pressure-actuated check valve equalises through it, and the valve closes when the differential falls below its crack pressure. This premise underlies F-36-3.
A4. The US requirement that each claim have at least one natural-person inventor who contributed significantly to it, and the formal requirements cited in F-36-11, are stated as general practice, not as legal opinion.
A5. `prior-art/INDEX.md` is taken as accurate in recording requester-supplied reference S1 as fictional.

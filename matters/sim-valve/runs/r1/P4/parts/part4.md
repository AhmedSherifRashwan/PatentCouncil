#### Manufacture and assembly

FIG. 7 shows the sequence by which the seat ring 30 of the prototype valve cores was made and fitted: a laser cutting step 510, a shape-setting step 512, a quenching step 514, an electropolishing step 516 and a press-fitting step 518.

In the laser cutting step 510, the seat ring 30 is cut from a tube of NiTi alloy to the profile of the convoluted wall 34, the tube being held on a mandrel. The tube used for the prototype valve cores had an outside diameter of 2.7 mm and a wall of 0.20 mm.

In some embodiments the convolution 36 is formed in the shape-setting step 512, a fixture pressing the plain wall of the tube into one circumferential convolution or two, and the laser cutting step 510 trims the ends of the tube. In other embodiments the laser cutting step 510 cuts a pattern in the wall, as described under "Forms of the radially deflectable element".

In the shape-setting step 512, the seat ring 30 is held in a fixture that holds the convolution geometry, and is heated while so held. For the prototype valve cores the shape-setting was at a temperature of 500 °C for a period of 10 minutes. The shape-setting fixes in the NiTi alloy the convolution geometry held by the fixture, so that the convoluted wall 34 has that geometry at rest and returns to it on shape recovery.

In the quenching step 514, the seat ring 30 is cooled rapidly after the shape-setting. The seat rings of the prototype valve cores were quenched in water.

In the electropolishing step 516, the oxide layer and the heat-affected edge of the seat ring 30 are removed. Electropolishing is described for a seat ring 30 formed from a metal such as superelastic NiTi alloy.

In some embodiments the oxide layer and the heat-affected edge are removed by chemical etching or by abrasive-flow finishing in place of electropolishing.

In the press-fitting step 518, the seat ring 30 is pressed into the core body 20 with an interference fit and seated against the locating shoulder 24. In the prototype valve cores the interference fit was about 0.03 mm on diameter. Other ways of retaining the seat ring 30 are described under "Purge gap arrangements and retention of the seat ring".

The core body 20 and the poppet 40 are made by ordinary turning. No special process is used for them.

In some embodiments the polymer seat ring 130 is turned from rod, for example from rod of PEEK. In such embodiments the wall of the polymer seat ring 130 is supported during cutting, a wall thickness of 0.20 mm being thin for a turned polymer part. The shape-setting step 512 and the electropolishing step 516 are steps for a seat ring 30 of metal and are not used for the polymer seat ring 130.

In some embodiments the radially deflectable element is a slotted sleeve 170 that is stamped, deep-drawn or rolled from strip of spring-temper metal. No shape-setting is used for such a slotted sleeve 170.

In some embodiments the convolution amplitude is controlled at the fixture of the shape-setting step 512, so that the convolution amplitude of each seat ring 30 lies within a chosen part of the stated range. This control guards against a convolution amplitude too small to give the radial expansion that opens the purge gap 60.

In some embodiments a tolerance band is set for the convolution amplitude, and the assembled valve core 5 is checked with a gauge, before it is supplied, to confirm that the purge gap 60 is closed at rest. This check guards against a convolution amplitude so large that the seat ring 30 strains the interference fit or leaves the purge gap 60 open at rest.

In some embodiments the interference fit is held within a controlled range, and each valve core 5 is tested to confirm that the purge gap 60 opens at the intended over-travel differential 112. This control guards against an interference fit so tight that it restricts the radial expansion of the seat ring 30.

In some embodiments a batch of seat rings is verified after the shape-setting step 512 and before the press-fitting step 518, for example by a check of the transformation temperature. This verification guards against a seat ring 30 whose shape-setting was incomplete.

In some embodiments the electropolishing step 516 is carried out within limits of time and of current, and the wall thickness is checked in process, so that the convoluted wall 34 keeps its wall thickness and the profile of the convolution 36.

In some embodiments a minimum duration of the electropolishing step 516, or a surface finish to be reached by it, is specified, so that the oxide layer and the heat-affected edge are removed from the sealing edge 32.

#### Use: cleaning during an inflation cycle

FIG. 6 shows, in four panels, the states through which the valve core 5 passes in one inflation cycle: at rest 500, normal stroke 502, over-travel 504 and return 506. Each panel shows the poppet 40 with the sealing face 42 and the purge shoulder 44, the seat ring 30 and the purge gap 60. The behaviour of the valve core 5 in each state is described under "Principal embodiment: operation". The present section describes the inflation cycle as a method of cleaning the tyre valve 1.

The method starts with the valve core 5 at rest 500: the sealing face 42 is seated on the sealing edge 32 and the purge gap 60 is closed. A pump head 90 is coupled to the tyre valve 1 and applies a pressure differential across the poppet 40. When the pressure differential exceeds the crack pressure 110, the poppet 40 lifts through the normal stroke 114, as shown in the normal stroke panel 502, and air flows through the main passage 26 into the tyre chamber 80. The purge gap 60 remains closed.

When the pressure differential exceeds the over-travel differential 112, the poppet 40 moves through the over-travel 116, as shown in the over-travel panel 504. The purge shoulder 44 bears on the seat ring 30, the seat ring 30 undergoes radial expansion and the purge gap 60 opens. Air at pump pressure flows through the purge gap 60, alongside the air flowing through the main passage 26, across surfaces of the valve core 5 on which sealant film accumulates. This air purge carries sealant film into the tyre chamber 80.

When the pump head 90 is removed, the pressure differential collapses and the poppet 40 returns, as shown in the return panel 506. The axial load on the seat ring 30 is removed and the seat ring 30 undergoes shape recovery, which closes the purge gap 60. In embodiments in which the seat ring 30 is superelastic, the shape recovery also produces the snap-back wipe, as described under "Principal embodiment: operation".

The cleaning is part of the inflation cycle. The user couples the pump head 90, inflates the tyre chamber 80 and removes the pump head 90. The valve core 5 stays in place in the valve stem 10 and is cleaned without a tool. The cleaning takes place in each inflation cycle in which the pressure differential exceeds the over-travel differential 112.

The method can be carried out with any pump head 90 that applies a pressure differential exceeding the over-travel differential 112. In Example 1 the pump head 90 was that of a floor pump, the tyre chamber 80 was inflated to 6 bar, and over-travel was confirmed on every inflation of a prototype valve core. The rate at which the floor pump delivered air was not recorded.

Inflation from a CO₂ cartridge has not been tested. A CO₂ cartridge inflator chills the tyre valve 1 to well below 0 °C for a few seconds. If the temperature of a seat ring 30 of NiTi alloy falls below the martensite start temperature of the NiTi alloy, the seat ring 30 loses its superelastic recovery until it warms.

In some embodiments the valve core 5 is supplied with instructions for use that recommend inflation with a floor pump. The choice of the austenite finish temperature for cold conditions is described under "Materials and shape recovery".

The sealant with which the method has been tested is a latex emulsion sealant. Sealant of this kind typically contains ammonia and suspended particles. No ammonia-free sealant has been tested.

#### Examples and data

Example 1 is an actual example: the valve cores were built and the results were measured. Examples 2, 3 and 4 are prophetic examples: none of them has been built or tested, and no result is reported for any of them.

#### Example 1 (actual): valve core having a seat ring of superelastic NiTi alloy

Three prototype valve cores, designated P-1, P-2 and P-3, were built. Each was a valve core 5 as described under "Principal embodiment: structure", fitted in a Presta-pattern valve stem 10 of brass having an outer diameter of 6 mm. The core body 20 was turned from brass of grade CW614N. The poppet 40 was of brass and had a conical sealing face 42 and, below the sealing face 42, a purge shoulder 44. The return spring 50 was of stainless music wire and had a two-rate characteristic. The seat ring 30 was of superelastic NiTi alloy of nominal composition 55.8 wt% Ni, with an austenite finish temperature specified as below 5 °C, and had a convoluted wall 34.

The prototype values were: seat ring inner diameter 2.3 mm; wall thickness 0.20 mm; free length 3.0 mm; convolution amplitude 0.12 mm; normal stroke 0.6 mm; over-travel distance 0.5 mm; purge gap width, fully open, 0.15 mm, stated as a radial value; crack pressure 1.0 bar; over-travel differential 3.5 bar.

The seat rings were made and fitted by the steps 510 to 518 described under "Manufacture and assembly", with shape-setting at a temperature of 500 °C for a period of 10 minutes, quenching in water, electropolishing, and an interference fit of about 0.03 mm on diameter.

Three conventional valve cores, designated C-1, C-2 and C-3, were tested for comparison. They were standard Presta-pattern valve cores of a single manufacturer.

Each of the six valve cores was fitted to a tyre chamber 80 in the form of a bicycle inner tube of 700×28 size. The inner tubes were identical and each was charged with a volume of 60 ml of latex emulsion sealant. One inflation cycle of the test consisted of inflating the tyre chamber 80 to 6 bar with a floor pump, holding for a period of 24 hours at a temperature of 22 °C, and deflating fully through the tyre valve 1. The inflation cycle was then repeated. The test is recorded as carried out from 12 August to 20 August of 2026.

A valve core was recorded as having failed when either of two conditions was met: a leak-down of more than 0.5 bar in a period of 24 hours, or the pump being unable to open the tyre valve 1 within 5 seconds of engagement of the pump head 90. The inflation time was measured from engagement of the pump head 90 until 6 bar was reached, with the same pump and the same operator throughout.

The results are set out in the following table. In the column of inflation times at the last inflation cycle, the number in brackets is the inflation cycle at which that inflation time was measured. It belongs to that inflation time only, and not to the other values in the same row.

| Core | Type | Cycles to first failure | Failure mode | Inflation time, cycle 1 (s) | Inflation time, last cycle (s) | Leak-down at last cycle (bar/24 h) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-1 | Conventional | 14 | blocked (pump could not open) | 41 | 68 (cycle no. 13) | 0.1 |
| C-2 | Conventional | 12 | leak-down 0.7 bar | 40 | 44 (cycle no. 11) | 0.7 |
| C-3 | Conventional | 18 | blocked | 42 | 75 (cycle no. 17) | 0.2 |
| P-1 | Prototype (seat ring of NiTi alloy) | none up to 40 | — | 43 | 47 (cycle no. 40) | 0.1 |
| P-2 | Prototype (seat ring of NiTi alloy) | none up to 40 | — | 41 | 46 (cycle no. 40) | 0.1 |
| P-3 | Prototype (seat ring of NiTi alloy) | none up to 40 | — | 44 | 50 (cycle no. 40) | 0.2 |

The three conventional valve cores failed at cycle counts of 14, 12 and 18 respectively, two by blocking and one by leak-down. Each of the three prototype valve cores completed a total of 40 inflation cycles with no failure, at which point the test was stopped. For the prototype valve cores the mean inflation time at the last inflation cycle was within 15% of the mean inflation time at the first inflation cycle. The leak-down of the prototype valve cores at the last inflation cycle was 0.1 bar, 0.1 bar and 0.2 bar in a period of 24 hours, against the failure criterion of 0.5 bar.

After the last inflation cycle the seat rings of the prototype valve cores were inspected under a stereo microscope. Cured sealant film was present on the outer surface of the seat ring 30 in the region of the purge gap 60. The sealing edge 32 and the sealing face 42 of the poppet 40 were clean. The surfaces on which the poppet 40 seals were therefore free of sealant film after more inflation cycles than those at which the conventional valve cores had failed by blocking or by leak-down.

Over-travel was confirmed on every inflation of a prototype valve core by an audible snap on removal of the pump head 90.

The evidence of Example 1 has the following limits. The test covered a total of 40 inflation cycles, one sealant, one floor pump, one temperature, one size of tyre chamber 80 and three valve cores of each type. No fatigue test and no cold test was carried out. The comparison was between the prototype valve cores and conventional valve cores. No valve core having the air purge alone or the snap-back wipe alone was tested, so the test does not show the separate contribution of each cleaning action. The behaviour of the seat ring 30 beyond the inflation cycles tested, including whether the purge gap 60 remains closed at rest after longer service, has not been established, and no statement is made as to service life.

#### Prophetic example 2: polymer seat ring

This example has not been built or tested. The valve core 5 would be as in Example 1, except that the polymer seat ring 130, of PEEK, would take the place of the seat ring 30 of NiTi alloy. The polymer seat ring 130 would have a sealing edge 132 and a convoluted wall 134 of the same geometry as in Example 1, with a seat ring inner diameter of 2.3 mm, a wall thickness of 0.20 mm, a free length of 3.0 mm and a convolution amplitude of 0.12 mm. No value is given for the radial expansion or for the purge gap width that these dimensions would give in PEEK.

The polymer seat ring 130 would be turned from rod as described under "Manufacture and assembly" and press-fitted against the locating shoulder 24. In use the poppet 40 would lift through the normal stroke 114 and, above the over-travel differential 112, would reach the over-travel 116 as in Example 1. The polymer seat ring 130 has some radial compliance. Its shape recovery on removal of the pump head 90 would be slower, and of lower force, than that of the seat ring 30 of NiTi alloy.

The air purge is expected to operate as in Example 1, because it depends on the opening of the purge gap 60 and not on the material of the seat ring. The snap-back wipe is expected to be weaker than in Example 1.

#### Prophetic example 3: two-piece seat

This example has not been built or tested. The valve core 5 would have a two-piece seat in place of the seat ring 30 of Example 1. A rigid seat 140 of brass would provide the sealing edge 142 on which the sealing face 42 of the poppet 40 seats. A wiper ring 144, being a thin ring of stainless steel of spring character, would lie below the rigid seat 140. The poppet 40, the return spring 50 and the remaining components would be as in Example 1. No dimensions are given for the rigid seat 140 or for the wiper ring 144.

At rest the seal would be made on the rigid seat 140 and would not depend on the wiper ring 144. During the over-travel 116 the purge shoulder 44 would deflect the wiper ring 144 outward. On removal of the pump head 90 the wiper ring 144 would undergo shape recovery and would wipe on its return.

Sealing on the rigid seat 140 is expected to be more predictable than sealing on an element that also deflects, and the two-piece seat may be easier to make. The two-piece seat would have one more part and a greater axial length than the seat ring 30 of Example 1.

#### Prophetic example 4: thermal variant

This example has not been built or tested, and there are no data for it. The seat ring would be the thermal variant 150, being a seat ring of NiTi alloy trained with two-way shape memory and having an austenite finish temperature of about 35 °C to 40 °C. Warming the valve core 5, for example by hand, would take the thermal variant 150 through that temperature range, whereupon the thermal variant 150 would change shape and open the purge gap 60 without a pump. On cooling, the thermal variant 150 would return to its earlier shape. Opening by warming would not use the over-travel 116 of the poppet 40. The thermal variant 150 is further described under "Materials and shape recovery".

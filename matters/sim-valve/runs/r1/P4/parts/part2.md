### Description

#### Principal embodiment: structure

A principal embodiment of the tyre valve 1 is described with reference to FIGS. 1 to 3 and FIG. 5. FIG. 1 is an axial section of the tyre valve 1 at rest. FIG. 2 is the same section with the poppet 40 in the over-travel 116. FIG. 3 is an exploded perspective view of the valve core 5. FIG. 5 is a qualitative force–travel diagram. The valve cores tested in Example 1, under "Examples and data", are valve cores according to the principal embodiment.

The tyre valve 1 comprises a valve stem 10 and a valve core 5. The valve stem 10 is a tubular member that is fixed to a tyre chamber 80 and has a bore that receives the valve core 5. The tyre valve 1 admits air into the tyre chamber 80 and retains it there. The tyre chamber 80 is indicated in FIG. 1 by a boundary in broken lines and contains sealant in use.

A pump head 90 is a device that is coupled to the tyre valve 1 and applies a pressure differential across the poppet 40. The pressure differential is the difference between the pressure upstream of the poppet 40 and the pressure in the tyre chamber 80. The pump head 90 is shown in broken outline in FIG. 1, where it is not engaged, and in solid outline in FIG. 2, where it is engaged on the valve stem 10. The pump head 90 is a conventional external device and is not a part of the valve core 5.

In the principal embodiment the valve core 5 is an assembly of a core body 20, a seat ring 30, a poppet 40 and a return spring 50. In some embodiments the valve core 5 also carries a lock nut 70. FIG. 3 shows these parts separated along the valve axis in their order of assembly: the core body 20, the seat ring 30, the poppet 40 with the return spring 50 concentric on the stem of the poppet 40, and the lock nut 70.

The core body 20 is fixed in the valve stem 10. In the principal embodiment the core body 20 has a thread by which it is screwed into the bore of the valve stem 10. The core body 20 carries the seat ring 30 and guides the poppet 40 along the valve axis. The core body 20 has a core body bore 22, which is the internal surface of the core body 20 that faces the outer surface of the seat ring 30. The core body 20 also has a locating shoulder 24, which is an internal step of the core body 20 against which the seat ring 30 is seated axially. Materials of the core body 20 are described under "Materials and shape recovery".

The seat ring 30 is a short tubular element. An end edge of the seat ring 30, also referred to as its upper edge, forms a sealing edge 32 on which the sealing face 42 of the poppet 40 seats to close the tyre valve 1. The seat ring 30 is pressed into the core body 20 with a light interference fit and is located against the locating shoulder 24.

The seat ring 30 has a convoluted wall 34, that is, a wall that is not straight in section. The convoluted wall 34 has at least one convolution 36, and each convolution 36 is shallow. In some embodiments the convoluted wall 34 has one convolution 36, as shown in FIG. 1. In other embodiments the convoluted wall 34 has two convolutions. The convoluted wall 34 has the effect that an axial load applied to the seat ring 30, for example at its upper edge, translates into radial expansion of the seat ring 30.

In the principal embodiment the seat ring 30 is a single part that both carries the sealing edge 32 and undergoes the radial expansion. The closing of the tyre valve 1 and the opening of the purge gap 60 are thereby provided by one part, without the additional part and the additional axial length of the two-piece seat described under "Forms of the radially deflectable element".

The seat ring 30 is an example of a radially deflectable element. Other forms of the radially deflectable element are described under "Forms of the radially deflectable element". Materials of the seat ring 30, including the superelastic NiTi alloy of the valve cores of Example 1, are described under "Materials and shape recovery".

The poppet 40 is a member that is movable along the valve axis. The poppet 40 has a sealing face 42, which in the principal embodiment is conical and which seats on the sealing edge 32 to close the tyre valve 1. From the closed position the poppet 40 travels away from the sealing edge 32 through a normal stroke 114 and, beyond the normal stroke 114, through an over-travel 116.

The poppet 40 has a purge shoulder 44. The purge shoulder 44 is a surface of the poppet 40 that is separate from the sealing face 42. In the principal embodiment the purge shoulder 44 is located below the sealing face 42, that is, it is spaced from the sealing face 42 along the valve axis. In some embodiments the purge shoulder 44 is annular. The purge shoulder 44 is spaced from the seat ring 30 when the poppet 40 is at rest and throughout the normal stroke 114. The purge shoulder 44 contacts the seat ring 30 only during the over-travel 116, in which it applies an axial load to the seat ring 30.

Because the purge shoulder 44 is spaced from the seat ring 30 up to the end of the normal stroke 114, the seat ring 30 is loaded by the purge shoulder 44 only in the over-travel 116. At rest the seat ring 30 carries on its sealing edge 32 the load with which the sealing face 42 is held closed. That load is distinct from the axial load applied by the purge shoulder 44.

The return spring 50 biases the poppet 40 towards the closed position. In the principal embodiment the return spring 50 is a compression spring arranged under the poppet 40. The return spring 50 has a two-rate characteristic: a first spring stage 52 of lower rate acts through the normal stroke 114, and a second spring stage 54 of higher rate must be overcome for the poppet 40 to reach the over-travel 116. The return spring 50 thereby sets two values of the pressure differential. The first is a crack pressure 110, at which the poppet 40 first lifts off the sealing edge 32 against the first spring stage 52. The second is an over-travel differential 112, greater than the crack pressure 110, which overcomes the second spring stage 54 and produces the over-travel 116.

The two-rate characteristic separates the over-travel differential 112 from the crack pressure 110. Inflation at a pressure differential between the two takes place through the normal stroke 114 alone, and the over-travel 116 is reached only at the higher pressure differential.

FIG. 5 shows the two-rate characteristic qualitatively. The horizontal axis represents the travel of the poppet 40 from the closed position and the vertical axis represents the force on the poppet 40. The first spring stage 52 appears as a segment of lower slope extending over the normal stroke 114, and the second spring stage 54 as a segment of higher slope extending over the over-travel 116. The crack pressure 110 and the over-travel differential 112 are marked on the curve. FIG. 5 is schematic: it carries no scale and represents no measured values. FIG. 5 does not depend on how the two-rate characteristic is produced, which may be by a single spring or by more than one spring or biasing element, as described under "Return bias and load-applying surface".

The normal stroke 114 and the over-travel 116 are two successive ranges of the travel of the same poppet 40. In the principal embodiment the end of the normal stroke 114 can be identified in the valve core 5 in two ways. It is the travel at which the first spring stage 52 gives way to the second spring stage 54, which appears in FIG. 5 as the change of slope. It is also the limit up to which the purge shoulder 44 remains spaced from the seat ring 30; beyond that limit, in the over-travel 116, the purge shoulder 44 bears on the seat ring 30.

A main passage 26 is the path by which inflation air flows into the tyre chamber 80 when the poppet 40 is lifted off the sealing edge 32. The main passage 26 runs past the sealing edge 32, between the sealing edge 32 and the sealing face 42. In FIG. 1 the poppet 40 is seated, so that the main passage 26 is closed and is indicated as a path.

A purge gap 60 is a secondary flow path into the tyre chamber 80 that is separate from the main passage 26. The purge gap 60 is bounded by the outer surface of the seat ring 30 and by a bore surrounding the seat ring 30. The purge gap 60 is closed at rest and is open when the seat ring 30 has undergone the radial expansion. In the principal embodiment the bore surrounding the seat ring 30 is the core body bore 22, and the purge gap 60 is an annular clearance between the outer surface of the seat ring 30 and the core body bore 22. In FIG. 1 the purge gap 60 is closed and is drawn as a single line.

In some embodiments the bore surrounding the seat ring 30 is the bore of the valve stem 10, so that the purge gap 60 lies between the seat ring 30 and the bore of the valve stem 10. This arrangement is described further under "Purge gap arrangements and retention of the seat ring".

The purge gap 60 differs from the main passage 26 in where it runs and in which surfaces it sweeps. The main passage 26 runs between the poppet 40 and the seat ring 30, past the sealing edge 32. The purge gap 60 runs along the outer surface of the seat ring 30, between the seat ring 30 and the bore surrounding it. Air flowing through the purge gap 60 passes over the outer surface of the seat ring 30 and over the surrounding bore. The poppet 40 is not one of the boundaries of the purge gap 60. A main passage 26 that is made larger, whether by further travel of the poppet 40 or by deflection of the seat ring 30, remains the main passage 26 and is not a purge gap 60.

Arrangements for retaining the seat ring 30 in the core body 20 while leaving the seat ring 30 free to undergo the radial expansion, and the exit of the air purge into the tyre chamber 80, are described under "Purge gap arrangements and retention of the seat ring".

In some embodiments a lock nut 70 is threaded onto the stem of the poppet 40 outside the core body 20. The lock nut 70 is conventional. The lock nut 70 is used to close the poppet 40 manually for storage and transport, and it plays no part in the opening of the purge gap 60.

#### Principal embodiment: operation

The operation of the principal embodiment over one inflation cycle is described with reference to FIGS. 1, 2 and 5. An inflation cycle is one application and removal of the pressure differential by the pump head 90. The steps of use are set out under "Use: cleaning during an inflation cycle".

At rest, as shown in FIG. 1, the return spring 50 holds the sealing face 42 of the poppet 40 on the sealing edge 32 of the seat ring 30, and the main passage 26 is closed. The purge shoulder 44 is spaced from the seat ring 30. The seat ring 30 is in its at-rest shape and the purge gap 60 is closed, so that no air purge passes through the purge gap 60 and the tyre valve 1 retains the air in the tyre chamber 80.

When the pump head 90 is coupled to the tyre valve 1 and the pressure differential exceeds the crack pressure 110, the poppet 40 lifts off the sealing edge 32 against the first spring stage 52 and travels through the normal stroke 114. Air flows through the main passage 26 into the tyre chamber 80.

Throughout the normal stroke 114 the purge shoulder 44 remains spaced from the seat ring 30. The seat ring 30 is not loaded by the purge shoulder 44 and keeps its at-rest shape, and the purge gap 60 remains closed. The geometry of the sealing edge 32 is therefore the same after a normal stroke 114 as before it. At a pressure differential below the over-travel differential 112 the tyre chamber 80 is inflated through the main passage 26 alone.

When the pressure differential exceeds the over-travel differential 112, the poppet 40 overcomes the second spring stage 54 and travels beyond the normal stroke 114 into the over-travel 116, as shown in FIG. 2. In the over-travel 116 the purge shoulder 44 bears on the seat ring 30 and applies an axial load to it. Under that axial load the seat ring 30 undergoes radial expansion, and the purge gap 60 opens.

The convoluted wall 34 converts the axial load into the radial expansion. In some embodiments the convolution 36 flattens under the axial load, and FIG. 2 shows the convolution 36 flattened in comparison with FIG. 1. The convoluted wall 34 then deforms by bending at the convolution 36. The radial expansion is obtained from this change in the shape of the convoluted wall 34 and not from a uniform circumferential stretching of the convoluted wall 34.

The purge gap 60 is opened by the mechanical load that the poppet 40 applies through the purge shoulder 44. The purge gap 60 is not opened by the flow of air acting on the seat ring 30. Whether the poppet 40 reaches the over-travel 116 depends on the pressure differential applied by the pump head 90. While the poppet 40 is in the over-travel 116, the purge shoulder 44 holds the seat ring 30 in its expanded shape whatever the rate at which air passes through the purge gap 60.

With the purge gap 60 open, air at the pressure delivered by the pump head 90 flows through the purge gap 60 into the tyre chamber 80. This flow is the air purge. The main passage 26 remains open during the over-travel 116, so that air flows into the tyre chamber 80 through the main passage 26 and through the purge gap 60 at the same time, as indicated by the two flow arrows in FIG. 2. The air purge passes over the outer surface of the seat ring 30 and over the bore surrounding it, and acts on sealant film present on those surfaces so as to carry it into the tyre chamber 80.

When the pump head 90 is removed, the pressure differential collapses and the return spring 50 returns the poppet 40. As the poppet 40 leaves the over-travel 116 the purge shoulder 44 lifts from the seat ring 30 and the axial load is removed. The seat ring 30 undergoes shape recovery, returning to its at-rest shape, and the purge gap 60 closes. The poppet 40 then returns through the normal stroke 114 and the sealing face 42 seats again on the sealing edge 32.

In some embodiments, in particular those in which the seat ring 30 is of a superelastic material, the shape recovery gives a second cleaning action, the snap-back wipe. The shape recovery of a superelastic seat ring 30 is fast and stiff. It is believed that on shape recovery the seat ring 30 moves across the sealing face 42 of the poppet 40 and shears partially cured sealant film from it. The surfaces concerned in the snap-back wipe are the seat ring 30 and the sealing face 42.

An inflation cycle in which the poppet 40 reaches the over-travel 116 therefore provides the air purge while the purge gap 60 is open and, in the embodiments that have it, the snap-back wipe when the pump head 90 is removed. Both cleaning actions take place in the course of the inflation cycle, without a tool and without removal of the valve core 5 from the valve stem 10. The air purge takes place before the shape recovery and does not require the snap-back wipe. The snap-back wipe is an additional cleaning action that is present in some embodiments.

The test reported in Example 1 was carried out on valve cores according to the principal embodiment having a seat ring 30 of superelastic NiTi alloy. That test does not isolate the air purge from the snap-back wipe, and no statement is made as to the share of each cleaning action in the result reported there. When those valve cores were inspected at the end of that test, the sealing edge 32 and the sealing face 42 were clean, and cured sealant film was present on the outer surface of the seat ring 30 in the region of the purge gap 60.

In the test reported in Example 1, a snap was heard on removal of the pump head 90 after each inflation of the valve cores according to the principal embodiment. The snap was taken in that test as an indication that the poppet 40 had reached the over-travel 116. The snap is recorded as an observation, and no embodiment requires it.

#### Return bias and load-applying surface

The return spring 50 is an example of a return bias, that is, of an arrangement that biases the poppet 40 towards the closed position. A return bias having a two-rate characteristic can be constructed in several ways, which are described in the following paragraphs. Each construction provides a first spring stage 52 that the crack pressure 110 overcomes and a second spring stage 54 that the over-travel differential 112 overcomes. The rates of the first spring stage 52 and of the second spring stage 54 are selected so that the crack pressure 110 and the over-travel differential 112 have the values described under "Parameters and ranges".

In some embodiments the return spring 50 is a single compression spring of variable pitch. The closely pitched coils of the variable-pitch spring close solid during the normal stroke 114. The widely pitched coils that remain active are fewer and so give a higher rate, and they form the second spring stage 54. The first spring stage 52 and the second spring stage 54 are thereby obtained from one part, and the parts that adjoin the return spring 50 are unchanged. FIGS. 1 and 2 show a return spring 50 of this construction by way of example.

In some embodiments the return bias comprises a soft spring and a stiff spring arranged in series, with a stop that takes the soft spring out of the load path at the end of the normal stroke 114. The soft spring provides the first spring stage 52 and sets the crack pressure 110. The stiff spring provides the second spring stage 54 and sets the over-travel differential 112.

In some embodiments the return bias comprises nested springs that act in parallel with staggered engagement. An outer spring acts over the whole travel of the poppet 40. A shorter inner spring engages only at the end of the normal stroke 114, so that the rate in the over-travel 116 is the sum of the rates of the two springs. This construction occupies less axial length than two springs in series.

In some embodiments the return bias comprises a return spring 50 of a single rate and an elastomeric bumper that the poppet 40 reaches at the end of the normal stroke 114. The elastomeric bumper provides the second spring stage 54. The material of the elastomeric bumper is selected to withstand the sealant, including sealant that contains ammonia, and to resist compression set, since compression set would shift the over-travel differential 112.

In some embodiments the return bias comprises a soft coil spring stacked with a disc spring, the disc spring being engaged at the end of the normal stroke 114. The disc spring provides a second spring stage 54 that is stiff and occupies little axial length.

In some embodiments a detent holds the poppet 40 at the end of the normal stroke 114 until the over-travel differential 112 overcomes the detent. The detent is, for example, a spring-loaded ball, or a magnet and a keeper. In these embodiments the crack pressure 110 and the over-travel differential 112 are separated by a step in force in place of a change of rate, and the entry of the poppet 40 into the over-travel 116 is abrupt.

Where the two-rate characteristic is provided by a single spring, the over-travel differential 112 does not depend on the seating of two springs relative to one another. In some embodiments a single variable-pitch spring is used for that reason in preference to a construction having two springs.

In some embodiments the return spring 50 is subjected to a pre-setting step, that is, a stabilising step, before it is assembled into the valve core 5. The pre-setting step is directed to limiting relaxation of the return spring 50 under repeated compression, since relaxation would cause the crack pressure 110 and the over-travel differential 112 to drift in service.

In some embodiments the normal stroke 114 ends at a spring-loaded stop. The poppet 40 meets a stop washer at the end of the normal stroke 114, and the stop washer is itself held by a spring that is stiffer than the return spring 50. The over-travel 116 is the travel in which the poppet 40 pushes the stop washer back against the stiffer spring. The stop washer defines the end of the normal stroke 114 and the stiffer spring sets the over-travel differential 112, and the return spring 50 can then have a single rate.

The purge shoulder 44 is an example of a load-applying surface, that is, of a surface that is moved by the poppet 40 in the over-travel 116 and that applies the axial load to the seat ring 30 or to another radially deflectable element. In the principal embodiment the load-applying surface is the purge shoulder 44 formed on the poppet 40 itself.

In some embodiments the load-applying surface is provided on a collar that is a separate part carried by the poppet 40. The collar is pressed, crimped or threaded onto the poppet 40 below the sealing face 42. The position of the collar on the poppet 40 sets the over-travel distance, so that the over-travel distance can be set when the valve core 5 is assembled.

In some embodiments the purge shoulder 44 is conical in place of a flat annulus. A conical purge shoulder 44 is used with forms of the radially deflectable element that are spread by a cone, namely the slotted sleeve, the split seat ring and the tapered seat ring described under "Forms of the radially deflectable element".

In some embodiments the axial load is applied to the seat ring 30 through the return bias. A spring seat washer is arranged between the return spring 50 and the poppet 40 and bears on the seat ring 30 once the first spring stage 52 has been taken up. In these embodiments the load-applying surface is a surface of the spring seat washer, and the spring seat washer, not the poppet 40, touches the seat ring 30.

In each of these embodiments the load-applying surface is moved by the poppet 40, and the radially deflectable element is loaded by the load-applying surface only in the over-travel 116.

#### Tyre valve patterns and product forms

In the principal embodiment the tyre valve 1 is a Presta-pattern tyre valve. The valve stem 10 is a Presta-pattern valve stem of brass with an outer diameter of 6 mm and a threaded bore that receives the valve core 5. The valve stem 10 is conventional. The Presta pattern is an example of a pattern of the tyre valve 1.

In the principal embodiment the valve core 5 is a removable threaded valve core. The core body 20 is screwed into the threaded bore of the valve stem 10, and the valve core 5 can be unscrewed from the valve stem 10 and replaced as a unit.

In some embodiments the valve core is integral with the valve stem, as shown in FIG. 13B, in which the valve core integral with the valve stem is designated 230. The core body is formed in the valve stem 10 and is not threaded into it. The seat ring 30, the poppet 40 with its purge shoulder 44, and the return spring 50 are as in the principal embodiment. A valve core of this form is not removed from the valve stem 10 for service.

The tyre chamber 80 is the pressurised volume into which the tyre valve 1 admits air. In some embodiments the tyre chamber 80 is a bicycle inner tube. In some embodiments the tyre chamber 80 is the cavity formed by a tubeless tyre and a rim. In each case the tyre chamber 80 contains sealant in use, and the sealant is introduced into the tyre chamber 80 through the tyre valve 1.

In some embodiments the valve core 5 is supplied as a unit separate from the valve stem 10 and from the tyre chamber 80, for fitting to an existing valve stem 10 in place of a valve core that has been removed. A valve core 5 so supplied comprises the core body 20, the seat ring 30, the poppet 40 and the return spring 50. Where the bore surrounding the seat ring 30 is the core body bore 22, a valve core 5 so supplied contains both boundaries of the purge gap 60.

In some embodiments the seat ring 30 is supplied separately as a replacement part for fitting into a core body 20.

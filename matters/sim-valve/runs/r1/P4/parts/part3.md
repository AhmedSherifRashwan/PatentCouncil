### Description

#### Forms of the radially deflectable element

The seat ring 30 described under "Principal embodiment: structure" is one form of a radially deflectable element, as that term is defined under "Definitions and interpretation". The radially deflectable element is also embodied by the polymer seat ring 130 shown in FIG. 8 and by the wiper ring 144 of the two-piece seat shown in FIG. 9A and FIG. 9B, and in some embodiments by the further forms shown in FIG. 11A to FIG. 11C. In each form, a load-applying surface moved by the poppet 40, for example the purge shoulder 44, bears on the radially deflectable element only in over-travel, the axial load so applied deflects the element radially, and the element returns by shape recovery when the load is removed.

In some embodiments the radially deflectable element carries the sealing edge, as the seat ring 30 carries the sealing edge 32 and the polymer seat ring 130 carries the sealing edge 132. In other embodiments the radially deflectable element is separate from the element that carries the sealing edge, as in the two-piece seat, in which the rigid seat 140 carries the sealing edge 142 and the wiper ring 144 is the radially deflectable element.

In some embodiments the radially deflectable element has a load-converting geometry, that is, a geometry by which the axial load applied in over-travel is converted into radial expansion. The convoluted wall 34 of the seat ring 30 is the load-converting geometry of the principal embodiment. In some embodiments the conversion is made by the shape of the radially deflectable element, by the shape of the load-applying surface that bears on it, or by both.

The effect of the load-converting geometry is the opening of a flow path. The axial load is converted into radial expansion, and the radial expansion opens the purge gap 60, which is a secondary flow path distinct from the main passage 26 and closed at rest. A wavy or undulating shape of a resilient metal seal is described in US11549591B1, where the shape pre-loads a seat interface to provide a seating and sealing force. The load-converting geometry described here has a different effect: it acts when the poppet 40 is in over-travel, and its deformation opens the purge gap 60 through which the air purge passes.

In some embodiments the convoluted wall 34 has one convolution 36, as shown in FIG. 4A. The convolution 36 is a single shallow wave in the section of the convoluted wall 34, between the sealing edge 32 and the end of the seat ring 30 that is seated against the locating shoulder 24. Axial load applied to the seat ring 30 in over-travel is translated by the convolution 36 into radial expansion of the convoluted wall 34.

In other embodiments the convoluted wall 34 has two convolutions, as shown in FIG. 4B: a convolution 36 nearer the sealing edge 32 and a convolution 38 nearer the fit band 35. The two convolutions lie in series along the convoluted wall 34 and together translate the axial load into radial expansion.

In FIG. 4A and FIG. 4B the wall thickness is marked t, the free length is marked L and the convolution amplitude is marked a. Values of these dimensions are given under "Parameters and ranges".

In some embodiments the convolution 36 is formed in the section of the wall by shape-setting. A plain tube wall is pressed into one or two circumferential waves in a shape-setting fixture, no pattern is cut in the wall, and laser cutting serves only to trim the ends of the seat ring 30. Under axial load the waves flatten and the wall moves outward.

In some embodiments the load-converting geometry is a strut pattern cut in the wall by laser cutting, for example a zig-zag pattern or a pattern of diamond-shaped cells. Under axial load the strut pattern shortens axially and bulges radially, so that the radial expansion is obtained by bending of the struts. In such embodiments the cells of the strut pattern are closed at rest, and the cut portion of the wall provides a route for the purge flow when the wall deforms, as described under "Purge gap arrangements and retention of the seat ring".

In some embodiments the load-converting geometry is a pre-bowed wall, that is, a wall having a single outward bow along the free length. The bow increases under axial load in the manner of a pre-buckled column. The pre-bowed wall corresponds to the convoluted wall 34 with one convolution 36, written as a bow, and its purpose is a radial expansion that increases progressively with the axial load.

In some embodiments the load-converting geometry is a helical slot through the wall along part of the free length. Axial compression unwinds the helix slightly and increases the diameter of the wall, and the helical slot gives the wall an axial compliance. The helical slot is itself a route for the purge flow when it is open.

In some embodiments the convolution 36 has a root radius sized to reduce the concentration of strain at the root of the convolution 36, where repeated radial expansion and shape recovery concentrate the strain.

In some embodiments the convolution amplitude is chosen towards the lower end of the range given under "Parameters and ranges", to reduce the peak strain in the convoluted wall 34. No service life is stated for any form of the radially deflectable element; the extent of the test carried out is stated under "Examples and data".

In some embodiments the radially deflectable element is a slotted sleeve 170, shown in FIG. 11A. The slotted sleeve 170 is a thin-walled cylinder in which axial slots running from one end form fingers 172. The purge shoulder 44 is conical in these embodiments and, in over-travel, enters between the fingers 172 and spreads them outward towards the core body bore 22. The radial expansion is obtained by bending of the fingers 172 and not by hoop strain of a continuous wall. The axial slots between the fingers 172 are a route for the purge flow when the fingers 172 are spread.

In some embodiments the radially deflectable element is a split seat ring 180, shown in FIG. 11B. The split seat ring 180 has a C-shaped section with one axial split 182, and opens circumferentially when a cone on the poppet 40 enters it in over-travel. The purpose of the axial split 182 is a change of diameter obtained with little strain of the material. In such embodiments the axial split 182 is closed at rest, or the seal at rest is made on a face beside the axial split 182, so that the axial split 182 does not interrupt the seal at rest.

In some embodiments the radially deflectable element is a tapered seat ring 190, shown in FIG. 11C. The tapered seat ring 190 has a conical inner surface, the taper 192, and the poppet 40 has a matching taper 194 in place of the purge shoulder 44. In over-travel the matching taper 194 wedges the tapered seat ring 190 outward, so that the axial load is converted into radial expansion without a convolution. In some such embodiments the angle of the taper 192 is chosen greater than the friction angle between the taper 192 and the matching taper 194, so that the tapered seat ring 190 is released when the poppet 40 returns.

In some embodiments the element deflected by the load-applying surface is a disc spring of conical form, the rim of which seals on a face at rest. In over-travel the load-applying surface flattens the disc spring, the rim lifts from the face, and a purge gap opens at the face. In these embodiments the deflection is axial and the purge gap is a gap at a face, so that the element is referred to as a deflectable element and its deflection is not a radial expansion. The disc spring also provides a second spring stage. No figure shows this form and no test of it is reported.

In some embodiments the deformation of the deflectable element that opens the purge gap is radial, axial, tilting or a combination of these. Radial expansion is the deformation of the principal embodiment.

In some embodiments the valve core 5 has a two-piece seat, shown at rest in FIG. 9A and in over-travel in FIG. 9B. The two-piece seat comprises a rigid seat 140 and a wiper ring 144. The rigid seat 140 carries the sealing edge 142, on which the sealing face 42 of the poppet 40 seats. The wiper ring 144 is a separate thin ring located below the rigid seat 140. In some embodiments the rigid seat 140 is made of brass and the wiper ring 144 is made of stainless spring steel.

At rest the sealing face 42 is seated on the sealing edge 142 of the rigid seat 140, so that the seal at rest is made on the rigid seat 140 and does not depend on the wiper ring 144. In over-travel the purge shoulder 44 bears on the wiper ring 144 and deflects it outward, as shown in FIG. 9B. When the load is removed the wiper ring 144 returns by shape recovery and wipes on its return.

The two-piece seat separates the sealing function, which is on the rigid seat 140, from the function of the element deflected by the purge shoulder 44, which is on the wiper ring 144. The two-piece seat has one more part than the seat ring 30 and a greater axial length, and may be simpler to make. No two-piece seat has been built or tested.

Of the forms described in this section, the seat ring 30 having the convoluted wall 34 and made of superelastic NiTi alloy has been built and tested, as reported under "Examples and data". The other forms are described as embodiments, and no test result is stated for them.

#### Purge gap arrangements and retention of the seat ring

The purge gap 60 is bounded by an outer surface of the seat ring 30 and by a bore surrounding the seat ring 30, as described under "Principal embodiment: structure". This section describes embodiments in which the seat ring 30 is retained in the core body 20 while a portion of it remains free to deflect, and embodiments of the route taken by the purge flow.

In some embodiments the interference fit acts on a fit band 35 of the seat ring 30 and not on the whole of its outer surface. The fit band 35 is a short band of the outer surface next to the locating shoulder 24, and the remaining length of the outer surface is a free portion 37, as shown in FIG. 4A and FIG. 4B. The core body bore 22 has a core body bore relief 202, for example a counterbore or an undercut, which begins just past the fit band 35 and extends along the free portion 37, as shown in FIG. 12A.

In these embodiments the seat ring 30 closes the purge gap 60 at rest by line contact at one end of the core body bore relief 202. In over-travel the free portion 37 expands into the core body bore relief 202 and the purge gap 60 opens, while the fit band 35 remains retained in the core body bore 22.

The effect of the fit band 35 and the free portion 37 is that the retention of the seat ring 30 and the radial expansion are carried by different portions of the same outer surface. The core body 20 has one more machined diameter than a core body having a plain core body bore 22.

In some embodiments the purge gap 60 is bounded by the outer surface of the seat ring 30 and by the bore of the valve stem 10. In one such arrangement the seat ring 30 sits at the end of the core body 20, so that the outer surface of the seat ring 30 faces the bore of the valve stem 10. The valve stem 10 then provides one boundary of the purge gap 60, and that boundary remains in the valve stem 10 when the valve core 5 is removed.

In some embodiments the purge flow passes through the wall of the seat ring 30. In these embodiments wall slots 204, or perforations, are provided through the convoluted wall 34 in the free portion 37, below the sealing edge 32, as shown in FIG. 12B. The wall slots 204 are closed at rest, either because they lie against the bore surrounding the seat ring 30 or because their edges abut, and they open when the convoluted wall 34 deforms in over-travel.

In the embodiments having the wall slots 204, the purge flow sweeps the inner surface and the outer surface of the seat ring 30, and the outer surface of the seat ring 30 can be retained along its whole length. The purge gap is then not annular.

In some embodiments the purge gap is a secondary flow path opened by deformation of the seat ring 30 and located around the seat ring 30, through it or within it. The secondary flow path is continuous in some embodiments, as in the annular purge gap 60 around the seat ring 30, and segmented in others, for example a purge gap formed at one or more lobes of the seat ring 30 or at several wall slots 204 in the convoluted wall 34.

In the embodiments having the slotted sleeve 170, the axial slots between the fingers 172 provide the route for the purge flow. In the embodiments having the strut pattern or the helical slot, the cut portion of the wall provides it. These forms are described under "Forms of the radially deflectable element".

The purge flow leaves the purge gap 60 into the tyre chamber 80. Air at pump pressure flows through the open purge gap 60, across the surfaces on which the sealant film accumulates, and into the tyre chamber 80, alongside the flow through the main passage 26, as shown by the flow arrows of FIG. 2. This flow is the air purge.

In the principal embodiment the seat ring 30 is pressed into the core body 20 with a light interference fit and is seated against the locating shoulder 24. The amount of the interference fit is given under "Manufacture and assembly".

In some embodiments the seat ring 30 is retained by axial clamping in place of the interference fit. The seat ring 30 is clamped between the locating shoulder 24 and a retainer sleeve, which is pressed or threaded into the core body 20. The seat ring 30 is then retained without radial preload on the free portion 37.

In some embodiments the seat ring 30 is held against the locating shoulder 24 by staking, the rim of the core body 20 being staked over the end of the seat ring 30. This retention likewise applies no radial preload to the free portion 37, and it adds one operation where the retainer sleeve adds one part.

In some embodiments the valve core 5 has a positive stop which fixes the position of the sealing face 42 against the sealing edge 32 at rest, independently of the shape recovery of the seat ring 30. The seal at rest then does not depend solely on complete shape recovery of the seat ring 30 after repeated over-travel.

In some embodiments an anti-rotation key or a flat is provided between the seat ring 30 and the core body bore 22, in addition to the interference fit, to hold the seat ring 30 against axial shift and against rotation in the core body bore 22.

In some embodiments an alignment chamfer is provided on the locating shoulder 24 or on the core body bore 22. The alignment chamfer aligns the seat ring 30 during press-fitting, so that the seat ring 30 is not seated at an angle to the locating shoulder 24, and the contact at the sealing edge 32 and the radial expansion are uniform around the seat ring 30.

As reported under "Examples and data", a cured sealant film was found on the outer surface of the seat ring 30 in the region of the purge gap 60 at the end of the test. In some embodiments electropolishing is applied to the surfaces facing the purge gap 60, namely the outer surface of the seat ring 30 and, where needed, the core body bore 22, to reduce adhesion of the sealant in that region.

The core body bore relief 202, the wall slots 204, the retention by axial clamping or by staking, the positive stop, the anti-rotation key and the alignment chamfer are described as embodiments. They are not stated to be the construction of the valve cores tested under "Examples and data", and no test result is stated for them.

#### Materials and shape recovery

In the principal embodiment the seat ring 30 is made of superelastic NiTi alloy of nominally 55.8 wt% Ni, in the form of drawn tube which is shaped by shape-setting and finished by electropolishing. The austenite finish temperature of the NiTi alloy is below 5 °C, so that the seat ring 30 is superelastic at riding temperatures.

In some embodiments the radially deflectable element is made of an elastically recovering material having radial compliance. Superelastic NiTi alloy, a polymer and a spring-tempered metal are examples of such a material. Shape recovery of the radially deflectable element does not require a transformation of the material.

In some embodiments the seat ring 30 is made of a ternary superelastic alloy, for example a Ni–Ti–Cr alloy or a Ni–Ti–Cu alloy, in place of the binary NiTi alloy. The addition of chromium shifts the transformation temperature, and the addition of copper narrows the hysteresis. The parameters of shape-setting are adapted to the alloy.

In some embodiments the seat ring 30 is made of a copper-based alloy that is superelastic, for example a Cu–Al–Ni alloy or a Cu–Zn–Al alloy. The recoverable strain of such alloys is lower than that of NiTi alloy, and their compatibility with an ammonia-bearing sealant is not established.

In some embodiments the valve core 5 has a polymer seat ring 130 in place of the seat ring 30, as shown in FIG. 8. The polymer seat ring 130 has a sealing edge 132 and a convoluted wall 134, the convoluted wall 134 being the same as the convoluted wall 34 of the seat ring 30. In some embodiments the polymer seat ring 130 is made of polyether ether ketone (PEEK). The core body 20, the core body bore 22, the locating shoulder 24, the poppet 40 with the sealing face 42 and the purge shoulder 44, the return spring 50 and the purge gap 60 are as in the principal embodiment.

The polymer seat ring 130 has some radial compliance and a lower cost than the seat ring 30 of superelastic NiTi alloy. Its shape recovery is slower and the force of its return is lower, so that the snap-back wipe is weaker. No polymer seat ring 130 has been built or tested.

In some embodiments the polymer seat ring 130 is made of another high-modulus thermoplastic, for example polyphenylene sulphide (PPS) or polyetherimide (PEI), in place of PEEK. The chemical compatibility of each such polymer with an ammonia-bearing sealant is to be confirmed for that polymer.

In some embodiments the radially deflectable element is made of a spring-tempered metal, for example spring-tempered stainless steel or copper–beryllium, and has a form in which the deflection is dominated by bending, such as the slotted sleeve 170 or the strut pattern. The bending-dominated deflection keeps the material within its elastic limit, so that the shape recovery is the elastic recovery of a metal that is not superelastic. A spring-tempered metal is not proposed for a continuous convoluted wall 34.

In some embodiments the shape recovery is assisted by a garter spring, that is, a circumferential coil spring placed around the slotted sleeve 170, the split seat ring 180 or the polymer seat ring 130. The garter spring supplies a closing force, so that the closing of the purge gap does not depend only on the stiffness of the radially deflectable element. The garter spring lies in the region of the purge gap and passes the purge flow between its coils.

In some embodiments the austenite finish temperature of the NiTi alloy is selected with a margin below the coldest temperature expected at the tyre valve 1 in use, for example the coldest riding or storage climate anticipated, or the temperature reached at the tyre valve 1 when a CO₂ cartridge is discharged through it. The purpose of the margin is that the seat ring 30 remains superelastic at that temperature. No value of the margin is stated, and no test at low temperature is reported.

In the principal embodiment the core body 20 and the valve stem 10 are made of free-machining brass, for example of grade CW614N. In some embodiments the core body 20 is made of stainless steel, for example of 303 grade, in place of brass, for corrosion resistance where the sealant is an ammonia-bearing sealant. A core body 20 of stainless steel has been machined; no test of it with sealant is reported.

In the principal embodiment the poppet 40 is made of brass. In some embodiments the sealing face 42 of the poppet 40 is of hard-anodised aluminium, to resist wear and scoring of the sealing face 42 by repeated seating and to resist corrosion. No poppet 40 having such a sealing face 42 has been built.

In the principal embodiment the return spring 50 is made of stainless music wire. The effect described does not depend on the material of the return spring 50.

In some embodiments the seat ring is a thermal variant 150, shown schematically in FIG. 10, made of a NiTi alloy trained with two-way shape memory and having an austenite finish temperature of approximately 35 °C to 40 °C. Warming of the thermal variant 150, for example by hand, opens the purge gap 60 without a pump, and FIG. 10 accordingly shows the poppet 40 with the sealing face 42 seated and the purge gap 60 open. The thermal variant 150 has not been built and no data exist for it.

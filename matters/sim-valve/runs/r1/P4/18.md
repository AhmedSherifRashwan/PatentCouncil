#### Actual examples

**Example 1 — Superelastic NiTi seat ring valve core (prototype P-1, P-2, P-3)**

*Components.* Presta-pattern brass valve stem (10); core body (20) turned from brass CW614N; seat ring (30) formed with a convoluted wall; poppet (40) with a conical sealing face (42) and, below it, a purge shoulder (44); a return spring (50) with a two-rate characteristic under the poppet; an annular purge gap (60) between the outer surface of the seat ring and the core body bore; an optional lock nut (70) on the poppet stem. Source: `intake/disclosure.md` §3 items 1–7; `intake/drawings/README.md` FIG-1, FIG-2 captions.

*Materials.* Seat ring: superelastic NiTi alloy, nominal composition 55.8 wt% Ni, austenite finish temperature below 5 °C (§4, §5). Core body and valve stem: brass CW614N; a core body of stainless steel 303 was separately machined for corrosion resistance with ammonia-bearing sealant, but the disclosure does not state that this variant was tested with sealant (§5; open, see Q5). Poppet: brass; a hard-anodised aluminium sealing face was considered but not built (§5). Return spring: stainless music wire; the inventor states that nothing about the effect depends on the spring material (§5).

*Dimensions/ranges.* All figures are the prototype (representative) value tested, with the inventor's believed range in brackets, from §4: seat ring inner diameter 2.3 mm (2.0–2.6 mm); wall thickness 0.20 mm (0.15–0.30 mm); free length 3.0 mm (2.5–4.0 mm); convolution amplitude 0.12 mm (0.08–0.20 mm); normal stroke 0.6 mm (no range given); over-travel distance 0.5 mm (0.4–0.9 mm); purge gap width, fully open, 0.15 mm radial (0.10–0.25 mm); crack pressure 1.0 bar (0.8–1.5 bar); over-travel differential 3.5 bar (2.5–5.0 bar); interference fit approx. 0.03 mm on diameter (no range given); NiTi tube stock 2.7 mm OD × 0.20 mm wall; valve stem outer diameter 6 mm. Source: §3, §4, §7.

*Fabrication/assembly.* The seat ring is laser-cut from the NiTi tube stock to the convoluted profile on a mandrel; shape-set at 500 °C for 10 minutes in a fixture holding the wave geometry; water-quenched; electropolished to remove the oxide layer and the heat-affected edge. It is then press-fitted into the core body with the interference fit, seated against the locating shoulder. The core body and poppet are made by ordinary turning; the disclosure states no other special process for them (§7). The construction by which the return spring's two-rate characteristic is produced (a single variable-pitch spring, two springs, or some other means) is not stated in the disclosure and is not assumed here (§7; open, see Q3).

*Operating sequence.* (1) At rest: the sealing face is seated on the sealing edge of the seat ring; the purge gap is closed. (2) Normal inflation: the pressure differential exceeds the crack pressure (1.0 bar); the poppet lifts through the normal stroke (0.6 mm) against the first spring stage. (3) Over-travel: the differential exceeds the over-travel differential (3.5 bar); the poppet overcomes the second spring stage and moves a further 0.5 mm; the purge shoulder bears on the seat ring; the seat ring undergoes radial expansion (to a purge gap width of 0.15 mm); air at pump pressure flows through the purge gap into the tyre chamber alongside the main passage. (4) Pump-head removal: the differential collapses; the poppet returns; the seat ring undergoes shape recovery, producing the snap-back wipe across the sealing face of the poppet; the purge gap closes. Source: §2; §3 items 4–6. Note: how the seat ring's outer surface simultaneously carries the interference fit (assembly step) and forms the purge gap that opens on radial expansion (step 3) is not reconciled in the disclosure; this example follows the disclosure's own description without resolving that point (see Q1).

*Inputs/outputs.* Input: the pressure differential applied by a pump head, at two thresholds (crack pressure, over-travel differential). Output: air flow through the main passage and, during over-travel, through the purge gap, into the tyre chamber. Output: displacement of the sealant film from the swept surfaces. Output (observed in test, not asserted as an intended indicator): an audible snap on pump-head removal. Source: §2; bench-test.md notes.

*Expected/actual technical effect — measured.* Bench test, 2026-08-12 to 2026-08-20 (§8; `intake/data/bench-test.md`): three prototype cores (P-1, P-2, P-3), each in a 700×28 tube charged with 60 ml latex emulsion sealant, cycled to 6 bar and held 24 h at 22 °C, then fully deflated, repeated as one inflation cycle. All three completed 40 inflation cycles with no failure, against a failure criterion of leak-down above 0.5 bar/24 h or the pump unable to open the valve within 5 s. Three conventional cores (C-1, C-2, C-3) failed at cycles 14 (blocked), 12 (leak-down 0.7 bar), and 18 (blocked) respectively. Inflation time at cycle 40 stayed within 15% of cycle 1 (P-1: 43→47 s; P-2: 41→46 s; P-3: 44→50 s). Leak-down at cycle 40: P-1 0.1 bar/24 h, P-2 0.1 bar/24 h, P-3 0.2 bar/24 h. Microscope inspection at cycle 40 found the sealing edge and poppet sealing face clean, with cured sealant film present only on the outer surface of the seat ring in the purge-gap region (bench-test.md, notes). Over-travel was confirmed on every prototype inflation by the audible snap. The test stopped at 40 cycles; fatigue behaviour, residual strain and purge-gap closure beyond that point are not established (§9 item 1). No test isolated the air purge from the snap-back wipe, so the relative contribution of each cleaning action to this result is the inventor's characterisation (§2), not a measured separation (§9; open, see Q1 of `runs/r1/P1/01.md`).

#### Prophetic examples

**Example 2 — Polymer (PEEK) seat ring valve core**

Instantiates Alternative A (§6): the confirmed one-piece seat ring architecture (Example 1's components 10, 20, 40, 42, 44, 50, 60, 70) with the seat ring (30) formed from PEEK in place of superelastic NiTi.

*Components.* As Example 1, substituting the seat ring material only; sealing edge, purge shoulder, purge gap and return spring unchanged in kind.

*Materials.* Seat ring: PEEK (polyetheretherketone). Other components as Example 1. Source: §6, Alternative A.

*Dimensions/ranges.* The disclosure states that Alternative A retains "the same convoluted wall" as the tested configuration (§6); this example therefore takes the same convoluted-wall geometry and dimensions as Example 1 (seat ring inner diameter 2.3 mm, wall thickness 0.20 mm, free length 3.0 mm, convolution amplitude 0.12 mm). Whether these dimensions, derived from and tested only with NiTi, give a comparable radial expansion and purge gap width in PEEK is not stated and is not assumed to be equal (see Assumption A4).

*Fabrication/assembly.* The disclosure does not state a manufacturing process for the polymer seat ring. In some embodiments the seat ring would be formed by a process suited to a high-modulus thermoplastic, for example machining from rod stock or moulding; this is not itself disclosed and is stated here only as a reasonable prophetic instantiation, not as a described process. Assembly (press-fitting against the locating shoulder with an interference fit) would follow Example 1's assembly step; the same reconciliation gap noted in Example 1 applies.

*Operating sequence.* Would follow Example 1's four-step sequence. The disclosure states that the polymer ring gives "some" radial compliance and a slower recovery, so the sealing face would still lift through the normal stroke and the poppet would still reach over-travel above the over-travel differential; on pump-head removal the seat ring would recover more slowly and with lower stiffness than the NiTi seat ring of Example 1 (§6).

*Inputs/outputs.* As Example 1.

*Expected technical effect — prophetic, not tested.* The air purge cleaning action is expected to operate as in Example 1, because it depends on the purge gap opening geometrically, not on the seat ring material. The snap-back wipe is expected to be weaker than in Example 1, as the inventor states (§6): "some" radial compliance and a slower, lower-force recovery are expected to give a less vigorous wipe. Whether the air purge alone (without a strong wipe) reproduces the 40-cycle result of Example 1 has not been tested; no data exists for this configuration (§6; §9; open, see Q1 of `runs/r1/P1/01.md`).

**Example 3 — Two-piece seat valve core (rigid seat and wiper ring)**

Instantiates Alternative B (§6).

*Components.* A rigid seat providing the sealing edge on which the sealing face of the poppet seats, and a separate wiper ring below the rigid seat, deflected outward by the purge shoulder during over-travel. Poppet, return spring and the remaining components as Example 1, except that the seat ring of Example 1 is replaced by the rigid-seat/wiper-ring pair. Source: §6, Alternative B.

*Materials.* Rigid seat: brass. Wiper ring: stainless steel, of spring character (§6).

*Dimensions/ranges.* Not given in the disclosure for either the rigid seat or the wiper ring. No range is proposed here by analogy to Example 1's seat ring, because the disclosure does not state that the wiper ring's deflection or recovery behaviour corresponds to the one-piece seat ring's (see Assumption A4-adjacent gap and Q1).

*Fabrication/assembly.* Not described. The disclosure states only that the two-piece seat "separates the sealing function from the purge/wipe function" and may be "easier to make" (§6); no manufacturing steps are given for either part.

*Operating sequence.* At rest, the sealing face of the poppet seats on the rigid seat's sealing edge; the seal at rest does not depend on the wiper ring. During over-travel, the purge shoulder deflects the wiper ring outward (§6). On pump-head removal, the wiper ring undergoes shape recovery, giving the snap-back wipe. The disclosure does not describe what flow path, if any, the wiper ring's deflection opens, where such a path would begin or end, or which surfaces it would sweep. This is not invented here: Example 3 is written only as far as the disclosure supports, and the missing purge path is carried forward as `disclosure_requests` item 1 and Open Question Q2, rather than assumed.

*Inputs/outputs.* Input: the pressure differential, as Example 1. Output: sealing on the rigid seat; a mechanical wipe by the wiper ring on recovery. Whether an air-purge output exists for this embodiment, and by what path, is undisclosed.

*Expected technical effect — prophetic, not tested.* The two-piece seat is expected to give more predictable sealing than a seat ring that must both seal and deflect, because sealing occurs on a rigid, non-deflecting surface (§6). Whether it reproduces the air-purge cleaning action of Example 1, in addition to a mechanical wipe, cannot be stated without inventing the purge path; no claim of an air-purge effect for this embodiment is made here.

**Example 4 — Thermal variant (two-way trained NiTi seat ring)**

Instantiates Alternative C (§6). The inventor states being unsure whether this variant is worth pursuing, and no data exists for it; it is drafted here only because it is the inventor's own stated alternative, and it is flagged throughout as the least developed of the four.

*Components.* A seat ring of the same one-piece, convoluted-wall architecture as Example 1, but requiring no purge shoulder-driven over-travel: actuation is by warming the seat ring itself, so the poppet, return spring and over-travel mechanism of Example 1 are not needed for the purge function (though the disclosure does not state that they are removed).

*Materials.* NiTi alloy trained for two-way shape memory, with an austenite finish temperature of around 35 °C to 40 °C, distinct from the one-way superelastic training and sub-5 °C austenite finish temperature of Example 1's seat ring (§6).

*Dimensions/ranges.* Only the austenite finish temperature range (35 °C to 40 °C, "around") is given; no other dimension is stated for this variant (§6).

*Fabrication/assembly.* Not described. How two-way training is imparted, and whether it is compatible with the convoluted-wall geometry and shape-setting process of Example 1, is not stated.

*Operating sequence.* A user would warm the valve core, for example by hand; as the seat ring passes through its austenite finish temperature range it would change shape, opening a purge path without a pump-driven pressure differential; on cooling it would revert. This sequence has no over-travel step and no over-travel differential, unlike Examples 1–3.

*Inputs/outputs.* Input: applied warmth (thermal), not a pressure differential. Output: an opened or closed purge path, thermally triggered. Not tested; no measured output exists.

*Expected technical effect — prophetic, not tested.* A purge action without pump actuation is the stated aim (§6), but the inventor records no confidence in it, and whether a single seat ring could be trained to show both the pump-driven, sub-5 °C-superelastic behaviour of Example 1 and this warm-triggered, two-way behaviour, or whether the two are mutually exclusive species of seat ring, is not addressed by the disclosure (§6; open, see Q4).

## Rationale

Principal embodiments are limited to the four alternatives the inventor has actually stated (main NiTi embodiment, Alternatives A, B and C of §6), read together with the N0–N6 ladder of `03.md`. The Design-Around Risk Register (`11.r1.md`) and Equivalent Architecture Library (`12.r1.md`), though carried in `CONFIRMED.md`, are competitor-escape and genus-breadth analyses, not disclosed embodiments; nearly every row in them is independently flagged "needs inventor confirmation" or "candidate," and Gate 2's promotion of the file to `CONFIRMED.md` is recorded as an automatic, unattended action rather than a human decision. Treating that promotion as licence to describe those rows as worked implementations would risk exactly what is barred: describing a candidate embodiment as though it were the inventor's own. They remain available to Prompt 16 (blueprint breadth) and Prompt 19 (genus-level definitions), where analysis of alternatives, not worked examples, is the point.

Stainless steel 303 core body and hard-anodised aluminium sealing face (Alternatives D and E of the Combination Matrix) are treated as material options within Example 1 rather than separate principal embodiments, because they do not change the architecture, dimensions or operating sequence; the Combination Matrix (`14.md`) already lists the NiTi-seat-ring/stainless-body pairing as the primary claimed combination's fallback.

Example 3 is deliberately incomplete on the purge path: the disclosure states the wiper ring deflects and recovers, but not what flow path that opens, and inventing one would be new matter. The same discipline applies to the return spring's two-rate construction in Example 1 and to the manufacturing process of Examples 2 and 4: each gap is named in place rather than filled.

No new terms were needed; every component, material and parameter is named with the locked preferred term.

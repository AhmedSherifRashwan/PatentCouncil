## Rationale

Each independent claim is the corresponding aspect of the Summary ([0008], [0012], [0014], [0015]) with the definitions of [0039] to [0048] behind every term, so that the Examiner's support matrix can find the words of each limitation in a paragraph. I drafted from the inventive nucleus outward and stopped at the smallest combination the matrix says distinguishes the charted art: 09 D1 (separate purge gap, closed at rest, opened by deflection), 09 D2 (over-travel of the same valve member above a higher threshold, produced by the pressure differential) and 09 D3 (purge flow of the inflation gas). 09 D4 (purge shoulder), D5 and D6 (recovery wipe), D7 (convoluted wall), D8 (superelastic alloy), D10 (two-rate characteristic) and D11 (wiper ring) are dependent rungs.

Limitations tested against "is this needed to distinguish the charted art?" and removed: "relative to the valve seat" as an anchor for over-travel (the three-range wording and the "produced by the pressure differential acting on the valve member against the biasing member" clause already exclude the US20080188352A1 reading, and the anchor would give up a seat that moves with the valve member); "radially outward" and every location of the purge gap (core bore, outer surface of the seat ring, annular form); the interference fit and the locating shoulder; the purge shoulder; the two-rate characteristic; the recovery wipe; "sealant" in any apparatus body; every numeric value; "removable from the valve stem"; and "conical" for the sealing face. The one clause I added beyond the bare three distinctions is "the over-travel being produced by the pressure differential acting on the valve member against the biasing member". Without it, "movable ... through an over-travel when the pressure differential exceeds an over-travel threshold" could be read on a valve member that is free to be pushed by hand or by the inflation device's coupling whenever the pressure happens to be high, which is the US11179979B2 [0101] reading. The clause costs the coupling-driven escape, which cannot be closed without reading on that reference and which the description gives no basis to claim.

Claim 2 differs from claim 1 in three phrases, each chosen for support rather than scope: "acting on the valve member" for the pressure differential ([0039], [0043]), "of the valve core" for the purge gap ([0031], [0038]) and "bounding the purge gap" for the sealant-accumulation surfaces ([0037], [0047]). The valve core claim recites a valve seat because [0070] defines the valve core as including the rigid valve seat where present, and the seat ring provides the valve seat in the other embodiments; it does not say which part carries the rigid valve seat, because the description does not (19 A9).

Claim 3 refers to claim 1 rather than to a valve core received in a valve stem because the valve claim is broader; Prompt 23 can add the alternative. I left "containing sealant" out of the claim body because [0079] defines the inflatable body as containing the sealant, and a positive recital would let a tube maker who fits the valve without adding sealant escape; counsel may prefer the recital, and Q6 asks.

Claim 4 follows [0015] step for step. "Removing the inflation device" is the wording of [0015] and [0111]; [0039] also covers the device ceasing to apply pressure, so a wider final step has basis if counsel wants it, and the table says so.

Numerals are given for registered components only; the valve seat and the deflectable element have no numeral of their own in the registry (32 and 132, 30 and 130 are species), so none is given. As a preview of the Prompt 25 checks, I ran the antecedent and claims-format checks on the four claims; the results are reported in the task comment, not here, because the support map and those checks are Prompt 25 deliverables.

Statutory provisions are named for orientation only; counsel applies them.

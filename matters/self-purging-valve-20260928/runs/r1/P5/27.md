## Rationale

The list is ordered by how much a competitor or a spare-parts seller could take without infringing the present set, and then by how far the description already supports the concept. Concept 1 leads because the seat ring is the one part with an express article-level disclosure ([0095]), a real market (spares and component supply) and no claim in the present set that catches it when sold alone. Concept 2 follows because it is the alternative nucleus that `21`, `24` and `25` deliberately held back, and the first filing must carry [0011] so that the choice remains open after the inventor answers 25 Q4. Concept 3 is the design-around that the present genus covers but that could be pursued as its own invention by a competitor who develops the two-piece form; the first filing should carry the passage that makes it ours, which today it does only in outline.

I drafted concepts 4 to 9 at the depth the description gives and no deeper; where the description's words are the inventor's ([0108], [0122], [0129]) the concept quotes them, and where they entered as amendment basis from AI-proposed embodiments ([0119] to [0122]) the concept says so, because a divisional resting on an unconfirmed construction would be a claim the inventor has not conceived. Concept 9 is included against my own preference, because the case in which it matters (structural fallbacks anticipated, effect remaining) is the case in which counsel will want the test conditions already stated in claim-ready form, and [0129] provides them.

The thermal purge variant is listed last and marked as a separate invention, because [0097] states it cannot be combined with the over-travel embodiments and [0096] states there are no data; it is here so that the disclosure request is on record, not because it is claimable.

I did not raise a disclosure request for the kit or the system, since no commercial case for either exists in `02` and I will not invent one. Statutory provisions are named for orientation only; counsel applies them.

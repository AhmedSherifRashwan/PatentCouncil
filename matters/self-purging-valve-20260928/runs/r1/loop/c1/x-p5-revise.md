## Rationale

**What the four findings allow a claims revision to do.** F-33-2 is a claims finding and is answered in full by new claims. F-28-3, F-30-1 and F-30-2 are findings about what the Description says the prototype is. x-p4-revise left every paragraph they name unchanged, because the facts are in no confirmed entry and no intake file. A claim cannot supply a fact the Description lacks, so for those three the honest result is: no claim reworded, the defect reported as open, and the alternatives set out for the human.

**Why claims 7, 14, 16, 17, 39, 40 and 41 are not recast now.** I tested three recasts. (1) Claim 7 without "below the sealing face (42)": supportable, because [0029] and [0054] say the position is only that of the prototype. (2) Claims 16 and 40 as "annular and bounded at least in part by an outer surface (38) of the seat ring (30)": supportable from [0037], [0046], [0058] and [0103]. (3) Claims 17 and 41 without the closing clause on what the radial expansion opens: supportable from [0030] and [0064]. Each would take the contested words out of the claims. I did not apply them, for three reasons. First, each removes from the claims a statement the inventors made about their own prototype, and only they can say which of the conflicting statements is the wrong one; if "below the sealing face" and "between the outer surface and the core bore" turn out to be right and "upper edge" or "expands" wrong, the recast would have cut the correct words. Second, the adjudicated action for F-30-1 makes recasting the fallback "only if unavailable", the inventors have not yet been asked (the run is unattended), and the Specification Drafter recorded the same step as one for the human. Third, the recast would lower the severity of the finding at claim level while the Description stayed defective, which would make the draft look nearer to filing than it is. "Upper edge" in claims 14 and 39 cannot be recast at all: it is the registry name of numeral 32 and the Description has no orientation-free term for it.

**The new family.** [0011] has provided an aspect founded on the recovery wipe since the first composed Description; [0011a], [0048a] and the amended [0016] now free the term "deflectable element" from the purge gap in that aspect, and [0015a] and [0111a] give the method. The independent claims keep the three ranges of claim 1 because they are what separate the claims from the wiping art once the purge gap is gone: the references read wipe at every closing, and US3417778A has its lip deflected at rest. Without the ranges, claim 61 would be close to a resilient seat that rubs the valve body. I dropped the main flow passage and the inflation gas from the new independents: in claim 1 they exist to define the purge gap as a second path, and with no purge gap they distinguish nothing. I did not recite the return of the valve member in the valve and valve core claims ([0055] mentions it) because every valve member returns; it is in the method claim, where [0015a] lists it as a step. The ranges are stated positively ("remains undeflected"), following claim 1 and lesson L1.

**Ladder.** Each rung repeats wording already in the set, so the two families read alike: purge shoulder (structure of the deflection), two-rate characteristic (means of the threshold), superelastic alloy (the material the inventors say gives the strongest recovery wipe), seat ring that provides the valve seat, convoluted wall, and wiper ring with a rigid valve seat. The seat ring rungs are drafted from [0048] ("the deflectable element may provide the valve seat, in which case it is a seat ring") and recite neither "upper" nor "below", so they do not inherit F-28-3 at claim level. I did not add to the new family a rung that the upper edge slides across the sealing face (claims 57 and 59 have it for the first family): it rests on [0034a], which is confined to the first embodiment, and it recites the contested edge. I did not add range rungs (pressures, over-travel distance): F-28-2 is open on whether the ranges hold beyond the first embodiment. No rung recites the purge gap under claim 61, because claim 61 with a purge gap is claim 5.

**The wiper ring.** Claims 67 and 76 give the third embodiment a parent that does not need a purge gap. That is the home the Specification Drafter pointed to for F-28-1. Claims 27 to 29, 45, 58 and 60 are untouched; whether they go is for the human.

**Method claim.** The finding asked for valve and valve core claims. I added the method because the same escape avoids claim 4, the category already exists in the set, and [0015a] was written for it. It is the first candidate for pruning if counsel wants fewer independent claims.

**Cost of the new family.** Six independent claims in three categories, two per category. At the EPO that needs Rule 43(2)(c) (alternative solutions) or an election; in the US it is three independent claims over the basic fee and many multiple dependencies. Unity rests on the feature common to claims 1 and 61: over-travel of the valve member above a second, higher threshold deflecting a deflectable element that is undeflected during the normal stroke. If that common feature is found known, unity falls with it.

**What the new family does not do.** It does not catch a valve whose over-travel widens the passage at the valve seat and which does not wipe (x-loopback-p3 AE-3); that needs matter the inventors have not confirmed. And it shares with claim 1 the dependence on a working arrangement for the deflection, which is F-28-3.

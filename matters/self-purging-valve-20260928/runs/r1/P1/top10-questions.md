# Top 10 inventor questions — self-purging valve (run r1, Gate 1)

This is a drafting aid prepared by AI. It is not legal advice or a filing decision; qualified patent counsel signs off before anything is filed.

These are the ten questions whose answers would change the claims most, ranked. Tick one answer (or more where marked), or write under "Other". Pick an option only if it matches what you built or have actually thought of. The options are prompts, not suggestions, and "don't know" is a good answer. The full list of 56 questions for counsel is in `05.md`; each item below gives its number there.

**1. How does the purge gap open?** (05 Q1–Q4)
The disclosure says the seat ring expands outward and opens a gap against the bore. It also says the ring is pressed into that bore, and expanding outward against a bore would close a gap, not open one.
- a) The ring is held at one end only; its free end moves, and the gap opens between that free end and the core bore.
- b) The gap opens on the inside of the ring, between the ring and the valve member, not against the bore.
- c) The ring lifts off, or moves away from, the locating shoulder, and the gap opens there.
- d) Other (please describe or sketch):

*What it changes:* whether the broadest claim is supported at all. Every claim that mentions the purge gap is written around this answer, and so is the figure showing it. Until it is answered, the core of the application is at risk of an enablement objection.

**2. Which effect keeps the valve working?** (05 Q7, Q51)
- a) Mainly the air flow through the purge gap.
- b) Mainly the wipe as the ring springs back across the sealing face.
- c) Both are needed together.
- d) Don't know; no test separates them.
- e) Other:

*What it changes:* a) keeps the purge flow as the core of the main claim; b) moves the wipe into the main claim and makes the purge flow a narrower fallback; c) puts both in the main claim, which is narrower and easier to design around; d) keeps both routes open as parallel claims and the application relies on the description.

**3. What sets the higher pressure that causes over-travel?** (05 Q13–Q14)
- a) Two separate springs.
- b) One spring with varying coil pitch.
- c) The stiffness of the seat ring itself acts as the second stage.
- d) A spring plus a stop or detent.
- e) Other:

Also: could the over-travel be set by something other than a two-rate spring? Yes (describe) / No / Not considered.

*What it changes:* if only a two-rate spring works, it may have to appear in the main claim; if other means work, the main claim can say "a higher threshold" in general terms, which is harder to design around.

**4. Is the two-piece version (rigid seat plus separate wiper ring) a real option?** (05 Q22)
- a) Yes, and I can describe the wiper ring (shape, material, how it is held, where the purge gap forms).
- b) Yes in principle, but I have no further detail.
- c) No; drop it.

*What it changes:* a) gives cover against the most obvious competitor workaround (avoiding the one-piece NiTi seat ring); b) allows only a brief mention, which may not support a claim; c) the claims stay closer to the one-piece seat ring and are easier to design around.

**5. Which valves and uses should the application cover?** (05 Q45, Q46) Tick all that apply.
- a) Presta-pattern bicycle valves only.
- b) Also Schrader valves.
- c) Also Dunlop/Woods valves.
- d) Also tubeless valve stems.
- e) Non-bicycle tyres (for example motorcycle or wheelchair).
- f) Other:

And: does the valve core fit existing Presta-pattern valve stems with the standard thread? Yes / No / Not yet decided.

*What it changes:* the breadth of the main claims and of the prior-art search. Only what you tick can be described, and only what is described can be claimed. A "yes" on retrofit supports a separate claim to the replaceable valve core on its own, which is the part that is sold.

**6. When does over-travel happen during an inflation?** (05 Q11–Q12)
- a) Only at the moment the pump head is pushed on.
- b) On every pump stroke.
- c) Throughout inflation, until the tube is nearly full.
- d) Only when the pump head is removed.
- e) Other / don't know:

And: is the pressure difference that drives it measured between the pump side and the tube, or between the pump side and the outside air?

*What it changes:* how the "over-travel threshold" is defined, and whether the method claim describes one purge per inflation or repeated purges. A wrong definition here makes the key claim term unclear.

**8. What is the seat ring's wall like?** (05 Q20–Q21)
- a) Continuous (no slots), with the wave formed by shape-setting.

*What it changes:* whether the seat ring can be made from the description alone, which the law requires. It also sets how broad the claim to the wavy wall can be.

**9. How firm are the ranges in §4?** (05 Q34, Q16)

*What it changes:* estimated ranges are weak fallbacks; ranges with a stated basis and a preferred sub-range give counsel narrower positions to retreat to if the broad claim is rejected.

**10. Has the invention been made public?** (05 Q54)
- a) No: only shown under a confidentiality agreement, or not at all.
- b) Yes: shown, sold, offered, posted or published (give the date and to whom):

*What it changes:* for Europe and the UK, any public disclosure before filing can destroy novelty entirely; the US gives the inventor a one-year grace period for their own disclosures. A "yes" may change which countries can be filed in. Counsel decides.

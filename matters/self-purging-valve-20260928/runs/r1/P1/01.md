#### (a) Problem being solved

- Liquid sealant (typically a latex emulsion with ammonia and suspended particles) is injected into bicycle inner tubes and tubeless set-ups through the valve stem (§1, para 1).
- On each inflation a small quantity of sealant is drawn into the valve core bore "by the pressure surge and by capillary action along the poppet stem" (§1, para 1). Between rides the sealant film dries.
- After 10–20 inflation cycles the valve seat is coated with cured latex; the valve then either leaks slowly (the valve member no longer seats) or blocks (the pump cannot open it) (§1, para 1). Bench data corroborates: conventional cores failed at cycles 12, 14 and 18, two by blockage and one by leak-down.
- Current remedy: unscrew the core with a tool, scrape or replace it, re-inject sealant; cores are sold in multipacks for this reason (§1, para 1).
- The inventor states that existing attempts (low-adhesion polymer coating on the valve member, enlarged bore, removable insert) only slow build-up and do not remove a film once formed (§1, para 2). This is the inventor's characterisation, not a prior-art finding; it is recorded here as background and is for the Prior-Art Strategist to test against saved references.

#### (b) Core inventive concept

As stated (§2, paras 1–2):

1. The valve member has a normal stroke during normal inflation, and travels beyond it ("over-travel") when the inflation device applies a pressure differential above a threshold (§2, para 1).
2. The over-travel drives the purge shoulder (44) against the seat ring (30), which flexes radially outward and opens a secondary annular gap, the purge gap (60) (§2, para 1; §3 items 4, 6).
3. Air at pump pressure flows through the purge gap "across the exact surfaces on which sealant accumulates" and blows the sealant film down into the tube (§2, para 1).
4. When the inflation device is removed the pressure differential collapses, the valve member returns, and the seat ring recovers its shape (§2, para 2).
5. Because superelastic recovery is fast and stiff, the recovering seat ring "wipes across the poppet face with a snap", shearing off partially cured latex (§2, para 2). The inventor calls the result cleaning "twice per inflation": purge flow, then recovery wipe (§2, para 2).

Inconsistency to flag: §2 places the purge gap "between the ring and the stem bore"; §3 item 6 places it "between the seat ring outer surface and the core body bore". This reply uses §3's wording (core bore) as the more specific statement, labelled as assumption A1. See also (m) M1: the disclosure does not explain how outward radial expansion of a ring held by an interference fit in the core bore opens, rather than closes, a gap against that bore.

#### (c) Technical effect

- Stated: "the valve keeps sealing and keeps opening after far more sealant-loaded inflation cycles than a conventional core, with no tool and no rider action" (§2, para 3).
- Evidence: 3 of 3 prototype cores completed 40 cycles with no failure; 3 of 3 conventional cores failed between cycles 12 and 18 (§8; bench data).
- Mechanistic effects asserted but not separately measured: removal of sealant film by purge flow; shearing of partially cured latex by the recovery wipe (§2). No test isolates either mechanism (see (l) and M8).

#### (d) Essential components/steps

As the inventor presents the device (§3). Which of these is essential to the effect is analysed in Prompt 03; this list records what the disclosure says the device has, not a judgement of essentiality.

1. Valve member (40) movable relative to a valve seat through a normal stroke, with a sealing face (42) that seats on the upper edge of the seat ring (§3 item 4).
2. A purge shoulder (44) on the valve member below the sealing face that contacts the seat ring only during over-travel (§3 item 4).
3. Seat ring (30): a short tube of superelastic NiTi with a convoluted wall ("one or two shallow convolutions (a wave)") so that axial load on its upper edge becomes radial expansion; pressed into the core body with a light interference fit and located against a shoulder (the locating shoulder) (§3 item 3; §7).
4. Biasing member (50): a light compression spring under the valve member with a two-rate characteristic, a soft first stage for normal opening and a stiffer second stage that the pressure differential must overcome to reach over-travel (§3 item 5).
5. Purge gap (60): annular clearance, closed at rest, that opens when the seat ring expands (§3 item 6).
6. Core body (20) carrying the seat ring and guiding the valve member, threaded to the valve stem (§3 item 2).

Sequence of operation (steps), from §2:

- S1 Inflation device applies a pressure differential; valve member lifts through its normal stroke (opening pressure, first stage of the biasing member).
- S2 Differential exceeds the over-travel threshold; valve member over-travels against the second stage.
- S3 Purge shoulder bears on the seat ring; seat ring deflects radially; purge gap opens.
- S4 Purge flow passes through the purge gap across the sealant-accumulation surfaces into the inflatable body.
- S5 Inflation device removed; differential collapses; valve member returns; seat ring recovers; recovery wipe across the sealing face; purge gap closes.

Inferred, not stated: that S2–S4 occur during the inflation rather than only at the moment of pump-head engagement or removal (the bench notes record "an audible snap on pump-head removal", which fits S5). Labelled assumption A3; question M5.

#### (e) Optional components

- Lock nut (70): conventional, for manually closing the valve member for storage and transport (§3 item 7).
- Valve stem (10): conventional Presta-pattern brass stem, 6 mm outer diameter, with a threaded bore to accept the core (§3 item 1). Inferred that the valve core is intended to fit a conventional stem (assumption A4).
- Electropolished finish on the seat ring (§5, §7): presented as a manufacturing step "to remove the oxide and the heat-affected edge"; the disclosure does not say whether it matters to sealing or wiping (M19).

#### (f) Alternative implementations

As conceived by the inventor; none built (§6).

- Alternative A — polymer seat ring: a PEEK seat ring with the same convoluted wall; some radial compliance, cheaper; slower recovery and lower recovery force, so a weaker recovery wipe. Considered for a lower-cost product line; not built (§6, para 1).
- Alternative B — two-piece seat: a rigid brass valve seat for sealing plus a separate thin stainless spring wiper ring below it, deflected outward by the purge shoulder. Separates sealing from purge/wipe; one more part, more axial length; "might be easier to manufacture". Not built (§6, para 2).
- Thermal variant: a NiTi seat ring trained with two-way shape memory, Af around 35–40 °C, so that warming by hand opens the purge gap for a manual purge without a pump. The inventor is "not sure this is worth pursuing and [has] no data" (§6, para 3).
- Material alternatives: core body in stainless 303 (machined, §5); hard-anodised aluminium considered for the sealing face (§5); spring material not critical (§5).

No other alternative is recorded. Other valve standards, other deflectable-element geometries or other materials are not disclosed and are not supplied here (M12, M13).

#### (g) Inputs/outputs

- Input: gas (air) from an inflation device (floor pump, pump head) at a pressure differential across the valve (§2). CO2 cartridge inflators are mentioned as an untested case (§9 item 2).
- Input (contaminant): liquid sealant present in the inflatable body and drawn into the valve (§1).
- Outputs: inflation of the inflatable body; purge flow carrying sealant film back into the inflatable body (§2, para 1); a closed, sealing valve at rest (§3 item 6).
- Observable: "audible snap on pump-head removal" confirming over-travel (bench data, notes).

#### (i) Control logic/software

None. The device is purely mechanical; the "logic" is the two-threshold behaviour set by the biasing member: below the opening pressure the valve stays closed; between the opening pressure and the over-travel threshold the valve member makes its normal stroke; above the over-travel threshold it over-travels and the purge gap opens (§3 item 5; §4).

#### (j) Manufacturing methods

From §7:

1. Laser-cut seat rings from NiTi tube (2.7 mm OD, 0.20 mm wall) "to the convoluted profile on a mandrel".
2. Shape-set at 500 °C for 10 minutes in a fixture holding the wave geometry.
3. Water-quench.
4. Electropolish to remove oxide and the heat-affected edge.
5. Press the seat ring into the core body with ≈0.03 mm diametral interference, seated against the internal (locating) shoulder.
6. Core body and valve member by ordinary turning; "no other special process".

Unclear: what the laser cut produces. A convolution in the wall's axial section is not usually made by cutting through a wall, so the cut may form slots or an edge profile rather than the wave itself (M3). Not resolved here.

#### (k) Use cases

- Bicycle inner tubes and tubeless set-ups run with liquid sealant (§1).
- Inflation by a floor pump (§2, §8); the disclosure says the over-travel threshold is one "which every floor pump reaches" (§2). That is an unevidenced generalisation (A6).
- CO2 cartridge inflation is a named but untested case (§9 item 2).
- Storage and transport with the lock nut closed (§3 item 7).

Other valve standards (Schrader, Dunlop/Woods), other vehicles and other sealant chemistries are not disclosed (M12).

#### (l) Experimental evidence

Bench test, 2026-08-12 to 2026-08-20 (§8; bench data):

- Set-up: 3 prototype cores, 3 conventional cores of the same manufacturer's standard Presta core; identical 700×28 tubes, 60 ml latex sealant each; cycle = inflate to 6 bar with a floor pump, hold 24 h at 22 °C, deflate fully through the valve.
- Failure criterion: leak-down > 0.5 bar in 24 h, or pump unable to open the valve within 5 s of engagement.
- Conventional: C-1 blocked at cycle 14; C-2 leak-down 0.7 bar at cycle 12; C-3 blocked at cycle 18.
- Prototype: P-1, P-2, P-3 no failure in 40 cycles; inflation time cycle 1 → cycle 40: 43 → 47 s, 41 → 46 s, 44 → 50 s (increase 9 %, 12 %, 14 %; the mean of 42.7 s → 47.7 s is 12 %, consistent with "within 15 %"); leak-down at cycle 40: 0.1, 0.1, 0.2 bar/24 h.
- Over-travel confirmed on every prototype inflation by an audible snap on pump-head removal.
- Inspection at cycle 40 (stereo microscope): cured film on the ring outer surface in the purge-gap region; sealing edge and poppet face clean.
- Not performed: fatigue testing, cold-shock testing, ammonia-free sealants, isolation of purge flow versus recovery wipe, tests beyond 40 cycles, tests of alternatives A, B or the thermal variant.

Limits of the evidence as recorded: n = 3 per arm; one sealant; one temperature; one pressure; the test stopped at 40 cycles with no prototype failure, so it gives a lower bound and not the service life.

## Rationale

I used the inventor's §3 component list as the spine and quoted the section for each item so the support trail starts here. I renamed three inventor terms for neutrality and kept them consistent: "poppet" → valve member (a poppet is one kind of valve member); "return spring" → biasing member (the two-rate behaviour, not the coil form, is what matters); "snap-back wipe" → recovery wipe (describes the action, not the sound). "Over-travel" and "purge gap" are the inventor's own terms and are already neutral; both will need express definitions.

I introduced "deflectable element" as a genus for the seat ring because alternative B places the purge/wipe function in a separate wiper ring. Without a genus term the one-piece and two-piece versions cannot be described together. I also introduced "valve seat" as distinct from "seat ring" for the same reason: in alternative B the valve seat is rigid brass.

The main physical inconsistency (M1) is recorded, not resolved. Choosing the more plausible reading of how the purge gap opens would be inventing a mechanism, and the answer decides whether the device as described is enabled.

(d) lists what the device has, not what is essential. Essentiality belongs to Prompt 03. The disclosure contains no personal data unrelated to the invention; the only name is the inventor's, which relates to inventorship.

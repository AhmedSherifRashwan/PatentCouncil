### Equivalent Architecture Library

Status: candidate. This is a drafting and analysis aid prepared by AI. It is not legal advice or a filing decision; qualified patent counsel signs off before anything is filed. Nothing here is specification content until Human Gate 2 confirms that it is technically real and within what the inventors have conceived.

Sources: `P1/01` (structured disclosure; `§` is `intake/disclosure.md` as 01 records it), `P1/03` (nucleus and feature classification), `P2/09` (differentiation matrix), `P2/10` (white space), `P1/02` (commercial value map) and `terminology/LOCKED.md`. `intake/answers.md` does not exist, so no Gate 1 answer is relied on. `P3/11` had not been written when this library was prepared, so no design-around ids are cross-referenced; the Orchestrator can map them at Gate 2 from the "avoids" wording in each entry.

Coverage: one H4 per feature that 03 classifies essential or advantageous, plus the conventional features that carry the mechanism (core body and its retention interface, valve core and valve stem, inflation device and inflatable body) and the manufacturing steps. Each entry is labelled `E<section>.<n>` so that Prompts 13 to 15 and the Gate 2 form can cite it.

Each entry gives: **Structure** (what it is), **Purpose** (how it achieves the same technical purpose), **Changes** (interfaces, ordering, materials), **Genus** (the wording that would embrace the prototype and the alternative), **Credibility** (the technical specialist's view, including why an alternative is excluded where relevant), **Rests on** (the disclosure passage, or "nothing yet" with the Gate 2 question that must be answered), and **Status**. "Rests on: nothing yet" means the alternative is a proposal of this library, not a conception recorded in the dossier; Gate 2 must confirm or strike it.

Two facts limit every entry. First, the disclosure does not explain how outward deflection of a seat ring held by an interference fit opens a purge gap against the core bore (01 Q1; 03 enablement flag; 09 Q2). The purge gap section therefore offers coherent topologies for the inventor to pick from, not a statement of what the prototype does. Second, no test isolates purge flow from the recovery wipe (03 Q1), so an alternative that weakens one action is not known to weaken the effect.

#### valve member

1. **E1.1 One-piece turned valve member with conical sealing face and integral purge shoulder (prototype).** Structure: a turned brass part carrying the sealing face (42) and, below it, the purge shoulder (44); guided by the core body; urged towards the valve seat by the biasing member (50). Purpose: seals in the rest state, makes the normal stroke and the over-travel. Changes: none. Genus: valve member. Credibility: built and tested (§3 item 4; §8). Rests on: §3 items 2, 4; 01 (d) 1–2. Status: candidate.
2. **E1.2 Two-part valve member: sealing head plus separate stem joined by a threaded or pressed joint.** Structure: the sealing face is on a head; the guided stem is a separate part fixed to it; the purge shoulder is either on the head or is a separate collar on the stem (see E5.3). Purpose: same sealing, normal stroke and over-travel; the joint lets the axial distance between the sealing face and the purge shoulder be set at assembly, which fixes the normal stroke and the over-travel threshold contact point without re-machining. Changes: assembly adds a joint; a calibration step becomes possible (10 Q7). Genus: "a valve member, in one piece or assembled from a head and a stem". Credibility: high; conventional valve cores already carry a threaded tip and lock nut on the valve member. Rests on: nothing yet; Gate 2 must say whether an adjustable purge shoulder position was conceived (Q2). Status: candidate.
3. **E1.3 Valve member guided below the valve seat instead of above it.** Structure: the valve member is guided by a bore or by a spring cup in the core body on the inflatable-body side of the deflectable element, so that the region above the valve seat carries no guide surface. Purpose: same motion; removes a guide clearance on the pump side where sealant is drawn in "by capillary action along the poppet stem" (§1 as recorded in 01 (a)), so fewer sealant-accumulation surfaces sit upstream of the valve seat. Changes: guide location and core body machining; the lower guide must let purge flow and the main flow passage exit. Genus: "the valve member being guided by the core body" without stating where. Credibility: high; both guide positions are ordinary valve-core practice. Rests on: §3 item 2 says only that the core body guides the valve member; where is not stated (nothing yet on position). Status: candidate.
4. **E1.4 Valve member of stainless steel or titanium alloy instead of brass.** Structure: same geometry in an austenitic stainless steel or a titanium alloy. Purpose: same function; harder sealing face for repeated contact with the recovering edge of the deflectable element; corrosion resistance with ammonia-bearing sealant, the reason given for the stainless 303 core body (§5). Changes: material and machining only. Genus: "a metal valve member". Credibility: high. Rests on: §5 gives brass (prototype) and a hard-anodised aluminium sealing face (considered); other metals are nothing yet (Q3). Status: candidate.

#### sealing face

1. **E2.1 Conical sealing face seating on the upper edge of the seat ring (prototype).** Structure: a conical face (42) whose line of contact with the ring's upper edge is the valve seat. Purpose: rest-state seal; the surface swept by the recovery wipe. Changes: none. Genus: sealing face. Credibility: built (§3 item 4; §8 inspection found the sealing face clean at cycle 40). Rests on: §3 item 4; LOCKED species "conical sealing face (42)". No cone angle is in the dossier; Prompt 13 must not ladder one without the inventor (Q4). Status: candidate.
2. **E2.2 Spherical or toroidal crown sealing face.** Structure: the sealing face is a convex surface of revolution rather than a straight taper. Purpose: the same line seal against the upper edge of the deflectable element; a convex face tolerates small misalignment of the valve member and keeps a line contact as the edge recovers across it. Changes: turning geometry only; the recovery wipe path becomes a curve rather than a straight taper. Genus: "a sealing face that is a surface of revolution converging towards the valve seat, for example conical, spherical or toroidal". Credibility: high; ball-seat and spherical-crown check valves are standard. Rests on: nothing yet (Q4). Status: candidate.
3. **E2.3 Flat annular sealing face on a narrow raised upper edge.** Structure: the valve member carries a flat face; the valve seat is a narrow, raised edge of the deflectable element. Purpose: line seal on a plane; on recovery the edge slides radially inward across the plane, so the recovery wipe becomes a defined radial sliding motion (bears on 01 Q7). Changes: the purge shoulder must then be a separate step because a flat face has no taper to bear on the ring; the seal is more sensitive to squareness of the valve member. Genus: as E2.2, widened to "a sealing face against which an edge of the deflectable element seats". Credibility: moderate; flat-on-edge seals work with hard, well-finished parts but are less tolerant of tilt than a taper. Rests on: nothing yet (Q4, Q7 of 01). Status: candidate.
4. **E2.4 Stepped sealing face with a cylindrical land below the seating line.** Structure: below the conical seating line the valve member has a short cylindrical land on which the upper edge of the deflectable element rides during over-travel and recovery. Purpose: defines the recovery wipe as an axial sliding of the edge along the land, and keeps the seating line itself out of the sliding contact so it is not worn. Changes: adds a land between the sealing face and the purge shoulder; the over-travel must be at least the land length. Genus: "the sealing face and a region of the valve member adjacent to it across which the deflectable element slides on recovery". Credibility: high. Rests on: nothing yet; this is one of the answers 01 Q7 could take (Q7). Status: candidate.
5. **E2.5 Surface treatment of the sealing face: hard-anodised aluminium (considered), hard chrome, nitrided stainless or diamond-like carbon.** Structure: a hard, smooth surface layer on the sealing face. Purpose: resists wear from the recovering edge and keeps the face smooth so sealant film shears rather than keys into scratches. Changes: material or coating step. Genus: "a sealing face having a hardness greater than the deflectable element's wiping edge" or "a hard surface layer". Credibility: high for the treatments named; note that the disclosure records low-adhesion polymer coatings as a prior attempt that only slows build-up (§1 para 2 as recorded in 01 (a)), so a soft coating is not entered. Rests on: §5 for hard-anodised aluminium (whether whole valve member or face only is 01 Q18); the other treatments are nothing yet (Q3). Status: candidate.

#### over-travel

1. **E3.1 Over-travel produced by the pressure differential acting on the valve member (prototype).** Structure: the inflation device's pressure differential, acting over the valve member's exposed area, overcomes the second stage of the biasing member. Purpose: the trigger for purge, with no tool or rider action. Changes: none. Genus: over-travel. Credibility: built; over-travel confirmed by the audible snap on each prototype inflation (§8; bench notes). Rests on: §2 para 1; §3 item 5; §4. Status: candidate.
2. **E3.2 Over-travel limited by a hard stop on the core body.** Structure: a shoulder of the valve member meets a stop face of the core body at the end of the over-travel. Purpose: fixes the over-travel, and so the purge gap width, independently of how hard the inflation device pushes; protects the deflectable element from over-strain at high pressure differential (relevant to the untested CO2 case, §9 item 2). Changes: adds a stop face; the biasing member never coil-binds. Genus: "the over-travel being limited by a stop". Credibility: high. Rests on: nothing yet; the disclosure gives an over-travel of 0.5 mm (§4) but not what limits it (Q5). Status: candidate.
3. **E3.3 Over-travel limited by coil-bind of the biasing member or by the deflectable element's own stiffness.** Structure: no stop face; the second stage of the biasing member goes solid, or the axial stiffness of the deflected element rises steeply enough to end the travel. Purpose: same limit with one part fewer. Changes: the over-travel then varies with the pressure differential applied, so the purge gap width is less repeatable than with E3.2. Genus: as E3.2 with "a stop, the biasing member or the deflectable element". Credibility: moderate; works, but the deflectable element then carries the full load of a high-pressure inflation device. Rests on: nothing yet (Q5). Status: candidate.
4. **E3.4 Over-travel produced by mechanical engagement of the inflation device.** Structure: a depressor of the inflation device's head pushes the valve member through its normal stroke and into over-travel when the head is fitted, as Schrader-pattern heads already depress the valve member. Purpose: purge on every fitting of the head whatever pressure the device reaches; it removes the dependence on a pressure differential above the over-travel threshold, which the disclosure asserts but does not evidence for all inflation devices (01 A6). Changes: the trigger moves from pressure to the head; for a Presta-pattern valve stem this needs a head that engages the valve member, which standard Presta heads do not. Genus: "the over-travel being produced by the pressure differential and/or by engagement of the inflation device with the valve member". Credibility: high for Schrader-pattern valves; low for the Presta-pattern prototype without a special head. This is also the escape route 09 D2 names; disclosing it closes that route only if the inventor has conceived it. Rests on: nothing yet (Q6). Status: candidate.
5. **E3.5 Deflection without over-travel: thermal purge variant.** Structure: the deflectable element is a two-way shape-memory seat ring with an austenite finish temperature around 35 to 40 °C; warming by hand opens the purge gap. Purpose: a manual purge without an inflation device. Changes: the trigger is temperature, not travel; it lies outside 03 N0 and is a separate concept (02 rank 12; 03 Q6). Genus: none shared with over-travel; "the purge gap being opened by deflection of the deflectable element, the deflection being produced by over-travel or by a change of temperature". Credibility: two-way shape memory is real, but how warming opens a gap that over-travel opens by axial load is not described; the inventor has no data (§6 para 3). Rests on: §6 para 3; how it opens the purge gap is 01 Q14 (Q8). Status: candidate.

#### biasing member

1. **E4.1 Single coil compression spring of stainless music wire under the valve member (prototype).** Structure: a compression spring (50) between the core body and the valve member. Purpose: returns the valve member to the valve seat and sets the opening pressure. Changes: none. Genus: biasing member. Credibility: built (§3 item 5; §5). Rests on: §3 item 5; §5 ("nothing in the concept depends on the spring material"). Status: candidate.
2. **E4.2 Wave spring or stack of disc springs.** Structure: a multi-turn wave spring, or disc springs stacked in series, in place of the coil spring. Purpose: the same return force in less axial length, which matters because the valve core has little length to spare and alternative B "adds axial length" (§6 para 2); disc springs also give a non-linear force curve that can provide the two-rate characteristic (see E5.4). Changes: spring form and its seats; a spring cup may be needed to centre the stack. Genus: "a biasing member, for example a coil spring, a wave spring or a stack of disc springs". Credibility: high; both are standard at this scale. Rests on: nothing yet (Q9). Status: candidate.
3. **E4.3 Biasing member integral with the deflectable element.** Structure: a single laser-cut tube of superelastic alloy in which the upper region is the seat ring and a lower region, cut as a compliant flexure, bears on a spring cup or on the lower end of the valve member and urges it towards the valve seat. Purpose: one part provides the valve seat, the deflectable element and the biasing member; fewer parts and no separate spring to corrode. Changes: the core body no longer seats a spring; the flexure region must be shielded from sealant or tolerate it; the two-rate characteristic would be cut into the tube. Genus: "the biasing member and the deflectable element being formed in one piece" under the genus biasing member. Credibility: moderate. Laser-cut compliant structures in this alloy are established at similar wall thickness, and the loads are small: with the seat ring inner diameter of 2.3 mm and an opening pressure of 1.0 bar (§4), the opening force is of the order of 0.4 N, and the over-travel threshold of 3.5 bar is of the order of 1.5 N (this library's estimate for credibility only, not a disclosure value). Fatigue of the flexure under 40-plus cycles is untested. Rests on: nothing yet (Q9). Status: candidate.
4. **E4.4 Spring materials other than stainless music wire.** Structure: the same spring in 17-7 PH stainless, a nickel-based superalloy wire, titanium alloy wire or superelastic alloy wire. Purpose: same return force; corrosion resistance with ammonia-bearing sealant; the disclosure says the concept does not depend on the spring material (§5). Changes: material only. Genus: "a metal spring". Credibility: high for the alloys named. Copper-alloy spring wire (phosphor bronze, beryllium copper) is not entered because copper alloys are exposed to stress-corrosion cracking in ammonia environments, and a spring under sustained load is the worst case; the disclosure's own brass parts are not springs. Rests on: §5 for the statement of non-dependence; specific alloys are nothing yet (Q9). Status: candidate.

#### two-rate characteristic

1. **E5.1 Variable-pitch single spring.** Structure: a coil spring whose close-pitched coils bind after the normal stroke, leaving fewer active coils and a higher rate. Purpose: a soft first stage sets the opening pressure; the bound length sets the transition; the remaining coils set the over-travel threshold. Changes: none to the core body; the transition point is fixed by the spring design. Genus: "a biasing member having a two-rate characteristic". Credibility: high; this is the most common way a single spring is given two rates, and is probably how the prototype spring is made, though the disclosure does not say (01 Q6). Rests on: §3 item 5 (two-rate characteristic); how it is realised is nothing yet (Q10). Status: candidate.
2. **E5.2 Two springs in series with an intermediate collar and stop.** Structure: a soft spring and a stiff spring stacked end to end through a floating collar; the collar meets a stop on the core body at the end of the normal stroke, after which only the stiff spring deflects. Purpose: the two rates are set by two ordinary springs and the transition by a machined stop, so each of the opening pressure, the normal stroke and the over-travel threshold can be set independently. Changes: two springs, a collar and a stop; more axial length. Genus: as E5.1, or "a first spring setting the opening pressure and a second spring setting the over-travel threshold". Credibility: high. Rests on: nothing yet (Q10). Status: candidate.
3. **E5.3 Two nested springs of different free length.** Structure: an inner and an outer coil spring, coaxial; the shorter, stiffer one is engaged only after the normal stroke. Purpose: as E5.2 with no collar and no added length. Changes: two springs; the free-length difference sets the normal stroke. Genus: as E5.2. Credibility: high. Rests on: nothing yet (Q10). Status: candidate.
4. **E5.4 Linear spring plus the deflectable element's own stiffness as the second stage.** Structure: an ordinary single-rate spring sets the opening pressure; when the purge shoulder meets the deflectable element after the normal stroke, the element's axial stiffness adds to the spring rate and sets the over-travel threshold. Purpose: the same three-band behaviour (10 item 5) with no special spring; the over-travel threshold becomes a property of the deflectable element's wall thickness and convolution amplitude. Changes: the biasing member is single-rate; the seat ring design now carries a second function; the over-travel threshold range of 2.5 to 5.0 bar (§4) would have to be met by the element's stiffness, which is not known. Genus: "the over-travel threshold being set by the biasing member, by the deflectable element, or by both". Credibility: high in principle; the convoluted wall has a definite axial stiffness, and 03 Q3 raised this reading. Finding for Claims Architect: if the inventor confirms this, the two-rate characteristic of the biasing member is preferred, not essential, which matches 03's provisional classification. Rests on: nothing yet; 01 Q6 and 03 Q3 (Q10). Status: candidate.
5. **E5.5 Preloaded second stage (disc spring stack or preloaded spring behind a shoulder).** Structure: the second-stage spring is held under preload by a shoulder so that it does not deflect until the load exceeds the preload. Purpose: gives a sharp, well-defined over-travel threshold rather than a gradual rate change, which makes the audible snap and the purge threshold repeatable. Changes: a preload shoulder or retainer in the core body. Genus: as E5.1. Credibility: high. Rests on: nothing yet (Q10). Status: candidate.
6. **E5.6 Differential-area second stage.** Structure: after the normal stroke a land of the valve member enters a close-fitting bore so that the pressure differential acts on a smaller effective area, and a higher differential is needed for further travel. Purpose: a second threshold set by geometry rather than by spring rate. Changes: adds a close-fitting bore, which is itself a sealant-accumulation surface and a friction surface. Genus: "means setting an over-travel threshold above the opening pressure". Credibility: moderate to low at this scale with sealant present; entered because it is a known valve principle, and Gate 2 may strike it. Rests on: nothing yet (Q10). Status: candidate.

#### purge shoulder

1. **E6.1 Radial annular shoulder below the sealing face (prototype).** Structure: a step (44) on the valve member that bears axially on the upper edge of the seat ring only during over-travel. Purpose: converts over-travel into axial load on the deflectable element. Changes: none. Genus: purge shoulder. Credibility: built (§3 item 4). Rests on: §3 item 4; FIG-1 and FIG-2 captions as recorded in 01. Status: candidate.
2. **E6.2 Tapered (wedge) shoulder.** Structure: the shoulder is a taper that enters the bore of the deflectable element during over-travel and bears on its inner edge. Purpose: applies a radial component directly, so the element is spread outward by wedge action as well as by axial load; less of the deflection then depends on the convoluted wall. Changes: the contact becomes a sliding wedge contact; friction at the wedge adds to the over-travel threshold. Genus: "a purge shoulder that bears on the deflectable element axially and/or radially during over-travel". Credibility: high for a slotted element (E7.3); limited for a continuous ring, because spreading a continuous ring of 2.3 mm inner diameter by a purge gap width of 0.15 mm (§4) by hoop stretch alone needs a hoop strain of the order of 13 % (this library's estimate), beyond the recoverable strain of a superelastic alloy; the convoluted wall avoids that by bending rather than stretching. Rests on: nothing yet (Q11). Status: candidate.
3. **E6.3 Separate collar forming the purge shoulder.** Structure: a collar pressed or threaded onto the valve member stem provides the shoulder; its axial position is set at assembly. Purpose: same contact during over-travel; the clearance between collar and deflectable element in the rest state (which equals the normal stroke) becomes adjustable, giving a calibration step (10 Q7). Changes: one added part; assembly step. Genus: "a purge shoulder carried by the valve member, integral or as a separate collar". Credibility: high. Rests on: nothing yet (Q2). Status: candidate.
4. **E6.4 Discrete lugs or fingers instead of a full shoulder.** Structure: three or four lugs on the valve member contact the deflectable element at points. Purpose: the same axial load; the spaces between lugs leave open flow paths through the contact zone, so purge flow is not throttled at the moment of contact. Changes: point contact concentrates stress on the element's edge. Genus: "a purge shoulder that is continuous or interrupted". Credibility: high. Rests on: nothing yet (Q11). Status: candidate.
5. **E6.5 Contact from below: the deflectable element deflected at its lower end.** Structure: the deflectable element is held at its upper region, and during over-travel a spring cup or a shoulder at the lower end of the valve member bears on the element's lower edge. Purpose: same deflection with the load path reversed; the valve seat region is not the loaded region, so the seating line is not distorted during purge. Changes: which end of the element is held changes, which is precisely the geometry 01 Q1 asks about; the purge shoulder is then not "below the sealing face" but at the valve member's lower end. Genus: "a portion of the valve member that bears on the deflectable element only during over-travel", without stating where. Credibility: moderate; it works mechanically, but sealing at the upper end and deflection at the lower end must be reconciled with the purge gap topology (E9). Rests on: nothing yet (Q1, Q11). Status: candidate.
6. **E6.6 Purge shoulder as a continuation of the sealing face beyond the seating line.** Structure: no separate step; the taper of the sealing face simply continues, and once the valve member has moved past the normal stroke the taper itself bears on the element's upper edge and spreads it. Purpose: one surface seals and, further down, deflects. Changes: the element is then loaded progressively from the start of the normal stroke unless a small step or relief separates the two regions, which conflicts with "contacts the ring only during over-travel" (§3 item 4; 09 D4). Genus: "a purge shoulder, which may be a region of the sealing face beyond the seating line". Credibility: moderate; a real geometry, but it blurs the distinction 09 D4 relies on and may deflect the element during the normal stroke. Entered so that the description can define the purge shoulder functionally and so that Gate 2 can decide whether to keep it. Rests on: nothing yet (Q11). Status: candidate.

#### deflectable element

1. **E7.1 Seat ring with convoluted wall (prototype).** Structure: the seat ring (30), a short tube whose upper edge is the valve seat and whose convoluted wall converts axial load into radial expansion. Purpose: deflection opens the purge gap; recovery closes it and makes the recovery wipe. Changes: none. Genus: deflectable element. Credibility: built (§3 item 3). Rests on: §3 item 3; §7. Status: candidate.
2. **E7.2 Wiper ring separate from a rigid valve seat (alternative B).** Structure: a rigid valve seat on the core body plus a thin stainless spring wiper ring below it, deflected outward by the purge shoulder. Purpose: separates sealing from purge and wipe. Changes: one more part; more axial length; the wiper ring's lip, not the valve seat, wipes the sealing face. Genus: deflectable element. Credibility: conceived, not built; "might be easier to manufacture" (§6 para 2). Its geometry, mounting and how purge flow passes it are not described (01 Q13; 02 Q2). Rests on: §6 para 2. Status: candidate.
3. **E7.3 Slotted deflectable element (collet-type fingers).** Structure: a tube with axial slots from its upper end forming cantilever fingers, splayed outward by a tapered purge shoulder (E6.2); a continuous base region is retained in the core body. Purpose: large, low-force radial deflection of the fingers by bending; on recovery the finger tips spring back across the sealing face. Changes: the slots pass through the wall, so the finger tips cannot themselves form a continuous rest-state seal; the rest-state closure of the purge gap must be made elsewhere (for example the aperture-uncovering topology E9.3 or an inner seal E9.5), and the valve seat is interrupted unless the fingers carry a separate continuous seat above the slots. Genus: "a deflectable element having a wall that is continuous or slotted". Credibility: moderate; the mechanism is sound, the sealing is the difficulty, and the slots are extra sealant-accumulation surfaces. Rests on: nothing yet (Q12). Status: candidate.
4. **E7.4 Split ring whose slit is the purge gap.** Structure: a resilient ring with one axial slit, fitted under hoop preload so that the slit ends abut in the rest state; over-travel spreads the ring (by wedge action or by a convoluted wall) and opens the slit. Purpose: the purge gap is the slit itself, so purge flow passes through the ring wall across the ring's own inner and outer surfaces. Changes: sealing in the rest state relies on a butt joint under preload, which is a poorer seal than a continuous ring; a small leak-down through the slit is the risk. Genus: "a purge gap opened by deflection of the deflectable element, between the deflectable element and the core bore or through the deflectable element". Credibility: moderate to low, because of the rest-state seal; entered because it is a genuinely different topology and cheap to make. Rests on: nothing yet (Q12). Status: candidate.
5. **E7.5 Integral skirt of the core body.** Structure: the deflectable element is a thin-walled skirt turned or cut integrally with the core body (in a resilient metal), with no press fit; the purge gap forms between the skirt's outer surface and the bore of the valve stem. Purpose: no interference fit, no separate ring, no retention interface; the gap lies against the valve stem, which is the wording of §2 ("between the ring and the stem bore") rather than of §3. Changes: the deflectable element is no longer replaceable separately from the core body; the material of the core body must be resilient enough (a stainless or titanium core body rather than brass); the valve stem bore becomes a sealing surface. Genus: "a deflectable element carried by, or formed in one piece with, the core body". Credibility: moderate; thin integral skirts are machinable in stainless at this scale, but the recoverable strain of stainless is far below that of a superelastic alloy, so the skirt must be long and thin to give the purge gap width. Rests on: the §2/§3 inconsistency about where the purge gap lies (01 A1) is the only textual anchor; otherwise nothing yet (Q1, Q12). Status: candidate.
6. **E7.6 Dished (disc-type) deflectable element.** Structure: a shallow conical disc, outer edge against the core bore, inner edge forming the valve seat; axial load on the inner edge flattens the disc and pushes its outer edge outward. Purpose: axial-to-radial conversion by flattening, as a disc spring does, with very short axial length. Changes: the wiping edge is the inner edge of a disc, not the upper edge of a tube; the purge gap topology is at the disc's outer edge. Genus: "a deflectable element having a wall shaped so that axial load on the valve seat produces radial deflection, the wall being tubular with a convolution, or dished". Credibility: moderate to high; disc springs do grow in outer diameter when flattened, and the geometry suits a short valve core. Rests on: nothing yet (Q12). Status: candidate.
7. **E7.7 Materials of the deflectable element.** The material entries are under the superelastic alloy heading (E12), because the lock treats the alloy as the feature and the dossier's alternatives A and B are material changes. An elastomer deflectable element is not entered: the disclosure's premise is that a soft seat lets sealant film embed (09 D8 citing US4050473A [16]), and the lock's avoid list excludes elastomer-part wording.

#### seat ring

1. **E8.1 Seat ring whose upper edge is the valve seat, held by interference fit against the locating shoulder (prototype).** Structure: as §3 item 3 and §7. Purpose: one part is both valve seat and deflectable element. Changes: none. Genus: seat ring. Credibility: built. Rests on: §3 items 3–4; §7. Status: candidate.
2. **E8.2 Seat ring with an inward-turned lip as the valve seat.** Structure: the upper end of the seat ring is turned inward so that the valve seat is the inner edge of a lip rather than the cut end of the tube. Purpose: a defined, rounded seating and wiping edge; the lip stiffens the upper end so that axial load goes into the convoluted wall rather than into local buckling of the cut edge. Changes: forming step (shape-setting can set the lip); the electropolishing of a cut edge (§7) becomes less critical. Genus: "a seat ring providing the valve seat at its upper end". Credibility: high. Rests on: nothing yet (Q13). Status: candidate.
3. **E8.3 Seat ring with a radiused or chamfered wiping edge.** Structure: the wiping edge is deliberately rounded or chamfered on its inner side. Purpose: a controlled shear angle for the recovery wipe and less wear of the sealing face; a sharp edge shears film better but wears the face. Changes: finishing step. Genus: "the upper edge of the seat ring having a wiping edge". Credibility: high; both edge forms are used on metal wiping edges. Which the prototype has is not stated (01 Q19 asks whether the electropolish matters). Rests on: nothing yet (Q13, Q17). Status: candidate.
4. **E8.4 Hybrid seat ring: polymer seat ring with a metal wiping edge insert.** Structure: a polymer seat ring (alternative A) carrying a thin superelastic alloy or stainless edge ring at its upper end, over-moulded or pressed in. Purpose: the low-cost polymer body gives the convoluted wall's compliance; the metal edge restores the stiff recovery wipe that alternative A is said to weaken (§6 para 1). Changes: two materials, a joint. Genus: "a seat ring of one material, or of a polymer body with a metal valve seat". Credibility: moderate; over-moulding metal inserts into PEEK is practised, but the joint is another sealant-accumulation surface and the polymer wall's recovery force drives the wipe. Rests on: §6 para 1 for the polymer seat ring; the insert is nothing yet (Q13). Status: candidate.
5. **E8.5 Valve seat and deflectable element ordered the other way: wiper ring above the rigid valve seat.** Structure: alternative B with the wiper ring on the pump side of the rigid valve seat, so the wiper is met by the purge shoulder before the sealing face reaches the valve seat. Purpose: the wiper sweeps the sealing face on every stroke, not only on over-travel; the rigid seat still seals. Changes: the wiper then deflects during the normal stroke, which departs from 09 D4 and adds drag to every opening; it is entered as a variant of alternative B for Gate 2 to strike or keep. 2. **E9.2 Flared (conical) upper portion.** Structure: the upper portion of the wall is flared outward at a small angle; axial load on the flared edge increases the flare. Purpose: the same radial expansion of the upper edge by bending of a conical shell, with no wave. Changes: the deflection is concentrated at the upper edge, which is the valve seat, so seating and deflection share a region. Genus: "a wall shaped in axial section so that axial load on the upper edge produces radial expansion, for example by one or more convolutions or by a flare". Credibility: high. Rests on: nothing yet (Q14). Status: candidate.
3. **E9.3 More than two convolutions (multi-convolution wall).** Structure: three or more shallow convolutions along the free length. Purpose: more axial compliance and a lower over-travel threshold contribution per unit radial expansion. Changes: longer free length or shallower waves; the free length range of 2.5 to 4.0 mm (§4) limits how many will fit. Genus: "one or more convolutions". Credibility: high in principle; whether more than two were conceived is 01 Q4. Rests on: §3 item 3 ("one or two"); more is nothing yet (Q14). Status: candidate.
4. **E9.4 Oblique (angled) wall section producing a rolling deflection.** Structure: the wall between the retained region and the upper edge is set at an angle to the axis, like the lip of a lip seal, so that axial load rolls the lip outward about the retained region. Purpose: a large radial travel of the upper edge for a small axial travel, at lower strain than a convolution. Changes: the ring becomes a lip rather than a tube; it suits the wiper ring of alternative B (E7.2). Genus: as E9.2. Credibility: high; this is the standard geometry of a metal lip. Rests on: nothing yet (Q14). Status: candidate.
5. **E9.5 Helical or circumferential laser-cut pattern giving axial compliance.** Structure: the wall carries a laser-cut pattern (a helical slit or rows of circumferential slits) so that the tube is axially compliant and barrels outward when compressed. #### purge gap

The disclosure places the purge gap between the seat ring's outer surface and the core bore (§3 item 6) or the stem bore (§2), closed in the rest state and opened when the ring expands. Outward expansion of a ring held by an interference fit in that bore would close, not open, such a gap (01 Q1; 03 enablement flag). The entries below are coherent topologies. Gate 2 must say which, if any, is the prototype's; the others are alternatives the inventor may or may not have conceived.

1. **E10.1 Rest-state closure by the lower edge of the seat ring on the locating shoulder; opened by shortening of the ring.** Structure: the ring is retained by the interference fit in its upper region; below that region the ring's outer surface is a clearance fit in a counterbore of the core bore, forming an annulus that is open to the main flow passage at the top (through the clearance) and closed at the bottom by the ring's lower edge bearing on the locating shoulder. Over-travel compresses the convoluted wall, the ring shortens, and the lower edge lifts off the locating shoulder, opening an axial purge gap; recovery re-seats it. Purpose: a purge gap that is closed in the rest state by metal-to-metal face contact and opened by deflection; purge flow sweeps the ring's outer surface, where the bench inspection found film at cycle 40 (§8). Changes: the closure is axial, not radial; the "radial expansion" of §3 is then a by-product, not the opening action. Genus: "a purge gap closed in the rest state by contact between the deflectable element and the core body and opened by deflection of the deflectable element". Credibility: high as a mechanism; it is a reading of the prototype, not a statement of it. Rests on: §3 items 3 and 6 and §7 (interference fit, locating shoulder, closed at rest) are consistent with it; the opening action is nothing yet (Q1). Status: candidate.
2. **E10.2 Rest-state closure by a convolution crest in a tapered section of the core bore; opened by lifting the crest out of the taper.** Structure: the ring is retained at its upper region; its lower convolution crest seats as a line contact in a section of the core bore that narrows downward; shortening on over-travel lifts the crest up into the wider part of the taper, opening a radial purge gap around the crest. Purpose: a line seal in the rest state with a radial gap on deflection, which matches "annular gap" and "radial" more closely than E10.1. Changes: a tapered core bore; the seal is a line contact between the alloy crest and the brass or stainless bore. Genus: as E10.1. Credibility: high as a mechanism; not stated in the dossier. Rests on: nothing yet (Q1). Status: candidate.
3. **E10.3 Aperture-uncovering (spool-type) purge gap.** Structure: the ring wall carries one or more apertures in the region between the valve seat and the retained region; in the rest state the apertures lie against the core bore wall (closed); on over-travel the wall region carrying the apertures moves (axially, radially or both) so that the apertures come opposite a groove or counterbore in the core bore, connecting the inside of the ring to the outer annulus and thence, through notches in the locating shoulder, to the inflatable body. Purpose: a positively closed gap in the rest state (apertures blanked by the bore) and a positively opened one on deflection; the purge flow passes through the apertures and along the ring's outer surface. Changes: apertures in the ring (laser-cut in the same operation as §7); a groove in the core bore; the seal in the rest state is a land seal, not a line contact. Genus: "a purge gap ... between the deflectable element and the core bore or through the wall of the deflectable element". Credibility: high; this is ordinary spool-valve practice scaled down, and it works with a slotted element (E7.3) where the finger tips cannot seal. Rests on: nothing yet (Q1, Q15). Status: candidate.
4. **E10.4 Purge gap between the deflectable element and the valve stem bore.** Structure: as §2's wording: the deflectable element's outer surface seals against the bore of the valve stem, not of the core body, either because the element is an integral skirt of the core body (E7.5) or because the element projects below the core body into the valve stem. Purpose: the same closed-at-rest, opened-by-deflection gap, with the core bore replaced by the stem bore. Changes: the valve stem's bore becomes a sealing surface, so retrofit into an arbitrary valve stem is no longer assured (01 Q23; 02 A3); the row "core bore" of the lock would need its stem-bore synonym promoted (LOCKED note 4). Genus: "a purge gap between the deflectable element and a surrounding bore of the valve core or of the valve stem". Credibility: high as a mechanism; it resolves the §2/§3 inconsistency as a second embodiment rather than as an error, if the inventor confirms it. Rests on: §2 para 1 wording only (Q1). Status: candidate.
5. **E10.5 Inner purge gap between the deflectable element and the valve member.** Structure: below the sealing face the valve member carries a cylindrical land; a lip of the deflectable element (the wiper ring of alternative B, or a second inward lip of the seat ring) hugs the land under preload in the rest state; the purge shoulder lifts the lip off the land during over-travel. Purpose: purge flow passes between the lip and the valve member, sweeping the valve member's surface along which sealant is drawn by capillary action (§1), and the lip wipes that surface on recovery. Changes: the purge gap is inside, not outside, the deflectable element; the core bore plays no part in the purge; the main flow passage (past the valve seat) and the purge gap are still separate paths. Genus: "a purge gap between the deflectable element and an adjacent surface of the valve core or of the valve member". Credibility: high; a preloaded metal lip on a land is a standard closed-at-rest seal. It departs from 09 D1's "between the seat ring's outer surface and the core bore" and would need its own description. Rests on: §6 para 2 for the wiper ring's existence; the inner gap is nothing yet (Q1, Q15). Status: candidate.
6. **E10.6 Purge gap width defined by a stop rather than by the pressure differential.** Structure: any of E10.1 to E10.5 combined with the hard stop of E3.2, so that the fully open purge gap width (0.15 mm, range 0.10 to 0.25 mm, §4) is set by machining. ## Rationale

The library is organised by the 03 feature list because that is the list a competitor works down: each essential or advantageous feature is a place where they substitute. For each, the entries run from the prototype (always first, so that Gate 2 sees what is real before what is proposed) through the inventor's own alternatives A and B, to alternatives this library proposes. Entries that rest on nothing are the majority, and the "Rests on" line says so every time, so that the Gate 2 form can be answered row by row.

The purge gap section is the centre of the library and the reason the status is partial. The dossier's description of the purge gap is not mechanically coherent as written, and every other section depends on it. Rather than pick one reading, the section lays out five coherent topologies and asks the inventor which is theirs. Whichever is confirmed, the others become the alternatives that close the design-around 09 D1 flags.

Two entries are findings for Claims Architect rather than embodiments: E5.4 (the deflectable element's stiffness can set the over-travel threshold, so the two-rate biasing member is preferred, not essential) and E12.4 (the wipe is relative motion, so the claim should not require the valve member to be stationary). Both are conditional on Gate 2.

Credibility notes carry two order-of-magnitude estimates (the opening force in E4.3 and the hoop strain in E6.2). They are there only to justify including or limiting an entry; they are not disclosure values and must not be laddered.

Excluded on credibility: elastomer deflectable elements (contrary to the disclosure's premise and the avoid list), copper-alloy springs (ammonia), buckling walls (uncontrolled), and any sensing or processing (nothing conceived). Beryllium copper and unnamed polymers are excluded rather than padded in.

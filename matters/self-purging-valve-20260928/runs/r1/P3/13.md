How to read the tables:

- A rung marked **§4**, **§7**, **§8** or **bench data** is a value the inventor gave or measured. The inventor says of the §4 ranges only that they are those "we believe would work"; no range end was tested (01 A5). A §4 range is therefore a supported disclosure rung, not a tested limit.
- A rung marked **proposed** does not appear anywhere in the dossier. It is a fallback position nested inside the inventor's range around the prototype value, or an arithmetic combination of disclosed values. Every proposed rung carries `needs confirmation` and is struck if the inventor cannot confirm it.
- No rung wider than the inventor's own range is proposed anywhere. Widening is an inventor question (Q1), not a drafting choice.
- No §4 range has evidence of criticality (09 Q6). The ladders are fallback positions for dependent claims and amendment basis, not distinctions over the art.
- "Endpoints" lists the range ends that the specification should name individually so that each can be claimed alone later.

**Ladder notes**

1. Physical consistency check that the inventor must resolve before any purge gap rung is used: the seat ring is held by an interference fit of about 0.03 mm on diameter (§7) and is said to open a purge gap width of 0.15 mm against the same core bore by expanding outward (§3 item 6). As written, those two numbers cannot both describe the same surface (03 enablement flag; 01 Q1). Until the geometry is explained, the purge gap width rows in Tables A and E are numbers without a mechanism (Q5).
2. The over-travel threshold of 3.5 bar is below the 6 bar inflation pressure reached in the test. Whether the threshold is a differential between the inflation device and the inflatable body, or an absolute value, decides what the Table B rungs mean (01 Q5; Q6).
3. The only pressure rung that needs no confirmation is qualitative: the over-travel threshold is higher than the opening pressure. Every numeric pressure rung is an untested estimate. The Claims Architect should not rely on any pressure range as a distinction; the 2.5–4.5 bar intermediate rung is offered only because the top of the §4 range overlaps the art (08 N8).
4. Finding for the Claims Architect: no dimension in Table A is shown to be essential. The inventor's alternative B (§6 para 2) has no seat ring at all, and alternative A keeps the convoluted wall in a polymer seat ring, so every Table A rung is a fallback for a seat-ring dependent claim, not a limitation of the nucleus (03 N0).
5. The thermal purge variant's 35–40 °C austenite finish temperature (§6 para 3) is recorded so it is not lost; the inventor has said there is no data and they are unsure it is worth pursuing (03 Q6).

## Rationale

The prompt file sets `include_rationale: false`; this section is kept to the contract minimum. Every intermediate and preferred rung is nested inside the inventor's own range and contains the prototype value, so that confirming it never asserts more than the disclosure already does. Ratios are proposed because a competitor's cheapest escape from a dimensional dependent claim is to rescale the valve for another valve standard; a ratio rung covers that if, and only if, the inventor recognises the relationship as a design rule. Performance rungs go downward from the measured values only. Shape-setting and austenite finish temperature rungs beyond the dossier are proposed because §7 and §9 name the parameters and the physics gives the direction, but no value is asserted.

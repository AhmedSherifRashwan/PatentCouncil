**Footnotes (keys and numbers).**

- [G] Genus column: the feature presupposes a particular form of deflectable element (tubular, retained, of alloy). It is compatible with C1 only where C1's element takes that form; the description should attach the feature to that form, not to the genus.
- [S] Substitution, not addition: the feature replaces a defining feature of the column (the convoluted wall, the superelastic alloy). The result is a distinct embodiment to be described as an alternative, never as "the seat ring of C2 further comprising" the sibling.
- [T] Thermal variant: conditional on 12 Q8 (01 Q14; 03 Q6). Below its austenite finish temperature of 35 to 40 °C the two-way shape-memory ring is wholly or partly martensitic at riding temperatures, so it does not recover elastically when the pressure differential falls. Any pressure-driven over-travel may leave it deformed, and the purge gap open, until it is warmed. The inventor has no data (§6 para 3). No over-travel feature is asserted for C8 until this is answered.
- [H] Only with an inflation device head that engages the valve member: a Schrader-pattern head (R30) or a special head. Standard Presta-pattern heads do not (12 E3.4). Rests on nothing yet (12 Q6).
- [K] Schrader pattern: the head's depressor holds the valve member open during inflation, so the over-travel threshold must be set against the head's push as well as the pressure differential (12 E15.2). Rests on nothing yet (12 Q20).
- [P] The opening pressure and the over-travel threshold are set by the biasing member and carry to any architecture. The normal stroke, over-travel and purge gap width values are prototype values for the alloy seat ring; for another architecture they carry only if the inventor confirms them (13 Table A; 13 Q1, Q2, Q5).
- [U] Untested: CO2 cold shock (§9 item 2; 01 Q16), tubeless set-ups (§1 names them; not tested), ammonia-free sealant (§9; 01 Q17). Disclosed as uses the valve may serve, not as tested performance.
- [L] Whether a closed lock nut prevents over-travel is 01 Q22; the description should not say either way until answered.
- [1] Compatible; the inventor states the recovery wipe is weaker for a polymer seat ring (§6 para 1), and the same follows for a stainless skirt whose recoverable strain is about one tenth of a superelastic alloy's (12 E13.6). State as the inventor's statement, not as data.
- [2] The finger tips wipe in segments, not as a continuous edge, and only if the tips reach the sealing face or a cylindrical land (12 E7.3, E2.4). Whether an interrupted wipe shears sealant film as the continuous edge does is unknown (03 Q1).
- [3] Subject to 01 Q1: the disclosure does not explain how outward deflection of an interference-fitted ring opens a gap against the core bore (03 enablement flag). E10.1 and E10.2 are readings, not statements, of the prototype. For C3 add creep of the polymer at the rest-state contact (12 E13.7).
- [4] The wiper ring's outer surface and the core bore can form the gap, but alternative B's purge flow path is not described (01 Q13; 02 Q2).
- [5] Slotted finger tips cannot form a continuous rest-state closure. The closure must be at the continuous base region (the E10.1 form) or the gap must be R3 (12 E7.3).
- [6] The gap would lie at the disc's outer edge against the core bore, closed by the rim seated on a shoulder; not described in 12 beyond the topology.
- [7] By definition C7's purge gap is against the bore of the valve stem, not the core bore (12 E10.4).
- [8] Apertures laser-cut in the same operation as the ring (§7) pass through the convoluted wall, reduce its axial stiffness and add sealant-accumulation surfaces; the convolution amplitude and wall thickness ranges of §4 were not estimated for an apertured wall.
- [9] A disc has no wall region lying against a bore to blank an aperture; an equivalent needs apertures in the disc uncovered by a groove in the shoulder face, which is a different geometry.
- [10] Apertures in the skirt would be blanked against the bore of the valve stem, a surface that is not controlled in a retrofit; works only with a matched valve stem (R31).
- [11] Needs a second, inward lip of the deflectable element below the seating edge hugging a land of the valve member; the prototype ring has none (12 E10.5). For C3 add preload creep of a polymer lip; for C7 add the machining of an inward lip on an integral skirt.
- [12] The disc's inner edge is the valve seat; a second inner lip needs a second disc.
- [13] Moderate credibility: the pulse variant is a proposal needing a stop and an inlet restriction (12 E11.5, Q16); when over-travel occurs during an inflation is itself 01 Q5.
- [14] The thermal purge has no inflation device stroke to pulse against.
- [15] Whether the convoluted wall's axial stiffness can supply an over-travel threshold in the 2.5 to 5.0 bar range is not known (12 E5.4, Q10). If confirmed, the two-rate characteristic is preferred, not essential (finding for Claims Architect, 03 A2).
- [16] Polymer stiffness is lower, creeps under load and varies with temperature, so a threshold set by the polymer wall would drift.
- [17] A thin spring wiper ring (§6 para 2) or a long thin stainless skirt (12 E7.5) is low in axial stiffness; unlikely to set the threshold alone.
- [18] The fingers are designed for low-force deflection (12 E7.3); using them as the second stage conflicts with that purpose. Low credibility.
- [19] A dished element has a disc-spring stiffness; this is the natural pairing for R9 (12 E7.6, E5.4). Rests on nothing yet (12 Q10, Q12).
- [20] One laser-cut alloy tube: seat ring or slotted fingers above, compliant flexure below (12 E4.3). Moderate credibility; fatigue untested.
- [21] A polymer flexure as the return spring creeps under sustained load, risking leak-down; low credibility.
- [22] The wiper ring is small and separate from the rigid valve seat; a flexure on it is possible but the geometry is undescribed (01 Q13).
- [23] A disc has no axial length for a flexure region, and the disc's inner edge is the valve seat, so it cannot also urge the valve member towards itself.
- [24] A flexure integral with the core body acting on the valve member is machinable in principle; not described.
- [25] A two-way shape-memory element changes shape with temperature and is martensitic at riding temperatures; it is not a usable return spring.
- [26] On a continuous ring, wedge spreading beyond what the convolution absorbs by bending strains the wall by hoop stretch (12 E6.2 estimate) and wedge friction adds to the over-travel threshold; for a polymer wall the wedge indents it.
- [27] The wiper ring is a lip; a wedge on a lip is the standard metal-lip geometry (12 E9.4).
- [28] Flattening a disc is by axial load on its inner edge; a wedge would stretch that edge in hoop rather than flatten the disc.
- [29] Alternative B already adds axial length (§6 para 2); a lower guide adds more, and it must let both the main flow passage and the purge flow exit.
- [30] §6 para 2 gives the wiper ring no geometry; a convoluted wall or an oblique lip (R19) are both possible (01 Q13).
- [31] The fingers deflect by cantilever bending, so a convolution adds nothing; a convoluted continuous region above the slots is a different element.
- [32] The dished element and the convoluted tubular wall are alternative means of the same axial-to-radial conversion (12 E7.6 genus); disclose as alternatives, never as one part.
- [33] A convolution is hard to form in a thin skirt machined integrally with the core body; a flare or an oblique lip is the machinable form (12 E9.2, E9.4).
- [34] The oblique lip is the natural wiper ring form (12 E9.4).
- [35] The slots and a laser-cut compliant pattern are both through-cuts; combine only as one cut pattern with a single rest-state closure (R3 or R4).
- [36] C3 is a polymer seat ring by definition; the hybrid with a metal wiping edge is R22.
- [37] §6 para 2 names a stainless spring wiper ring; a superelastic wiper ring is credible but not conceived (12 E13.6, Q18).
- [38] Laser-cut slotted superelastic-alloy tubes and shape-set superelastic-alloy discs are established practice at this scale (12 E7.3, E7.6).
- [39] C7 would need a core body of superelastic alloy; 12 E7.5 specifies stainless or titanium. Not credible at the cost of a valve core.
- [40] C8's ring has an austenite finish temperature of 35 to 40 °C by definition; one ring cannot also have one below 5 °C, and C8 is not a polymer ring.
- [41] Substitution: a stainless seat ring with a convoluted wall is a distinct embodiment; at one tenth of the recoverable strain the wall must be thinner and longer for the same purge gap width, and the recovery wipe is weaker (12 E13.6).
- [42] Stainless spring steel is the standard disc-spring material.
- [43] Not a shape-memory material; no thermal purge.
- [44] The hybrid replaces the all-alloy ring; it is C3 with R22, not an addition to C2.
- [45] A polymer wiper ring with a metal lip: the recovery force comes from the polymer, so the benefit of the insert is small.
- [46] Over-moulded metal finger tips or a metal inner edge on a polymer disc are makeable; not described anywhere.
- [47] The skirt is integral metal.
- [48] Prototype values; the §4 ranges are estimates and no range end was tested (01 A5; 13 A1).

- [50] Not applicable: C4 has no seat ring and the wiper ring's dimensions are not in the dossier; a disc has a thickness and two diameters, not a free length or convolution amplitude.
- [51] The seat ring inner diameter and wall thickness may carry; free length and convolution amplitude do not (C5 fingers; C7 longer skirt per 12 E7.5).
- [52] Polymer under interference creeps; retention by R27 is the safer pairing (12 E13.7).

- [54] A disc is normally clamped at its rim, not pressed axially into a bore; a light rim interference against a shoulder is plausible but undescribed.

**Exclusions between rows** (never to be written as one embodiment):

- R2, R3 and R4 are alternative purge gap topologies; a valve has one rest-state closure of the purge gap. The description presents them as alternatives (12 Q1 decides which is the prototype's).
- R18 and R19: each sibling wall form replaces the one-or-two convolution wall [S].
- R20, R21 and R22 are alternative materials of the deflectable element; C8's alloy is a fourth, exclusive of all three.
- R26 and R27 are alternative retentions [56]; R27's clamping at the core-to-stem interface (12 E14.4) also conflicts with R29 retrofit, since the valve stem then takes part in retention.
- R29 and R31 are exclusive: a valve core is removable or the mechanism is integral with the valve stem [63].
- R8 and R9 may coexist (the element's stiffness adding to a two-rate biasing member), but the over-travel threshold is then the sum and the §4 threshold values do not carry [P].

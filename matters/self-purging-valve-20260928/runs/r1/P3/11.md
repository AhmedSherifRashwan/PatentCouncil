Basis. The "proposed claim limitations" attacked are the nucleus N0, the alternative nucleus N0-W and the ladder rungs N1–N10 in `P1/03`, and the possible claim language D1–D11 in `P2/09`. The competitor is assumed to want the same commercial result: a valve core (or valve) that keeps sealing and opening through sealant-loaded inflations with no tool and no rider action (01 (c)).

Ranking. Rows are ordered by risk: the product of how cheaply the route can be built, how much of the market it keeps, and how far the dossier is from closing it today. Plausibility says whether the alternative works, not whether it is as good. "Supported" means the disclosure or an earlier output already describes the closing genus or embodiment; "needs inventor confirmation" means the closing description would be new matter unless the inventor confirms at Gate 2 that it is within the conception.

Findings on essential versus preferred, for the Claims Architect (each `candidate`):

- The routes that keep the effect and cost least (DA-2, DA-3, DA-4, DA-5, DA-10, DA-11, DA-19) all avoid features 03 already classifies as advantageous or conventional. None avoids the N0 clauses. That supports 03's placing of the nucleus at the mechanism level and confirms that the seat ring, the convoluted wall, the superelastic alloy, the two-rate characteristic and the purge shoulder are preferred, not essential.
- Three routes attack N0 clauses (DA-1, DA-7/DA-15, DA-8/DA-14), and each closing genus costs a novelty distinction recorded in 08 and 09: DA-1 costs D4–D6 and the wipe; DA-7 and DA-15 cost D2 against US11179979B2; DA-8 and DA-14 cost D2 and the "deflected by the valve member" clause against ref-02 and US3417778A. The recommendation is to close DA-1 only if 03 Q1 shows purge flow alone gives the effect, and to leave DA-8 and DA-14 open. A competitor on those routes ships a worse valve that sits closer to the art.
- The single answer that changes this register most is 01 Q1 (the purge-gap geometry). Until it is known which portion of the seat ring is held and which is free, DA-6, DA-9, DA-13 and DA-19 cannot be closed with confidence, because the closing genus must describe the rest-state closure and the disclosure does not yet do so.

## Rationale

The competitor's cheapest escapes are not clever: they copy the mechanism and drop the expensive part. So the register leads with alternative B and the material and biasing-member substitutions, which the inventor has half-conceived and the dossier can close with genus language and one or two confirmed embodiments. The rigid-port route (DA-1) is ranked first because it is the only cheap escape that attacks an N0 clause and because the bench data cannot yet say whether the wipe it gives up matters.

Routes that attack the pressure threshold (DA-7, DA-8, DA-14, DA-15) are ranked in the middle although they are technically easy, because closing them means giving up D2, the second leg of the novelty case in 08. I have said so in each row rather than offering a genus that would read on the art. Attacking the wording of D1 (DA-9, DA-13) is ranked below the structural routes because those escapes are met by functional definitions that the disclosure already supports in principle.

Every closing embodiment that is not in §2–§7 is marked as needing inventor confirmation, including the ones that seem obvious (spring steel, crimped retention). The inventor's playbook rule is that nothing is added that they have not conceived, and Gate 2 is where they say so.

No personal data unrelated to the invention was found in the inputs.

## Assumptions

- A1 The proposed claim limitations are 03's N0, N0-W and N1–N10 and 09's D1–D11; no claims exist yet. Not blocking.
- A2 The purge gap lies between the seat ring's outer surface and the core bore (§3 item 6; 01 A1), and its rest-state closure mechanism is unexplained (01 Q1). Blocking for P4 and P5 on DA-6, DA-9, DA-13 and DA-19.
- A3 Both cleaning actions contribute to the effect (03 A1); if purge flow alone suffices, DA-1 and DA-16 rise to the top of the register. Blocking for P3 (Gate 2 decision on DA-1) and P5.
- A4 Plausibility ratings are engineering judgement from the disclosure and the charted references; no route has been built or modelled. Not blocking.
- A5 The competitor's market is the same as the inventor's: replacement valve cores first (§1), OEM valves second (02 ranks 2–3). Not blocking.
- A6 `intake/answers.md` does not exist, so no inventor answer has confirmed or struck any alternative. Not blocking for P3 to proceed; every row stays candidate.

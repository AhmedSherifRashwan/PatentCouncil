### Definitions

*Drafting note, not specification text: these definitions are a drafting and analysis aid prepared by AI from `CONFIRMED.md`, the Phase 1 outputs, the differentiation matrix and the locked terminology. They are not legal advice or a filing decision; qualified patent counsel signs off before anything is filed. Each definition carries a stable id (D1 to D41) for citation by the compose step, the Claims Architect and the reviewers; the compose step assigns `[0001]`-style paragraph numbers. The definitions are given in order of dependence, so that no definition relies on a term defined after it. Only constructions whose `CONFIRMED.md` entry rests on the inventors' disclosure are relied on; no AI-proposed alternative appears in any definition.*

**D1 — pressure differential.** As used herein, "pressure differential" means the difference between the gas pressure acting on the valve member from the side of the valve to which the inflation device is applied and the gas pressure acting on the valve member from the side of the inflatable body, the difference being positive when the pressure on the inflation-device side is the higher. Every value of pressure stated herein for the opening pressure and for the over-travel threshold is a value of the pressure differential so defined, and is not an absolute pressure and not the pressure in the inflatable body. The pressure differential falls when the inflation device ceases to apply pressure to the valve or is removed from it.

**D2 — opening pressure.** As used herein, "opening pressure" means the pressure differential at which the valve member leaves the valve seat, so that inflation gas begins to pass the valve seat along the main flow passage. In the embodiments described the opening pressure is set by the biasing member. In the prototype the opening pressure is 1.0 bar; in some embodiments it is from 0.8 bar to 1.5 bar.

**D3 — normal stroke.** As used herein, "normal stroke" means the travel of the valve member away from the valve seat under a pressure differential that exceeds the opening pressure but does not exceed the over-travel threshold. Throughout the normal stroke the deflectable element is not deflected by the valve member. In the embodiments described, the normal stroke ends at the position of the valve member at which the purge shoulder first bears on the deflectable element. In the prototype the normal stroke is 0.6 mm. A valve whose valve member has made the normal stroke and no more is, during that inflation, operating as a conventional valve.

**D4 — over-travel threshold.** As used herein, "over-travel threshold" means the pressure differential, higher than the opening pressure, which must be exceeded for the valve member to travel beyond the end of the normal stroke. The over-travel threshold is a property of the valve as a whole, whatever provides it; in the prototype it is provided by the second stage of the two-rate characteristic of the biasing member. In the prototype the over-travel threshold is 3.5 bar; in some embodiments it is from 2.5 bar to 5.0 bar. Where an inflation device applies a pressure differential that does not exceed the over-travel threshold, the valve member makes the normal stroke only, no over-travel occurs, and the valve operates as a conventional valve.

**D5 — over-travel.** As used herein, "over-travel" means the travel of the valve member, in the direction of the normal stroke and beyond the end of the normal stroke, that occurs when the pressure differential exceeds the over-travel threshold. Over-travel is travel of the same valve member that makes the normal stroke, and it is produced by the pressure differential acting on the valve member against the biasing member. During over-travel the valve member bears on the deflectable element and deflects it. In the prototype the over-travel is 0.5 mm beyond the end of the normal stroke; in some embodiments it is from 0.4 mm to 0.9 mm. The term denotes controlled travel of the valve member against the biasing member and does not denote any instability of the valve member.

**D6 — rest state.** As used herein, "rest state" means the condition of the valve in which the pressure differential does not exceed the opening pressure, including the condition in which no inflation device is applied to the valve and the inflatable body holds its pressure. In the rest state the valve member is seated on the valve seat, the deflectable element is undeflected, and the purge gap is closed. The rest state is the condition of the valve between inflations.

**D7 — main flow passage.** As used herein, "main flow passage" means the path along which inflation gas passes through the valve from the inflation-device side to the inflatable body between the sealing face of the valve member and the valve seat when the valve member is lifted from the valve seat. Inflation gas passes along the main flow passage during the normal stroke and during over-travel. While the purge gap is closed, the main flow passage is the only path for inflation gas through the valve.

**D8 — purge gap.** As used herein, "purge gap" means a flow path through the valve for inflation gas which is separate from the main flow passage; which is closed in the rest state and throughout the normal stroke, so that no purge flow passes through it; which is opened by deflection of the deflectable element during over-travel; and which is closed again by recovery of the deflectable element when the pressure differential falls. The purge gap is bounded, at least in part, by a surface of the deflectable element, and the surfaces bounding the purge gap are sealant-accumulation surfaces. "Separate from the main flow passage" means that gas passing through the purge gap does not pass between the sealing face and the valve seat: the purge gap is a second path through the valve, in parallel with the main flow passage and not in series with it, and it may communicate at its ends with the same spaces on the inflation-device side and on the inflatable-body side as the main flow passage. "Closed", in relation to the purge gap, means that the purge gap does not admit purge flow. The term is defined by these functions and not by the position of the purge gap within the valve, by the parts between which it lies, or by the direction in which the deflectable element deflects. In the prototype the purge gap is annular.

**D9 — purge flow.** As used herein, "purge flow" means inflation gas, supplied by the inflation device during the inflation in which the over-travel occurs, that passes through the purge gap while the purge gap is open, along the sealant-accumulation surfaces bounding the purge gap, and into the inflatable body. Purge flow passes through the purge gap in addition to, and not through, the main flow passage. Purge flow is gas from the inflation device; it is not gas from a store within or separate from the valve. The term refers to the flow of gas itself and does not, of itself, state any result of that flow.

**D10 — deflectable element.** As used herein, "deflectable element" means an element of the valve that has an undeflected shape in which the purge gap is closed, that is deflected from the undeflected shape so that the purge gap opens, and that recovers towards the undeflected shape, so that the purge gap closes, when the load or condition that deflected it is removed. In the valves described herein other than the thermal purge variant, the deflectable element is deflected by the valve member during over-travel, is not deflected during the normal stroke, and its recovery is the elastic recovery of the element itself, which occurs when the pressure differential falls. The deflectable element may provide the valve seat, in which case it is a seat ring, or it may be separate from a rigid valve seat, in which case it is a wiper ring. In the embodiments described, the deflection of the deflectable element includes outward radial deflection. The term does not itself specify a material; in the embodiments described the deflectable element is of a superelastic alloy, of a polymer, or of a resilient metal.

**D11 — valve seat.** As used herein, "valve seat" means the surface of the valve on which the sealing face of the valve member seals when the valve member is seated, as it is in the rest state. The valve seat may be provided by the deflectable element: in the first embodiment the valve seat is the upper edge of the seat ring. Alternatively the valve seat may be a rigid surface that is not deflected during over-travel, referred to herein as a "rigid valve seat": in the embodiment having a wiper ring, the rigid valve seat is of brass. The valve seat and the deflectable element are distinct concepts even where a single part provides both.

**D12 — sealing face.** As used herein, "sealing face" means the surface of the valve member that seals against the valve seat when the valve member is seated, and across which the deflectable element slides in the recovery wipe. In the prototype the sealing face is conical and its line of contact with the upper edge of the seat ring is where sealing occurs. The term does not require a conical form.

**D13 — seat ring.** As used herein, "seat ring" means a one-piece deflectable element of generally tubular form whose upper edge provides the valve seat and which is received in the core bore of the core body. In the embodiments described the seat ring has the convoluted wall; in the first embodiment it is of a superelastic alloy, and in the polymer seat ring it is of a polymer. The term "seat ring" does not itself require any particular material or any particular form of wall.

**D14 — polymer seat ring.** As used herein, "polymer seat ring" means a seat ring made of a polymer. In the embodiment described the polymer is polyether ether ketone (PEEK) and the polymer seat ring has the same convoluted wall as the seat ring of superelastic alloy.

**D15 — wiper ring.** As used herein, "wiper ring" means a deflectable element that does not provide the valve seat and is separate from a rigid valve seat, that is deflected by the purge shoulder during over-travel, and that on recovery slides across the sealing face of the valve member. In the embodiment described the wiper ring is a thin ring of stainless spring steel, positioned on the inflatable-body side of the rigid valve seat, and is deflected outward by the purge shoulder.

**D16 — purge shoulder.** As used herein, "purge shoulder" means a portion of the valve member that is clear of the deflectable element throughout the normal stroke and bears on the deflectable element only during over-travel, so that the over-travel of the valve member is applied to the deflectable element as the load that deflects it. The term is defined by this function and not by the position or the form of the portion. In the prototype the purge shoulder is an annular step of the valve member below the sealing face.

**D17 — recovery wipe.** As used herein, "recovery wipe" means the sliding of the deflectable element across the sealing face of the valve member that occurs as the deflectable element recovers when the pressure differential falls and the valve member returns towards the valve seat. The sliding is relative motion, in contact, between the deflectable element and the sealing face. The term does not specify the direction of the sliding relative to the axis of the valve, and it does not specify which of the deflectable element and the valve member is moving relative to the core body during the sliding; in the embodiments described both are in motion as the pressure differential falls. The term denotes the sliding contact itself and not any result attributed to it.

**D18 — sealant.** As used herein, "sealant" means a liquid puncture sealant carried within the inflatable body, a quantity of which is drawn into the valve in use and dries or cures there. The sealant used in the Examples is a latex emulsion containing ammonia and suspended particles. The term is not limited to any particular sealant chemistry; the Examples report tests with one sealant, as there stated.

**D19 — sealant film.** As used herein, "sealant film" means a layer of sealant left on a surface of the valve after sealant drawn into the valve has dried or cured, wholly or in part. Sealant film that has dried or cured in part is referred to as partially cured sealant film.

**D20 — sealant-accumulation surfaces.** As used herein, "sealant-accumulation surfaces" means the internal surfaces of the valve that are exposed to sealant drawn into the valve in use and on which sealant film forms between inflations, namely the valve seat, the sealing face of the valve member, and the surfaces bounding the purge gap, including the outer surface of the deflectable element where it bounds the purge gap. The term identifies these surfaces by their position and exposure within the valve; it does not depend on whether sealant film is present on them at any particular time.

**D21 — inflation cycle.** As used herein, "inflation cycle" means one inflation of the inflatable body through the valve by an inflation device and the subsequent deflation of the inflatable body through the valve. In the Examples an inflation cycle is inflation to 6 bar with a floor pump, a hold of 24 hours at 22 °C, and full deflation through the valve.

**D22 — leak-down.** As used herein, "leak-down" means loss of pressure from the inflatable body through the valve while the valve is in the rest state. In the Examples the failure criterion for leak-down is a loss of more than 0.5 bar in 24 hours.

**D23 — blockage.** As used herein, "blockage" means the failure of the valve member to leave the valve seat when the inflation device is applied to the valve. In the Examples the failure criterion for blockage is that the inflation device is unable to open the valve within 5 seconds of engagement.

**D24 — biasing member.** As used herein, "biasing member" means a member that urges the valve member towards the valve seat and that resists the normal stroke and the over-travel of the valve member. In the prototype the biasing member is a compression spring of stainless music wire acting under the valve member; the material of the biasing member is not essential.

**D25 — two-rate characteristic.** As used herein, "two-rate characteristic" means a force behaviour of the biasing member having a soft first stage, which the pressure differential overcomes during the normal stroke and which sets the opening pressure, and a stiffer second stage, which the pressure differential must overcome for over-travel and which sets the over-travel threshold. The term describes the force behaviour of the biasing member and not any particular construction by which that behaviour is obtained. The valves described herein require an over-travel threshold higher than the opening pressure; in the prototype that over-travel threshold is provided by the two-rate characteristic of the biasing member.

**D26 — convoluted wall.** As used herein, "convoluted wall" means the wall of the seat ring where that wall, seen in axial section, is not straight but has at least one convolution, a convolution being a wave in which the wall departs radially from, and returns to, the line of the adjacent plain portions of the wall, the convoluted wall being shaped so that an axial load applied to the upper edge of the seat ring is converted into radial expansion of the seat ring. In the embodiments described the convoluted wall has one or two convolutions. The term does not specify whether a convolution projects radially outward or radially inward.

**D27 — convolution amplitude.** As used herein, "convolution amplitude" means the radial depth of a convolution of the convoluted wall, that is the greatest radial distance, measured in axial section, by which the wall of the convolution departs from the line of the adjacent plain portions of the wall of the seat ring. In the prototype the convolution amplitude is 0.12 mm; in some embodiments it is from 0.08 mm to 0.20 mm.

**D28 — purge gap width.** As used herein, "purge gap width" means the width of the purge gap, measured perpendicular to the surfaces that bound it, when the purge gap is fully open, that is when the valve member is at the end of its over-travel. In the rest state the purge gap width is zero. In the prototype the purge gap width is measured radially and is 0.15 mm; in some embodiments it is from 0.10 mm to 0.25 mm.

**D29 — core body.** As used herein, "core body" means the part of the valve core that carries the deflectable element, guides the valve member, seats the biasing member and is received in the valve stem. In the prototype the core body is turned from free-machining brass CW614N; in another embodiment it is of stainless steel 303, for corrosion resistance with ammonia-bearing sealant.

**D30 — core bore.** As used herein, "core bore" means the bore of the core body in which the deflectable element is received. In the first embodiment the seat ring is received in the core bore, is retained in it by an interference fit of approximately 0.03 mm on diameter, and is located against the locating shoulder, which is an internal step of the core body.

**D31 — valve member.** As used herein, "valve member" means the part of the valve that carries the sealing face and moves relative to the valve seat so as to seal against the valve seat in the rest state, to make the normal stroke and to make the over-travel. In the embodiments described the valve member also carries the purge shoulder, is guided by the core body, and is urged towards the valve seat by the biasing member. In the prototype the valve member is of brass; a hard-anodised aluminium sealing face is a described alternative. The term is not limited to any particular form of the part.

**D32 — valve core.** As used herein, "valve core" means the assembly comprising the core body, the valve member, the biasing member and the deflectable element, together with the rigid valve seat where one is present, which is receivable as a unit in a valve stem. In the embodiments described the valve core is threaded into the valve stem and is removable from it, so that the valve core may be fitted to, or replaced in, a valve stem as a unit. The term does not itself import any thread form or valve standard.

**D33 — valve stem.** As used herein, "valve stem" means the tubular part fixed to the inflatable body which has a bore that receives the valve core. In the prototype the valve stem is a brass valve stem of Presta pattern and 6 mm outer diameter with a threaded bore.

**D34 — valve.** As used herein, "valve" means the assembly through which the inflatable body is inflated and which retains the inflation gas, comprising at least the valve seat, the valve member, the biasing member and the deflectable element. In the embodiments described the valve comprises the valve stem and, received in it, the valve core, and it further comprises the lock nut by which the valve member is closed manually for storage and transport.

**D35 — superelastic alloy.** As used herein, "superelastic alloy" means a metallic alloy which, at the temperature of use, is in its austenitic state and recovers strain imposed on it, including strain accommodated by a stress-induced transformation, on removal of the load, so that it recovers large strain elastically at the temperature of use. In the first embodiment the superelastic alloy is a nickel–titanium (NiTi) alloy of nominally 55.8 wt% nickel. The abbreviation "NiTi" denotes that alloy.

**D36 — austenite finish temperature.** As used herein, "austenite finish temperature", abbreviated "Af", means the temperature above which the superelastic alloy is fully austenitic on heating, and hence the temperature above which the deflectable element of superelastic alloy exhibits superelastic recovery. Where a value of austenite finish temperature is stated herein, it is the value on heating determined by a thermal analysis method, for example differential scanning calorimetry, unless stated otherwise. In the first embodiment the austenite finish temperature is specified to be below 5 °C, so that the seat ring is superelastic at the temperatures at which the inflatable body is used.

**D37 — martensite start temperature.** As used herein, "martensite start temperature", abbreviated "Ms", means the temperature below which, on cooling, the superelastic alloy begins to transform to martensite and thereby begins to lose superelastic recovery until it is warmed again. A value of martensite start temperature for the prototype is not stated herein.

**D38 — shape-setting.** As used herein, "shape-setting" means a heat treatment of the seat ring of superelastic alloy, carried out in a fixture that holds the convoluted wall in its intended geometry, which fixes that geometry as the undeflected shape of the seat ring. In the first embodiment the shape-setting is carried out at 500 °C for 10 minutes and is followed by a water quench.

**D39 — thermal purge variant.** As used herein, "thermal purge variant" means the distinct variant in which the seat ring is of a two-way shape-memory alloy having an austenite finish temperature of about 35 °C to 40 °C, so that warming the valve, for example by hand, deflects the seat ring and opens the purge gap without an inflation device. In the thermal purge variant the purge gap is opened by a change of temperature rather than by over-travel. The term is used only for that variant.

**D40 — inflation device.** As used herein, "inflation device" means a device that is applied to the valve and supplies inflation gas at a pressure differential across the valve, including a floor pump, the head of a pump, and a cartridge inflator supplying carbon dioxide. The term is not limited to any particular device. Whether over-travel occurs during an inflation depends on whether the pressure differential applied by the inflation device exceeds the over-travel threshold; an inflation device that does not apply such a pressure differential inflates the inflatable body through the main flow passage with the valve operating as a conventional valve, and no purge flow occurs.

**D41 — inflatable body.** As used herein, "inflatable body" means a body that is inflated through the valve, retains the inflation gas, and contains the sealant, including a bicycle inner tube and a tubeless tyre set-up. The inflatable body of the Examples is a 700×28 bicycle inner tube.

## Rationale

The definitions are ordered by dependence so that none is circular: the pressure differential first, then the four travel and pressure terms defined against one another and against the position at which the purge shoulder first bears on the deflectable element (D3), then the rest state, the two flow paths, the deflectable element and its species, and only then the component and material terms. This is the order S8 of the blueprint fixed, and it is the order that lets Claims Architect cite one definition per limitation.

Three findings shaped the core definitions. PF-2 required "normal stroke", "over-travel", "opening pressure" and "over-travel threshold" to be defined together and non-circularly, with an express statement that the deflectable element is not deflected during the normal stroke; D3 to D5 do that, and D5 states that over-travel is travel of the same valve member and is produced by the pressure differential, which is the clause that separates the concept from a second rigid valve member and from a lip deflected directly by gas pressure. I anchored the end of the normal stroke to the first contact of the purge shoulder with the deflectable element rather than to the change of spring rate, because §3 item 4 states the contact rule directly, whereas how the second spring stage is realised is not disclosed. PF-1 required "purge gap" and "rest state" to be functional; D8 gives no location, no bore and no direction of deflection, and says so, so that neither the core-bore nor the stem-bore wording of the disclosure is adopted. PF-5 required the description to say that purge flow passes in addition to, and not through, the main flow passage; D8 defines "separate" as parallel-not-series, which is the fact that distinguishes an in-series slit, and D9 repeats the point for the flow. "Closed" is defined as "does not admit purge flow" rather than "gas-tight", because gas-tightness of the purge gap in the rest state is an open question and the leak-down data measure the whole valve.

"Deflectable element" (D10) is defined by shape, deflection and recovery, and is material-neutral. I did not exclude elastomers from the definition. The inventors' premise is that a soft seat lets film embed, and the library therefore proposed no elastomer element, but a definition that excluded elastomers would hand a competitor a design-around for the price of a stiff polymer that is arguably an elastomer, and the inventors' own polymer seat ring shows that the concept is not tied to the alloy. The premise belongs in the description of the advantages of the superelastic alloy, not in the definition. The one structural qualifier I did keep is that the recovery is the elastic recovery of the element itself, which is what the disclosure says and what distinguishes the concept from a seat that is merely pushed back by the valve member. The thermal purge variant is carved out of the over-travel clause in D10 rather than excluded from the term, because the blueprint records the variant in the description and PF-20 says record it, do not claim it.

"Purge shoulder" (D16) and "recovery wipe" (D17) follow PF-15 and PF-16: function without position for the shoulder, sliding contact without direction for the wipe. D17 also says that both parts are in motion as the pressure differential falls. That is not the AI-proposed combined-motion reading; §2 para 2 itself says the valve member returns and the seat ring recovers, and a definition that left the valve member stationary would invite the escape 09 D6 identifies. "Two-rate characteristic" (D25) is written exactly as PF-9 directs: the concept requires an over-travel threshold above the opening pressure, the prototype provides it by the two-rate characteristic, and nothing is said about other means because none is disclosed. Because D4 defines the over-travel threshold as a property of the valve whatever provides it, a claim can recite the threshold without reciting the biasing member's characteristic.

Breadth I declined to write, because the confirmed material does not support it: a purge gap against the valve stem bore or a core body formed by the valve stem (the §2 wording, resolved only if the inventor confirms it as a second embodiment); over-travel produced by engagement of the inflation device; a valve member assembled from a head and a stem; three or more convolutions; the intermediate and preferred rungs of the parameter ladders; hand pumps, compressors and inflatable bodies other than the bicycle inner tube and tubeless set-up. D40 and D41 are genus statements that name only what the disclosure names. Where the lock's own row was narrower than the concept, I widened the definition rather than the embodiment: "seat ring" (D13) does not require the convoluted wall or a material, because the convoluted wall is a separate rung of the ladder and the polymer seat ring exists; "purge gap width" (D28) is measured perpendicular to the bounding surfaces, with the radial measurement recorded as the prototype's, because the topology is unresolved. Where a broad definition would have read on something the dossier excludes, I narrowed it: "over-travel" (D5) excludes instability, and "convoluted wall" (D26) is defined by a wave that departs from and returns to the plain wall, so that a wall relying on buckling is not a convoluted wall.

Two conventions had to be chosen where the dossier is silent, and each is flagged as an assumption rather than silently adopted: the convolution amplitude is measured as the greatest radial departure from the plain wall (D27), and the austenite finish temperature is the value on heating by a thermal analysis method (D36). Neither is a fact about the invention; each is the measuring convention without which the numerical rungs would draw a clarity objection at the EPO and an indefiniteness objection in the US. The inventor should confirm both.

The general-construction paragraphs are ordinary conventions with three deliberate points. G4 extends "bears on" to load transmitted through an intervening part, and records that in the embodiments described the contact is direct, so that a competitor's washer between shoulder and ring does not escape the term; counsel may prefer to strike the extension. G6 discloses each endpoint individually and each prototype value as a usable endpoint; the EPO does not accept a general statement that every intermediate value is disclosed as basis for an arbitrary sub-range, so I did not write one, and the individually named endpoints and prototype values are the reliable amendment basis in all three jurisdictions. G9 states that the functional terms denote structural parts and are not means-plus-function expressions; that matters in the US, where "member" and "element" with functional language can invoke 35 U.S.C. §112(f) and confine the claim to the described structures, and it costs nothing at the EPO or in the UK, where a functional feature covers every means that performs the function provided the description enables it.

The avoid-list words of the lock do not appear in the Deliverable; the inventor's own word for the valve member and the words for the purge gap that suggest pressure relief are the ones most likely to creep into definitions, and each locked preferred term is used verbatim. The only new term is "plain portion", introduced in D26 and D27 for the unconvoluted length of the seat ring wall, which no locked term names.

## Deliverable

### Description

### TITLE

Valve with a deflectable element deflected by over-travel of the valve member to open a purge gap, valve core, inflatable body and purging method

### TECHNICAL FIELD

[0001] The present disclosure relates to valves for inflatable bodies, and in particular to valves, and to removable valve cores for such valves, through which an inflatable body containing sealant is inflated by an inflation device. It relates also to the valve core as an article, to an inflatable body fitted with such a valve, and to a method of purging such a valve during inflation.

### BACKGROUND

[0002] Bicycle inner tubes and tubeless tyre set-ups are increasingly run with a liquid sealant, typically a latex emulsion containing ammonia and suspended particles, which is injected into the inflatable body through the valve stem. Each time the inflatable body is inflated, a small quantity of sealant is drawn up into the bore of the valve core by the pressure surge and by capillary action along the valve member. Between rides the sealant film so formed dries and cures.

[0003] After about 10 to 20 inflation cycles the valve seat of a conventional valve core is coated with cured sealant, and the valve then either leaks slowly because the valve member no longer seats (leak-down), or blocks entirely because the inflation device cannot open it (blockage). The remedy is then to remove the valve core with a tool, clean or replace it, and re-inject sealant.

[0004] US11338632B2 states, at paragraph [0004], that valves of small bore are frequently obstructed by sealant, and answers that problem by the geometry of the valve and the size of its bore. US11179979B2 describes, at paragraphs [0016] and [0101], a sealing lip at the tyre-side end of a valve which is displaced or deformed so as to open a slit under pressure when a tyre is inflated, the sealing lip being arranged in series with a removable valve core.

[0005] US3417778A describes, in claim 1, a check valve having a tubular valve seat of resilient material which is radially expandable under fluid pressure. US20080188352A1 describes, at paragraph [26], a two-stage check valve in which a first valve means opens at a first pressure and a second valve means opens a second orifice at a higher second pressure.

[0006] US3570510A describes, at paragraphs [22] and [26], a valve in which gas from a separate store is blown through an annular slit between seat rings. US4050473A and US4236547A describe, at paragraph [13] and at paragraph [6] respectively, a flexible disc, or a cutting edge, carried on the head of a valve and acting on the valve seat as the valve closes. US6053992A describes, at paragraph [10], sealing plugs of a shape-memory alloy that seal statically.

### SUMMARY

[0007] The present disclosure addresses the sealant film that forms on the valve seat and on the sealing face of the valve member and cures between inflation cycles. Once such a film has formed it is not removed by an ordinary inflation; the valve then fails by leak-down or by blockage, and removing the film requires a tool and an action by the rider. The valves described herein remove sealant film that has already formed, at every inflation in which the inflation device applies a pressure differential above a threshold, by the action of one moving part, with no tool, no action by the rider, no stored gas and no valve additional to the valve member.

[0008] In a first aspect there is provided a valve for an inflatable body, the valve comprising a valve seat; a valve member having a sealing face which seals on the valve seat in a rest state; a biasing member which urges the valve member towards the valve seat; and a deflectable element. The valve member is movable away from the valve seat through a normal stroke when a pressure differential across the valve exceeds an opening pressure, and beyond the normal stroke through an over-travel when the pressure differential exceeds an over-travel threshold which is higher than the opening pressure. The over-travel of the valve member deflects the deflectable element. The deflection of the deflectable element opens a purge gap which is separate from the main flow passage past the valve seat and through which purge flow is prevented in the rest state, so that purge flow of inflation gas passes through the purge gap, along sealant-accumulation surfaces of the valve, and into the inflatable body. When the pressure differential falls, the deflectable element recovers and the purge gap closes. It is the same valve member that makes the normal stroke and the over-travel, and it is the over-travel of that valve member, produced by the pressure differential acting on it against the biasing member, that deflects the deflectable element.

[0009] The valve so defined has three ranges of pressure differential. Below the opening pressure the valve is in the rest state: the valve member is seated on the valve seat, the deflectable element is undeflected and the purge gap is closed. Between the opening pressure and the over-travel threshold the valve member makes the normal stroke, inflation gas passes the valve seat along the main flow passage, and the deflectable element is not deflected; in this range the valve operates as a conventional valve. Above the over-travel threshold the valve member makes over-travel, the deflectable element is deflected, and the purge gap opens.

[0010] Two cleaning actions follow from one deflection of the deflectable element, and each is stated here as the inventors state it. First, while the deflectable element is deflected, purge flow of the inflation gas itself, admitted through the purge gap that exists only while the deflectable element is deflected, passes along the surfaces on which sealant film forms and carries sealant film into the inflatable body. Second, when the pressure differential falls and the deflectable element recovers, the deflectable element slides across the sealing face of the valve member, which is referred to herein as the recovery wipe; according to the inventors, the recovery wipe shears partially cured sealant film from the sealing face. Neither action is stated herein to be sufficient alone; the Examples report the behaviour of a valve in which both occur.

[0011] In a further aspect there is provided a valve for an inflatable body comprising a valve seat, a valve member having a sealing face, a biasing member and a deflectable element as in the first aspect, in which the over-travel of the valve member deflects the deflectable element and in which, when the pressure differential falls and the deflectable element recovers, the deflectable element slides across the sealing face of the valve member. This aspect is founded on the recovery wipe; it may be combined with the purge gap and purge flow of the first aspect, and every feature described herein for the first aspect applies to it.

[0012] In a further aspect there is provided a valve core for a valve for an inflatable body, the valve core comprising a core body, and, carried by the core body, the valve member, the biasing member and the deflectable element of the first aspect or of the further aspect, the valve core being receivable as a unit in a valve stem. In some embodiments the valve core is threaded into the valve stem and is removable from it, so that the valve core may be fitted to, or replaced in, a valve stem as a unit.

[0013] In a further aspect there is provided a valve comprising a valve stem and, received in the valve stem, the valve core of the preceding aspect.

[0014] In a further aspect there is provided an inflatable body, for example a bicycle inner tube or a tubeless tyre set-up, containing sealant and fitted with a valve as described herein, the inflatable body being inflatable through the valve.

[0015] In a further aspect there is provided a method of purging a valve for an inflatable body during inflation, the valve having a valve seat, a valve member with a sealing face, a biasing member and a deflectable element, the method comprising: applying, by an inflation device, a pressure differential across the valve which exceeds the opening pressure, so that the valve member makes the normal stroke away from the valve seat and inflation gas passes the valve seat along the main flow passage; the pressure differential exceeding the over-travel threshold, which is higher than the opening pressure, so that the valve member makes over-travel beyond the normal stroke; the over-travel of the valve member deflecting the deflectable element, so that a purge gap separate from the main flow passage opens; passing purge flow of the inflation gas through the purge gap, along sealant-accumulation surfaces of the valve, and into the inflatable body; and removing the inflation device, so that the pressure differential falls, the deflectable element recovers and the purge gap closes. In some embodiments the method further comprises, as the deflectable element recovers, the deflectable element sliding across the sealing face of the valve member.

[0016] The following optional features apply to every aspect. The deflectable element, as it recovers when the pressure differential falls, may slide across the sealing face of the valve member. The over-travel threshold may be provided by a biasing member having a two-rate characteristic, a first stage of which sets the opening pressure and a stiffer second stage of which sets the over-travel threshold; this is the means by which the over-travel threshold is provided in the prototype, and the concept requires an over-travel threshold higher than the opening pressure however it is provided. The valve member may have a purge shoulder which is clear of the deflectable element throughout the normal stroke and bears on the deflectable element only during over-travel. The deflectable element may be a seat ring whose upper edge provides the valve seat, the seat ring being received in a core bore of the core body and located against a locating shoulder; or the valve seat may be a rigid valve seat and the deflectable element a wiper ring separate from the rigid valve seat. The seat ring may have a convoluted wall which converts an axial load applied to its upper edge into radial expansion of the seat ring. The seat ring may be of a superelastic alloy having an austenite finish temperature below 5 °C, or may be a polymer seat ring. The valve core may be replaceable as a unit. The dimensions and pressures given in the section on ranges apply in some embodiments. The seat ring of superelastic alloy may be made by the route described in the section on manufacture, and the parts may be of the materials there described.

[0017] The valves described herein purge themselves of sealant film at every inflation in which the over-travel threshold is exceeded, with no tool and no action by the rider. Below the over-travel threshold the valve behaves as a conventional valve. The purge flow is the inflation gas itself, so the valve needs no stored gas and no valve additional to the valve member, and the whole mechanism is contained in the valve core. In the bench test reported in the Examples, three valve cores according to the first embodiment completed forty sealant-loaded inflation cycles without leak-down or blockage, whereas three conventional valve cores failed at cycle counts of 12, 14 and 18; the test stopped at forty cycles with no failure of any valve core according to the first embodiment. No statement is made herein that every inflation device reaches the over-travel threshold, and no service life beyond forty inflation cycles is stated.

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION

#### General arrangement

[0027] Referring to FIG. 1, a valve 92 for an inflatable body comprises a valve stem 10 and a valve core 90 received in the valve stem 10. The valve stem 10 is a tubular part fixed to the inflatable body and has a threaded bore 12 which receives the valve core 90. In the prototype the valve stem 10 is a conventional brass valve stem of Presta pattern and 6 mm outer diameter. The valve core 90 comprises a core body 20, a valve member 40, a biasing member 50 and a deflectable element, which in the first embodiment is a seat ring 30. A lock nut 70 carried on the exposed upper end of the valve member 40 closes the valve member 40 manually for storage and transport. In some embodiments the valve core 90 is removable from the valve stem 10 as a unit, the core body 20 being threaded into the threaded bore 12 by an external thread 26 of the core body 20.

[0028] The core body 20 is turned from free-machining brass CW614N in the prototype; in another embodiment it is of 303 stainless steel, for corrosion resistance with ammonia-bearing sealant. The core body 20 has a core bore 22 in which the seat ring 30 is received, a locating shoulder 24, which is an internal step of the core body 20 against which the seat ring 30 is seated, and a guide portion 28 by which the valve member 40 is guided. The guide portion 28 is shown schematically; the core body 20 guides the valve member 40, and the position of the guide portion 28 along the core body 20 is not otherwise limited.

[0029] The valve member 40 carries a sealing face 42 and a purge shoulder 44. In the prototype the valve member 40 is a one-piece turned brass part, the sealing face 42 is conical, and the purge shoulder 44 is an annular step of the valve member 40 below the sealing face 42. Hard-anodised aluminium has been considered for the sealing face 42. The sealing face 42 seals against the valve seat when the valve member 40 is seated. The purge shoulder 44 is clear of the deflectable element throughout the normal stroke and bears on the deflectable element only during over-travel; it is that function which defines the purge shoulder 44, its position below the sealing face 42 being the position in the prototype.

[0030] In the first embodiment a single part, the seat ring 30, both provides the valve seat and is the deflectable element. The seat ring 30 is a short tube whose upper edge 32 provides the valve seat on which the sealing face 42 seals. The wall of the seat ring 30 is a convoluted wall 34: seen in axial section it is not straight but has one or two shallow convolutions 36, forming a wave, so that an axial load applied to the upper edge 32 is converted into radial expansion of the seat ring 30. The seat ring 30 is received in the core bore 22 and is located against the locating shoulder 24. The valve seat and the deflectable element are distinct concepts even where, as in the first embodiment, a single part provides both: in the third embodiment, described below with reference to FIG. 3, the valve seat is a rigid valve seat 132 and the deflectable element is a separate wiper ring 130.

[0031] The valve 92 has a purge gap 60, which is a flow path for inflation gas separate from the main flow passage 62. The purge gap 60 is closed in the rest state and throughout the normal stroke, so that no purge flow passes through it; it is opened by deflection of the deflectable element during over-travel; and it is closed again by recovery of the deflectable element when the pressure differential falls. In the first embodiment the purge gap 60 is annular, is formed between the outer surface 38 of the seat ring 30 and the core bore 22 of the core body 20, and opens when the seat ring 30 expands. The purge gap 60 is defined by these functions and not by its position within the valve 92.

[0032] When the valve member 40 is lifted from the valve seat, inflation gas 66 passes through the valve 92 from the side of the inflation device 84 to the inflatable body 80 between the sealing face 42 and the valve seat, along the main flow passage 62. Inflation gas 66 passes along the main flow passage 62 during the normal stroke and during over-travel. During over-travel, purge flow 64 passes in addition through the purge gap 60, along the sealant-accumulation surfaces which bound it, and into the inflatable body 80, as shown by the arrows in FIG. 2. Purge flow 64 passes through the purge gap 60 in addition to, and not through, the main flow passage 62 past the valve seat: the purge gap 60 is a second path through the valve 92, in parallel with the main flow passage 62 and not in series with it. The purge flow 64 is the inflation gas itself, supplied by the inflation device 84 during the inflation in which the over-travel occurs; the valve 92 has no store of gas and no valve additional to the valve member 40.

[0033] The biasing member 50 urges the valve member 40 towards the valve seat and resists the normal stroke and the over-travel. In the prototype the biasing member 50 is a compression spring of stainless music wire acting under the valve member 40; nothing in the concept depends on the material of the biasing member 50. The biasing member 50 of the prototype has a two-rate characteristic: a soft first stage, which the pressure differential overcomes during the normal stroke and which sets the opening pressure, and a stiffer second stage, which the pressure differential must overcome to reach over-travel and which sets the over-travel threshold. The valves described herein require an over-travel threshold higher than the opening pressure; in the prototype that over-travel threshold is provided by the two-rate characteristic of the biasing member 50. The two-rate characteristic describes the force behaviour of the biasing member 50 and not any particular construction by which that behaviour is obtained.

[0034] When the inflation device 84 is removed, the pressure differential falls, the valve member 40 returns towards the valve seat under the biasing member 50, the deflectable element recovers its undeflected shape, and the purge gap 60 closes. As the deflectable element recovers it slides across the sealing face 42 of the valve member 40; that sliding contact is the recovery wipe. According to the inventors, the recovery of a seat ring 30 of superelastic alloy is fast and stiff, so that the recovering seat ring 30 wipes across the sealing face 42 with a snap, and partially cured sealant film is sheared off the sealing face 42 rather than embedded, as it would be in a soft seat. In the bench test described in the Examples, removal of the inflation device 84 from a valve core 90 according to the first embodiment was accompanied by an audible snap on every inflation.

[0035] In the first embodiment the seat ring 30 is retained in the core bore 22 by an interference fit of approximately 0.03 mm on diameter and is seated against the locating shoulder 24. The interference fit retains the seat ring 30 in the core body 20; the seat ring 30 is pressed into the core body 20 during assembly, as described in the section on manufacture.

[0036] The valve stem 10 of the prototype is a conventional brass valve stem of Presta pattern and 6 mm outer diameter, with the threaded bore 12 receiving the valve core 90. The lock nut 70 is conventional and closes the valve member 40 manually for storage and transport.

[0037] The sealant with which the valves were tested is a latex emulsion containing ammonia and suspended particles. Ammonia-free sealant, which cures differently, has not been tested. The sealant-accumulation surfaces of the valve 92 are the valve seat, the sealing face 42 of the valve member 40, and the surfaces bounding the purge gap 60, which in the first embodiment include the outer surface 38 of the seat ring 30 and the core bore 22.

[0038] The valve core 90 contains the whole of the mechanism described: the core body 20, the valve member 40, the biasing member 50 and the deflectable element. That mechanism may be provided as a valve core 90 receivable in a valve stem 10, as a valve 92 comprising the valve stem 10 and the valve core 90, or fitted to an inflatable body 80 containing sealant 82, as shown in FIG. 8. Every feature described herein for the valve 92 is a feature of the valve core 90 where the feature lies within the valve core 90, and of the inflatable body 80 fitted with the valve 92.

#### Definitions

[0039] **"pressure differential".** As used herein, "pressure differential" means the difference between the gas pressure acting on the valve member from the side of the valve to which the inflation device is applied and the gas pressure acting on the valve member from the side of the inflatable body, the difference being positive when the pressure on the inflation-device side is the higher. Every value of pressure stated herein for the opening pressure and for the over-travel threshold is a value of the pressure differential so defined, and is not an absolute pressure and not the pressure in the inflatable body. The pressure differential falls when the inflation device ceases to apply pressure to the valve or is removed from it.

[0040] **"opening pressure".** As used herein, "opening pressure" means the pressure differential at which the valve member leaves the valve seat, so that inflation gas begins to pass the valve seat along the main flow passage. In the embodiments described the opening pressure is set by the biasing member. In the prototype the opening pressure is 1.0 bar; in some embodiments it is from 0.8 bar to 1.5 bar.

[0041] **"normal stroke".** As used herein, "normal stroke" means the travel of the valve member away from the valve seat under a pressure differential that exceeds the opening pressure but does not exceed the over-travel threshold. Throughout the normal stroke the deflectable element is not deflected by the valve member. In the embodiments described, the normal stroke ends at the position of the valve member at which the purge shoulder first bears on the deflectable element. In the prototype the normal stroke is 0.6 mm. A valve whose valve member has made the normal stroke and no more is, during that inflation, operating as a conventional valve.

[0042] **"over-travel threshold".** As used herein, "over-travel threshold" means the pressure differential, higher than the opening pressure, which must be exceeded for the valve member to travel beyond the end of the normal stroke. The over-travel threshold is a property of the valve as a whole, whatever provides it; in the prototype it is provided by the second stage of the two-rate characteristic of the biasing member. In the prototype the over-travel threshold is 3.5 bar; in some embodiments it is from 2.5 bar to 5.0 bar. Where an inflation device applies a pressure differential that does not exceed the over-travel threshold, the valve member makes the normal stroke only, no over-travel occurs, and the valve operates as a conventional valve.

[0043] **"over-travel".** As used herein, "over-travel" means the travel of the valve member, in the direction of the normal stroke and beyond the end of the normal stroke, that occurs when the pressure differential exceeds the over-travel threshold. Over-travel is travel of the same valve member that makes the normal stroke, and it is produced by the pressure differential acting on the valve member against the biasing member. During over-travel the valve member bears on the deflectable element and deflects it. In the prototype the over-travel is 0.5 mm beyond the end of the normal stroke; in some embodiments it is from 0.4 mm to 0.9 mm. The term denotes controlled travel of the valve member against the biasing member and does not denote any instability of the valve member.

[0044] **"rest state".** As used herein, "rest state" means the condition of the valve in which the pressure differential does not exceed the opening pressure, including the condition in which no inflation device is applied to the valve and the inflatable body holds its pressure. In the rest state the valve member is seated on the valve seat, the deflectable element is undeflected, and the purge gap is closed. The rest state is the condition of the valve between inflations.

[0045] **"main flow passage".** As used herein, "main flow passage" means the path along which inflation gas passes through the valve from the inflation-device side to the inflatable body between the sealing face of the valve member and the valve seat when the valve member is lifted from the valve seat. Inflation gas passes along the main flow passage during the normal stroke and during over-travel. While the purge gap is closed, the main flow passage is the only path for inflation gas through the valve.

[0046] **"purge gap".** As used herein, "purge gap" means a flow path through the valve for inflation gas which is separate from the main flow passage; which is closed in the rest state and throughout the normal stroke, so that no purge flow passes through it; which is opened by deflection of the deflectable element during over-travel; and which is closed again by recovery of the deflectable element when the pressure differential falls. The purge gap is bounded, at least in part, by a surface of the deflectable element, and the surfaces bounding the purge gap are sealant-accumulation surfaces. "Separate from the main flow passage" means that gas passing through the purge gap does not pass between the sealing face and the valve seat: the purge gap is a second path through the valve, in parallel with the main flow passage and not in series with it, and it may communicate at its ends with the same spaces on the inflation-device side and on the inflatable-body side as the main flow passage. "Closed", in relation to the purge gap, means that the purge gap does not admit purge flow. The term is defined by these functions and not by the position of the purge gap within the valve, by the parts between which it lies, or by the direction in which the deflectable element deflects. In the prototype the purge gap is annular.

[0047] **"purge flow".** As used herein, "purge flow" means inflation gas, supplied by the inflation device during the inflation in which the over-travel occurs, that passes through the purge gap while the purge gap is open, along the sealant-accumulation surfaces bounding the purge gap, and into the inflatable body. Purge flow passes through the purge gap in addition to, and not through, the main flow passage. Purge flow is gas from the inflation device; it is not gas from a store within or separate from the valve. The term refers to the flow of gas itself and does not, of itself, state any result of that flow.

[0048] **"deflectable element".** As used herein, "deflectable element" means an element of the valve that has an undeflected shape in which the purge gap is closed, that is deflected from the undeflected shape so that the purge gap opens, and that recovers towards the undeflected shape, so that the purge gap closes, when the load or condition that deflected it is removed. In the valves described herein other than the thermal purge variant, the deflectable element is deflected by the valve member during over-travel, is not deflected during the normal stroke, and its recovery is the elastic recovery of the element itself, which occurs when the pressure differential falls. The deflectable element may provide the valve seat, in which case it is a seat ring, or it may be separate from a rigid valve seat, in which case it is a wiper ring. In the embodiments described, the deflection of the deflectable element includes outward radial deflection. The term does not itself specify a material; in the embodiments described the deflectable element is of a superelastic alloy, of a polymer, or of a resilient metal.

[0049] **"valve seat".** As used herein, "valve seat" means the surface of the valve on which the sealing face of the valve member seals when the valve member is seated, as it is in the rest state. The valve seat may be provided by the deflectable element: in the first embodiment the valve seat is the upper edge of the seat ring. Alternatively the valve seat may be a rigid surface that is not deflected during over-travel, referred to herein as a "rigid valve seat": in the embodiment having a wiper ring, the rigid valve seat is of brass. The valve seat and the deflectable element are distinct concepts even where a single part provides both.

[0050] **"sealing face".** As used herein, "sealing face" means the surface of the valve member that seals against the valve seat when the valve member is seated, and across which the deflectable element slides in the recovery wipe. In the prototype the sealing face is conical and its line of contact with the upper edge of the seat ring is where sealing occurs. The term does not require a conical form.

[0051] **"seat ring".** As used herein, "seat ring" means a one-piece deflectable element of generally tubular form whose upper edge provides the valve seat and which is received in the core bore of the core body. In the embodiments described the seat ring has the convoluted wall; in the first embodiment it is of a superelastic alloy, and in the polymer seat ring it is of a polymer. The term "seat ring" does not itself require any particular material or any particular form of wall.

[0052] **"polymer seat ring".** As used herein, "polymer seat ring" means a seat ring made of a polymer. In the embodiment described the polymer is polyether ether ketone (PEEK) and the polymer seat ring has the same convoluted wall as the seat ring of superelastic alloy.

[0053] **"wiper ring".** As used herein, "wiper ring" means a deflectable element that does not provide the valve seat and is separate from a rigid valve seat, that is deflected by the purge shoulder during over-travel, and that on recovery slides across the sealing face of the valve member. In the embodiment described the wiper ring is a thin ring of stainless spring steel, positioned on the inflatable-body side of the rigid valve seat, and is deflected outward by the purge shoulder.

[0054] **"purge shoulder".** As used herein, "purge shoulder" means a portion of the valve member that is clear of the deflectable element throughout the normal stroke and bears on the deflectable element only during over-travel, so that the over-travel of the valve member is applied to the deflectable element as the load that deflects it. The term is defined by this function and not by the position or the form of the portion. In the prototype the purge shoulder is an annular step of the valve member below the sealing face.

[0055] **"recovery wipe".** As used herein, "recovery wipe" means the sliding of the deflectable element across the sealing face of the valve member that occurs as the deflectable element recovers when the pressure differential falls and the valve member returns towards the valve seat. The sliding is relative motion, in contact, between the deflectable element and the sealing face. The term does not specify the direction of the sliding relative to the axis of the valve, and it does not specify which of the deflectable element and the valve member is moving relative to the core body during the sliding; in the embodiments described both are in motion as the pressure differential falls. The term denotes the sliding contact itself and not any result attributed to it.

[0056] **"sealant".** As used herein, "sealant" means a liquid puncture sealant carried within the inflatable body, a quantity of which is drawn into the valve in use and dries or cures there. The sealant used in the Examples is a latex emulsion containing ammonia and suspended particles. The term is not limited to any particular sealant chemistry; the Examples report tests with one sealant, as there stated.

[0057] **"sealant film".** As used herein, "sealant film" means a layer of sealant left on a surface of the valve after sealant drawn into the valve has dried or cured, wholly or in part. Sealant film that has dried or cured in part is referred to as partially cured sealant film.

[0058] **"sealant-accumulation surfaces".** As used herein, "sealant-accumulation surfaces" means the internal surfaces of the valve that are exposed to sealant drawn into the valve in use and on which sealant film forms between inflations, namely the valve seat, the sealing face of the valve member, and the surfaces bounding the purge gap, including the outer surface of the deflectable element where it bounds the purge gap. The term identifies these surfaces by their position and exposure within the valve; it does not depend on whether sealant film is present on them at any particular time.

[0059] **"inflation cycle".** As used herein, "inflation cycle" means one inflation of the inflatable body through the valve by an inflation device and the subsequent deflation of the inflatable body through the valve. In the Examples an inflation cycle is inflation to 6 bar with a floor pump, a hold of 24 hours at a temperature of 22 °C, and full deflation through the valve.

[0060] **"leak-down".** As used herein, "leak-down" means loss of pressure from the inflatable body through the valve while the valve is in the rest state. In the Examples the failure criterion for leak-down is a loss of more than 0.5 bar over a period of 24 hours.

[0061] **"blockage".** As used herein, "blockage" means the failure of the valve member to leave the valve seat when the inflation device is applied to the valve. In the Examples the failure criterion for blockage is that the inflation device is unable to open the valve within 5 seconds of engagement.

[0062] **"biasing member".** As used herein, "biasing member" means a member that urges the valve member towards the valve seat and that resists the normal stroke and the over-travel of the valve member. In the prototype the biasing member is a compression spring of stainless music wire acting under the valve member; the material of the biasing member is not essential.

[0063] **"two-rate characteristic".** As used herein, "two-rate characteristic" means a force behaviour of the biasing member having a soft first stage, which the pressure differential overcomes during the normal stroke and which sets the opening pressure, and a stiffer second stage, which the pressure differential must overcome for over-travel and which sets the over-travel threshold. The term describes the force behaviour of the biasing member and not any particular construction by which that behaviour is obtained. The valves described herein require an over-travel threshold higher than the opening pressure; in the prototype that over-travel threshold is provided by the two-rate characteristic of the biasing member.

[0064] **"convoluted wall".** As used herein, "convoluted wall" means the wall of the seat ring where that wall, seen in axial section, is not straight but has at least one convolution, a convolution being a wave in which the wall departs radially from, and returns to, the line of the adjacent plain portions of the wall, the convoluted wall being shaped so that an axial load applied to the upper edge of the seat ring is converted into radial expansion of the seat ring. In the embodiments described the convoluted wall has one or two convolutions. The term does not specify whether a convolution projects radially outward or radially inward.

[0065] **"convolution amplitude".** As used herein, "convolution amplitude" means the radial depth of a convolution of the convoluted wall, that is the greatest radial distance, measured in axial section, by which the wall of the convolution departs from the line of the adjacent plain portions of the wall of the seat ring. In the prototype the convolution amplitude is 0.12 mm; in some embodiments it is from 0.08 mm to 0.20 mm.

[0066] **"purge gap width".** As used herein, "purge gap width" means the width of the purge gap, measured perpendicular to the surfaces that bound it, when the purge gap is fully open, that is when the valve member is at the end of its over-travel. In the rest state the purge gap width is zero. In the prototype the purge gap width is measured radially and is 0.15 mm; in some embodiments it is from 0.10 mm to 0.25 mm.

[0067] **"core body".** As used herein, "core body" means the part of the valve core that carries the deflectable element, guides the valve member, seats the biasing member and is received in the valve stem. [0068] **"core bore".** As used herein, "core bore" means the bore of the core body in which the deflectable element is received. [0069] **"valve member".** As used herein, "valve member" means the part of the valve that carries the sealing face and moves relative to the valve seat so as to seal against the valve seat in the rest state, to make the normal stroke and to make the over-travel. [0073] **"superelastic alloy".** As used herein, "superelastic alloy" means a metallic alloy which, at the temperature of use, is in its austenitic state and recovers strain imposed on it, including strain accommodated by a stress-induced transformation, on removal of the load, so that it recovers large strain elastically at the temperature of use. In the first embodiment the superelastic alloy is a nickel–titanium (NiTi) alloy of nominally 55.8 wt% nickel. The abbreviation "NiTi" denotes that alloy.

[0074] **"austenite finish temperature".** As used herein, "austenite finish temperature", abbreviated "Af", means the temperature above which the superelastic alloy is fully austenitic on heating, and hence the temperature above which the deflectable element of superelastic alloy exhibits superelastic recovery. Where a value of austenite finish temperature is stated herein, it is the value on heating determined by a thermal analysis method, for example differential scanning calorimetry, unless stated otherwise. #### Manufacture and assembly

The steps are performed in that order.

#### Operation and use

[0111] The operation of the valve 92 of the first embodiment is as follows, with reference to FIG. 1, FIG. 2 and FIG. 7, in which the method of purging is shown as a method 700. In a step 702, the inflation device 84 applies a pressure differential across the valve 92; when the pressure differential exceeds the opening pressure, the valve member 40 lifts from the upper edge 32 of the seat ring 30 through the normal stroke and inflation gas 66 passes the valve seat along the main flow passage 62. Throughout the normal stroke the purge shoulder 44 is clear of the seat ring 30 and the seat ring 30 is not deflected. In a step 704, when the pressure differential exceeds the over-travel threshold, the valve member 40 makes over-travel beyond the normal stroke against the second stage of the biasing member 50. In a step 706, the purge shoulder 44 bears on the upper edge 32 of the seat ring 30, an axial load is thereby applied to the upper edge 32, the convoluted wall 34 converts that axial load into radial expansion of the seat ring 30, and the purge gap 60 opens. In a step 708, purge flow 64 passes through the purge gap 60, along the sealant-accumulation surfaces bounding it, and into the inflatable body 80, in addition to the inflation gas 66 passing along the main flow passage 62. In a step 710, the inflation device 84 is removed and the pressure differential falls. In a step 712, the valve member 40 returns towards the valve seat under the biasing member 50, the seat ring 30 recovers its undeflected shape and, as it recovers, slides across the sealing face 42 in the recovery wipe, and the purge gap 60 closes; the valve 92 is again in the rest state. The point within an inflation at which over-travel occurs is not stated herein beyond the observation, in Example 1, of an audible snap on removal of the inflation device 84 on every inflation.

[0112] FIG. 5 shows the three ranges of pressure differential as a diagram of pressure differential against travel of the valve member 40. The two-rate characteristic 500 of the biasing member 50 has a first stage 502 of shallow slope and a second stage 504 of steeper slope. Below the opening pressure 506 the valve 92 is in the rest state 514 and the valve member 40 does not leave the valve seat. Between the opening pressure 506 and the over-travel threshold 508 the valve member 40 makes the normal stroke 510 against the first stage 502, and the deflectable element is not deflected. Above the over-travel threshold 508 the valve member 40 makes the over-travel 512 against the second stage 504, the purge shoulder 44 bears on the deflectable element and the purge gap 60 opens. In the prototype, by way of example, the opening pressure 506 is 1.0 bar, the normal stroke 510 is 0.6 mm, the over-travel threshold 508 is 3.5 bar and the over-travel 512 is 0.5 mm, the pressures being values of the pressure differential as defined herein.

[0113] With reference to FIG. 8, the valve 92 is fitted to an inflatable body 80 containing sealant 82, the valve stem 10 passing through the wall of the inflatable body 80 and the inflation device 84 being applied to the valve 92 for inflation. The inflatable body 80 may be a bicycle inner tube, which is the inflatable body 80 tested in Example 1, or a tubeless tyre set-up, which has not been tested. The inflation device 84 may be a floor pump, which is the inflation device 84 tested in Example 1, or a cartridge inflator supplying carbon dioxide, which has not been tested; a cartridge inflator supplying carbon dioxide cools the valve 92 to well below 0 °C for a few seconds, and whether an austenite finish temperature below 5 °C gives sufficient margin for the seat ring 30 to remain superelastic during such an inflation, or whether a seat ring 30 cooled below its martensite start temperature would remain deflected until it warmed, has not been determined. Where the inflation device 84 does not apply a pressure differential exceeding the over-travel threshold, the valve member 40 makes the normal stroke only, no purge flow 64 occurs, and the valve 92 behaves as a conventional valve; no statement is made herein that every inflation device 84 reaches the over-travel threshold.

[0114] For storage and transport the lock nut 70 is tightened to close the valve member 40 manually. Sealant 82 is injected into the inflatable body 80 through the valve stem 10, as is conventional. No statement is made herein as to whether manual depression of the valve member 40, for example to deflate the inflatable body 80, produces over-travel or purge flow 64, or as to whether a closed lock nut 70 prevents over-travel.

[0115] In the valve cores 90 tested in Example 1, over-travel was indicated by an audible snap on removal of the inflation device 84.

#### Alternatives and combinations

[0116] The deflectable element may take any form in which deflection of the deflectable element during over-travel of the valve member 40 opens the purge gap 60 and recovery of the deflectable element closes the purge gap 60. In the embodiments described, the deflectable element is a seat ring 30 whose upper edge 32 provides the valve seat, or a wiper ring 130 separate from a rigid valve seat 132. The principle is one of general application: over-travel of the valve member 40 deflects the deflectable element, deflection opens the purge gap 60, and recovery closes it; the seat ring 30 and the wiper ring 130 illustrate that principle.

[0117] Where the deflectable element recovers when the pressure differential falls, the deflectable element slides across the sealing face 42 of the valve member 40, producing the recovery wipe. The recovery wipe is produced by the seat ring 30 of superelastic alloy, by the polymer seat ring and by the wiper ring 130 alike; according to the inventors, the recovery force, and with it the recovery wipe, is greatest for a deflectable element of superelastic alloy.

[0118] The deflectable element may be of a superelastic alloy, which the inventors prefer for its fast and stiff recovery; of a polymer, for example polyether ether ketone (PEEK), which the inventors state gives slower recovery and a lower recovery force; or of a resilient metal, for example stainless spring steel, which is the material of the wiper ring 130. Where the deflectable element is of a superelastic alloy, the austenite finish temperature of the superelastic alloy is below the lowest temperature at which the valve 92 is intended to be used, for example below 5 °C.

[0119] The purge shoulder 44 is any portion of the valve member 40 that is clear of the deflectable element throughout the normal stroke and bears on the deflectable element only during over-travel. In the prototype it is an integral annular step of the valve member 40 below the sealing face 42. The purge shoulder 44 may alternatively be a separate collar fixed to the valve member 40, or may be interrupted, for example formed as a plurality of lugs of the valve member 40; in each case the purge shoulder 44 is clear of the deflectable element throughout the normal stroke and bears on it only during over-travel.

[0120] The over-travel threshold is provided in the prototype by the two-rate characteristic of the biasing member 50, in which the first stage sets the opening pressure and the stiffer second stage sets the over-travel threshold. The two-rate characteristic may be obtained by any construction giving that force behaviour, for example a single coil spring of variable pitch whose closer-pitched coils bind at the end of the normal stroke, two springs acting in series through a collar which meets a stop at the end of the normal stroke, two nested springs of different free length the shorter and stiffer of which is engaged only after the normal stroke, or a second stage held under preload so that it deflects only when the load exceeds the preload. During over-travel the purge shoulder 44 deflects the deflectable element against the deflectable element's own stiffness, so the resistance to over-travel includes that stiffness in addition to the second stage of the biasing member 50.

[0121] The biasing member 50 may be a coil spring, as in the prototype, or a wave spring, or a stack of disc springs. The material of the biasing member 50 is not essential; it may be stainless music wire, as in the prototype, or another spring material.

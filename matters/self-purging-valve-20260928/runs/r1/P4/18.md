#### Actual examples

**Example 1 — first embodiment: valve core with a seat ring of superelastic alloy, built and bench-tested.** An example is actual here because the disclosure (§3 to §5, §7, §8) records that three units were built and the bench data record the results. Every reported value below carries its source.

*Components.* A valve stem (10): a conventional brass valve stem of Presta pattern and 6 mm outer diameter with a threaded bore that receives the valve core (§3 item 1). A core body (20), threaded into the valve stem, carrying the seat ring and guiding the valve member (§3 item 2). A seat ring (30): a short tube of superelastic alloy whose upper edge provides the valve seat and whose wall is a convoluted wall with one or two shallow convolutions, so that axial load on the upper edge becomes radial expansion; received in the core bore with a light interference fit and located against the locating shoulder (§3 item 3; §7). A valve member (40) with a conical sealing face (42) that seats on the upper edge of the seat ring, and a purge shoulder (44) below the sealing face that bears on the seat ring only during over-travel (§3 item 4). A biasing member (50): a light compression spring under the valve member with a two-rate characteristic, a soft first stage for the normal stroke and a stiffer second stage that the pressure differential must overcome to reach over-travel (§3 item 5). A purge gap (60): an annular clearance between the outer surface of the seat ring and the core bore, closed in the rest state and opened when the seat ring expands (§3 item 6). A lock nut (70) for manual closing of the valve member for storage and transport (§3 item 7).

*Materials.* Seat ring: superelastic nickel–titanium alloy, nominally 55.8 wt% Ni, from drawn tube, shape-set and electropolished (§5). Core body and valve stem: free-machining brass CW614N; a variant of the core body was also machined in stainless steel of grade 303 for corrosion resistance with ammonia-bearing sealant (§5). Valve member: brass; hard-anodised aluminium was considered for the sealing face (§5; whether for the whole valve member or the sealing face only is 01 Q18). Biasing member: stainless music wire; "nothing in the concept depends on the spring material" (§5). Which core body material the three bench units used is not recorded (01 Q20).

*Dimensions and ranges.* Prototype values, with the ranges the inventors state they believe would work; no range end was tested (§4; 01 A5). Seat ring inner diameter 2.3 mm (range 2.0 mm to 2.6 mm). Wall thickness 0.20 mm (0.15 mm to 0.30 mm). Free length 3.0 mm (2.5 mm to 4.0 mm). Convolution amplitude 0.12 mm (0.08 mm to 0.20 mm); the measurement convention is not stated (13 Q3). Normal stroke 0.6 mm (no range given). Over-travel beyond the normal stroke 0.5 mm (0.4 mm to 0.9 mm). Purge gap width when fully open 0.15 mm radial (0.10 mm to 0.25 mm); zero in the rest state. Opening pressure 1.0 bar (0.8 bar to 1.5 bar) and over-travel threshold 3.5 bar (2.5 bar to 5.0 bar), both stated as the pressure differential in the locked sense (inflation-device side minus inflatable-body side); the measuring condition is not recorded (01 Q5; EN-3). Austenite finish temperature of the superelastic alloy specified below 5 °C so that the seat ring is superelastic at the temperatures of use (§4); the measured value is not recorded (01 Q20).

*Fabrication and assembly.* Seat rings were laser-cut from superelastic-alloy tube of 2.7 mm outer diameter and 0.20 mm wall to the convoluted profile on a mandrel; shape-set at a temperature of 500 °C for a period of 10 minutes in a fixture holding the wave geometry; water-quenched; then electropolished to remove the oxide and the heat-affected edge (§7). What the laser cut produces is recorded as stated and not interpreted (01 Q3). The seat ring was pressed into the core body with an interference of approximately 0.03 mm on diameter and seated against the locating shoulder (§7). The core body and the valve member were made by ordinary turning; "no other special process" (§7).

*Operating sequence.* (i) The inflation device applies a pressure differential; above the opening pressure the valve member lifts through the normal stroke and inflation gas passes the valve seat along the main flow passage. (ii) Above the over-travel threshold the valve member makes over-travel against the second stage of the biasing member. (iii) The purge shoulder bears on the seat ring, which deflects radially and opens the purge gap. (iv) Purge flow passes through the purge gap, along the sealant-accumulation surfaces, and into the inflatable body. (v) When the inflation device is removed the pressure differential falls, the valve member returns, the seat ring recovers its shape and, as it recovers, slides across the sealing face (the recovery wipe); the purge gap closes (§2 paras 1 to 2; 01 (d) S1 to S5). When within an inflation over-travel occurs is recorded only as the bench data record it: an audible snap on removal of the inflation device on every prototype inflation (bench data notes; 01 Q5).

*Inputs and outputs.* Input: air from a floor pump applied to the valve until the inflatable body reached 6 bar (§8). Environment: a bicycle inner tube of 700×28 size charged with a quantity of 60 ml of latex sealant containing ammonia and suspended particles (§8; §1). Outputs: inflation of the inflatable body; purge flow carrying sealant film into the inflatable body (§2 para 1); a closed, sealing valve in the rest state (§3 item 6); an audible snap on removal of the inflation device (bench data notes).

*Expected technical effect, and what was observed.* The inventors state that the valve keeps sealing and keeps opening after far more sealant-loaded inflation cycles than a conventional valve core, with no tool and no action by the rider (§2 para 3). The bench test of 2026-08-12 to 2026-08-20 (§8; bench data) compared three prototype valve cores (P-1 to P-3) with three conventional valve cores of the same manufacturer's standard Presta-pattern valve core (C-1 to C-3), each fitted to an identical inflatable body as above. One inflation cycle was: inflate to 6 bar with the floor pump, hold for a period of 24 hours at a temperature of 22 °C, deflate fully through the valve. Failure was leak-down of more than 0.5 bar over the 24-hour hold, or the inflation device unable to open the valve within 5 seconds of engagement (blockage). Results as recorded (bench data table):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-1 | conventional | 14 | blockage | 41 | 68 | 13 | 0.1 |
| C-2 | conventional | 12 | leak-down of 0.7 bar | 40 | 44 | 11 | 0.7 |
| C-3 | conventional | 18 | blockage | 42 | 75 | 17 | 0.2 |
| P-1 | first embodiment | none (test length: 40 cycles) | — | 43 | 47 | 40 | 0.1 |
| P-2 | first embodiment | none (test length: 40 cycles) | — | 41 | 46 | 40 | 0.1 |
| P-3 | first embodiment | none (test length: 40 cycles) | — | 44 | 50 | 40 | 0.2 |

All three conventional valve cores failed, at cycle counts of 12, 14 and 18. All three prototype valve cores completed forty inflation cycles without failure; the test stopped at that count with no prototype failure. The inflation time at the fortieth cycle was, for the three prototypes, 9 per cent, 12 per cent and 14 per cent above the first-cycle value, which the disclosure states as "within 15 %" (§8; bench data). Inflation time was measured from engagement of the inflation device to 6 bar with the same pump and the same operator (bench data notes). On disassembly after the fortieth cycle, inspection under a stereo microscope found cured sealant film on the outer surface of the seat ring in the region of the purge gap, and a clean upper edge of the seat ring and a clean sealing face of the valve member (§8; bench data notes). No test isolates purge flow from the recovery wipe, and neither action is stated here to be sufficient alone (EN-19). Limits of the evidence: three units per arm, one sealant, one temperature, one inflation pressure, one inflation device; the result is a lower bound of forty inflation cycles and not a service life; no fatigue, cold-shock, ammonia-free-sealant or range-end test was performed (§9; bench data notes; EN-11).

## Rationale

The only actual example is the bench test, and it is written as run: three units per arm, the test regime, the failure criteria, every value in the bench table with its source, and the cycle-40 inspection in full, including the cured sealant film found in the region of the purge gap, because that observation is evidence about where film forms and the audit (EN-7, EN-19) requires it reported. I did not compute or state anything the bench data do not record: the per-unit inflation-time increases are those the data give, the "within 15 %" figure is quoted as the disclosure's statement, and the result is stated as a lower bound of forty inflation cycles.

The two conceived alternatives are prophetic, headed as such, in the conditional tense, with no numbers, exactly at the depth of §6. For the polymer seat ring I declined to carry the §4 dimensions across, because the disclosure attributes them to the superelastic-alloy ring and 14 footnote [49] says a polymer wall of the same thickness has a different stiffness. For the rigid valve seat and wiper ring I declined to describe the wiper ring's geometry, mounting or purge flow path, because `CONFIRMED.md` offers only AI-proposed readings (E10.5, E9.4) and the audit (EN-13) forbids writing them as alternative B's construction.

The purge gap of Example 1 is described in §3 item 6's words only (annular; between the outer surface of the seat ring and the core bore; closed in the rest state; opens when the seat ring expands). The §2 "stem bore" wording is not reproduced, and the 0.03 mm interference fit appears in the fabrication paragraph, not beside the 0.15 mm purge gap width (PF-1; EN-1, EN-16). Pressures are stated as the locked pressure differential with no measuring procedure (PF-3; EN-3). The recovery wipe is described as §2 para 2 describes it, with no direction clause (PF-16; EN-6), and the shearing of partially cured film is the inventors' statement.

The thermal purge variant is not a principal embodiment for examples: it lies outside the over-travel concept, has no data, and its mechanism is not described. I said so and raised a `disclosure_requests` entry rather than invent a prophetic example for it. The bench table uses "first embodiment" for the prototype rows and "blockage" for the failure mode so that the example uses locked terms; the bench data's own wording for the failure mode ("blocked (pump could not open)") is the same event.

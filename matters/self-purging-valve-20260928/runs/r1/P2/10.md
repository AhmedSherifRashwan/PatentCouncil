### White-Space Opportunities

This is a drafting and analysis aid prepared by AI. It is not legal advice, a patentability opinion or a filing decision; qualified patent counsel signs off before anything is filed. Every opportunity below is `candidate`. Each one is measured only against the ten references charted in `P2/07` (eight real, two fictional test inputs), which is a thin set (07 Q1). `intake/answers.md` does not exist, and Gate 1 was decided unattended, so no opportunity here has been confirmed by the inventor. The quick profile has no Debate 1, so the matrix used is `P2/09`, not a debated `09r`.

Citations: `§` is `intake/disclosure.md`; "bench notes" is `intake/data/bench-test.md`; FIG-1 and FIG-2 are the captions in `intake/drawings/README.md` (the images have not been seen); `07 <reference> R<n>` is a chart row; `09 D<n>` is a matrix row; `08` is the per-concept table. Each item says what the disclosure already supports. Anything that would need new inventor input is under Open Questions, not here.

1. **Valve member whose purge shoulder engages the deflectable element only after the normal stroke (sub-combination; N3).**
   - *Aspect.* A valve member carrying the sealing face and, below it, a purge shoulder that is clear of the deflectable element throughout the normal stroke and bears on it only during over-travel. The valve member is a turned part that a valve-core maker produces on its own.
   - *Why clear of the charted art.* 07 R10 is `absent` in all ten charts. In US3417778A the portion that deflects the lip is the sealing portion and bears on it at rest (claim 1, [15]; 07 US3417778A R10). In ref-02 the valve member deflects the lip during normal opening (claim 4; 09 D4). 08 rates the N3 increment moderate and supports its obviousness only by an unsupported common-general-knowledge assertion (08 A4).
   - *Supporting passage.* §3 item 4 ("a purge shoulder (44) below the sealing face that only contacts the ring during over-travel"); FIG-1 and FIG-2 captions; 03 N3.
   - *Commercial note.* This is the feature that makes the valve behave as a conventional valve below the over-travel threshold. Write it by function (contact only during over-travel), not by position. The contact point is easy to move (09 D4, design-around risk). Alternative B uses the same purge shoulder, so the wording must not require a seat ring.

2. **Seat ring with a convoluted wall as a component in its own right, not tied to a material (article; N5 and N6-A).**
   - *Aspect.* A tubular seat ring whose upper edge provides the valve seat and whose convoluted wall (one or two shallow convolutions) converts axial load on that edge into radial expansion. It is claimed without the material, so the superelastic NiTi seat ring and the polymer seat ring (alternative A) fall under the same wording.
   - *Why clear of the charted art.* 07 R14 is `absent` in all ten charts, and no reference has a convoluted wall (07 "Combination analysis" item 4; 09 D7). The nearest is US3417778A, whose tubular seat expands radially under fluid pressure and against the valve body's increasing diameter, not by conversion of axial load ([14]–[15]; 07 US3417778A R14). 08 rates the N5 increment moderate, resting only on an unsupported common-general-knowledge assertion (08 A6).
   - *Supporting passage.* §3 item 3 ("a wall that is not straight in section but has one or two shallow convolutions (a wave), so that axial load on its upper edge translates into radial expansion"); §4 (convolution amplitude 0.08–0.20 mm; wall thickness 0.15–0.30 mm; free length 2.5–4.0 mm); §6 para 1 (PEEK seat ring "with the same convoluted wall"); §7.
   - *Commercial note.* The seat ring is the part that sets the product apart and the one a lower-cost line would change (§6 para 1). A material-neutral claim keeps alternative A inside it. Design-around risk is high: other wall forms are not disclosed (09 D7), and alternative B avoids the seat ring entirely. Its value is as a fallback and as protection for a spare-part article.

3. **Sequence: two cleaning actions from one deflection in one inflation cycle (sequencing and use protocol; N1).**
   - *Aspect.* While the inflation device applies a pressure differential above the over-travel threshold, the deflected deflectable element holds the purge gap open and purge flow passes. When the inflation device is removed, the same element recovers, closes the purge gap and makes the recovery wipe across the sealing face. The claim is to the order of events: deflect, purge, recover, wipe.
   - *Why clear of the charted art.* R6 (purge flow) appears only in US3570510A, supplied from a separate gas store ([22], [26]). R8 (recovery wipe) is `absent` in all ten charts, with partial sources in US4050473A [13], US4236547A [6] and US3417778A [9]. None of them uses a recovering deflectable element (07 "Combination analysis" items 1 and 3). No reference produces both actions from one element (09 D5). 08 rates N1 weak to moderate, noting that four references would be needed.
   - *Supporting passage.* §2 para 2 ("the valve cleans itself twice per inflation: once by air flow through the purge gap, and once mechanically by the snap-back wipe"); bench notes ("audible snap on pump-head removal"); §8 inspection (clean sealing edge and valve member face at cycle 40).
   - *Commercial note.* Supports a method claim for purging a valve during inflation (02 rank 4) as well as an apparatus clause. Enforcing a method claim against riders is impractical, so its main value is indirect, and counsel should weigh the US, EP and GB rules on indirect infringement. The row depends on both actions being real (03 Q1).

4. **Recovery wipe by the deflectable element across the sealing face (sub-combination; the N0-W route).**
   - *Aspect.* Over-travel deflects a deflectable element, and on recovery the element (not the valve member) slides across the sealing face of the valve member, shearing off sealant film where the valve seals.
   - *Why clear of the charted art.* 07 R8 is `absent` in all ten charts. In US4050473A a flexible disk carried on the valve member wipes the valve seat during driven closing ([13]), and in US4236547A a rigid edge on the valve member cuts deposits on closing ([6], [30]). In both the arrangement and the trigger are reversed (09 D6). 08 nevertheless rates N0-W moderate to strong on inventive step (ref-02 [0020] with US4236547A [6]). This is white space for a dependent claim or a fallback nucleus, not for a lead independent claim unless the wipe alone is shown to give the effect.
   - *Supporting passage.* §2 para 2; §8 inspection (clean sealing edge and clean valve member face at cycle 40); bench notes; 03 N0-W and N1.
   - *Commercial note.* This is the only mechanism that acts on the sealing face itself, which the bench inspection found clean (02 rank 5). Its breadth depends on which surfaces slide and in which direction (01 Q7). The disclosure does not yet say.

5. **Three-band pressure control from one biasing member on one valve member (control scheme and interface with the inflation device; N0 with N2).**
   - *Aspect.* A single valve member and a single biasing member with a two-rate characteristic give three bands of pressure differential: the valve is in the rest state below the opening pressure, makes the normal stroke between the opening pressure and the over-travel threshold, and makes over-travel above it. The purge is therefore triggered by the inflation device's own pressure differential, with no coupling feature, actuator or rider action.
   - *Why clear of the charted art.* R3 is explicit in no chart (07 "Combination analysis" item 1). US20080188352A1 reaches a second pressure with a second, rigid valve member and a second spring (07 US20080188352A1 R3 and R9 `ambiguous`; 09 D2, D10). In US11179979B2 the core rod is moved by hand after unlocking ([0101]; 09 D2). 08 rates the two-rate increment strong on its own. The white space is the three-band behaviour of one valve member ending in over-travel that deflects the deflectable element, not the two-rate biasing member alone.
   - *Supporting passage.* §3 item 5 ("a soft first stage for normal opening and a stiffer second stage that the pump differential must overcome to reach over-travel"); §4 (opening pressure 1.0 bar, range 0.8–1.5 bar; over-travel threshold 3.5 bar, range 2.5–5.0 bar); 01 (i).
   - *Commercial note.* This is the interface that lets the product work with inflation devices riders already own. Claim the relationship (over-travel threshold above the opening pressure), not a named inflation device: the inventor's statement that all floor pumps reach the threshold is unevidenced (01 A6). The ranges are estimates, and the over-travel threshold range overlaps US20080188352A1 [14] between about 4.5 and 5.0 bar (08 N8), so no range should be relied on as a distinction.

6. **Where the purge gap sits: between the outer surface of the deflectable element and the core bore (geometry and interface; N0 with N4).**
   - *Aspect.* The purge gap is annular and lies between the seat ring's outer surface and the core bore. It is closed in the rest state and opened by radial deflection, so purge flow runs over surfaces away from the valve seat, separate from the main flow passage.
   - *Why clear of the charted art.* No chart shows R5 opened by deflection. US20080188352A1 [26] and US3570510A [18] each have a separate flow path, but one is opened by a rigid member and the other is always open and gated by separate valves (07 "Combination analysis" item 1; 09 D1).
   - *Supporting passage.* §3 item 6 ("the annular clearance between the seat ring outer surface and the core body bore that opens when the ring expands; closed at rest"); §2 para 1; FIG-2 caption ("air flow through the main passage and through the purge gap"). The bench notes record cured film "on the ring outer surface in the purge-gap region" at cycle 40. That confirms sealant film forms there; it does not show that purge flow removes it.
   - *Commercial note.* This is the lead distinction on the art (09 D1) and the weakest point on enablement. The disclosure does not explain how outward expansion of a seat ring held by an interference fit opens, rather than closes, a gap against the core bore (01 Q1). It is white space in the art, but not yet claimable with confidence until that is answered.

7. **Replaceable valve core carrying the whole purge mechanism; the valve stem stays in place (disposable/reusable division; N7).**
   - *Aspect.* The valve core (core body, valve member, biasing member and deflectable element) is the replaceable unit. The valve stem stays fixed to the inflatable body. The same subject matter can be claimed at three points in the supply chain: the valve core, the valve (valve stem with valve core), and an inflatable body fitted with the valve and containing sealant.
   - *Why clear of the charted art.* In US11179979B2 the deflectable element (sealing lips) sits at the tyre end of the valve stem body, outside the removable valve core (claim 1, [0101]; 07 US11179979B2 R17 `absent`). Ref-01's valve core has no deflectable element (07 ref-01 R17 `absent`; 09 D9). 08 rates the N7 increment strong on its own, so this adds claim categories, not a distinction.
   - *Supporting passage.* §1 ("cores are sold in multipacks"); §3 items 1–3; §8 (700×28 tubes each charged with 60 ml of sealant, fitted with the valve cores); 03 N7; 02 ranks 2–3.
   - *Commercial note.* The valve core is the likely unit of sale and the item riders replace today (02 rank 2). The inflatable-body and valve levels catch OEM supply (02 rank 3). Retrofit to standard valve stems is inferred, not stated (01 Q23).

8. **Rigid valve seat with a separate wiper ring (two-piece division; N4-B, alternative B).**
   - *Aspect.* A rigid valve seat does the sealing. A separate wiper ring beside it is deflected outward by the purge shoulder during over-travel and does the purge and the wipe.
   - *Why clear of the charted art.* US4050473A separates wiping from sealing, but its wiper is carried on the valve member ([4], [15]; 07 US4050473A R13 `ambiguous`; 09 D11). No chart shows a wiper ring deflected by a valve member's over-travel. 08 rates the increment moderate to strong.
   - *Supporting passage.* §6 para 2 ("a rigid brass seat for sealing plus a separate thin stainless spring wiper ring below it that is deflected outward by the purge shoulder"). Not built; one paragraph.
   - *Commercial note.* This is the most likely design-around of items 2 and 6 (02 rank 9). Its main value is to make sure the broad wording (deflectable element, purge shoulder) covers it. A claim of its own needs more description than one paragraph (01 Q13).

9. **Seat-ring manufacturing route (manufacturing steps; N9).**
   - *Aspect.* Laser cutting of the seat ring from NiTi tube to the convoluted profile on a mandrel; shape-setting at 500 °C for 10 minutes in a fixture holding the convoluted wall geometry; water quench; electropolishing to remove oxide and the heat-affected edge; press fit (interference fit about 0.03 mm on diameter) against the locating shoulder.
   - *Why clear of the charted art.* 07 R19 is `absent` in all ten charts. US6053992A deforms and heat-recovers a static plug ([26]; 07 US6053992A R19). But the manufacturing route was not searched ("Coverage" in `prior-art/INDEX.md`; 08 N9, "not rateable on supplied art; expected moderate to strong"). Clearance here reflects a gap in the search, not evidence of novelty.
   - *Supporting passage.* §7; 01 (j).
   - *Commercial note.* Infringement happens in a factory and is hard to prove from the product. At most this supports a dependent or product-by-process fallback unless a search shows the route is new. What the laser cut produces is itself unclear (01 (j), Q3).

10. **Thermal variant: purge gap opened by warming, without an inflation device (alternative trigger).**
    - *Aspect.* A seat ring trained with two-way shape memory, with an austenite finish temperature around 35–40 °C, so that warming it by hand opens the purge gap for a purge without an inflation device.
    - *Why clear of the charted art.* 07 has no row for it. The only shape-memory reference, US6053992A, is a static plug recovered by heat, with no valve member and no opening or closing (07 US6053992A R1–R10 `absent`). Clearance is therefore unassessed rather than shown.
    - *Supporting passage.* §6 para 3 only. The inventor is "not sure this is worth pursuing and [has] no data".
    - *Commercial note.* A different product (the thermal purge variant) with a different trigger (02 rank 12). The disclosure supports putting it on record in the description so it is not lost. Claiming it needs the inventor's confirmation and a description of how the purge gap opens (01 Q14; 03 Q6).

Considered and not listed, because the dossier gives them no protectable content against this art: the austenite finish temperature below 5 °C (08 rates the increment strong on US6053992A [3]); the stainless 303 core body for ammonia-bearing sealant and the other prototype materials (08 N10); the §4 ranges as distinctions (no criticality shown; 09 Q6). Calibration methods, deflation behaviour, lock-nut behaviour, other valve standards, CO2 inflation and retrofit kits are not supported by the disclosure. Each is under Open Questions as blocking P3.

## Rationale

Items are ordered by how clearly the charted art leaves them open and how much disclosure already supports them. Items 1 and 2 hit rows that are `absent` in every chart (R10, R14) and have a specific supporting passage. Items 3 and 4 hit R8, also `absent` everywhere, but depend on 03 Q1 and 01 Q7. Item 5 is the interface that makes the product usable, but 08 rates its spring increment strong, so it is framed as behaviour of the valve member rather than as a spring. Item 6 is the strongest point on the art and the weakest on enablement, so it sits in the middle rather than at the top. Items 7 and 8 are claim categories and design-around cover, not distinctions. Items 9 and 10 are clear only because nothing was searched or charted against them, and each says so.

Nothing was listed that would need the inventor to add structure or a step. Deflation, the lock nut, other valve standards, CO2 use and calibration are gaps in the disclosure, not white space, and are turned into questions.

- Q7 Is there a way to set or adjust the opening pressure and the over-travel threshold during manufacture or assembly (a calibration step)? Nothing is disclosed; without an answer there is no calibration white space. Blocks P3.

- Q9 Does closing the lock nut prevent over-travel (01 Q22)? Bears on a storage and transport mode. Blocks P3.
- Q10 Is the valve core designed to retrofit standard valve stems, and is a retrofit kit contemplated (01 Q23)? Would support a kit claim under item 7. Blocks P3, P5.
- Q11 Which other valve standards (Schrader, Dunlop/Woods), inflatable bodies and uses outside bicycles does the inventor contemplate (01 Q12)? Blocks P3.

- Q15 How does the valve behave with a CO2 cartridge inflator, and is an austenite finish temperature below 5 °C enough margin (01 Q16)? Any CO2 use protocol depends on it. Blocks P3, P4.

#### US

- **Enablement and written description, 35 U.S.C. §112(a)** — the only built embodiment describes a purge gap that opens between the outer surface 38 of the seat ring 30 and the core bore 22 "when the seat ring expands" ([0031]; claims 16, 17, 40, 41), while the seat ring is held in that same bore by an interference fit ([0035], claim 15). Expanding outward against the bore closes that gap; it cannot open it. No outlet from the gap into the inflatable body is described. See F-30-1.
- **Full-scope enablement** — claims 1, 2 and 4 cover any "deflectable element" whose deflection opens any "purge gap", and [0116] calls the principle "one of general application". The only coherent teaching of the mechanism would have to come from the first embodiment, which F-30-1 puts in doubt. The scope of a genus claim must be enabled across its breadth (*Amgen Inc. v. Sanofi*, 598 U.S. 594 (2023)). See F-30-2.
- **Drawings, 35 U.S.C. §113 and 37 C.F.R. §1.81–1.84** — the description refers to FIG. 1 to FIG. 8 ([0018]–[0025]), but the package contains only a figure plan and drawing instructions, with no sheets. See F-30-3.
- **Claim construction and §112(f)** — "deflectable element" (claim 1) is a generic placeholder ("element") followed by functional language ("opened by deflection … closed by recovery"). An examiner may read it under §112(f), limiting it to the seat ring 30, the wiper ring 130 and their equivalents despite [0088]. Under broadest reasonable interpretation, "deflection" may also be read as "displacement", which brings displaced spring-loaded poppets into scope. See F-30-4.
- **Definiteness, §112(b)** — "sealant-accumulation surfaces" (claims 1, 2, 4) are defined by exposure to sealant "in use" ([0058]). The austenite finish temperature "below 5 °C" (claims 21, 38, 42) has no fixed measuring method ([0074]: "a thermal analysis method, for example differential scanning calorimetry"). See F-30-5 and F-30-7.
- **Multiple dependency, 37 C.F.R. §1.75(c)** — many multiple dependent claims depend on other multiple dependent claims (for example claim 8 on claim 6, claim 22 on claim 21, claim 49 on claim 48, claim 54 on claim 48). There are also multiple-dependent-claim and excess-claim fees for 60 claims. See F-30-8.
- **Paragraph numbering, 37 C.F.R. §1.52(b)(6)** — [0034a], [0094a], [0132a]. See F-30-9.
- **Eligibility, §101** — no issue identified; the claims are to a mechanical article and a physical method.
- **Priority** — none claimed in the package; nothing to assess.

### F-30-1 [critical] [enablement] Purge gap of the first embodiment cannot open by radial expansion, and has no outlet

- **Text attacked:** claim 16, "the purge gap (60) is annular and is formed between an outer surface (38) of the seat ring (30) and the core bore (22), and opens when the seat ring expands". Claim 17, "radial expansion of the seat ring, the radial expansion opening the purge gap (60)". The same wording is in claims 40 and 41. Also [0031], [0035], [0068], [0111] step 706 and claim 15 ("retained in the core bore (22) by an interference fit").
- **Attack (US §112(a), EPO Art. 83, UK s.14(3)):** the seat ring is pressed into the core bore with an interference of about 0.03 mm on diameter ([0035], [0109]), so its outer surface 38 already bears on the core bore 22. Radial expansion moves the outer surface outward, towards the core bore. The gap between them therefore narrows, or the contact pressure rises; it does not open. The text gives no other movement, such as inward contraction of part of the wall, axial lifting or a relief in the bore, that could open it. The seat ring also stands on the locating shoulder 24 ([0030]), and no path is described by which gas leaving the lower end of the gap reaches the inflatable body. The drawing instructions confirm this: the purge flow arrows "may end at the lower end of the gap" and no passage is to be drawn. The skilled person, working from the text, cannot build a purge gap that opens as claims 16, 17, 40 and 41 require. The Example 1 data ([0101]–[0103]) show that the tested cores worked; they do not show how.
- **Least narrowing action:** add the inventors' actual gap geometry to the description, and to FIG. 1 and FIG. 2, from the inventors' own sketches or records. That means stating which surfaces separate, in which direction, and how the gap communicates with the inflatable body. If the inventors cannot supply it, claims 16, 17, 40 and 41 must be deleted or recast. Claim 1 then depends on F-30-2.
- **Requires new disclosure:** yes. **Suggested target:** P4.

### F-30-2 [high] [enablement] Genus claims 1, 2 and 4 rest on a mechanism that is not coherently disclosed in any embodiment

- **Text attacked:** claim 1, "a deflectable element … a purge gap (60) … which is opened by deflection of the deflectable element". Also [0116], "The principle is one of general application", and [0094], "The geometry and mounting of the wiper ring 130 and the position of its purge gap 60 are not otherwise limited".
- **Attack:** claims 1, 2 and 4 cover every form of deflectable element and every purge gap location. There are two embodiments with a stated gap. In the first, the gap cannot open as described (F-30-1). In the third, the wiper ring embodiment, the gap location, mounting and thickness are "not stated" ([0094], [0094a]). Under *Amgen v Sanofi*, *Regeneron v Kymab* and Art. 83/84 EPC, a claim of this breadth needs a teaching the skilled person can extend across the genus without undue burden; this package offers a result and a label.
- **Least narrowing action:** supply the missing disclosure (F-30-1), plus at least one described purge gap topology for the wiper ring. Narrowing claim 1 to a stated gap topology is the fallback only if the inventors cannot supply it. That would give up every unstated gap location, and the human should decide.
- **Requires new disclosure:** yes. **Suggested target:** P4.

### F-30-3 [high] [formal] No drawings; the figure files are drafting instructions carrying internal references and damaging statements

- **Text attacked:** `figures.md` begins "### Figure Plan" and cites `CONFIRMED.md`, `P1/01`, blueprint section ids and "PF-1". It states "no purge gap topology is drawn, because the disclosure does not explain how the purge gap opens". `registry.md` is headed "### Figure Instructions" and contains "Draftsperson's note, not to appear on the filed sheet". The description refers to FIG. 1 to FIG. 8 in [0018]–[0025].
- **Attack:** no drawing sheet exists in the package. The US requires drawings where necessary for understanding (35 U.S.C. §113); a missing drawing at filing can cost the filing date or force a later date. The EPO applies Rule 56 EPC to missing drawings. The UK requires drawings referred to in the description (s.15). If either file were filed as it stands, it would put on the record an admission, in the applicant's own papers, that the disclosure does not explain how the purge gap opens. That is a sufficiency concession an opponent would quote.
- **Least narrowing action:** produce drawing sheets for FIG. 1 to FIG. 8. Match FIG. 1 to FIG. 3 to the inventors' sketches once supplied. Keep the figure plan and drawing instructions out of anything filed.
- **Requires new disclosure:** yes (the drawing content of the purge gap region). **Suggested target:** P4.

### F-30-4 [medium] [support] "Deflectable element" construction: §112(f) placeholder in the US, and a displacement reading under broadest reasonable interpretation

- **Text attacked:** claim 1, "a deflectable element", "opened by deflection of the deflectable element and closed by recovery of the deflectable element". Also [0048] and [0088].
- **Attack:** in the US, "element" plus function may be treated as a means-plus-function term. That confines claim 1 to the seat ring 30 and the wiper ring 130 and their equivalents; [0088]'s disclaimer does not bind the examiner or a court. Conversely, if §112(f) is not applied, broadest reasonable interpretation of "deflection" may cover rigid displacement, and claim 1 then reads on any spring-loaded second poppet (see F-32-5). The EPO (G 1/24) and the UK read [0048]'s "undeflected shape" into the term, so the risk is mainly in the US.
- **Least narrowing action:** put the [0048] meaning on the face of claim 1, for example "a deflectable element which is elastically deflected from an undeflected shape". This recites structure-in-function and excludes rigid displacement. It gives up nothing that is described. Add one sentence to [0048] listing the structural forms (tubular ring with convoluted wall; thin spring ring) as examples of the class.
- **Requires new disclosure:** no. **Suggested target:** P5.

### F-30-5 [medium] [formal] "Sealant-accumulation surfaces" is defined by use

- **Text attacked:** claim 1(c), "along sealant-accumulation surfaces bounding the purge gap". The same term is in claims 2 and 4. Also [0058], "exposed to sealant drawn into the valve in use and on which sealant film forms between inflations".
- **Attack:** in an apparatus claim, the term identifies surfaces by what happens to them in use. An examiner may call it unclear (§112(b), Art. 84), or give it no weight, because every surface bounding the gap qualifies. It then contributes nothing to novelty, which leaves the mechanism to carry the claim alone.
- **Least narrowing action:** in claims 1, 2 and 4, replace it with "along surfaces bounding the purge gap". The second half of [0058] already says the surfaces are identified "by their position and exposure … not … whether sealant film is present", and the sealant context can stay in the description. The phrasing is the Claims Architect's to choose.
- **Requires new disclosure:** no. **Suggested target:** P5.

### F-30-6 [medium] [support] Range claims extend to alternatives for which the description says no ranges are stated

- **Text attacked:** claim 19 (convolution amplitude) and claim 25 ("any one of claims 14 to 19 … a polymer seat ring"). Claims 30 to 34 ("any one of claims 1 to 29"/"1 to 32", which include claims 25 to 29) and claim 47. Also [0093], "The dimensions given in the section on ranges … are not stated for the polymer seat ring". [0124], "The dimensions and pressures of the section on ranges are those of the first embodiment and are not stated for the alternatives". [0104] and [0106].
- **Attack:** at the EPO, claims whose combinations the description disclaims invite an Art. 84 support objection. In prosecution, any amendment toward "polymer seat ring with amplitude 0.08–0.20 mm" meets the description's own statement that no such value is stated (Art. 123(2)). In the US, the original claims are their own written description, but the contrary statements in [0093] and [0124] will be quoted against them.
- **Least narrowing action:** restructure dependencies so that claims 19, 23, 30–34 and 47 hang from the superelastic seat ring claims (claims 20, 42) or from claim 1 directly. The combinations with claims 25–29 and 44–45 should not be recited. Alternatively, if the inventors confirm that the ranges also apply to the alternatives, correct [0093] and [0124].
- **Requires new disclosure:** no (dependency restructure); yes if the inventors extend the ranges. **Suggested target:** P5.

### F-30-7 [medium] [formal] Austenite finish temperature has no fixed measuring method

- **Text attacked:** claim 21, "an austenite finish temperature below 5 °C". The same limitation is in claims 38 and 42. Also [0074], "determined by a thermal analysis method, for example differential scanning calorimetry, unless stated otherwise", and [0128], "the measured austenite finish temperature … of the prototype are not recorded".
- **Attack:** Af depends on the method (DSC against bend-free-recovery, for example) and on the specimen state. "A thermal analysis method, for example" leaves the parameter open, so the boundary of the claim is uncertain (Art. 84 EPC, Guidelines F-IV 4.11; §112(b); s.14(5)(b)). The prototype's Af is not recorded, so nothing anchors the value.
- **Least narrowing action:** name a single measuring method in [0074], for example DSC on the finished seat ring. Only the inventors or their supplier can say which method specified the prototype material.
- **Requires new disclosure:** yes (the method the inventors used to specify Af). **Suggested target:** P4.

### F-30-8 [medium] [formal] US multiple dependency on multiple dependent claims; claim fees in all three offices

- **Text attacked:** claim 8, "any one of claims 1 to 7", which includes claim 6 ("claim 1 or claim 5"). Claim 22, "any one of claims 10, 20 and 21", with claim 21 on "claim 10 or claim 20". Claim 49, "any one of claims 5 to 34 and 48". Claim 54, "any one of claims 1, 5 to 34 and 48". Also claims 9–12, 30–34, 37–39, 46–47, 50–51, 53, 55–56 and 58.
- **Attack:** 37 C.F.R. §1.75(c) forbids a multiple dependent claim serving as a basis for another multiple dependent claim. The US claim set needs a preliminary amendment. The multiple-dependent-claim fee applies, and each dependency counts for excess-claim fees. At the EPO, 60 claims pay fees for claims 16–50 and the higher rate for 51–60. The UK charges an excess claims fee beyond 25.
- **Least narrowing action:** file a US-specific claim set with single dependencies, using the EPO set as filed. Counsel to decide the number of claims per office.
- **Requires new disclosure:** no. **Suggested target:** in-place.

### F-30-9 [low] [formal] Lettered paragraph numbers

- **Text attacked:** [0034a], [0094a], [0132a].
- **Attack:** 37 C.F.R. §1.52(b)(6) requires consecutive four-digit paragraph numbering. Lettered numbers are non-standard at every office.
- **Least narrowing action:** renumber the description consecutively and update internal cross-references.
- **Requires new disclosure:** no. **Suggested target:** in-place.

### F-30-10 [medium] [enablement] The evidence that over-travel and purge flow occurred is an audible snap

- **Text attacked:** [0102], "Over-travel was confirmed on every inflation … by an audible snap on removal of the inflation device 84". Also [0111], "The point within an inflation at which over-travel occurs is not stated", and [0131], "that attribution is their statement rather than a measured result". Claim 1(c) and claim 4.
- **Attack:** the over-travel threshold is a pressure differential across the valve member ([0039], [0042]), 3.5 bar in the prototype. During floor-pump inflation to 6 bar the differential across the valve was not measured. A snap on removing the pump head is not evidence that 3.5 bar of differential was exceeded, nor that gas passed through the purge gap. At the EPO, an effect relied on for inventive step must be derivable from the application as filed (G 2/21). Here the effect ("purge themselves", [0017]) rests on the inventors' attribution. That weakens the inventive-step case (F-32-5). It does not by itself defeat sufficiency.
- **Least narrowing action:** if the inventors have any measurement of the differential, travel or flow during inflation, add it to Example 1. Otherwise keep the effect statements expressly tied to the bench outcome, as [0131] already does. Nothing should be invented.
- **Requires new disclosure:** yes, if a measurement exists. **Suggested target:** P4.

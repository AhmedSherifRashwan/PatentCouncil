### F-38-1 [high] [enablement] Example 1 is internally impossible as written

**Paragraphs cited.** [0099]: "one inflation cycle being: inflation to 6 bar ...; a hold of 24 hours at a temperature of 22 °C; and full deflation ... The test ran from 2026-08-12 to 2026-08-20." [0102]: "All three valve cores 90 according to the first embodiment completed forty inflation cycles". Table 1 [0101], row C-2: cycles to first failure "12", failure mode "leak-down of 0.7 bar", last measured cycle "11", leak-down at last measured cycle "0.7".

**Attack.** There are two separate problems.

1. **The dates cannot hold 40 cycles.** Forty consecutive cycles, each with a 24-hour hold, take at least forty days on each valve core. The stated test window is eight days. Running the cores in parallel does not help, because each core's cycles are sequential.
2. **Row C-2 contradicts the failure criterion.** A leak-down of 0.7 bar at cycle 11 exceeds the 0.5 bar criterion of [0100], so C-2 would have failed at cycle 11, not cycle 12. The alternative is that the "last measured cycle" column means something the description does not say.

Example 1 is the only actual example. It is the sole evidence for the forty-cycle statements in [0017], [0102], [0129] and [0131], and for the rest-state sealing of the purge gap in [0132a].

An opponent will use the date arithmetic to impeach the whole record. In the US, a filed misstatement of experimental fact raises a duty-of-candour exposure. After filing, the error can be corrected only where both the error and its correction are obvious (EPC Rule 139). Here the correct dates or hold time are not derivable from the text, so the error could not be corrected later. It has to be fixed before filing.

**Least narrowing action.** Obtain the actual test record from the inventor and correct:
- the dates in [0099], or the hold duration in [0099], [0100] and [0059];
- the C-2 row of Table 1 and the column headings, so that the failure cycle, the failure value and the "last measured cycle" are consistent with the failure criterion.

No claim change is needed. This requires new disclosure, because the true record is not in the package. Target: P4.

### F-38-2 [medium] [enablement] The summary states a cleaning effect that the only data partly contradict

**Paragraphs cited.** [0007]: "The valves described herein remove sealant film that has already formed". [0017]: "The valves described herein purge themselves of sealant film at every inflation in which the over-travel threshold is exceeded". Against these, [0103]: "Cured sealant film was present on the outer surface 38 of each seat ring 30 in the region of the purge gap 60". [0131] repeats the observation.

**Attack.** The sealant-accumulation surfaces include "the surfaces bounding the purge gap" ([0037], [0058]). The description's own inspection found cured film on exactly those surfaces after forty cycles. Its unqualified statements that the valves remove, or purge themselves of, sealant film are therefore contradicted for part of the claimed effect.

At the EPO, an examiner applying problem-solution will say that the effect of purge flow on film is not credibly shown across the claim scope. The examiner will reformulate the problem as a less ambitious one, which weakens inventive step. In litigation, the contradiction is used to attack the purge flow as the claimed mechanism. [0010] and [0131] are carefully qualified. [0007] and [0017] are not.

**Least narrowing action.** Recast [0007] and [0017] to state what the valve does (purge flow passes along the sealant-accumulation surfaces; the recovery wipe slides across the sealing face) and what Example 1 observed (a clean upper edge 32 and sealing face 42, with film remaining on the outer surface 38). Do not state removal of film as a result. See Corrections 1 and 2.

No claim change is needed, because the claims recite the flow and not a result (see [0047]: "does not, of itself, state any result"). No new disclosure is needed. Target: P4.

### F-38-3 [medium] [support] Two definitions import dependent-claim features into independent-claim terms

**Paragraphs cited.**
- [0050]: "'sealing face' means the surface of the valve member that seals against the valve seat when the valve member is seated, and across which the deflectable element slides in the recovery wipe."
- [0079]: "'inflatable body' means a body that is inflated through the valve, retains the inflation gas, and contains the sealant".

**Attack.** In the US, an express "means" definition controls the claim term (lexicography).

- **Sealing face.** Claim 1 recites "a valve member (40) having a sealing face (42)". Through [0050], that sealing face must be one across which the deflectable element slides in the recovery wipe. This imports claim 5 into claims 1, 2 and 4, and leaves claims 5, 35 and 52 without effect. A competitor whose deflectable element does not slide across the sealing face falls outside claim 1 in the US. An example is a wiper ring placed clear of the sealing face, which still opens a purge gap by over-travel.
- **Inflatable body.** Through [0079], claim 3 ("An inflatable body (80) fitted with a valve") requires a body that "contains the sealant". That makes claim 50 redundant and lets a tube supplied dry, with the sealant added by the user, escape direct infringement of claim 3.

**Least narrowing action.** Keep each definition to what the term is. Move the recovery wipe to a conditional sentence, and make the sealant a condition of use. See Corrections 5 and 6. Support is unchanged: [0029], [0055] and [0079] already describe the same things. No new disclosure is needed. Target: P4 (mechanical).

### F-38-4 [medium] [support] "The valves described herein" statements exclude viable embodiments

**Paragraphs cited.**
- [0007]: "The valves described herein remove sealant film ... by the action of one moving part, with no tool, no action by the rider, no stored gas and no valve additional to the valve member."
- [0017]: "so the valve needs no stored gas and no valve additional to the valve member, and the whole mechanism is contained in the valve core."

**Attack.** These are general statements about "the valves described herein", not about an embodiment. In the US they are the language from which disavowal of claim scope is argued. Three consequences follow:

- **An added valve.** A competitor who adds a second valve in series, such as a tyre-side one-way lip of the kind reference 2 describes, can argue that the claims were disavowed for valves having "a valve additional to the valve member". This is despite "comprising" ([0080]).
- **One moving part.** "By the action of one moving part" is also inaccurate on the description's own terms. The deflectable element and the biasing member move too ([0034], [0111]).
- **The whole mechanism in the valve core.** This sits against claim 1, which does not require a valve core, and against [0122], which retains the seat ring by "clamping between the core body 20 and the valve stem 10".

**Least narrowing action.** Recast these statements as statements about the embodiments described, and drop "one moving part". See Corrections 1 and 4. No claim change and no new disclosure are needed. Target: P4 (mechanical).

### F-38-5 [medium] [support] The description disclaims the ranges for the alternatives, yet claims 30–34 and 47 are claimed in combination with them

**Paragraphs cited.**
- [0093]: "The dimensions given in the section on ranges are those of the seat ring of superelastic alloy and are not stated for the polymer seat ring."
- [0106]: "No dimension is stated for the wiper ring 130."
- [0124]: "The dimensions and pressures of the section on ranges are those of the first embodiment and are not stated for the alternatives of this section."
- [0125]: "The following values are those of the seat ring 30 of superelastic alloy of the prototype".

**Claims affected.**
- Claims 30 (purge gap width), 31 (over-travel), 32 and 33 (opening pressure, over-travel threshold) and 34 all depend on "any one of claims 1 to 29" (or 30 to 33). That brings in claims 25 and 26 (polymer seat ring) and claims 27 to 29 (wiper ring).
- Claim 47 depends on claims 44 (polymer seat ring) and 45 (wiper ring).

**Attack.** For those combinations the description expressly says that the ranges are not stated. Under EPC Art. 84, the claims are not supported by a description that denies the combination. Under 35 U.S.C. § 112(a), the written description for, for example, "a polymer seat ring" with "a purge gap width ... from 0.10 mm to 0.25 mm" is contradicted by the description itself.

The originally filed claims are themselves disclosure, so there is no added-matter problem at filing. But an examiner will object to the inconsistency, and an opponent will cite the description's own denial.

The pressures in claims 32 to 34 and 47 are values of the pressure differential, set by the biasing member ([0033], [0040], [0042]). [0127] says: "In every embodiment the over-travel threshold is higher than the opening pressure". It is therefore unclear whether [0124] and [0125] were meant to deny the pressure ranges to the other embodiments at all.

**Least narrowing action.**
1. Ask the inventor whether the opening pressure, the over-travel threshold, the over-travel and the purge gap width ranges apply to the polymer seat ring and to the wiper ring.
2. If they do, amend [0093], [0106], [0124] and [0125] to say so. That keeps every claim combination.
3. If they do not, restrict the dependencies of claims 30 to 34 and 47 to exclude claims 25 to 29 and 44 to 45. That narrowing loses only those combinations.

The pressures are the likeliest to be confirmable. This requires new disclosure, because the answer is the inventor's. Target: P4, then P5.

The practical effect is small, because reference 1 (US11338632B2) already shows a sprung valve member lifting from a seat. So the severity is low. But the phrase adds nothing the claims need.

**Least narrowing action.** Replace it with "operates without purge flow". See Corrections 3 and 7 to 10. No new disclosure is needed. In place.

### F-38-7 [low] [support] The summary of the method is narrower than claim 4

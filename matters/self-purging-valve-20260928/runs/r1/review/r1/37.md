### F-37-1 [high] [formal] Claim 4: the distinguishing steps are conditional, so the method reads on any inflation of any valve

**Claim language.** Claim 4: "applying, by the inflation device, a pressure differential across the valve; when the pressure differential exceeds an opening pressure (506), the valve member making a normal stroke (510) ...; when the pressure differential exceeds an over-travel threshold (508) which is higher than the opening pressure, the valve member making an over-travel (512) ...; while the purge gap is open, passing purge flow (64) ...".

**Attack.** Under the broadest reasonable interpretation, the USPTO treats a method step that is performed only "when" a condition occurs as not required, if the method can be practised without the condition ever arising (*Ex parte Schulhauser*, PTAB precedential, 2016; MPEP 2111.04 II). Claim 4 positively requires only that a pressure differential is applied across a valve by an inflation device. The over-travel, the deflection, the opening of the purge gap and the purge flow are all tied to a threshold being exceeded. The claim never requires that threshold to be exceeded.

An examiner can therefore read claim 4 on any inflation of a tyre valve by a pump in which the second threshold is not reached. The inflation valve of reference 1 (US11338632B2), inflated through its valve body, is enough. This is a credible anticipation rejection in the US, and it needs an amendment to overcome.

At the EPO and UKIPO the claim would be read as a whole, and the risk there is lower.

Claims 52 to 56 inherit the defect. Claim 56 ("at each inflation cycle in which the pressure differential ... exceeds the over-travel threshold") is acceptable only once claim 4 positively recites the exceedance.

**Least narrowing action.** Recite the rise of the pressure differential as a positive step, and turn the two "when" clauses into consequences of it. The corrections below do this. Scope is unchanged, because the preamble ("A method (700) of purging") and [0015] already describe a method in which over-travel occurs.

Support: [0015], [0111], FIG. 7 steps 702 and 704. No new disclosure is needed. In place (claims drafter).

### F-37-2 [medium] [formal] Claims 8–12 and 30–33 depend on claims of another category

**Claim language.** Claim 8: "The valve of any one of claims 1 to 7". Claim 12: "The valve of any one of claims 1 to 11". Claim 30: "The valve of any one of claims 1 to 29". Claims 9, 10, 11, 31, 32 and 33 follow the same pattern.

**Attack.** Each range includes:

- claim 2, a valve core "for a valve";
- claim 3, an inflatable body;
- claim 4, a method.

"The valve of claim 4" might mean the valve used in the method, or it might be a claim to the method. "The valve of claim 2" has no valve in claim 2 to refer to. "The valve of claim 3" loops back through claim 1.

This draws an objection under EPC Art. 84 and UK Patents Act s.14(5)(b) for lack of clarity. In the US it draws an objection that a claim of one statutory class depends on a claim of another class (35 U.S.C. § 112(b) and (d)). The drafter's intent is plain from claims 49 and 54, which list "claims 1, 5 to 34 and 48" and so skip claims 2 to 4.

**Least narrowing action.** Change each range to "claim 1 or any one of claims 5 to N" (see Corrections). No combination that the claims were meant to cover is lost. The valve-core family already has its own dependants, claims 35 to 47. No new disclosure is needed. In place.

### F-37-3 [medium] [formal] Claims 25 and 44 each include a self-contradictory branch

**Claim language.** Claim 25 ("The valve of any one of claims 14 to 19, wherein the seat ring (30) is a polymer seat ring") reaches claim 10 ("the deflectable element is of a superelastic alloy") through claim 14, then claim 12, then claim 10. Claim 44 ("The valve core of any one of claims 39 to 41, wherein the seat ring (30) is a polymer seat ring") reaches claim 38 ("the deflectable element is of a superelastic alloy having an austenite finish temperature below 5 °C") through claim 39, then claim 38.

**Attack.** In those branches the one seat ring has to be both of a superelastic alloy and a polymer seat ring. [0097] states that these are alternatives. A claim whose dependency chain includes a contradictory combination is unclear under EPC Art. 84. In the US, that branch fails to further limit consistently (35 U.S.C. § 112(d)).

The defect is mechanical, and each claim still has consistent branches.

**Least narrowing action.** Exclude the contradictory branch with "as dependent directly or indirectly on ..." (see Corrections). Every consistent combination is kept. No new disclosure is needed. In place.

### F-37-4 [medium] [design-around] "Undeflected" in ranges (a) and (b) is an unnecessary and measurable limitation

**Claim language.** Claim 1 (a): "the deflectable element is undeflected and the purge gap is closed". Claim 1 (b): "the deflectable element remains undeflected and the purge gap remains closed". Claim 2 (a) and (b) are identical. Claim 4: "while the deflectable element remains undeflected and a purge gap (60) ... remains closed".

**Attack.** A competitor can make the deflectable element take a small elastic deflection during the normal stroke without opening the purge gap. Two ways to do this:

- a light preload contact between the purge shoulder and the element, or
- a seat ring that is compressed slightly by the seating load.

The competitor then argues that the element "remains undeflected" is not met. Deflection is measurable by the competitor and hard for the patentee to disprove.

[0048] defines the "undeflected shape" as the shape "in which the purge gap is closed", which helps. But that definition sits uneasily with "deflected" in its ordinary sense, and a court may give the claim word its ordinary meaning where the definition is circular. The distinction the claim actually relies on is already recited: the purge gap is closed below the over-travel threshold and is opened by deflection caused by over-travel.

**Least narrowing action.** Delete "the deflectable element remains undeflected and" from range (b) of claims 1 and 2. Delete "while the deflectable element remains undeflected and" from claim 4, and keep "a purge gap (60) ... remains closed". Keep "the deflectable element is undeflected" in range (a), where it is needed as the reference state for "recovers".

This slightly broadens the claims. Support: [0031], [0041] and [0046] describe the purge gap as closed throughout the normal stroke, and [0048] ties "undeflected" to that closure. This is a scope decision for the claims drafter and counsel, so no mechanical correction is proposed. No new disclosure is needed. Target: P5.

### F-37-5 [medium] [formal] "Deflectable element" invites a means-plus-function construction in the US

**Claim language.** Claim 1: "a deflectable element", which the claim then characterises only by function: the purge gap "is opened by deflection of the deflectable element and closed by recovery of the deflectable element".

**Attack.** Without the word "means" there is a presumption against § 112(f). It is rebutted where the claim uses a generic placeholder ("element") and the modifier conveys no definite structure (*Williamson v. Citrix*, Fed. Cir. 2015 (en banc); MPEP 2181 I.A lists "element" among such placeholders).

If § 112(f) applies, the scope becomes the structures described (the seat ring 30 with its convoluted wall 34, and the wiper ring 130) and their equivalents. That loses the genus that [0116] and [0134] claim. [0088] expressly disclaims a means-plus-function reading, but a construction statement in the description does not bind a court.

The risk is medium. "Deflectable" does connote a structural property, and claim 1 recites the element's structural relationship to the valve member and the purge gap.

**Least narrowing action.** Keep the term. Make sure the description already names the general structure: an element that is elastically deflectable, bounds the purge gap at least in part ([0046]) and is engaged by the valve member ([0043], [0054]). It does. Counsel may add "elastically" before "deflectable element" in claims 1, 2 and 4 if the US prosecution strategy calls for it, but that would exclude the thermal purge variant, which is not claimed. No correction is proposed now. No new disclosure is needed. Target: P5.

### F-37-6 [low] [formal] Claim 7: "below" has no reference direction in the claim

**Claim language.** Claim 7: "an annular step of the valve member (40) below the sealing face (42)".

**Attack.** "Below" is defined only in [0084], as towards the inflatable body. Read without the description, the claim is orientation-dependent. The EPO expects claims to be clear in themselves (Guidelines F-IV 4.2).

**Least narrowing action.** State the direction in the claim's own terms (see Corrections). Support: [0029], [0084]. No new disclosure is needed. In place.

### F-37-7 [low] [design-around] Claim 24 is a product-by-process fallback of little value

**Claim language.** Claim 24: "made by laser cutting (602) ..., shape-setting (604) ... at a temperature of 500 °C for a period of 10 minutes, water quenching (606) and electropolishing (608), in that order".

**Attack.** For novelty, the EPO and the USPTO read a product-by-process claim as the product. So claim 24 is no narrower than claim 20 against the art unless the route leaves a detectable structural signature, and the package states none. In infringement, the US limits such a claim to the recited process (*Abbott v. Sandoz*, Fed. Cir. 2009 (en banc)). A competitor can shape-set at 510 °C for 12 minutes, or air-cool, and avoid it. The fixed values ("500 °C", "10 minutes") are exact under [0086], which makes that easier.

**Least narrowing action.** Keep the claim as a fallback if counsel wants it. Better value would come from a separate process claim, "a method of making the seat ring (30)", with the values moved to dependants. Support: [0108], FIG. 6. No correction is proposed. No new disclosure is needed. Target: P5.

### F-32-1 [critical] [enablement] Radial expansion of an interference-fitted seat ring cannot open a gap against its own bore, and the gap has no outlet

- **Text attacked:** claim 16, "the purge gap (60) is annular and is formed between an outer surface (38) of the seat ring (30) and the core bore (22), and opens when the seat ring expands". Claim 17, "radial expansion of the seat ring, the radial expansion opening the purge gap (60)". The same wording is in claims 40 and 41. Also [0031], [0035] ("interference fit of approximately 0.03 mm on diameter"), [0109] and [0111] step 706.
- **Attack:** the seat ring's outer surface is pressed against the core bore by interference. Expanding it radially drives that surface further into the bore. As written, the gap claimed to open is the one the expansion closes. The ring stands on the locating shoulder 24, and nothing is said about how gas leaving the lower end of the gap reaches the inflatable body. The drawing instructions tell the draftsperson to draw no such passage. Claims 16, 17, 40 and 41 describe a mechanism that, taken at its word, cannot work. They fall for lack of enablement or sufficiency as drafted. Example 1 proves that some device worked; it does not teach how its gap opened.
- **Least narrowing action:** add the prototype's actual gap geometry and outlet path from the inventors' sketches or records, and draw it. Delete or recast these four claims only if that is unavailable.
- **Requires new disclosure:** yes. **Suggested target:** P4.

### F-32-2 [high] [enablement] The directions of seat, sealing face, purge shoulder and spring cannot all be true at once

- **Text attacked:** claim 7, "the purge shoulder (44) is an annular step of the valve member (40) below the sealing face (42)". Claim 14, "a seat ring (30) whose upper edge (32) provides the valve seat". Also [0033], "a compression spring … acting under the valve member". [0084] defines "upper" as towards the inflation device and "below" as towards the inflatable body. [0111]: "the purge shoulder 44 bears on the upper edge 32".
- **Attack:** inflation pressure acts from the upper side, so the valve member opens by moving down. For the sealing face to leave the seat by moving down, it must engage the seat from below, but the seat is the upward-facing upper edge of a tube. A spring "under" the valve member pushes it up, away from any upward-facing seat below it. The purge shoulder is "below the sealing face". After further downward travel it is further from the upper edge, yet it is said to bear on that edge. On any reading of [0084], the sealing face (closing direction) and the purge shoulder (opening direction) would have to load the same upper edge from opposite sides, while both are on one member with the shoulder below the face. The skilled person cannot reconstruct the first embodiment from these words. The drawing instructions for FIG. 1 add to the problem: they place the shoulder "below the sealing face" yet "above the upper edge" on which the sealing face seats.
- **Least narrowing action:** correct the orientation words to match the inventors' actual construction, for example which end of the seat ring is the seat and on which side the shoulder sits, from their sketches. Add one sentence stating the direction of the normal stroke relative to the upper edge. Claims 7, 14, 17, 39 and 41 follow the corrected words.
- **Requires new disclosure:** yes. **Suggested target:** P4.

### F-32-3 [high] [enablement] The wiper-ring purge gap lies below the rigid valve seat, so it is in series with the main flow passage, not in parallel

- **Text attacked:** claim 28, "the wiper ring (130) is positioned below the rigid valve seat (132), towards the inflatable body (80)". Claim 1, "a purge gap (60), which is a second path for inflation gas through the valve in parallel with the main flow passage". Also [0046], "gas passing through the purge gap does not pass between the sealing face and the valve seat". [0094a], "the manner in which the wiper ring 130 is mounted … not stated herein".
- **Attack:** in the third embodiment all inflation gas enters from above, and the only described way past the rigid valve seat is between it and the sealing face. A gap bounded by a wiper ring below that seat can only receive gas that has already passed the seat. That is series flow, which [0046] excludes from "purge gap". No bypass around the rigid valve seat is described. Claims 27 to 29, 45, 58 and 60, read with claim 1, are internally contradictory and not enabled.
- **Least narrowing action:** add a described inlet for the purge gap that bypasses the rigid valve seat (for example a passage in the core body, if the inventors conceived one). Otherwise delete the wiper-ring claims, or reframe the third embodiment as founded on the recovery wipe ([0011]) without the parallel purge gap.
- **Requires new disclosure:** yes. **Suggested target:** P4.

### F-32-4 [high] [enablement] The genus is claimed on a principle with no coherently disclosed embodiment

- **Text attacked:** claim 1, "a deflectable element … a purge gap (60) … opened by deflection of the deflectable element and closed by recovery". The same features are in claims 2 and 4. Also [0116], "The principle is one of general application", and [0026], "the direction in which the deflectable element deflects [is] not limited to what is shown".
- **Attack:** claim 1 covers every deflectable element, every gap location and every direction of deflection. The first embodiment's gap cannot open as described (F-32-1), its kinematics are incoherent (F-32-2), and the second embodiment is only a change of material ([0093]). The third embodiment's gap is in series and unlocated (F-32-3). What remains is a statement of result. An examiner will object to lack of support and sufficiency across the claimed breadth (Art. 83/84 EPC, §112(a), s.14(3)).
- **Least narrowing action:** cure F-32-1 to F-32-3 with disclosure. Claim 1 then stands on at least two coherent species. Narrowing claim 1 to a named gap topology is the only fallback if the inventors cannot supply that disclosure; it would give up all unstated topologies, and the human should decide.
- **Requires new disclosure:** yes. **Suggested target:** P4.

### F-32-5 [high] [novelty] Obvious over US20080188352A1 in view of US3417778A; under broadest reasonable interpretation, possible anticipation

- **Text attacked:** claim 1 as a whole, and in particular "an over-travel (512) of the valve member beyond the normal stroke … in which the over-travel of the valve member deflects the deflectable element, the purge gap opens". The same limitation is in claims 2 and 4, and claim 3 through claim 1.
- **Attack:** US20080188352A1 (ref. 4) discloses a check valve with a valve member (ball 3), a seat 10 and a spring 5. The ball's stroke is limited by surface 12 of shuttle 4, which is part of poppet 2 ([25]). At a second, higher pressure the poppet leaves seat 11 against spring 6, opening a second orifice. Flow through it passes "over the exterior of outer poppet 2", in parallel with the flow through seat 10 ([26], [31], [37]); the valve closes in stages as pressure falls ([38]). That gives an opening pressure, a normal stroke, a higher threshold, further travel of the same valve member (carried with the poppet) and a second parallel path that closes again. The difference is that poppet 2 is displaced rigidly rather than deflected in shape. US3417778A (ref. 3) supplies a resilient tubular seat element that is "radially expandable under fluid pressure" and flexed "radially outwardly" by the valve member (claim 1, [14]–[15]). An examiner will argue that replacing a spring-and-rigid-poppet second stage with a single resilient deflecting element is an obvious simplification. "For an inflatable body", "inflation gas" and "sealant-accumulation surfaces" add no structure (F-32-7). Under US broadest reasonable interpretation, "deflects" may be read as "displaces", and claim 1 is then exposed to anticipation by ref. 4 alone. Ref. 1 (US11338632B2, [0004]) supplies the tyre-valve context and the sealant-clogging problem.
- **Least narrowing action:** state in claim 1 what [0048] already defines: the deflectable element is "elastically deflected from an undeflected shape", and the purge gap is "bounded at least in part by a surface of the deflectable element" ([0046]). This excludes rigid displaced poppets and gives up no described embodiment. The inventive-step answer then rests on the single-element over-travel purge of the inflation gas, which needs the effect evidence of F-32-10.
- **Requires new disclosure:** no. **Suggested target:** P5.

### F-32-9 [medium] [novelty] The recovery wipe is known in kind from US4050473A and US4236547A

- **Text attacked:** claim 5, "the deflectable element slides across the sealing face (42) of the valve member (40) in a recovery wipe". The same limitation is in claims 35 and 52; claims 57 and 59 also carry it.
- **Attack:** ref. 5 (US4050473A, [13]) discloses a flexible element that, driven by valve-member motion, wipes the valve seat "as the flexible disk moves against and finally past the valve seat". Ref. 6 (US4236547A, [6]) discloses a cutting edge that "cleans the skirt portion of foreign matter" on closing. Wiping sealing surfaces by sliding contact on closing is therefore known. Adding it to claim 1 is an aggregation unless a synergy with the over-travel is shown, and [0103] and [0131] say the two contributions were not isolated. The sliding direction and contact portion are "not stated" ([0034a], [0055]), so the feature is broad but thinly supported.
- **Least narrowing action:** add, if the inventors can state it, how the recovery of the deflectable element produces the sliding in the first embodiment (the direction and which surfaces move). That is what distinguishes it from a valve-head wiper. Otherwise treat the wipe claims as secondary.
- **Requires new disclosure:** yes (only if the inventors can state it). **Suggested target:** P4.

### F-32-10 [medium] [enablement] The technical effect rests on an audible snap and the inventors' attribution

The examiner will reformulate the problem as "an alternative two-stage check valve", which makes F-32-5 easier.

- **Requires new disclosure:** yes, if a measurement exists. **Suggested target:** P4.

### F-32-14 [medium] [formal] Pressure thresholds are claimed with no measuring condition

- **Text attacked:** claim 1, "below an opening pressure (506) … above the over-travel threshold". Claim 33, "the over-travel threshold is from 2.5 bar to 5.0 bar". Claims 32, 34 and 47. Also [0039] and [0042].
- **Attack:** [0039] fixes the quantity (the differential across the valve member) but not the condition: static or quasi-static loading, or flowing gas at a given rate. Under flow, the differential on the valve member depends on the flow rate and on the geometry of the main flow passage. The numerical claims 32–34 and 47 therefore have uncertain boundaries (Art. 84, §112(b)).
- **Least narrowing action:** state in [0039] the condition under which the values are determined, for example a static differential with no flow. Only the inventors can say how the prototype values were set, so this needs their answer.
- **Requires new disclosure:** yes. **Suggested target:** P4.

### F-32-15 [medium] [enablement] "Undeflected" at rest, although the seating load bears on the upper edge that converts axial load into expansion

- **Text attacked:** claim 1(a), "a rest state (514) in which the valve member is seated on the valve seat, the deflectable element is undeflected". Also [0030], "an axial load applied to the upper edge 32 is converted into radial expansion", and [0044], which says the rest state includes "the inflatable body holds its pressure".
- **Attack:** in the first embodiment the sealing face seats on the upper edge under the biasing member and under the inflatable body's pressure (up to 6 bar in Example 1). By [0030] that axial load deflects the convoluted wall. Claim 1(a) requires the element to be undeflected, so the claim either excludes the first embodiment or "undeflected" is unclear. The text gives no tolerance or reference condition.
- **Least narrowing action:** define "undeflected" in [0048] as the shape in which the purge gap is closed, whatever seating load acts, and use "the purge gap is closed" as the operative rest-state limitation. [0048] already ties the undeflected shape to the closed gap, so this is a clarification.
- **Requires new disclosure:** no. **Suggested target:** P4.

### F-32-17 [low] [formal] The method recites events and parts' behaviour rather than steps

## Assumptions

A1. This reply is an AI drafting and analysis aid, not legal advice. Qualified patent counsel signs off before filing.

A2. The package has no abstract, no drawing sheets and no priority document. `figures.md` and `registry.md` are read as what would currently be filed for the figures.

- (i) A member expanding radially inside a fixed bore narrows, not widens, the gap between them.

### F-28-1 [high] [enablement] The first embodiment's stated geometry cannot perform the claimed over-travel

Claims 1(c), 2(c) and 4 ("the over-travel of the valve member deflects the deflectable element"), claim 7 ("an annular step of the valve member (40) below the sealing face (42)"), claims 14, 17, 39, 41, 53, 57 and 59.

Attack. [0084] fixes "upper" as towards the inflation device and "below" as towards the inflatable body. [0030] makes the upper edge 32 the valve seat on which the sealing face 42 seals. [0029] and [0054] place the purge shoulder 44 "below the sealing face". [0111] step 706 and [0034a] have the purge shoulder bear on the upper edge 32, applying an axial load that the convoluted wall converts into radial expansion. When the valve is seated, a shoulder below the sealing face is below the upper edge. It can reach the upper edge only by moving upwards, towards the inflation device. Moving upwards is against the pressure differential, which [0039] defines as higher on the inflation-device side. A load applied from below would also lift the seat ring off the locating shoulder 24 rather than compress it. If the valve member instead moves downwards, as the drawing instructions for FIG. 2 require, the shoulder moves further from the upper edge and cannot bear on it. The direction of the normal stroke is stated nowhere in the description. Nor does it explain how inflation pressure acting from above lifts a sealing face that seals on the top of a ring below it. An examiner would say the only built embodiment is not described so that the skilled person can carry it out (EPO Art. 83, 35 U.S.C. §112(a) enablement, UK s.14(3)), and that the example data of [0099] to [0103] cannot be tied to the claimed mechanism. The functional claims 1, 2 and 4 do not recite this geometry, so they do not fall as drafted. Their only working embodiment is incoherent, though, and claims 7, 14, 17, 39 and 41 recite the arrangement itself.

Least narrowing action. Add disclosure, not narrowing. The inventors need to state the actual arrangement: the direction in which the valve member moves in the normal stroke; which side of the seat ring the sealing face seals on; where the purge shoulder lies relative to the sealing face and the upper edge; and in which direction it loads the seat ring. Then align [0029], [0030], [0054], [0084], [0111] and the FIG. 1 and FIG. 2 instructions with that statement. If the prototype's arrangement differs from these words, claims 7, 14 and 17 need rewording to match it. That rewording does not narrow them. New disclosure is required, and it must come from the inventors or the prototype drawings, not from drafting.

### F-28-2 [high] [enablement] The purge gap cannot open where claims 16 and 40 put it, and its outlet is not described

Claim 16 / 40: "the purge gap (60) is annular and is formed between an outer surface (38) of the seat ring (30) and the core bore (22), and opens when the seat ring expands". Claim 15: "retained in the core bore (22) by an interference fit". Claim 9: "outward radial deflection". Claim 1(c): purge flow "into the inflatable body".

Attack. [0031] and [0126] say the gap lies between the outer surface 38 and the core bore 22 and opens when the seat ring expands. [0048] says the deflection is outward and radial. [0035] and [0068] retain the seat ring in the core bore by an interference fit of 0.03 mm on diameter. Outward expansion of a ring already pressed into a bore moves its outer surface towards the bore, not away from it. That closes or tightens any gap on that surface; it cannot open one. [0064] leaves the direction of the convolution open, but "radial expansion" is stated throughout ([0030], [0095], [0111] step 706). Separately, the seat ring stands on the locating shoulder 24, an internal step ([0028]), and nothing describes how gas leaving the lower end of that annular gap passes the shoulder into the inflatable body. [0046] says only that the gap "may communicate at its ends" with those spaces. Claim 16, through claim 15, recites the interference fit and the opening-on-expansion together, which is the contradictory combination itself. An examiner would object under EPO Art. 83 and Art. 84 and US §112(a) and (b). At opposition this is the attack most likely to succeed against the dependent fallback claims.

Least narrowing action. Add disclosure. The inventors need to state three things: where along the seat ring the interference fit acts and where the outer surface 38 is in clearance; which surface of the seat ring moves away from which surface of the core body when the ring is loaded; and the passage by which purge flow leaves the gap past the locating shoulder 24 (for example a slot, a flat or a castellation, if that is what the prototype has). If the gap actually opens by inward movement, or at the upper edge, correct [0031], [0048], [0126] and claims 9, 16 and 40 to match. New disclosure is required.

### F-28-3 [high] [support] A wiper ring below the rigid valve seat is not described with a purge gap in parallel with the main flow passage

Claim 27: "the deflectable element is a wiper ring (130) which is separate from the rigid valve seat"; claim 28 / 45: "positioned below the rigid valve seat (132), towards the inflatable body (80)". Read with claim 1: purge gap "a second path for inflation gas through the valve in parallel with the main flow passage". Also claims 29, 58 and 60.

Attack. [0046] defines "separate from the main flow passage" as gas that "does not pass between the sealing face and the valve seat". In the third embodiment the wiper ring sits below the rigid valve seat ([0053], [0094]), so any gas that reaches it has already passed between the sealing face and the rigid valve seat. The purge gap is then in series with the main flow passage, which is what [0032] and [0046] exclude. [0094] and [0094a] also say the position of the purge gap, the mounting of the wiper ring and its geometry are "not otherwise limited" or "not stated", and FIG. 3 is drawn schematically. The combination recited by claims 27 and 28 with claim 1 is therefore not disclosed. Worse, the text points against it. An examiner would object for lack of support (EPO Art. 84; §112(a) written description). As a reply to a rejection, a combination of claim 1 with claim 28 would face an added-matter objection (Art. 123(2)). The same defect weakens the genus of claim 1, which relies on the wiper ring as its second species (F-28-5).

Least narrowing action. Add disclosure. The inventors need to state where the purge gap of the third embodiment lies and how inflation gas reaches it without passing the rigid valve seat 132: for example, a passage in the core body 20 that bypasses the rigid valve seat and is closed by the wiper ring 130 at rest. If no such path exists in their conception, the third embodiment supports only the recovery-wipe aspect of [0011], not claim 1. In that case claims 27 to 29, 45, 58 and 60 should hang from a recovery-wipe claim, which is narrowing and is the human's decision. New disclosure is required.

### F-28-4 [high] [support] Ranges disclosed for the first embodiment are claimed for embodiments the description expressly leaves without them

Claims 30 ("purge gap width ... from 0.10 mm to 0.25 mm"), 31 ("from 0.4 mm to 0.9 mm"), 32 ("from 0.8 bar to 1.5 bar"), 33 ("from 2.5 bar to 5.0 bar") and 34, each "of any one of claims 1 to …" and so dependent from claims 25 to 29. ### F-28-5 [medium] [support] The breadth of "deflectable element" and "purge gap" rests on a principle plus two species with open mechanisms

Claims 1, 2 and 4: "a deflectable element", "a purge gap (60) ... opened by deflection of the deflectable element".

Attack. The genus is supported by the statement of principle in [0116] and the definitions in [0046] and [0048], which disclaim position, parts and direction. Behind them sit two species: the seat ring (F-28-1, F-28-2) and the wiper ring (F-28-3). For each species, how the purge gap actually opens and where it leads is unresolved on the text. An examiner arguing lack of support across the whole scope (Art. 84; §112(a) written description for a functional genus) would say that the application teaches a result, a gap opened by deflection, without a single coherent way of achieving it. This is weaker than F-28-1 to F-28-3 because the claims are functional and a skilled person may know ways to open a bypass by deflection. It becomes high if those findings are not fixed.

Least narrowing action. Fix F-28-1 to F-28-3. Then add to [0116] any further purge gap arrangements the inventors themselves conceive; generated alternatives must not be added. No claim narrowing. New disclosure is required.

### F-28-6 [medium] [enablement] The only evidence of over-travel is a snap on removal, not purge flow during inflation

Claim 4: "while the purge gap is open, passing purge flow (64) of the inflation gas supplied by the inflation device ... in addition to the inflation gas passing along the main flow passage"; claims 1(c), 55 and 56.

Attack. [0102] and [0115] say over-travel "was confirmed ... by an audible snap on removal of the inflation device", and [0111] says "the point within an inflation at which over-travel occurs is not stated". The claims require over-travel, an open purge gap and purge flow while the inflation device supplies gas. The over-travel threshold of the prototype is 3.5 bar of pressure differential ([0042]), and the floor pump inflated to 6 bar ([0099]). Nothing states that the pump applied a differential of 3.5 bar at any moment, or when. A snap at the moment the device is removed is equally consistent with recovery from a deflection caused otherwise, for example by the pump head. An opponent would say the data of Example 1 do not show the claimed purge flow. That is a credibility attack on the examples, not on enablement as such, which is why this is medium.

Least narrowing action. The inventors should state what they observed about when over-travel occurs during pumping, and whether the differential was measured. If they have no observation, add a sentence saying so, so that [0102] is not read as proving purge flow. New disclosure is required, and only an actual observation may be recorded.
